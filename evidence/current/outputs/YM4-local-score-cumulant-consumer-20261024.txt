RESULT AND STATUS

The finite ordinary C^{1,1} consumer need not use the sup norm of an
extensive directional score. Exponential bounded-cylinder mixing gives
a summable third-cumulant bound for two quasilocal score roots and one
test with finite complete noise read. This removes ambient torus volume
from that part of the second normalized derivative. A local mixed
third derivative of the action is needed for the other term and is
supplied by the separately audited native calculation in Section6.
That hypothesis is stated below and is NOT inferred from a second
background derivative bound alone. Sections1--5 keep the finite lemma
and its original input boundary explicit. Section6 records its now
independently audited registration for the actual second-success law.

1. PRECISE FINITE REFERENCE PREMISES

Let Q_theta be a positive normalized law on a fixed product of real
color balls |x_i|<=R, with smooth Hamiltonian H(x;theta). Index sets
are subsets of fixed-dimensional lattices with uniformly bounded
local density. Each q footprint hat q has uniformly bounded diameter
and incidence, as for the native fixed-grade stencils; alternatively
assume the exponential root-sum bounds used below directly. All
distances include the fixed native scale conversion.
Let Q have the following two estimates, uniformly in the admitted
physical theta domain and finite torus:

 |Cov_Q(A,B)| <= C osc(A) osc(B) exp[-nu d(P_A,P_B)],       (1)

for bounded cylinder functions on arbitrary complete finite reads,
and

 |Cov_Q(A,B)| <= C sum_(i,j) a_i b_j exp[-nu d(i,j)],      (2)

when |partial_i A|<=a_i and |partial_j B|<=b_j. Color components
only change fixed constants. These are assumptions supplied, when
applicable, by the actual weighted-convex product reference. They
are not assumptions about a normalized hard-mask mixture.

Use the exact zero-noise convention H(0;theta)=0, with its subtracted
scalar retained in the outer density. Put S_q=partial_(theta_q) H.
Assume

 |partial_i S_q| <= A0 exp[-mu d(i,hat q)],               (3)

where hat q is the fixed footprint of a coarse parameter root. Thus
S_q(0)=0. A harmless parameter-only score constant can always be
removed for the covariances/cumulants below, without dropping its
outer density contribution. Put A=C R A0 after fixed geometric sums.

For the final second-derivative consumer we also require

 sup_i sum_(q,r) |partial_i partial_(theta_q theta_r) H|
                                      <= B,             (4)

uniformly on the whole fixed product and parameter domain. A bound
by B0 exp[-mu length(tree(i,hat q,hat r))] implies (4), but that
stronger conclusion is not required. Finite sums here range over
all actual background coordinates. Bounds on H_(q,r) alone do NOT
imply (4). Neither (1) nor (3) is claimed to imply it.

2. THE THREE-CYLINDER CONNECTED ESTIMATE

For real bounded f,g,h, define cum(f,g,h)=Q[(f-Qf)(g-Qg)(h-Qh)].
Let their complete reads be T,P,Qset. Write

 a=d(T,P), b=d(T,Qset), c=d(P,Qset),
 L=min(a+b,a+c,b+c).

This is the minimum three-root tree length in the graph of these
three sets; no metric triangle inequality between sets is assumed.
Isolating any one centered factor gives an EXACT covariance with
the product of the other two centered factors. For example

 cum(f,g,h)=Cov(f,(g-Qg)(h-Qh)).

The product has read P union Qset and oscillation at most
2 osc(g)osc(h). Applying (1) in each of the three possible ways yields

 |cum(f,g,h)| <= C osc(f)osc(g)osc(h)
    exp[-nu max{min(a,b),min(a,c),min(b,c)}]
 <= C osc(f)osc(g)osc(h) exp[-nu L/2].                    (5)

The maximum in the first exponent is the middle of the three
nonnegative numbers a,b,c. Their two smallest sum to L, so L is at
most twice that middle value. This proves the last inequality.
No third-cumulant estimate is presumed or inferred from a product
of independent probabilities. Complex tests follow by decomposition.

3. QUASILOCAL SCORES AND THE TWO-ROOT SUM

Set S_q^[m](x)=S_q(x on B_m(hat q), 0 elsewhere). This is a
genuine cylinder because Q's coordinate domain is a product and
zeroing coordinates stays in it. Integrating (3) along the omitted
coordinates gives

 ||S_q-S_q^[m]||_infinity
       <=C R A0 (1+m)^(d-1) exp(-mu m)
       <=C A exp(-mu' m),                               (6)

for any fixed mu'<mu. The annular increments Delta_q,m, starting
with m=0, sum to S_q and have support B_m(hat q) and sup norm
C A exp(-mu' m). This is an absolutely convergent expansion,
including on finite periodic lattices; past the lattice diameter
the increments are zero. No independent random-field expansion
or change of Q is used.

For bounded f with finite read T, apply (5) to f,Delta_q,m,Delta_r,n.
Enlarging the last two roots by radii m,n reduces the three-root
tree length by at most 2(m+n), up to fixed footprint constants.
Choose lambda>0 small relative to nu and mu'. Absorb the resulting
exp[2 lambda(m+n)] in (6), then sum m,n. Thus

 |cum_Q(f,S_q,S_r)| <= C osc(f) A^2
   exp[-lambda min{d(T,hat q)+d(T,hat r),
                   d(T,hat q)+d(hat q,hat r),
                   d(T,hat r)+d(hat q,hat r)}].           (7)

The same approximation and (1) give

 |Cov_Q(f,S_q)| <= C osc(f) A exp[-lambda d(T,hat q)].    (8)

Both constants are independent of the finite torus. For nonempty T,
the elementary lattice sum sum_q exp[-lambda d(T,hat q)]<=C|T|
is sufficient. Since exp[-lambda min(u,v,w)] is at most the sum
of the three exponentials, (7)--(8) imply

 sum_q |Cov_Q(f,S_q)| <= C osc(f) A |T|,
 sum_(q,r)|cum_Q(f,S_q,S_r)|
                  <=C osc(f) A^2 (|T|+|T|^2).            (9)

The quadratic source-read cost is deliberately retained. There is
no count of the ambient lattice and no assumption that T is a
single point. All determining, reconstruction and source reads
must be included in T before these estimates are used.

4. THE COMPLETE NORMALIZED SECOND DERIVATIVE

Let f(x;theta) be C^{1,1} in theta and C^1 in x. Suppose f, f_v and
f_vw have complete x-read in the SAME finite set T for all fixed
sup-norm parameter directions |v|_infinity,|w|_infinity<=1, and

 |f|<=b0, |f_v|<=b1, |f_vw|<=b2,
 sum_i sup|partial_i f|<=L_f.                            (10)

The derivatives may be weak derivatives, with bounds a.e. A strict
image/parameter margin permits mollification, as in the admitted
rare real source consumer. Exact differentiation of the normalized
ratio on the FIXED original product gives

 D_v Qf=Qf_v-Cov(f,S_v),
 D_vw Qf=Qf_vw-Cov(f_v,S_w)-Cov(f_w,S_v)
                    -Cov(f,H_vw)+cum(f,S_v,S_w).         (11)

Using (4), |partial_i H_vw|<=B. Estimate (2) and its summable
kernel imply |Cov(f,H_vw)|<=C B L_f. Finite linearity followed
by (9) bounds the other score terms. Therefore

 |Qf|<=b0,
 |D_v Qf|<=b1+C b0 A |T|,
 |D_vw Qf|<=b2+C b1 A |T|+C B L_f
                         +C b0 A^2(|T|+|T|^2).         (12)

These are source-read rather than ambient-volume bounds. Each
term is included; no extensive score norm is asserted uniformly
bounded. Uniform mollified bounds and weak compactness pass (12)
to C^{1,1} inputs on a smaller parameter domain.
