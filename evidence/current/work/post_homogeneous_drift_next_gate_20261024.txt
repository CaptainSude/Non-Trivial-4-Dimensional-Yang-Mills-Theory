3. THE TUNED RARITY SUM AND THE MISSING MICROSCOPIC FACTOR

Let eta(x)=exp[-c(log x)^(2p)], p>1/2. For every fixed d,A>=0,

 sum_(t>=0) (1+X+t)^d eta(c0(X+t)) <= C_(d,A)(1+X)^-A    (8)

after increasing the weak floor. To check this, the exponent
c(log x)^(2p) eventually dominates (d+A+2)log x; sum the resulting
power tail. Constants in(1) change c0 and finite constants only.
Thus tuned rarity pays fixed inverse-g, read-size and date polynomials.

It does NOT pay an unmodified original microscopic position count:

 L^(4t)(1+X+t)^d eta(c0(X+t))

grows exponentially with t. The negative logarithmic power is o(t).
The actual original observable is

 O_a(f)=c_a sum_p f_p[1-(1/2)Tr U(partial p)],             (9)

with the registered c_a and NO inserted a^4. Replacing its finest
labels by present cells would change the observable and its census.

There is a useful, strictly STATIC conditional summability consumer.
Suppose a genuinely matched rare birth of age k, h=L^-k, has after
complete tensor extraction per-original-root bounds

 |marginal read| <= eta(x_b) P(x_b,k) h^4,
 |neutral read|  <= eta(x_b) P(x_b,k) h^(4+alpha),        (10)

and the actual root count per present cell is at most C h^-4.
Here P is a fixed polynomial including actual once-born radius/read
prices and the original source amplitude. Then the neutral age sum
costs only sum_k (1+k)^d L^-alpha k<infinity. Its birth-date sum is
bounded by(8). Even the rare marginal VALUE sum over the finitely many
ages k<=t costs only an additional polynomial (1+t)^(d+1), also paid
by(8). The marginal must still be recorded exactly; this estimate
does not identify it with a scalar Wilson coefficient.

The same bookkeeping tolerates a proved multiplicative feedback
1+C q_j: its product is bounded by a fixed power of the ratio of
backward distances, because sum q_j grows only logarithmically.
It does not tolerate an unproved fixed factor C>1 at every elapsed
date, or arbitrary repeated inverse-radius losses.

Equations(8)--(10) are elementary consumers of the declared bounds.
They supply no actual quantum or mixed-history matching. In particular
Q[F(Phi(x))] is not F(Phi(0)); centered truncated Gaussian fluctuations
already create a new flat scalar for a quadratic insertion, and more
general insertions change its marginal tensor. The new source's actual
quantum path and every raw contact have to be controlled before(10)
can be assigned to it.

5. WHY THIS IS NECESSARY BUT NOT THE WHOLE EXISTENCE PROOF

The exact law of total covariance retains emitted within-step contacts,
covariances of compiled means, and between-record contacts. A proved
first fresh-pair column and canonical outer-debt transport control their
stated emitted columns, not every later covariance of compiled means.
Original separated-source position sums already work for that admitted
first column because exp[-c separation/a] beats a^-4. This is a valid
alternative to engineering gain at early scales, not a theorem for the
remaining columns.

Conversely a global L2 estimate for the original energy is the wrong
shortcut: the actual Wilson lower bound grows as
|c_a|^2 a^-4/t_a^2. On t_a=O(log a^-1), it excludes bounded global
variance for a bounded nonzero calibration. Scalar centering cannot
repair that. The working target is calibrated separated/collision-flat
correlations and their exact contacts, not a newly normalized source.

After these source bounds one still needs a common continuum and
infinite-volume state, the required field-theory properties and
Yang--Mills identification, and a nonzero interaction witness in THAT
state. A nonzero regular-core alpha or a reference Gaussian coefficient
does not establish them. Mass gap, uniqueness and universality are not
added requirements.
