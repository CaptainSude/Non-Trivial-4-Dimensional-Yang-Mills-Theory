PURPOSE

At a FIXED finite prefix, compare two period-P homogeneous regular birth
representations with exactly equal total physical values on every admitted
periodic minimum. One is the actual native birth; the other uses the new
M-cell R cohort ledger. Their independent-current/local coefficient
extensions may depend on P without converging as functions.

A numerical equality of their affine Maxwell coefficients up to o(1)
as P tends to infinity can nevertheless follow from physical Hessians,
under the following uniform conditions. Constants may depend arbitrarily
on the fixed prefix, its positive radii, and its couplings, but not on P.

1. EXACT PHYSICAL EQUALITY AND THE SAME LINEAR LIFT

Use the SAME coarse perturbations, fine background representative, units,
old current convention, and physical minimum map for both totals. Let
L_P(q) be the linear fine-field Bloch lift of a coarse Fourier mode at
the flat background, and J_P(q) its linear physical-current response.
The mode q is allowed on the finite coarse torus. Native/new equality
holds for all small amplitudes of every such periodic mode, so their
physical Hessians are exactly equal at each allowed q.

For an individually gauge-invariant unmarked germ, the first derivative
at (I,0) vanishes by global color invariance on the semisimple Lie algebra.
Thus its second physical derivative contains D^2F applied to the LINEAR
field/current lift; no D F times the second minimum response remains.
If only the total has this property, the equality must be used at that
complete total, rather than asserted coefficientwise.

2. UNIFORM COMPLETE KERNEL MOMENT TAILS

Require complete carrier and root norms for the independent AA, AJ, JJ
Hessians on an actual flat complex ball, together with physical response
kernels whose composition gives a uniform second-position-moment TAIL:

 sup_P sum_(|r|>R) |r|^2 ||K_P(r)|| ->0 as R->infinity.

The corresponding zeroth/first moments and operator norms must also be
uniformly finite. This is stronger than a uniformly bounded second
moment. For example mass of order P^-2 moving to distance P has bounded
second moment but an order-one normalized Taylor error at q~P^-1.

An actual positive exponential complete-carrier bound plus fixed-prefix
field Cauchy estimates can supply these tails: every fine-coordinate
count is finite at the fixed prefix and must be absorbed in its explicit
constant; all source variable supports and their root moments remain.
The physical lift/current kernels need their own complete response
tails. Historical/computational dependence of a coefficient on P is not
itself an obstruction if its VARIABLE carrier and derivative bound have
the required uniform control. Whole wrapping coefficients stay under
their complete-carrier tail; they are not split into arbitrary path
fragments or replaced by an affine germ on a noninjective support.

These assumptions give a uniform o(q^2) Fourier Taylor remainder after
the Ward-zero orders have been removed. A uniform third moment gives
the more convenient O(|q|^3) remainder, but is not logically necessary.

3. INDEPENDENT-CURRENT WARD IDENTITY IS ESSENTIAL

It is not enough to state J_phys(q)=O(q^2 A). The mixed AJ Hessian could
then contribute at order q^2 if its A-slot symbol had a nonzero constant
term. Gauge invariance on the INDEPENDENT pair domain gives

                    D_AJ F(d phi,J)=0

at the flat background, because the first J derivative vanishes by
color invariance. With the local kernel/moment conditions this forces
the mixed symbol's zero-momentum A term to vanish. Hence its physical
contribution is o(q^2), or O(q^3) with the stronger moment control.
The JJ term is O(q^4). Finite torus Ward identities at only the allowed
momenta must be converted with the same uniform tail estimates; an
arbitrary extension of a discrete Fourier symbol off the allowed grid
is not automatically constrained.

The physical current lift must be proved O(q^2) with the actual fine-
cell/Bloch factors, rather than assumed from a continuum mnemonic.
The known flat constrained Maxwell response is the intended supplier.

4. IDENTIFY THE LEADING AA TENSOR WITH THE ACTUAL AFFINE EXTRACTOR

The constant and first-q expansion of the fine minimum lift must agree
with the native affine physical lift, including cell correctors, gauge
returns, ordinary averaging, and all mesh factors. Then the q^2 term
of the physical AA Hessian is the curvature quadratic tensor used by
the complete rooted affine extraction. Its coefficient is not inferred
merely from QH=I or a pointwise propagator estimate.

A quadratic physical Hessian cannot distinguish the epsilon F wedge F
null Lagrangian: for a single Fourier mode the curvature is q wedge e,
whose wedge with itself is zero. Thus a general full-tensor conclusion
needs a null-Lagrangian convention or an additional test. For the
complete unmarked regular family, the actual reflection covariance
forbids this pseudoscalar ambiguity and hypercubic symmetry reduces the
surviving marginal to the Maxwell scalar. No such symmetry is imposed
on an original marked observable.

Use enough directions/polarizations for the tensor statement being
claimed, or compare the one allowed scalar to the SAME fine Wilson
reference's physical Hessian. The latter reference has a nonzero
q^2 coefficient in a transverse direction, with the exact normalization
kept. A mismatch between fine and coarse Wilson references is not a
small error merely because q is small.

5. THE RESULTING NUMERICAL ARGUMENT

Under the preceding hypotheses, at q_P=2 pi/P in suitable transverse
directions each representation has

  Hess_phys^P(q_P)=beta_aff^P Hess_W^P(q_P)+o(q_P^2),

with remainders uniform in P. Exact equality of the two physical
Hessians and Hess_W^P(q_P)/q_P^2 ->c_W>0 imply

                 beta_aff,new^P-beta_aff,native^P ->0.

Combine this with the separately registered actual native finite-
physical affine defect to identify the new coefficient with the native
coupling label numerically. No convergence of D_E^low or of the new
independent-current source germs is used. Vacuum constants disappear
from the Hessian but remain in the exact scalar ledger elsewhere.

For a causal finite-prefix proof, the moment/admission bounds at step j
must follow from the already constructed prefix and its current birth.
First build that birth on admitted inputs, then estimate its defect,
then choose the period sufficiently large for that finite step/prefix.
Do not assume a future strong-body bound to establish the moments needed
to close the current strong-body recurrence.
