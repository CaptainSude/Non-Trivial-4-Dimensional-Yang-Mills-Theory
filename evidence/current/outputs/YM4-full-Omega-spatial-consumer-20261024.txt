RESULT AND PURPOSE

A sufficiently small exponentially summable bounded interaction can be
added to a uniformly weighted-convex finite product law without losing
spatial decay for bounded local observables. The added interaction need
only be measurable; no Hessian, weighted accretivity, or differentiability
of that interaction is assumed. The proof uses overlapping block heat
baths, maximal coupling of ENTIRE protected marginals, and a hybrid
disagreement metric. Centered second moments remove the large spin-cutoff
radius from the constants. Thus the block size and final spatial exponent
can be fixed independently of that radius and of the lattice volume.

This is a consumer for an ACTUALLY proved small local interaction for
-log Z_Omega+log Z_success. It does not by itself construct that interaction
or identify the next physical law. Section8A now matches those separate
inputs from full_omega_local_interaction_20261024 and
full_omega_next_coarse_law_20261023 on their stated finite sector.

1. PRECISE FINITE INPUT

Let I be sites of a finite d-dimensional lattice torus, d=4 for the
application. Each spin x_i lies in a compact convex K_i in R^m, with fixed
m. A product of a fixed number of native free-bond balls may be one site.
All coordinate diameters are finite but need not have a uniform bound.
Use the lattice distance, with the usual uniform polynomial volume bounds.

The reference law is

       nu(dx)=Z_0^{-1} exp[-H_0(x)] product_i 1_(K_i) dx_i.    (1)

On the WHOLE containing product, H_0 is C2 and has the registered bounds
H_0'' >= c0 I, weighted accretivity c_nu>0, and absolute exponentially
weighted Hessian row/column bound M_nu, at one fixed spatial rate nu>0.
The same bounds hold under arbitrary fixed-coordinate conditioning by
principal restriction. No diagonal-dominance condition is imposed.

Let the real perturbation be

              V(x)=sum_(nonempty X subset I) psi_X(x_X),
              mu(dx)=Z^{-1} exp[-V(x)] nu(dx).                 (2)

Constants may be subtracted from each psi_X. Assume

 delta := sup_j sum_(X contains j) exp[kappa diam(X)]
                                    osc(psi_X) < delta_*,     (3)

for a fixed kappa>0. A stronger norm with |X| factors also suffices.
The number of terms can be finite or countable with this absolute norm.
The functions may be merely bounded measurable. In particular corners
of a cutoff-derived value interaction cause no problem here.

There are constants delta_*>0, lambda>0, C<infinity depending only on
d,m,c0,c_nu,M_nu,nu,kappa, not on the diameters of K_i or |I|, such that
the covariance conclusion in Section7 holds. The proof gives an explicit
finite choice of block size and smallness threshold, without optimizing
them. All estimates concern the exact normalized law (2).

2. THREE UNIFORM REFERENCE CONDITIONAL ESTIMATES

Let B subset I be any update region, and let nu_B^eta be the reference
conditional law in B with exterior eta. Suppose eta,eta' differ only
at j outside B, with s=|eta_j-eta'_j|. Convexity of K_j permits the
straight interpolation. The full action and original conditional
normalizer are retained.

(a) For every S subset B, including a very large or disconnected S,

 ||(nu_B^eta)_S-(nu_B^eta')_S||_TV
                    <= min(1,C s exp[-nu d(j,S)]).             (4)

This is TV of the ENTIRE joint marginal on S, not only of individual
coordinates. Indeed differentiate QF for any 0<=F(x_S)<=1. The bounded-
local projected-mean covariance estimate, applied to the complete score
H'_j, and the weighted Hessian column bound give
|D_(eta_j) QF|<=C exp[-nu d(j,S)]. Integrate the one-coordinate path and
take the supremum over F. Constants do not count |S| or |B|.

(b) If m_i^eta=nu_B^eta x_i, then

             |m_i^eta-m_i^eta'|<=C s exp[-nu d(i,j)].          (5)

Apply the weighted covariance inequality to a linear coordinate x_i
and H'_j, then integrate. Fixed color dimensions are absorbed in C.

(c) At every fixed exterior,

            nu_B^eta |x_i-m_i^eta|^2 <= sigma^2=m/c0.         (6)

This is Poincare for the m coordinate functions. It holds even if the
conditional mean is near a distant cutoff boundary. No uniform bound on
conditional means and no rare-event estimate is needed in this consumer.

3. ELEMENTARY CONSEQUENCES OF THE SMALL BLOCK TILT

The perturbed conditional law is exactly

 mu_B^eta = exp[-V_B^eta] nu_B^eta / nu_B^eta(exp[-V_B^eta]),
 V_B^eta=sum_(X intersects B) psi_X(x_(X intersect B),eta_(X\B)).

Writing n_B=|B|, (3) gives osc(V_B^eta)<=delta n_B=:a_B. If a_B<=1,
the density ratio lies between exp(-a_B) and exp(a_B). Consequently

 ||mu_B^eta-nu_B^eta||_TV <= C a_B,
 |mu_B^eta x_i-m_i^eta|<=C sigma a_B,
 Var_(mu_B^eta)(x_i)<=C sigma^2.                              (7)

The mean bound follows by integrating x_i-m_i^eta against the density
ratio minus one. The variance bound follows by using m_i^eta as a trial
center. Thus these statements do not contain the spin-cutoff diameter.

If eta,eta' differ only at j outside B and k=d(j,B), the difference of
the two conditional potentials, up to constants independent of x_B, has
oscillation at most

 2 sum_(X contains j, X intersects B) osc(psi_X)
                                      <=2 delta exp(-kappa k). (8)

Terms not containing j are identical. The harmless factor two allows
the difference of the two evaluations of one bounded function.

For any two probability laws P,Q and common positive bounded tilt f,
TV(T_f P,T_f Q)<=2 exp(osc(log f)) TV(P,Q). Also changing a tilt by a
potential of oscillation a changes its normalized law by at most C a
when a<=1. Applying these elementary ratio estimates to (4),(8) gives

 ||mu_B^eta-mu_B^eta'||_TV
       <=min(1,C[s exp(-nu k)+delta exp(-kappa k)]),           (9)

provided a_B<=1. The whole conditional normalization is included.

For a protected S subset B with d(j,S)>=r, simple TV triangle instead
gives

 ||(mu_B^eta)_S-(mu_B^eta')_S||_TV
                  <=min(1,C[s exp(-nu r)+a_B]).               (10)

Only (9) is used for far blocks. Using (10) for every distant block
would create an erroneous volume-sized perturbation price.

4. THE HYBRID COUPLING AND WHY THE CUTOFF RADIUS DISAPPEARS

For one coordinate define

             d_i(x_i,y_i)=1_(x_i!=y_i)+|x_i-y_i|.              (11)

The distance between configurations is the sum of these coordinate
distances, optionally with positive spatial weights. A one-site input
disagreement at j has cost D_j=1+s. The discontinuous unit term pays for
a merely bounded, possibly nonsmooth perturbation. The Euclidean term
absorbs a large boundary displacement s without a large cutoff constant.
The underlying state space and all kernels remain ordinary standard
Borel spaces; only the coupling cost is this bounded-on-each-finite-run
lower-semicontinuous metric.

We use the following elementary fact. Couple two laws on a coordinate
region so their S-marginals agree with probability 1-p, by maximal
coupling of those ENTIRE marginals, and extend with their conditional
laws on the remaining coordinates. If i is in S, then

 E d_i(X_i,Y_i)
 <=p[1+|m_i^mu-m_i^mu'|]+2 C_sigma sqrt(p).                   (12)

Indeed the coordinate difference vanishes on agreement of the whole S;
on failure subtract each law's own unconditional mean and use Cauchy--
Schwarz with its unconditional variance. No bound conditional on the
failure event is asserted. For an unprotected i, the same triangle
argument without that event gives

 E d_i(X_i,Y_i)<=1+|m_i^mu-m_i^mu'|+2C_sigma.                 (13)

Here (5),(7) imply

 |m_i^mu-m_i^mu'|<=C s exp[-nu d(i,j)]+C a_B.                 (14)

Take S=B\B_r(j), so at most C(r+1)^d block sites are unprotected.
Use (10) for p. Since
sqrt(s)/(1+s)<=1 and s/(1+s)<=1, equations (12)--(14) give

 E sum_(i in B) d_i(X_i,Y_i) / (1+s)
 <=C[(r+1)^d+n_B exp(-nu r/2)+n_B sqrt(a_B)].                 (15)

For the protected mean term it suffices to use p<=1, giving
s exp[-nu d(i,j)]p/(1+s)<=exp(-nu r). The other terms use
sqrt(p)/(1+s)<=C[exp(-nu r/2)+sqrt(a_B)] and a_B<=1.
This is the important radius-independent estimate.

For a far block with k=d(j,B)>=r, maximally couple the WHOLE B using
(9), so (12) applies to every i. The same calculation now gives

 E sum_(i in B) d_i(X_i,Y_i) / (1+s)
 <= C n_B[exp(-nu k/2)+sqrt(delta)exp(-kappa k/2)].            (16)

In the a_B p term use
p/(1+s)<=C[exp(-nu k)+delta exp(-kappa k)], rather than p<=1.
Thus no constant a_B floor is left at distant blocks. This is where
the exponentially localized interaction assumption is essential.

5. OVERLAPPING BLOCK HEAT BATHS CONTRACT

Take all translated lattice cubes B of side 2ell+1, and update their
spins by the EXACT mu conditional law. Let N=(2ell+1)^d. For a torus
larger than this block, exactly N blocks contain any specified j.
For small tori one can use the whole volume as a block; the same final
constants are enlarged by a fixed ell-dependent factor. Missing active
coordinates can be omitted while retaining the ambient block centers.

If the input pair differs only at j and B contains j, its exteriors are
identical. Couple the entire conditional update identically: this
erases the initial disagreement. If B misses j, leave j unchanged and
use the coupling from Section4 for the newly sampled block.

There are at most C ell^(d-1)(r+1) blocks missing j but within distance
r of it, for 1<=r<=ell. The number with distance k from j is bounded
by C(ell+k)^(d-1). Since n_B<=N and a_B<=delta N, the total expected
birth of disagreements divided by the initial cost is at most

 C ell^(d-1)(r+1)
       [(r+1)^d+N exp(-nu r/2)+N sqrt(delta N)]
 +C N sum_(k>=r) (ell+k)^(d-1) exp[-omega k],                 (17)

where omega=min(nu,kappa)/2 and delta<=1. The total erasure is N.

Choose r=ceil(A log(ell+2)), with A a sufficiently large fixed constant
depending on omega,d. Then choose a FIXED ell large enough that the
delta-free part of (17) is at most N/8. Its leading ratio is
C(r+1)^(d+1)/ell; all the exponential terms tend to zero faster.
Finally choose delta_* so delta_* N<=1 and

 C ell^(d-1)(r+1) sqrt(delta_* N)<=1/8.                       (18)

For example a sufficiently small fixed multiple of
[N ell^(2d-2)(r+1)^2]^{-1} suffices. No parameter here depends on the
spin diameters or the lattice volume.

For spatial control, choose a fixed lambda>0 so
lambda C_d ell<=log 2 and lambda<=omega/2. Redo the harmless exponent
allocation in (17) with omega/2 before choosing ell. For a near block,
all weights exp[lambda d(i,j)] are at most a fixed factor; for a far
block they are at most exp[lambda(k+C_d ell)]. Thus the same choices,
with constants tightened once, yield couplings satisfying

 sum_(B not containing j) E sum_(i in B)
        exp[lambda d(i,j)] d_i(X_i,Y_i)
                                  <=(N/2)(1+s).              (19)

One may take lambda smaller than a fixed multiple of ell^{-1}.
It is still fixed independently of the ultraviolet noise radius.

Let w_i>0 have lambda-Lipschitz logarithm and write
d_w(x,y)=sum_i w_i d_i(x_i,y_i). Triangle inequality gives
w_i<=w_j exp[lambda d(i,j)]. Combining the N exact erasures and
(19), a single-site input pair contracts infinitesimally at rate
at least gamma=N/2 for the generator

               L=sum_B (P_B-I),
 P_B F=E_mu[F | x_(I\B)].                                  (20)

The finite coordinate-by-coordinate path between arbitrary configurations
and gluing of the elementary couplings extend this bound to arbitrary
pairs. Equivalently use a discrete random-block step, whose contraction
factor is at most 1-N/(2 number_of_blocks), then Poissonize. The coupled
block densities and maximal couplings have measurable standard-Borel
versions. No simultaneous maximal coupling of multiple protected
marginals is required: each block update uses ONE chosen S.

Consequently its semigroup satisfies, for every d_w-Lipschitz F,

             Lip_(d_w)(P_t F)<=exp(-gamma t)Lip_(d_w)(F).      (21)

All block conditionals are those of the actual perturbed law mu. Finite
diameters make the finite-run coupling converge to its unique invariant
law; this invariant law is mu by heat-bath reversibility. Uniformity of
the constants does not require a uniform diameter for that convergence
argument.

6. LOCAL OBSERVABLES AND PROFILE OBSERVABLES

For a real bounded F depending only on x_S, write b_F=osc(F)/2 and take
w_i=exp[-lambda d(i,S)]. It is d_w-Lipschitz with constant 2b_F:
if F changes, at least one changed coordinate belongs to S and contributes
at least one to d_w. Thus (21) applies without derivatives of F.

More generally suppose G has a nonnegative coordinate profile a_i:

 |G(x)-G(y)|<=sum_i a_i d_i(x_i,y_i).                          (22)

A separately Lipschitz function with sup|partial_i G|<=a_i qualifies
using the Euclidean term only. A bounded local G on T qualifies with
a_i=2b_G 1_T(i). A spatially decaying first-source gradient can therefore
be consumed directly, even if its dependence is not strictly local.

When X_B,Y_B are independent samples of the SAME mu_B conditional,
(7) gives uniformly E d_i(X_i,Y_i)^2<=C for i in B: their means
cancel and their conditional variances are uniformly bounded. Cauchy--
Schwarz therefore bounds the product of sums of these coordinate
distances by C times the product of their coefficient sums.

7. EXACT COVARIANCE CONSUMER

The reversible heat-bath identity, followed by (21), gives

 Cov_mu(F,G)=integral_0^infinity sum_B
                         mu Cov_(mu_B)(P_t F,G) dt.           (23)

This follows by differentiating mu[(P_t F)G]; no Langevin regularity
or integration by parts in the nonsmooth perturbation is involved.
Use independent conditional resamples and (22) in each covariance.
For the bounded local F of Section6,

 |Cov_(mu_B)(P_t F,G)|
 <=C b_F exp(-gamma t)
           (sum_(i in B) w_i)(sum_(j in B) a_j).              (24)

If i,j belong to one block, w_i<=exp(lambda C_d ell)w_j. Summing
(24) over translated blocks therefore costs at most
C N^2 b_F exp(-gamma t) sum_j a_j w_j. Since gamma>=N/2,

 |Cov_mu(F,G)|<=C_ell b_F sum_j a_j exp[-lambda d(j,S)],        (25)

where C_ell is fixed. In particular for two bounded local tests,

 |Cov_mu(F,G)|
 <=C_ell b_F b_G min(|S|,|T|)
                              exp[-lambda d(S,T)].           (26)

Using two exponentially localized profiles in (22) likewise gives
exponential root separation, with a smaller fixed rate and the explicit
root/seed cardinality factors from their convolution. Real and imaginary
parts give the corresponding complex bilinear covariance with a fixed
factor. Constant observables have zero covariance; empty-read cases
are handled separately without a distance convention.

The constants in (25),(26) contain the fixed block size, so this proof
does not optimize a numerical mass. It does give a positive fixed
spatial rate without requiring diagonal dominance or a weighted
interpretation of the perturbation's singular second derivative.
