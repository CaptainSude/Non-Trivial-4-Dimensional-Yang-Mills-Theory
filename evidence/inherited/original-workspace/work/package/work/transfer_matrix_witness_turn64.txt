RESULT

There is an exact finite-period alternative to comparing a reflected
observable with a Gaussian law at a fixed macroscopic separation.
For a real bounded gauge-invariant slice observable F of the ORIGINAL
SU2 Wilson field, put

 v_N=Var_W(F_0),
 d_N=(1/2) E_W[(F_1-F_0)^2].

Here1 denotes ONE temporal lattice step a_t, and the same normalized
periodic Wilson law is used throughout. The positive transfer matrix
gives, for integer1<=m<=N,

 Cov_W(F_0,F_m) >= v_N (1-d_N/v_N)^m.                  (0.1)

Consequently a cutoff-uniform lower bound v_N>=v_*>0 and upper bound
d_N<=C a_t would imply a positive centered reflection correlation
at fixed physical separation. No Gaussian comparison and no temporal
ground-state limit is needed for this statement.

This note also proves a model-specific, fully normalized original-law
estimate, not just this sufficient criterion. For any specified set S
of temporal plaquettes in ONE time slab, with Wilson coefficients
beta_e>0, let h(W)=1-Tr(W)/2. Then

 E_W exp[r sum_(e in S) beta_e h(W_e)]
                  <= (1-r)^(-3|S|/2),   0<=r<1.       (0.2)

It holds at arbitrary finite spatial volume and temporal period with
the time-homogeneous spatial action, and passes to its fixed-spacing
periodic Gibbs limits. All temporal links, the gauge projection and
the complete original partition denominator are retained.
Section6A strengthens this to arbitrary temporal slabs by Schatten
Holder and to arbitrary four-dimensional plaquette sets by a symmetric
four-axis decomposition:

 E_W exp[s sum_(p in B) beta_p h(W_p)]
                  <=(1-2s)^(-3|B|/4),   0<=s<1/2.     (0.3)

Here the original couplings are homogeneous (or constant by plaquette
orientation) on a periodic four-dimensional lattice. Arbitrary
exterior conditioning is not covered.

The new estimate controls kinetic plaquette fluctuations. It does NOT
yet prove d_N=O(a_t) for the physically normalized flowed probe, or
v_N>=v_* along the tuned ultraviolet sequence. The exact finite-isotropic
Wilson Hamiltonian is a logarithm of the transfer matrix, not a local
electric Laplacian. Substituting the Kogut--Susskind differential form
without a temporal-continuum comparison would miss this distinction.

2. EXACT SU2 TRANSFER KERNEL AND PARTITION NORMALIZATION

Fix a finite spatial lattice with oriented spatial edge set E,
and N>=4 periodic time slices. All Haar measures are normalized.
Let a_s,a_t be the spatial and temporal spacings. At each time
the spatial action V(U) is the SAME real bounded gauge-invariant
function; the ordinary nonnegative Wilson spatial plaquette action
is the intended example. Allow spatially inhomogeneous temporal
coefficients beta_e>0, constant from slab to slab.

For W in SU2 define

 h(W)=1-Tr(W)/2,
 c(beta)=integral_SU2 exp[-beta h(W)]dW
        =2 exp(-beta) I_1(beta)/beta,
 q_beta(W)=exp[-beta h(W)]/c(beta).                    (2.1)

I_n is the modified Bessel function. Write
H_kin=L2(SU2^E,dU), P for the orthogonal projection obtained by
averaging the site gauge group, and H_phys=P H_kin.
Let C be product convolution with the q_beta_e and
M(U)=exp[-V(U)/2]. Both commute with P. On H_phys set

                         T=M C M.                     (2.2)

Equivalently on the full space use M C P M. Its positive kernel is

 K(U,V)=M(U)M(V) integral dg
          product_(e=(x,y)) q_beta_e[U_e g_y V_e^-1 g_x^-1].
                                                               (2.3)

The g_x are precisely the temporal links in this slab (with the
orientation convention displayed). Multiplying N kernels and taking
the trace assigns each spatial layer its full exp[-V] factor and
integrates every temporal link once. Therefore the literal original
partition function satisfies

 Z_W= [product_(e in E)c(beta_e)]^N Tr_phys(T^N).        (2.4)

Gauge Haar volume is1. No extra gauge-volume or vacuum factor is
suppressed. Gauge-invariant slice insertions are multiplication
operators on H_phys, and their normalized transfer traces equal
their original Wilson expectations; the scalar in(2.4) cancels.

For completeness, in spin j with dimension d=2j+1, the eigenvalue
of one-link convolution is

                       r_d(beta)=I_d(beta)/I_1(beta).  (2.5)

To check the normalization, SU2 class Haar measure is
(2/pi)sin^2(theta)dtheta, Tr(W)/2=cos(theta), and
chi_j=sin(d theta)/sin(theta). Thus
 integral exp(beta cos theta)chi_j dW
     =I_(d-1)(beta)-I_(d+1)(beta)=2d I_d(beta)/beta.
Divide by d times2 I_1(beta)/beta to obtain(2.5).
The Bessel series makes every I_d(beta)>0. Normalized convolution
is a self-adjoint positive contraction. Its trace is finite;
for example sum_d d^2 r_d(beta)=q_beta(I)<infinity.
The finite product C is positive trace class, and multiplication
by bounded M and projection P preserve these properties.
On H_phys, T has no kernel because M is invertible at this finite
volume and C is strictly positive. Its eigenvalues lambda_i are
positive, with possible accumulation only at0.

Let lambda_0=||T|| and define the EXACT vacuum-normalized Hamiltonian

              H= -a_t^-1 log(T/lambda_0).               (2.6)

It is nonnegative, self-adjoint and may be unbounded. The overall
normalization in(2.4) shifts an unnormalized Hamiltonian by a scalar;
it changes neither(2.6) nor any normalized correlation. The finite
period corresponds to inverse temperature N a_t. One should not
replace its trace state by a ground vector without taking that
additional limit.

3. FINITE-PERIOD SPECTRAL INEQUALITY FOR THE ACTUAL LAW

Let F be any real bounded gauge-invariant slice function. Let
mu_N=E_W F and A=F-mu_N. Put Z_T=Tr T^N and

 G_N(m)=Z_T^-1 Tr(T^(N-m) A T^m A),   0<=m<=N.         (3.1)

Then G_N(0)=v_N and G_N(m)=Cov_W(F_0,F_m). In an orthonormal
eigenbasis of T,

 G_N(m)=Z_T^-1 sum_(i,j)
           lambda_i^N |A_ij|^2 (lambda_j/lambda_i)^m.   (3.2)

All weights are nonnegative. If v_N>0, divide these weights by
v_N to obtain a probability measure. Jensen's inequality for
x^m, m>=1, proves

                G_N(m)>=v_N [G_N(1)/v_N]^m.            (3.3)

The countable spectral sums are legitimate: bounded F gives finite
correlations for0<=m<=N; Jensen may also be obtained by finite
truncation and passage to the probability measure. The ratios
lambda_j/lambda_i may exceed1. This causes no problem; no false
contraction assumption on a thermal Liouville spectrum is used.

Stationarity of the original periodic law gives

 d_N=v_N-G_N(1)
    =(1/2)E_W[(F_1-F_0)^2],    0<=d_N<=v_N.            (3.4)

The upper bound uses G_N(1)>=0 from(3.2), not just the classical
Cauchy--Schwarz inequality. Equations(3.3)--(3.4) prove(0.1).
This exact finite-period result applies directly to the literal
spatially flowed magnetic bounded function of TURN63: it is still
a multiplication operator on the ORIGINAL slice.

The same d_N is the following completely normalized bridge integral:

 d_N=(2Z_T)^-1 integral dU dV
        [F(U)-F(V)]^2 K(U,V) K^(N-1)(V,U).             (3.5)

It also has the exact double-commutator expression

 d_N=(2Z_T)^-1 Tr[T^(N-1)[F,[F,T]]].                   (3.6)

Indeed the kernel of[F,[F,T]] is(F(U)-F(V))^2 K(U,V);
or expand the commutators and use cyclicity. No division by a
small history component or an unknown local reference mass occurs.

4. PHYSICAL-TIME CONSEQUENCE, AND WHAT COUNTS AS ENERGY

Suppose along an actual sequence of Wilson regulators and volumes

                  v_N>=v_*>0,       d_N<=C a_t,        (4.1)

for one specified physical observable family. Assume the temporal
period contains the desired separation m a_t. For
a_t<=v_*/(2C), logarithmic concavity estimates give

 G_N(m)>=v_* exp[-2C m a_t/v_*].                        (4.2)

For a probe at physical time tau and its reflection, the separation
is approximately2tau, so this bounds its centered OS norm below by
a fixed positive number. If those observable laws and reflection
forms pass to a compatible limit, this is a nonzero vacuum-orthogonal
state. It does not by itself prove local algebra, nonabelian
interaction, Euclidean covariance or the rest of YM4 existence.
It also does not exclude variance in an additional zero-energy
sector if the limiting vacuum is degenerate. A propagating-state
claim needs a separate positive-energy or nonzero-momentum witness.

A stronger energy premise is available if it can actually be proved.
In the zero-temperature ground state Omega, set psi=A Omega and
E_H=<psi,H psi>, interpreted as a form. For finite E_H,

 <psi,exp(-tH)psi> >= v exp[-t E_H/v],   v=||psi||^2.   (4.3)

This is Jensen for the spectral probability measure of H on psi.
The energy equals (1/2)<Omega,[F,[H,F]]Omega> when the
commutator is defined by the corresponding quadratic form.
No ground-energy scalar survives that double commutator.

At finite temperature set rho_N=exp(-N a_t H)/Tr exp(-N a_t H)
and define the physical-time extension
g_N(t)=Tr[exp(-(N a_t-t)H) A exp(-tH) A]/Tr exp(-N a_t H).
Thus g_N(m a_t)=G_N(m), with no extra vacuum scalar.
The analogous form is
E_H=(1/2)Tr(rho_N [F,[H,F]])=-g_N'(0), provided the
absolute first spectral moment is finite. The same spectral Jensen
proof permits signed energy differences and gives the analogous
bound on0<=t<=N a_t.

It is important that

                       d_N/a_t <= E_H                 (4.4)

under that stronger premise, NOT the reverse. The inequality follows
from1-exp(-a_t x)<=a_t x, including signed thermal energy differences.
One cannot bound the logarithmic Hamiltonian energy merely by the
discrete Dirichlet form. The finite-period theorem needs only d_N;
it therefore avoids this unnecessary domain/upper-energy requirement.

Variance alone is insufficient, even with a positive transfer matrix.
For a stationary two-state chain take eigenvalues1 and
r_a=exp(-a^q),0<q<1, and centered F=+/-1. Then v=1,
d_a=1-r_a->0, but at physical separation t,
G_a(t)=r_a^(floor(t/a))->0.
This tests an inference, not the Wilson law. It shows that qualitative
smallness of an adjacent increment is weaker than the required scale.

5. A NEW EXACT ONE-SLAB ORIGINAL WILSON SOURCE BOUND

For any real nu>-1/2, the integral representation

 I_nu(beta)=constant_nu beta^nu
       integral_(-1)^1 exp(beta x)(1-x^2)^(nu-1/2)dx

and its derivative recurrence imply
I_(nu+1)/I_nu=E_(nu,beta) x. Differentiation gives

       d/d beta [I_(nu+1)/I_nu]=Var_(nu,beta)(x)>0.    (5.1)

Hence r_d(beta)=I_d/I_1, the product of adjacent ratios from1
through d-1, is increasing in beta. Equation(2.5) proves the
Loewner order of central convolution operators

                     C_(beta-t)<=C_beta, 0<=t<beta.    (5.2)

For a source t_e on specified temporal plaquettes in ONE slab,
its literal kernel is

 exp[t_e h(W)]q_beta_e(W)
       =[c(beta_e-t_e)/c(beta_e)] q_(beta_e-t_e)(W).

Tensor products preserve the order between positive operators.
The unchanged spatial multiplication factors and physical projection
therefore give

 0<=T_mark<=R_S T,
 R_S=product_(e in S)c(beta_e-t_e)/c(beta_e).            (5.3)

The rest of the time circle is the positive operator T^(N-1).
Multiplying in trace preserves this order:

 E_W exp[sum_(e in S)t_e h(W_e)]
  =Tr(T_mark T^(N-1))/Tr T^N <=R_S.                    (5.4)

This is the ORIGINAL normalized finite-volume Wilson law, not a
selected weak-field sector. The gauge averaging in(2.3) is inside
T_mark and is preserved exactly; spatial potentials need not be
small. Time homogeneity of the unmarked remainder is used.

There is an explicit gamma bound for R_S. Under normalized q_beta,
u=h(W) has density proportional to

      u^(1/2) exp(-beta u)(2-u)^(1/2) 1_(0<u<2).

This is a Gamma(shape3/2,rate beta) distribution tilted by the
nonnegative decreasing function w(u)=(2-u)^(1/2)1_(u<2).
For any increasing phi and independent identically distributed U,V,

 2 Cov(phi(U),w(U))
      =E[(phi(U)-phi(V))(w(U)-w(V))]<=0.

Thus its tilted mean of phi is at most its gamma mean. For
phi(u)=exp(tu),0<=t<beta, this yields

 c(beta-t)/c(beta)<= (1-t/beta)^(-3/2).                 (5.5)

Taking t_e=r beta_e proves(0.2).

For a first derivative at zero, (5.4) gives

 E_W h(W_e)<=1-I_2(beta_e)/I_1(beta_e)
                   <=min(1,3/(2beta_e)).               (5.6)

The mean bound also follows directly from the gamma tilt.
An independent exact check is the Riccati equation
r'=1-r^2-3r/beta for r=I_2/I_1. For beta>=3/2 the barrier
b=1-3/(2beta) starts below r and satisfies
1-b^2-3b/beta=9/(4beta^2)>b'=3/(2beta^2).
For beta<=3/2 the bound is immediate from r>0.

Higher mixed moment coefficients are NOT inferred by differentiating
the inequality(5.4). Such an inference is not generally valid.
The real-source inequality gives Chernoff estimates directly instead.

6. CONSEQUENCES AND EXACT SCOPE OF THE SLAB ESTIMATE

Let k=|S| and Y_S=sum_(e in S)beta_e h(W_e). For x>=0,

 P_W[Y_S>=(3k/2)+sqrt(3k x)+x] <=exp(-x).               (6.1)

For example,

 P_W[h(W_e)>=T/beta_e for EVERY e in S]
 <=exp{-k[T-3/2-(3/2)log(2T/3)]}.                     (6.2)

This follows by inclusion in Y_S>=kT and choosing

Bernoulli independence, conditional domination after arbitrary

All constants are independent of spatial volume, N and the spatial

6A. ALL SLABS AND ALL PLAQUETTES: A STRONGER ORIGINAL-LAW BOUND

Root proposed the all-slab argument during this derivation; I checked
it independently. Consider different sources0<=t_(j,e)<beta_e on
every temporal slab j. After extracting their scalar factors from
(5.3), the remaining positive physical operators are

 T_j=M C_(beta-t_j) M,       0<=T_j<=T.

The gauge projection is retained as in Section2. Their product
need not be self-adjoint, but generalized Schatten Holder gives

 |Tr(T_1...T_N)|<=product_j [Tr(T_j^N)]^(1/N)
                         <=Tr(T^N).                   (6.3)

The second step uses eigenvalue-by-eigenvalue monotonicity of
positive compact operators under T_j<=T. It does NOT assert the
false general operator inequality T_j^N<=T^N. All these operators
are trace class. The kernel product trace is nonnegative, so its
absolute value causes no loss. Restoring the extracted factors proves

 E_W exp[sum_(j,e)t_(j,e)h(W_(j,e))]
       <=product_(j,e)(1-t_(j,e)/beta_e)^(-3/2)          (6.4)

for arbitrary plaquettes temporal with respect to the chosen axis.
There is no factor growing with N. This is an unconditioned
original-law identity and bound, not a stochastic domination claim.

Now take a periodic four-dimensional Wilson lattice with couplings
constant by orientation, so every axis admits the time-homogeneous
base transfer construction. Let B be any specified plaquette set,
and Y=sum_(p in B)beta_p h(W_p). For mu=0,1,2,3 let Y_mu
be the same sum over plaquettes having mu as one of their two axes.
Each plaquette belongs to exactly TWO stars, and

                          Y=(1/2)sum_mu Y_mu.

Apply ordinary probability Holder with four factors, then(6.4)
using mu as the time direction in its own factor:

 E_W exp(sY)
 <=product_mu [E_W exp(2sY_mu)]^(1/4)
 <=(1-2s)^[-(3/8)sum_mu |B_mu|]
  =(1-2s)^(-3|B|/4).                                  (6.5)

This proves(0.3). The same proof gives the inhomogeneous source bound

 E_W exp[sum_(p in B)t_p h(W_p)]
       <=product_(p in B)(1-2t_p/beta_p)^(-3/4),
                         0<=t_p<beta_p/2.              (6.6)

The SOURCES may be inhomogeneous. The BASE coupling/geometry premise
is still the one explicitly stated above. Equation(6.6) is an exact
bound on the corresponding complete original partition ratio.

For k=|B| and x>=0, gamma Chernoff now gives

 P_W[Y>=3k/2+sqrt(6kx)+2x]<=exp(-x).                   (6.7)

In particular for T>3/2,

 P_W[beta_p h(W_p)>=T for EVERY p in B]
 <=exp{-k[T/2-3/4-(3/4)log(2T/3)]}.                   (6.8)

These statements are volume independent and apply to arbitrary
specified microscopic plaquette sets, not just a single slab.
They are not bounds on every generated-scale native region, nor

7. EXACT GRADIENT/PLAQUETTE REDUCTION OF THE ADJACENT INCREMENT

One can now connect the slab energy to d_N, but the estimate shows
precisely why an extra physical smoothing estimate is still needed.

Keep U,V and the actual temporal links g in the joint integral(3.5).
Gauge invariance gives F(V)=F(V^g). Choose shortest logarithms
V_e^g=U_e exp(X_e) with X_e=i x_e.sigma/2, |x_e|<=2pi.
The endpoint distance satisfies

                   |x_e|^2<=2pi^2 h(W_e).              (7.1)

Indeed h=1-cos(|x_e|/2) and1-cos z>=2z^2/pi^2 on[0,pi].
Use the simultaneous geodesic U_r,e=U_e exp(rX_e).
For any positive deterministic weights w_e, Cauchy--Schwarz gives

 d_N <=pi^2 E_W{
    [sum_e w_e h(W_e)]
    integral_0^1 sum_e w_e^-1 |grad_e F(U_r)|^2 dr }.   (7.2)

The expectation includes the actual two slices and temporal links.
The intermediate U_r is not assigned the original equilibrium law.
Its gradient factor cannot be replaced by a vacuum mean or declared
independent of the temporal plaquette energies.

If deterministic global link-gradient bounds |grad_e F|<=L_e
are available, Minkowski, (7.1) and (5.6) give the simpler bound

 d_N <=pi^2 [sum_e L_e sqrt(mu(beta_e))]^2,
 mu(beta)=1-I_2(beta)/I_1(beta)<=3/(2beta).              (7.3)

These are actual finite-Wilson consequences with no new probabilistic

For the physically normalized flowed probe, generic finite-ODE

The useful target is a joint quadratic influence/temporal-increment

8. WHY THE DIFFERENTIAL DIRICHLET IDENTITY IS NOT AUTOMATIC HERE

For a genuinely differential finite-spatial-lattice Hamiltonian

 H_diff=kappa sum_(e,c)(-D_(e,c)^2)+V-E_0,

with left-invariant derivatives normalized by
exp(i theta sigma_c/2), multiplication by real F gives the exact
identity

 (1/2)[F,[H_diff,F]]=kappa sum_(e,c)|D_(e,c)F|^2.       (8.1)

It follows by the product rule; V and E_0 commute with F.
Consequently in its ground state
E_H=kappa E_(Omega^2) sum|DF|^2, with the analogous thermal
identity when its traces exist. For the conventional SU2
Kogut--Susskind normalization, kappa=g_H^2/(2a_s).

At finite temporal spacing the EXACT Wilson electric logarithm,
even before the spatial multiplication factors, instead has spectrum

      a_t^-1 log[I_1(beta_t)/I_(2j+1)(beta_t)].         (8.2)

For fixed j and beta_t large this is
2j(j+1)/(a_t beta_t)+O(1/(a_t beta_t^2)).
With beta_t=4a_s/(g_H^2 a_t) it approaches the Casimir coefficient
g_H^2 j(j+1)/(2a_s). This fixed-spin asymptotic is not uniform over
the unbounded representation spectrum. Moreover -log(MCM) is not
-log C+V when the factors do not commute.

Thus(8.1) is a valid possible auxiliary route only after an actual
Hamiltonian limit/comparison and matching of the observable. It is
not an exact formula for the finite isotropic Wilson H in(2.6).
The finite-period discrete criterion(0.1) avoids requiring it.

9. THE FREE FLOWED PROBE MEETS THE NEW TARGET, AT ITS OWN SCOPE

For the canonical three-color magnetic process from TURN63,
c_s(t)=(6pi^2)^-1 integral k^3 exp(-2sk^2-tk)dk,
v_s=c_s(0), and mu_s=3sqrt(pi)/(4sqrt(2s)).
Set B(t)=exp[-|X_s(t)|^2/(2v_s)]. Then

 v_B=Var B(0)=3^(-3/2)-1/8>0,
 E[B(0)B(t)]=[4-(c_s(t)/v_s)^2]^(-3/2).

Because |c_s'(t)|<=v_s mu_s,

 d_B(t)=(1/2)E[(B(t)-B(0))^2]
        <=3 mu_s 3^(-5/2) t=mu_s t/(3sqrt(3)).         (9.1)

Equality holds for its derivative at0. This verifies the variance
and linear-time increment scales in the actual free benchmark,
without requiring its correlation at a distant time to be compared
first. The free lattice calculation in TURN63 gives corresponding
uniform bounds in its isotropic regime a_t=a_s=a at small a and
fixed s; it remains a FREE input.

For the original compact-link radial flowed F, finite flow is a
diffeomorphism of its finite product group (the smooth vector field
is complete in both time directions). On a nondegenerate spatial
plaquette graph F is therefore nonconstant. Strict positivity of
the finite Wilson density gives v_N>0 at each fixed regulator.
It gives no cutoff-uniform v_*.

In particular, the bare Gaussian law with coupling g_a and a fixed
physical divisor g_* has the collapsed variance noted in TURN63.
The new spectral argument does not circumvent physical calibration.
It replaces the old two distant Gaussian comparisons by different,
potentially more local, original-law estimates(4.1).
