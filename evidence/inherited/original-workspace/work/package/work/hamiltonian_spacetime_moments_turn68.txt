RESULT AND SCOPE

For the actual finite-spatial SU2 Hamiltonian ground process, the
closed-tile action moment bound extends to arbitrary simultaneous
space-time cells. The temporal cell length Delta can equal the
spatial spacing a. In that case the exponential-moment constant of
one cell is independent of a, g, the spatial volume, and the number
of occupied cells.

This is an actual normalized-law result. It does not prove connected
correlation decay on configurations without a bad bridge, a uniform
zero-free source logarithm, or a continuum Yang--Mills construction.
It concerns unflowed action marks, not flow-derivative weights.

1. MODEL AND CELL GEOMETRY

On a nondegenerate periodic three-dimensional spatial SU2 lattice
let n be its number of sites, with Pauli/2 metric convention, and put

 H=kappa K+v S,   kappa=g^2/(2a),   v=4/(g^2 a),
 S=sum_(spatial p) h_p,   h_p=1-Tr U_p/2 >=0.             (1.1)

The unique normalized positive ground state is Omega, its energy
is E0, and mu=Omega^2 dU. The previously proved exact trial bound is

                         E0 <= 9n/a.                    (1.2)

The ground state of the full compact product-space Hamiltonian is
gauge invariant by uniqueness and positivity. It is the SAME ground
state as the physical, gauge-invariant Hamiltonian.

The spatial periods N_i are even. Choose tile lengths l_i>=2 with
N_i/l_i=M_i a power of two; M_i=1 is allowed. Write V=l_1 l_2 l_3.
The spatial board consists of closed tiles B_b. S_b is the sum over
the SET of spatial plaquettes contained in B_b, with duplicates
collapsed when a periodic tile spans a whole direction.

Every plaquette belongs to at least one and at most two closed tiles:
it varies by one edge in two directions and hence belongs to a
unique tile interval in those directions. Its third coordinate is
fixed and may belong to both sides of a tile boundary. Therefore

                       S <= sum_b S_b <= 2S.             (1.3)

This is precisely the closed-tile geometry of
hamiltonian_bad_clusters_turn67.txt.

For a temporal cell length Delta>0 let

 I_j=[j Delta,(j+1)Delta],
 X_(b,j)=1/(a g^2) int_(I_j) S_b(U(t)) dt.               (1.4)

Time intervals overlap only at endpoints, a null set for this
Lebesgue integral. There is NO additional factor two from temporal
dissemination.

The asserted joint ground-law bound is

 log E_ground exp[sum_(c in A) t_c X_c]
       <= c_Delta sum_(c in A) t_c,
 c_Delta=9 V Delta/(2a),       0<=t_c<=2,               (1.5)

for any finite collection A of distinct cells. In particular,

 Delta=a:   log E exp[sum t_c X_c] <= (9V/2)sum t_c.    (1.6)

No independence of cells is asserted or used.

2. A CONVENIENT THERMAL APPROXIMATION AND ITS EXACT NORMALIZER

Use the FULL, unprojected trace at temporal period B:

 Z_B=Tr_full exp(-BH).

The compact product group has a finite heat trace for B>0. The
Feynman--Kac representation of Z_B is the finite positive measure
of product-group Brownian loops, with diffusion kappa K, weighted
by exp[-v int_0^B S(U(t))dt]. Its normalized probability law is
denoted pi_B.

This full thermal trace is deliberate. We do NOT assert that a
Gauss-projected finite-period trace has the same independent-link
Brownian-loop factorization. What is used is that

   pi_B -> the stationary Omega^2 ground process as B->infinity

on every fixed finite time window at this fixed spatial graph.
The simple positive ground state is the physical ground state,
so this limit is the desired actual law. It is not a substitute
reference measure.

For a constant source lambda on the entire temporal circle,
Feynman--Kac gives the exact normalized identity

 E_piB exp[lambda int_0^B S dt]
      = Tr exp[-B(H-lambda S)] / Tr exp(-BH).           (2.1)

For 0<=lambda<=v, form ordering and the min--max principle give

 H-lambda S = kappa K+(v-lambda)S >= kappa K,

 Tr exp[-B(H-lambda S)] <= Tr exp(-B kappa K).

Since Z_B>=exp(-BE0),

 log E_piB exp[lambda int_0^B S dt]
       <= BE0 + log Tr exp(-B kappa K).                 (2.2)

All factors, including the full original thermal normalizer, are
retained. The last trace is the product of 3n single-link SU2 heat
traces and tends to1 as B->infinity at fixed a,g,n. No simultaneous
spatial-cutoff/thermal-limit assertion is needed.

3. SPACE AND TIME REFLECTION POSITIVITY OF pi_B

Choose B=2^k Delta. The following RP facts follow directly from
the positive loop representation, rather than from an assertion
that a flowed law inherits reflection positivity.

Spatial site reflection:
Condition on the COMPLETE Brownian link paths lying in the two
fixed spatial planes. The product kinetic loop measure on all
remaining links splits into two independent reflected factors.
The bi-invariant SU2 Brownian law is invariant under link reversal
and group inversion as required by the reflected link convention.
Every spatial plaquette lies in one closed half or in a fixed
plane. Accordingly the action integral splits into a common
boundary-path factor and two reflected half factors.

The RP pairing is the integral, against a positive boundary-path
measure, of the absolute square of a conditional half integral.
Both half functions may read the shared boundary link paths.
Thus closed spatial tiles are admitted.

Temporal reflection:
Condition on the complete spatial configurations at the two
fixed times of the temporal circle. The Brownian loop law splits
into two time-reversed bridges with those endpoints. The integrated
potential also splits into reflected halves. The same conditional
absolute-square identity proves temporal RP. Both half functions
may read the shared endpoint configurations. Integrated action
sources receive no extra endpoint term.

These arguments also follow by first using finite-dimensional
Brownian bridge approximations and then bounded convergence for
the continuous bounded action functionals at this fixed graph.
All exponential cell functions used below are bounded at fixed
B,a,g,n, since 0<=h_p<=2.

The permitted mirror planes include every spatial tile boundary
and every time-cell boundary. The measure is invariant under the
associated translations and reflections.

4. THE MIXED FOUR-DIMENSIONAL CHESSBOARD INEQUALITY

The board has

                 M=(n/V)(B/Delta)

cells. Place each base-cell function by canonical alternating
reflection in all four directions, including time reversal in
odd temporal cells. Reflection in a tile boundary permutes these
copies exactly. In a direction of board size1 no reflection is
needed.

Here is the complete mixed-pattern step. For a finite alphabet of
nonnegative continuous cell functions f_l, first use f_l+epsilon.
Normalize each by

 z_l=[E_piB product_(all cells c) theta_c(f_l+epsilon)]^(1/M).

Write g_l=(f_l+epsilon)/z_l. Every homogeneous labeling of the
board by one g_l has expectation1. Maximize the expectation over
the finite set of all label patterns; let its maximum be A>=1.

For any board half cut, RP Cauchy--Schwarz gives

 Z(pattern)^2 <= Z(copy-positive-half) Z(copy-negative-half).

At a maximizing pattern both right factors are <=A and hence
both must equal A. Begin with the label in one cell. By successively
copying the half containing its monochromatic rectangle and
reflecting in that rectangle's right boundary, double its extent
in one board direction. It fits inside the retained half whenever
its current extent is at most half the dyadic board length.
Repeat in all four directions. Maximality is preserved and the
final pattern is homogeneous, of expectation1. Thus A=1.

Undoing the normalizations and taking epsilon down to0 proves

 E_piB product_c theta_c f_(sigma(c))
 <= product_c [E_piB product_d theta_d f_(sigma(c))]^(1/M).
                                                               (4.1)

Shared spatial boundary paths and temporal endpoint fields cause
no change: they are allowed in both closed-half RP functions.
Zero homogeneous expectations are handled by the epsilon limit.
Unoccupied cells carry the alphabet function1.

This proves the actual multipoint inequality; a one-function
dissemination estimate or an independence assumption would not
suffice.

5. DISSEMINATION, GROUND LIMIT, AND A SHARPER ENERGY-CURVE VERSION

For f_t=exp(t X_base), 0<=t<=2, all reflected copies are the
corresponding cell action exponentials. By(1.3),

 sum_(all c) X_c <= 2/(a g^2) int_0^B S dt.

The full-board source is therefore bounded by(2.1) with

 lambda=2t/(a g^2)=(t/2)v <=v.                         (5.1)

Combining(4.1),(2.2), for a finite active set gives

 log E_piB exp[sum_(c in A)t_c X_c]
 <= |A| (V Delta/(nB))
                [BE0+log Tr exp(-B kappa K)].          (5.2)

Take B through dyadic multiples of Delta to infinity, with the
active cells in a fixed finite time window. Bounded source words
converge to their fixed-unsourced-ground-endpoint expectations;
equivalently, isolate the long unmarked complementary interval
in the trace and use ground-state dominance. Formula(5.2) yields

 log E_ground exp[sum_(c in A)t_c X_c] <=9VDelta|A|/a.
                                                               (5.3)

There is a useful exact improvement. Define

 E_c=inf spec[kappa K+(1-c)v S],       0<=c<=1.

Then E_0=E0, E_1=0. At fixed graph the limiting log of the
homogeneous trace ratio in(5.1), divided by B, is

                         E0-E_(t/2).

Consequently the stronger mixed inequality is

 log E_ground exp[sum t_c X_c]
 <= (VDelta/n) sum_c [E0-E_(t_c/2)].                   (5.4)

No sourced ground vector replaces Omega at the two ends of the

Because

we have E_c>=(1-c)E0 and E0-E_c<=cE0. Substitution into(5.4)

For c<1 the energy in(5.4) has the exact coupling expression

 E_c = sqrt(1-c) E_ground(g_c),
             g_c^2=g^2/sqrt(1-c),                     (5.5)

on the same spatial graph and with the same spacing a. This follows
by checking both kinetic and magnetic coefficients. It does not

6. CONSEQUENCES FOR ACTUAL PHYSICAL MAGNETIC SOURCES

Let a bounded real plaquette profile f(t,p) have finite temporal
support and define the canonically normalized action insertion

 Y_f = int dt Q_f(t),
 Q_f(t)=4/(g^2 a) sum_p f(t,p) h_p(U(t)).               (6.1)

Assign every plaquette to one containing closed spatial tile,
solely to avoid double counting when bounding this source.
Let F_c be the supremum of |f| over the owned plaquettes and times
in cell c. The arbitrary ownership convention is NOT used in RP;
only the following pointwise inequality uses it:

                         |Y_f| <=4 sum_c F_c X_c.

For a complex scalar z with 4|z|F_c<=2 for every active cell,
positivity and(1.5) imply

 |E_ground exp(zY_f)|
 <= E_ground exp(|z||Y_f|)
 <= exp[(18VDelta/a)|z| sum_c F_c].                   (6.2)

More generally one can use independent complex cell coefficients,
with the same positive envelope. In particular Delta=a gives an
admissible source amplitude |z|<=1/(2||f||infinity) independent
of the cutoff. The logarithm of this ABSOLUTE VALUE bound still
costs the number of physical space-time cells, of order a^-4 for
a fixed space-time support and fixed spatial tile lengths.

This improves the simple stationary-Jensen slab bound, whose
admitted source coefficient shrinks like a/T. It gives actual
cutoff-independent admitted source AMPLITUDES, with an
extensive majorant. It does not prove a cutoff-independent
zero-free neighborhood for the connected logarithm, nor an O(1)
remainder after the required affine-in-time subtractions.

At every fixed regulator the source expectation is entire,
because Y_f is bounded. Its normalization at zero is exactly1.
Estimate(6.2) alone is not a source-derivative convergence theorem
in the spatial continuum limit.
