1. EXACT MODEL AND GROUND-STATE MULTIPLIER IDENTITY

Let X=SU2^E be a finite nondegenerate periodic spatial lattice with
n sites, 3n links and 3n spatial plaquettes. Use normalized Haar and
the Pauli/2 metric. Set

 H=-kappa Delta+v S,  kappa=g^2/(2a),  v=4/(g^2 a),
 S=sum_p h_p,  h_p=1-Tr U_p/2.

Let H Omega=E0 Omega, Omega>0, int Omega^2=1, and mu=Omega^2 dU.
The actual controlled temporal construction and TURN66 energy proof
give this exact H and E0<=9n/a. We retain those established scopes.

For every smooth real f, direct integration by parts gives

 kappa int |grad(f Omega)|^2 +v E_mu[S f^2]
               =E0 E_mu f^2+kappa E_mu|grad f|^2.        (1.1)

Indeed expand the square and use H Omega=E0 Omega multiplied by
f^2 Omega. There is no approximate ground-state equation.
In particular

 v E_mu[S f^2]<=E0 E_mu f^2+kappa E_mu|grad f|^2.         (1.2)

The nonnegative kinetic term discarded in(1.2) is stated explicitly.
For f=1 this recovers the energy mean bound; it does not justify
factoring a nonconstant f from its correlation with S.

The exact pointwise action bound used below is

                       |grad S|^2<=8S.                 (1.3)

For each incident edge,
|grad_e h_p|^2=(2h_p-h_p^2)/4<=h_p/2.
There are four incident spatial plaquettes per edge and four
edges per plaquette. Cauchy--Schwarz gives(1.3).

2. A NEW ACTUAL GROUND-LAW EXPONENTIAL ACTION BOUND

Define Z(lambda)=E_mu exp(lambda S). The finite compact configuration
space makes Z finite and smooth for every real lambda.
Use f=exp(lambda S/2) in(1.2), and then(1.3):

 v E_mu[S exp(lambda S)]
  <=E0 Z(lambda)+(kappa lambda^2/4)
                          E_mu[|grad S|^2 exp(lambda S)]
  <=E0 Z(lambda)+2kappa lambda^2 E_mu[S exp(lambda S)].

Thus for0<=lambda<2/g^2,

 d_lambda log Z(lambda)
    <= E0/(v-2kappa lambda^2)
    = (E0/v)/[1-(lambda g^2/2)^2].

Integrating from0, where Z(0)=1, proves

 log E_mu exp(lambda S)
      <=(a E0/2) artanh(lambda g^2/2)
      <=(9n/2) artanh(lambda g^2/2).                   (2.1)

Equivalently, for0<=t<2,

 log E_mu exp(t S/g^2)
                         <=(9n/2) artanh(t/2).         (2.2)

All constants, the normalization Z(0)=1, and the coupling radius
are exact. This proof uses the actual ground equation and no
isotropic Wilson one-edge conditional formula. It therefore does
not inherit the conditional-law limitation identified in TURN66.

For example, t=1 gives

 E_mu exp(S/g^2)<=3^(9n/4).                             (2.3)

The bound is extensive. Alone it does not bound the exponential
moment of a fixed local region with an extensive factor replaced
by that region's size. Such a replacement needs another argument.
It also does not give an exponential moment for the initial-field
flow derivative.

3. SPATIAL REFLECTION POSITIVITY OF THIS ACTUAL GROUND LAW

Take each spatial period N_i even. A spatial site reflection has
two fixed coordinate planes, at x_i=c and x_i=c+N_i/2. Links
lying in either plane are shared boundary variables. All other
links belong to exactly one reflected closed half; orientation
reversal uses inverse group elements and preserves Haar measure.

At finite temporal spacing delta and temporal period N_time,
the actual anisotropic Wilson density has positive spatial and
temporal couplings, constant by orientation. Every elementary
plaquette lies wholly in one closed spatial half or wholly in
one boundary plane. No plaquette contains both a strictly positive
and a strictly negative spatial vertex across a site plane.
Thus, conditional on the links in the two planes, the density is

            positive boundary weight times W_+ theta(W_+).

The measures on the two nonboundary link sets are reflected product
Haar. Integrating F theta(conjugate F) for a bounded function of
one closed half is an integral of an absolute square against that
positive boundary weight. This proves spatial site-reflection
positivity directly, including all temporal links and plaquettes.
The reflection is spatial, so the chosen temporal slice is fixed.

First send N_time to infinity at fixed delta, using the proved
simple Wilson transfer ground-state limit. Then send delta to0
at fixed spatial lattice, using Omega_delta->Omega in L2. Bounded
continuous spatial-half functions and their reflected products
pass to both limits. Hence mu=Omega^2 dU has the same spatial RP.
No uniformity in the spatial lattice is needed for this argument.

Only site planes are used below. This conditional-square proof
does not require a statement about more delicate diagonal mirrors,
does not modify any gauge-choice law, and does not assert that
(Phi_s)_*mu remains spatially reflection positive.

4. A LOCAL EXPONENTIAL ACTION BOUND WITH EXACT TILE CONVENTIONS

Let B be the rectangular cell box [0,l_1]x[0,l_2]x[0,l_3],
with integer l_i>=2 and N_i/l_i=2^k_i, k_i a nonnegative integer.
Write

             V_B=l_1 l_2 l_3,   K_B=n/V_B.

Use a translate if needed. Let P_B^int be the plaquettes whose
EVERY vertex has coordinate strictly between0 and l_i in each
direction. Thus no plaquette in this set touches a tile boundary.
Put S_B=sum_(p in P_B^int) h_p. Empty sets cause no difficulty.

For any nonnegative bounded F depending only on this strict
interior, repeated RP Cauchy--Schwarz gives

 E_mu F <= [E_mu product_(b in board) theta_b F]^(1/K_B). (4.1)

Here theta_b denotes the successive reflected copy, including
the induced orientations on links. For completeness, in direction1,
reflect a block of length l_1 across its right site plane to make
a pair of copies. Cauchy--Schwarz against1 yields a square-root
bound. The pair lies in a block of length2l_1; repeat across its
right site plane until the length is N_1. Each intermediate block
fits in a closed half because its length is at most N_1/2.
Then do directions2 and3. At each stage the existing product
has no boundary dependence in the direction being doubled.
The exponents multiply to1/K_B. This proves(4.1) for the single
function needed here without assuming a multipoint theorem.

Take F=exp(t S_B/g^2). Reflections preserve h_p, and the strict
interiors are disjoint. Therefore

       sum_b theta_b S_B <= S

pointwise. Apply(2.2) after(4.1) to obtain, for0<=t<2,

 log E_mu exp(t S_B/g^2)
                   <=(9 V_B/2) artanh(t/2).             (4.2)

In particular

                E_mu exp(S_B/g^2)<=3^(9V_B/4).         (4.3)

This is an actual local ground-state concentration bound. The
constant depends on the enclosing tile's cell volume, not the
ambient torus volume. A smaller plaquette set contained in the
same strict interior obeys the same bound by monotonicity.
We do not replace V_B by its number of plaquettes without proof.

For a simple uniform local consequence, suppose all N_i are
divisible by4 with N_i/4 a nonnegative integral power of2. A translate of
a4x4x4 tile has a full four-plaquette spatial edge star strictly
inside it: for an edge in direction1 use endpoints(1,2,2) and
(2,2,2), and permute directions for the others. Thus, with

                 rho_e=sum_(p containing e) sqrt(h_p),

Cauchy--Schwarz gives rho_e^2<=4 S_B, and(4.3) proves

             E_mu exp[rho_e^2/(4g^2)]<=3^144.           (4.4)

The explicit constant144 is9*64/4. It is not optimized.
The requirement on torus divisibility is part of this proved
version; it is harmless for a chosen dyadic regulator sequence,
but is not silently removed for arbitrary periods.

The standard chessboard context was checked against the primary

5. AN ACTUAL STATE-SENSITIVE MARKED CURVATURE INEQUALITY

Let W>=0 be a measurable mark with 0<m=E_mu W<infinity and
write nu=(W/m)mu. Relative entropy is

 H(nu|mu)=E_mu[(W/m)log(W/m)] in[0,infinity].

For finite entropy the entropy variational inequality and(4.4)
give

 E_nu rho_e^2 <=4g^2[H(nu|mu)+144log3],

and consequently

 E_mu[rho_e W]
   <=2g m sqrt[H(nu|mu)+144log3].                       (5.1)

The inequality is also valid with an infinite right side.
It is a bound under the ACTUAL ground law with the actual mark.
It has neither an independence assumption nor an ambient-volume
constant. It is not an inference that the curvature and W factor.

Only the links in the four-plaquette star are needed. Let pi_e
project onto their union (at most13 unoriented links), and put
mu_e=(pi_e)_*mu. By applying the same entropy variational inequality
on that finite star, the entropy in(5.1) can be replaced by

                    H((pi_e)_*nu | mu_e).              (5.2)

This is no larger than the full entropy, but need not be small.
The curvature marginal is genuinely that of Omega^2.

The same statement works at the CURRENT flowed configuration
without claiming RP for the flowed law. The compact smooth
spatial flow Phi_r is a diffeomorphism for every finite r:
its smooth vector field has complete forward and backward flow.
For a possibly r-dependent mark W_r>=0, m_r=E_mu W_r>0, define

 nu_(e,r)=(pi_e o Phi_r)_*[(W_r/m_r)mu],
 h_(e,r)=H(nu_(e,r)|mu_e).

Then, by applying(4.4) to the reference mu_e and comparing the
ACTUAL tilted pushforward to it,

 E_mu[rho_e(Phi_r U) W_r(U)]
                <=2g m_r sqrt[h_(e,r)+144log3].         (5.3)

For W_r=|J_(r,e)|^2 this is a precise consumer for the actual
marked Jacobi term. Zero m_r gives a zero left side and is
omitted. The information still needed is the entropy of a
finite-star marginal under the derivative-weighted flow.
Neither the unmarked energy nor its new local exponential
moment controls that entropy.

One should also retain the parabolic spatial structure. Even a

6. WHAT INTEGRATION BY PARTS ACTUALLY DOES TO THE FLOW MARK

Write c=4/a^2, b=-c grad S, and f_r=f o Phi_r for any fixed
smooth real physical f. Autonomy of the flow gives

                 partial_r f_r=b.grad f_r.

Put l=log Omega and

       B=Delta S+2 grad l.grad S=div_mu(grad S).

The exact action eigenfunction identity for a four-distinct-link
SU2 plaquette gives

              Delta S=3(|P_sp|-S).                     (6.1)

Indeed each of its four link Laplacians acts on its fundamental
matrix entry with eigenvalue-3/4. Consequently the Hessian
transport/product rule and weighted integration by parts give

 d_r E_mu|grad f_r|^2
  =-2c E_mu Hess S(grad f_r,grad f_r)
                            +c E_mu[B |grad f_r|^2].    (6.2)

This is an Eulerian identity with all coefficients evaluated at
the original U. It is equivalent to following Jacobi variations
along Phi_r; no time-dependent reference measure is discarded.

The second term is not zero merely because E_mu B=0. In fact
B is nonzero in this ACTUAL model: at a flat configuration,
grad S=0,S=0, so B=3|P_sp|>0. Since mu has a positive smooth
density, E_mu B^2>0. The smooth positive gauge-invariant mark
W=1+epsilon B, with0<epsilon<1/(2||B||infinity), obeys

                      E_mu[B W]=epsilon E_mu B^2>0.    (6.3)

This does not assert that W is a particular flow derivative.
It disproves a general symmetry or unmarked-centering argument
for eliminating the weighted divergence term.

Integrating that term by parts instead yields exactly

 E_mu[B |grad f_r|^2]
             =-2 E_mu Hess f_r(grad S,grad f_r).         (6.4)

The new term is a SECOND configuration derivative of the
flowed observable. The Fisher estimate
E_mu|grad l|^2<=18n/g^2 controls only an unweighted square.
Using it in(6.2) by Cauchy--Schwarz introduces
E_mu[|grad S|^2 |grad f_r|^4] in addition to the extensive
factor n/g^2. Neither quantity is presently controlled at
the desired scale. Thus integration by parts has exposed,
not removed, the correlated derivative.

There is a second exact way to display this cost. For any
smooth vector-valued mark J, apply(1.2) to
f_epsilon=(|J|^2+epsilon)^1/2 and send epsilon down to0.
Kato's inequality gives

 E_mu[S |J|^2]
   <=(9/4)n g^2 E_mu|J|^2
                          +(g^4/8)E_mu|nabla J|^2.     (6.5)

Here a metric covariant derivative can be used for a smooth
vector-bundle mark; the scalar norm regularization is sufficient.
If J is a flow tangent, nabla J includes a second variation
of the flow. Its coefficient is small in g, but no configuration
space diffusion/dissipation term in the deterministic Jacobi
equation absorbs E|nabla J|^2. Treating it as already controlled
would restate an unproved stronger estimate.

7. EXACT TRANSPORT ENTROPY AND A FREE REFERENCE DIAGNOSTIC

For a FIXED smooth nonnegative normalized density w relative
to mu, let nu_0=w mu and nu_r=(Phi_r)_*nu_0. The diffeomorphism
change of variables gives

 H(nu_r|mu)
   =H(nu_0|mu)+E_(nu_0)[log mu_density(U)
       -log mu_density(Phi_r U)-log Jac Phi_r(U)]
   =H(nu_0|mu)+c int_0^r E_(nu_t) B dt.                (7.1)

All densities are with respect to product Haar volume. Formula
(7.1) is first derived for positive w and then by regularization.
It does not posit invariance of mu under deterministic spatial
flow. A final-time-dependent tangent weight W_r can be held
fixed when applying this identity for that specific r; no
unjustified time differentiation of W_r is performed.

The right side again contains a marked B term. The global
Fisher estimate and current-action dissipation do not give a
cutoff-uniform bound on it. Moreover, replacing the local
entropy(5.2) by a global transported entropy can be intrinsically
far too expensive even in the free Gaussian comparison.

Indeed for independent real Gaussian modes X_k with any positive
variances sigma_k^2, a linear heat flow sends X_k to
exp(-r omega_k)X_k. Its full transported relative entropy is
EXACTLY

 H_r=(1/2)sum_k[exp(-2r omega_k)-1+2r omega_k].          (7.2)

The mode variance cancels. On a three-dimensional spatial lattice,
a positive proportion of transverse Maxwell modes have
omega_k comparable to a^-2, with their number comparable to n.
At fixed positive r, (7.2) is then bounded below by c n r/a^2
for small a, and has the same order as an upper bound.

This is only a FREE-reference calculation. It is not a claim
about the unknown nonlinear marked entropy or instability of
the actual ground state. It shows why imposing a bounded full
transport entropy would exclude even an otherwise useful free
heat smoothing. The finite-star entropy in(5.2)--(5.3), and a
parabolic estimate using its actual spatial placement, are the
more appropriate remaining quantities.
