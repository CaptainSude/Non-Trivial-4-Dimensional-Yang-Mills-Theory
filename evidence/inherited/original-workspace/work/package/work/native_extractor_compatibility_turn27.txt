2. SECOND DERIVATIVES OF THE PREPARING MAP DO NOT CONTRIBUTE

Work at the trivial pair (U,J)=(1,0), with no fixed colored spectator.
F is holomorphic and invariant under the COMPLETE own-field gauge
action. Constant color conjugations suffice to prove

                       DF(1,0)=0.                      (2.1)

Indeed each component of this derivative is an invariant covector on
the adjoint Lie algebra. For the source's semisimple group it vanishes.
For a simple group the invariant quadratic color form is a multiple of
the fixed trace form; this is the 21-coordinate case. For a product of
simple groups retain one such color sector for each factor.

B109(4.14) proves the corresponding first-jet cancellation; (4.32)--
(4.33) identifies the quadratic color form. For the independent pair
extension the same constant-conjugation proof applies separately to
the U and J derivative slots, so no unstated physical-current identity
on the independent J domain is needed.

Let P(B)=(U_i(exp iB),J_i(exp iB)). The literal chain rule gives

 D^2(F o P)(0)[b,c]
   =D^2F(1,0)[DP(0)b,DP(0)c]+DF(1,0)D^2P(0)[b,c]
   =D^2F(1,0)[DP(0)b,DP(0)c].                         (2.2)

This is precisely the reduction behind B109(4.35). Neither the
nonlinear constraint's second derivative, the second current derivative,
nor the second derivative of the selected gauge can add a quadratic
term. This does NOT say that the entire prepared Hessian equals the
raw U-U Hessian: mixed U-J and J-J pieces can matter on general data.

The assumptions matter. A mark invariant only after also rotating a
fixed colored spectator need not satisfy (2.1). Nor may one replace
the expansion point (1,0) by an arbitrary nontrivial background and
keep (2.1). Such cases are outside this conclusion.

3. THE AFFINE CONTRACTION OF THE FIRST DERIVATIVE

the line integral of an affine potential along a translated path is

critical point of the constrained quadratic action. Uniqueness of

    DJ_i(0)b_F = d* d H_i b_F = 0.                    (3.1)

This argument uses the HOMOGENEOUS full determining map, or a finite

whole-lattice coefficient are legitimate moment tests: cut them off,
use the source's exponential derivative kernels, and pass to the
limit in the finite position moments. Equivalently apply the local

Differentiating gauge invariance at (1,0), using (2.1), also yields

             D^2F(1,0)[(d omega,0),v]=0.               (3.2)
