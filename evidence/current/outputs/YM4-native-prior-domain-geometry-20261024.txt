3. NORMAL AND SECOND FUNDAMENTAL FORM

Write q_j=|w_j| near its positive level s0 and n_j=w_j/s0 there.
By(4)--(6), after shrinking the weak floor,

 grad q_j=(D w_j)^T n_j,
            c_L/2<=|grad q_j|<=C_L.                         (8)

The surface therefore is smooth even though several such surfaces
can coincide after exterior conditioning. For any tangent vector v
to this SINGLE surface, n_j dot D w_j v=0. The exact Hessian identity
is

 D^2 q_j[v,v]=|D w_j v|^2/s0+n_j dot D^2 w_j[v,v].          (9)

With outward normal grad q_j/|grad q_j|, its second fundamental form
satisfies

 II_j(v,v)>=-C_L g0 |v_(T_j)|^2,
 |II_j(v,v)|<=C_L(s0^(-1)+g0)|v_(T_j)|^2.                (10)

The upper bound refers to tangent vectors; positivity from the
first term in(9) is not discarded when useful. These bounds are in
the actual independent NOISE metric. In unscaled physical link-angle
coordinates the powers change. No assertion about an ambient
physical-group metric is silently used in a noise Poincare formula.

The supported coincident-pair construction of
native_prior_mask_jet_gate does not refute(8). In that construction
both gradients are the same NONZERO vector. Pairwise transversality
fails, while each individual surface remains regular with(10).

For the soft-penalty variant, a stronger ambient statement is useful.
On the ENTIRE region q_j>=s0 within the same containing product,

 D^2 q_j[v,v]
  =(|D w_j v|^2-(n_j dot D w_j v)^2)/q_j

  >=-C_L g0 |v_(T_j)|^2                                    (10a)

for EVERY v, without a tangency assumption. Here n_j=w_j/q_j.
The first term is positive semidefinite. The norm singularity at
w_j=0 is irrelevant to a penalty that is identically zero for q_j<s0.

4. A UNIFORM OUTWARD RADIAL INEQUALITY

The second derivative bound alone gives an especially useful identity:

 D w_j(x)x-[w_j(x)-w_j(0)]
       =integral_0^1 [D w_j(x)-D w_j(t x)]x dt,

so its norm is at most C_L g0 |x_(T_j)|^2. At q_j=s0,

 grad q_j dot x
  >=s0-|w_j(0)|-C_L g0 |x_(T_j)|^2
  >=s0/2-C'_L g0 S^2
  >=s0/4.                                                  (11)

The last step uses g0 S^2/s0<=C epsilon0, not smallness of the
unscaled radius S itself. This is why the physical first chart and
its fixed containing constant matter. Both the mean-field offset
and the exact retained z are retained in w_j(0).

The same estimate holds whenever q_j>=s0 in the containing product:
replace the first s0 in(11) by q_j, and keep the same lower bound.
Thus(12) and the weighted-flux estimate below also hold throughout
the ACTIVE PENALTY region, not only on the cut surface.

More strongly, choose a fixed gamma>0 sufficiently small. For every
y with |y_i|<=gamma s0, (4) implies

 grad q_j(x) dot (x-y)>=s0/8                               (12)

on every active boundary, uniformly over volume. The factor sqrt(v_L)
in the read norm is included when choosing gamma. Such y itself
satisfies every success test by(2),(4), after the same choice.
The hierarchy can also put the already used B_(Kr) core inside this
smaller product, but no such identification is needed for(12).

To prove star-shapedness, fix y in that product and x in D. The
segment from y to x remains inside the containing product. If a
particular q_j crossed from success to failure and later returned,
there would be a return crossing with nonpositive derivative along
the segment. At that crossing the segment vector is a positive
multiple of (current point-y), contradicting(12). Hence every mask
stays successful along the segment. Therefore

       D is star-shaped with respect to product_i B_(gamma s0).
                                                               (13)

In particular D is connected and contractible, and has no hidden
second component outside the completed product within its actual
prior chart. This conclusion uses the earlier proof that the full
original support is the unique small pivot branch in(1); it is not
a global claim about all configurations satisfying the compact-group
constraints on other branches.

At any boundary point, the radial vector has positive derivative
against ALL its active defining functions. Each surface can locally
be solved as a graph in that common direction; D is below their
minimum. Thus D is a finite Lipschitz domain locally, even with

5. SLOW SPATIAL WEIGHTS PRESERVE OUTWARD RADIAL FLUX

This verifies the native geometric input of the parent's trace
argument. Fix a mask read T_j and put

 chi_i=exp[-eta dist(i,T_j)],     X_i(x)=chi_i x_i.           (14)

For another mask k choose i0 in T_k. Its fixed read diameter gives
|chi_i/chi_i0-1|<=C_L eta for i in T_k, when eta is small. Using
(4),(11),(1),

 grad q_k dot X
  >=chi_i0[s0/4-C_L eta S]
  >=chi_i0 s0/8>0                                         (15)

after choosing eta once so C_L eta S/s0<=1/8. This choice is
independent of C, the number of masks and g0. On face j the chi
values on T_j equal1, so X dot the outward unit normal is at least

On the containing PRODUCT boundary, the same X has nonnegative flux
as well: on the i-th spherical coordinate face its component is
chi_i x_i and its outward normal is x_i/S, so the flux is chi_i S.
In the region q_k>=s0, (15) remains valid by the preceding radial
extension. These are the two positivity facts needed by integration
against an arbitrary increasing smooth mask penalty.

The lattice sums sum_i chi_i and sum_i chi_i^2 are finite uniform
constants, since T_j has bounded size. Consequently

            div X<=C_eta,       |X|<=C_eta S.               (16)

Coincident faces have the same actual outward orientation: opposite
outward normals would contradict(11). At a generic intersection one
uses the actual a.e. outward normal of the Lipschitz boundary. It is
one of the active smooth normals almost everywhere; (15) then gives
the required sign. This step does not count an edge as a separate

For completeness, the full first weighted action accretivity gives

Thus div X-X dot grad H has an O(1+r^2) upper bound. After dividing

6. TWO LIMITS IMPORTANT FOR SPATIAL MIXING

FIRST: arbitrary principal conditional sections do not inherit this
proof automatically. Fixing extra active coordinates changes w_j(0)
by their generally large affine contribution. It need not leave the
strict core margin(2). A row that was nonzero before restriction can
also vanish on the remaining coordinates, while its new offset is
arbitrarily close to the cutoff. Then the redundancy argument(7)
no longer applies. No uniform common core, normal lower bound or
conditional weighted covariance theorem is claimed for every such
section. This is a missing premise, not a demonstrated disconnected
native conditional section.

SECOND: the old convex-PRODUCT weighted-gradient argument has a
different boundary condition. On a Neumann mask face, grad f is
tangent: n dot grad f=0. A diagonal spatial weight W generally does
not preserve that tangent space when n has several noise sites.
Already a flat plaquette row proportional to x_i-x_k has tangent
vectors with v_i=v_k; unequal weights give

                 n dot Wv proportional to (w_i-w_k)n0 dot v_i,

which need not vanish. Actual interior plaquette masks have exactly
such multi-site linear reads. In a weighted Bochner integration this
produces an extra normal-boundary term; it cannot be discarded using
Neumann boundary data. Slow variation may help a NEW argument, but
small eta alone does not prove the needed trace bound for that term.

Thus a volume-uniform unweighted Neumann gap, if proved from the
trace absorption, is substantial but not yet the earlier exponential
spatial covariance estimate. One must establish the spatially weighted
operator estimate on these actual boundary conditions, or use another
locality/coupling mechanism that does not assume a product domain.
