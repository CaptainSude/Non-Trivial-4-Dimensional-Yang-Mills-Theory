TURN82 -- GLOBAL ORIGINAL-LINK HOLOMORPHY OF THE EXACT HS SOURCE CHANNEL
20 October 2026. history47. Finite-regulator theorem.

RESULT
The exact two-HS Gibbs channel smooths EVERY bounded measurable original
observable to a holomorphic function of the ORIGINAL link coordinates,
on a complex Hilbert ball of radius c/sqrt(beta+1) about every real
configuration. The operator norm there is bounded independently of
volume and of the original observable's number of reads. A stronger
bound depends only on the squared imaginary coordinate norm. There is
no restriction to a safe auxiliary-current event: the conditional source
is evaluated only at REAL auxiliary records. All source products and
normalizers are retained. This is a new finite-scale consumer of the
known exact two-HS representation, not a new Yang--Mills measure or a
continuum source-transport theorem.

1. EXACT ORIGINAL-LAW CHANNEL AND REAL COORDINATES

Use the finite nondegenerate four-dimensional SU(2) lattice and literal
Wilson law mu_beta of turn81_external_source_history.txt. Its exact
positive augmentation has, conditionally on the complete original U,

 G_p=m_p(U)+Z_p,       m_p=sqrt(beta)(A_p+B_p)/2,
 R_h=d_h(U)+G_p V_h^dagger+W_h,  d_h=sqrt(beta)U_h/2,    (1.1)

where h=(U_h,V_h) is one of the two paths in p, and all Z_p,W_h are
independent standard complex 2x2 Gaussian matrices. Their four complex
entries have variance1. The auxiliary marginal is the actual interacting
zero-link law nu_beta, including its literal normalization D_beta.
Conditionally on REAL xi=(G,R), the original links have the normalized
product tilted-Haar law k_xi from TURN81. Define

 (CB)(xi)=E[B(U)|xi],       (Kf)(U)=E[f(xi)|U],
 P=KC.                                                       (1.2)

Thus P1=1, ||PB||_infinity<=||B||_infinity, mu_beta P=mu_beta,
and P is positive self-adjoint on L2(mu_beta). These are actual-law
identities, not properties of an approximate reference distribution.

Identify each complex matrix with its eight REAL coordinates. Then its
Gaussian density is pi^-4 exp(-x dot x), and each real coordinate has
variance1/2. All complexifications below are of these real coordinates.
In particular, no conjugation or Re operation is applied to a complex
link parameter and then called holomorphic.

Fix ANY real original configuration U^0 and write

 U_e(z)=exp(i sum_{a=1}^3 z_{e,a} sigma_a/2) U_e^0,
                  z_e in C^3.                              (1.3)

Real coordinates z give SU(2) links. Extend every real coordinate of
m_p,d_h and of the real-linear map G -> G V_h^dagger analytically in z.
The dagger in a group expression is extended by the appropriate inverse;
the real and imaginary matrix entries in (1.1) are each extended as real
analytic functions. Equivalently, for a matrix-valued analytic extension
M(z), its real-coordinate extensions are

 [M(z)+overline(M(bar z))]/2,
 [M(z)-overline(M(bar z))]/(2i).                            (1.4)

The bar in M(bar z) acts also on the fixed REAL-coordinate coefficients
as indicated; the two expressions are holomorphic. This convention is
important: for example Im m(z) below means the imaginary part of its
complexified R8 coordinate vector, not the ordinary imaginary matrix
entries of the physical complex matrix m(U).

Collect the relevant coordinates into real Euclidean spaces. Write

 n(z,G)=d(z)+L(z)G.                                        (1.5)

Each plaquette block of L has two outputs, one per half-path; at real z
each output is right multiplication by a unitary matrix. L is block
diagonal in p. Its coefficients at complex z are holomorphic.

2. AN EXACT GAUSSIAN TOTAL-VARIATION FORMULA

For complex means define the complex kernel on REAL auxiliary variables

 kappa_z(G,R)=pi^[-(d_G+d_R)/2]
   exp[-(G-m(z)) dot (G-m(z))
       -(R-d(z)-L(z)G) dot (R-d(z)-L(z)G)].                (2.1)

The dot here is the BILINEAR extension of the real Euclidean square.
It is not the Hermitian square. For real z this is exactly (1.1).
Set

 mu=Re m(z),  a=Im m(z),  A=Im L(z),
 v=Im d(z)+A mu.                                          (2.2)

Here mu,a,A,v are ordinary real vectors/matrices. If ||A||_op<1, direct
integration, first over R and then over G, gives

 integral |kappa_z| dG dR
  = exp(|a|^2) det(I-A^T A)^(-1/2)
           exp[v^T (I-AA^T)^(-1) v].                     (2.3)

Indeed integral |pi^-d/2 exp[-(x-w)^2]| dx=exp(|Im w|^2).
After the R integral, G=mu+Y with density pi^-d_G/2 exp(-|Y|^2),
and one integrates exp(|AY+v|^2). Completing this REAL Gaussian square
gives (2.3); the determinant is over the G coordinates. Thus, if
||A||_op<=eta<1,

 log integral |kappa_z|
 <= |a|^2 + |v|^2/(1-eta^2)
                    + ||A||_HS^2/[2(1-eta^2)].            (2.4)

The determinant estimate uses -log(1-t)<=t/(1-eta^2) for
0<=t<=eta^2. There is no dimension prefactor hidden in (2.4).

Also, exactly,

                    integral kappa_z dG dR=1.             (2.5)

For each fixed real G the complex shifted R-Gaussian integrates to1;
the remaining shifted G-Gaussian also integrates to1. Absolute
integrability from (2.3) justifies this order and the equality. Equation
(2.5) is the source-zero normalizer on the complex domain. We do not
divide by an unverified complex partition function.

3. LOCAL INCIDENCE BOUNDS AND THE VOLUME-INDEPENDENT DOMAIN

There are numerical constants r_0,C depending only on the fixed local
path convention and maximal plaquette incidence, not on volume, beta,
U^0 or the source reads, such that on |Re z_e|<=r_0 and
|Im z_e|<=r_0 for every relevant link,

 ||Im L(z)||_op <= C ||Im z||_infinity,
 ||Im L(z)||_HS^2 <= C sum_e |Im z_e|^2,
 |Im m(z)|^2+|Im d(z)+(Im L(z))Re m(z)|^2
                         <= C beta sum_e |Im z_e|^2.      (3.1)

Here and below coordinate norms are the Euclidean norms on the three
Pauli/2 coordinates per independent link. To prove (3.1), compare each
local function along x+i t y, 0<=t<=1, to its REAL value at x=Re z.
All local matrix products, inverses and their first derivatives have
fixed bounds on this fixed chart. Each m_p has a sqrt(beta) factor and
reads four links, so its imaginary norm is at most
C sqrt(beta) sum_{e in p}|y_e|. Also |Re m_p|<=C sqrt(beta).
Each d_h reads one link. Each output block L_h reads only the second
path link, so ||Im L_h||_op+||Im L_h||_HS<=C|y_(second h)|.
Since there are two outputs per p, the block-diagonal operator bound
is controlled by the largest such link coordinate. Squaring and
summing costs only the bounded local incidences (six plaquettes per
link in d=4), proving all three assertions. The same argument covers
reflected path conventions and link inversions, with a fixed constant.

Shrink r_0 once so C||Im z||_infinity<=1/2. Equations (2.4),(3.1) give

       ||kappa_z||_TV <= exp[C_0(beta+1)||Im z||_2^2].     (3.2)

The constants are independent of the total number of coordinates.
The real part is harmless on the indicated real chart: it changes the
positive reference means. In particular, a complex Hilbert ball

        ||z||_2 < r/sqrt(beta+1),       0<r<=r_0,          (3.3)

about EVERY real U^0 has ||kappa_z||_TV<=exp(C_0 r^2).
One can reset the real center at any other real configuration; no
small-Wilson-action or large-current assumption is used anywhere.

Holomorphy is genuine. On a compact subdomain with ||Im L||_op<1,
integrating the absolute R-Gaussian leaves a positive G precision
bounded away from zero. All coordinate derivatives introduce finite
Gaussian polynomials, still dominated after a fixed further margin.
Fubini/Morera or dominated differentiation therefore proves
holomorphy when (2.1) is integrated against ANY bounded measurable
function of the REAL auxiliaries. That function need not have a
complex continuation of its own.

4. THE COMPLETE NORMALIZED SOURCE FAMILY

Let B_1,...,B_m be arbitrary measurable ORIGINAL observables with
0<=B_i<=1, and R_s=sum_i |s_i|. For real auxiliaries put

 C_s(xi)=E_kxi exp(sum_i s_i B_i),
 H(s,z)=integral kappa_z(xi) C_s(xi) dxi.                  (4.1)

For real z this is P exp(sum s_i B_i)(U(z)). Uniformly in real xi,
|C_s|<=e^R_s and |C_s-1|<=e^R_s-1. Equations (2.5),(3.2) now imply

 |H(s,z)| <= exp[C_0(beta+1)||Im z||^2+R_s],
 |H(s,z)-1| <= exp[C_0(beta+1)||Im z||^2](e^R_s-1).        (4.2)

This function is jointly holomorphic in the original complex link
coordinates and in all source parameters. On (3.3), the canonical
logarithm with log H(0,z)=0 exists whenever

              exp(C_0 r^2)(e^R_s-1)<1.                   (4.3)

There its absolute value is bounded by minus the logarithm of1 minus
the left side of (4.3). Thus a fixed source-parameter neighborhood
and an original-link Hilbert radius c/sqrt(beta+1) hold simultaneously,
with no discarded scalar factor, no auxiliary bad event and no
current-zero exclusion.

For general ||B||_infinity<=1, PB has the same background bound.
Cauchy gives, for a unit Hilbert direction h,

 |D_h^k PB(U^0)| <= k! exp(C_0 r^2)
                            [sqrt(beta+1)/r]^k.          (4.4)

Fixed-order multilinear derivatives follow by polarization; one may
use k^k instead of k! with the same product of directional norms.
This is an operator-jet estimate, not an all-arity coefficient-sum
Wiener norm or a strong polymer source norm.

The source family is essential. At source zero,

 partial_i H=PB_i,
 partial_i partial_j H=P(B_i B_j),
 partial_i partial_j log H=P(B_i B_j)-(PB_i)(PB_j).       (4.5)

Replacing H by exp(sum s_i PB_i) loses its actual mixed conditional
terms. Stationarity gives the exact ORIGINAL generating function

       E_mu H(s,0;U^0=U)=E_mu exp(sum_i s_i B_i).         (4.6)

It is the same Wilson law and the same original observables. This
identity is not a source-dependent change of beta or of the action.

4A. RELATIVE L2 DENSITY, ALL FIXED JETS, AND ITERATED SMOOTHING

At the same real center U^0 write kappa_0 for the actual probability
kernel and Lambda_z=kappa_z/kappa_0 on its real auxiliary space. Put
Delta m=m(z)-m(0) and Delta n(G)=n(z,G)-n(0,G). Gaussian square
completion gives the exact identity

 E_kappa0 |Lambda_z|^2
  = exp(2||Delta m||^2)
      E_{G~N(m(0)+2Re Delta m,I/2)} exp(2||Delta n(G)||^2).
                                                               (4A.1)

The norms on the right are HERMITIAN norms of the complexified real
coordinate vectors. To check constants, for Y~N(0,I/2) a mean shift
Delta has density ratio exp(2Y dot Delta-Delta dot Delta). Its squared
modulus tilts Y by 2Re Delta and leaves exp(2||Delta||^2). Apply this
first to each R shift at fixed G, then to the G shift.

Now Delta n=Delta d+Delta L G. Replace the complex vector Delta n by
the stacked real vector sqrt(2)(Re Delta n,Im Delta n). The same
determinant formula as(2.3) applies. Local incidence, now comparing z
to0, gives operator norm O(||z||_infinity), squared Hilbert--Schmidt
norm O(||z||_2^2), and squared shifted mean O(beta||z||_2^2).
Possibly shrinking r_0 and increasing a numerical C_1,

       E_kappa0 |Lambda_z|^2
                    <=exp[C_1(beta+1)||z||_2^2].          (4A.2)

This is an L2 bound for the ACTUAL normalized density ratio, not just
an unnormalized source numerator. The map z -> Lambda_z is holomorphic
with values in L2(kappa_0) on the indicated Hilbert ball. For example,
Gaussian domination on a slightly larger compact ball proves weak
holomorphy and the locally uniform L2 bound proves strong holomorphy.
Vector-valued Cauchy and polarization therefore give, for each fixed k,

 ||D^k Lambda_0[v_1,...,v_k]||_L2(kappa0)
       <=C_k(beta+1)^(k/2) product_i||v_i||_2,
 E_kappa0 D^k Lambda_0[v_1,...,v_k]=0,        k>=1.        (4A.3)

The second identity follows by differentiating the exact normalization
(2.5). All derivatives here are Frechet jets in the local exponential
coordinates(1.3), not yet covariant tensor derivatives.

For a real bounded original F, use (PF)(U)=E_kappaU CF. Subtracting its
conditional mean before applying(4A.3) gives the pointwise bound

 ||D^k PF(U)||_op^2
        <=C_k(beta+1)^k Var_kappaU(CF).                   (4A.4)

The operator norm is the supremum over unit Hilbert directions in every
slot; there is no sum over all coordinate k-tuples. P is positive
self-adjoint, and the exact conditional variance identity is

 E_mu Var_kappaU(CF)
       =||CF||_L2(nu)^2-||PF||_L2(mu)^2
       =<F,P(I-P)F>_mu.                                  (4A.5)

For F_n=P^n F, the spectral theorem consequently gives

 E_mu ||D^k P^(n+1)F||_op^2
  <=C_k(beta+1)^k <F,P^(2n+1)(I-P)F>_mu
  <=C_k(beta+1)^k Var_mu(F)/(2n+2),        n>=0.           (4A.6)

We used lambda^(2n+1)(1-lambda)<=1/(2n+2) for 0<=lambda<=1;
constant components have zero contribution. Constants C_k may change
between lines, but depend only on k and the fixed local incidence.
The same statements hold for a finite convex mixture of reflected
channels by retaining its independent choice label in the joint law.

There is also no repeated complex-radius loss for P^n exp(sum s_i B_i)
with n>=1: P^(n-1) exp(sum s_i B_i) and its difference from1 obey the
same real sup bounds e^R_s and e^R_s-1, so the LAST smoothing step
proves(4.2)--(4.3) on the same ball. Its read set can nevertheless grow
by n halos. Equations(4A.4)--(4A.6) are actual original-law derivative
estimates; they do not imply any fixed coefficient-sum polymer norm
or preservation of a physical channel after its buffer is exhausted.

5. EXACT FINITE READS, GAUGE COVARIANCE AND REFLECTION

If the B_i read only original links in S, C_s depends only on J_e for
e in S. Include the COMPLETE plaquette auxiliary blocks (G_p and both
R_h) for every p incident to S. This contains every auxiliary read of
those currents and is closed under their conditional generation(1.1).
All other blocks in (2.1) integrate to1. Therefore H(s,z) depends only
on the original links in the union of these plaquettes, an exact fixed
plaquette-star enlargement N(S). In (3.2)--(4.4) the coordinate sum
may be restricted to N(S). No unnecessary whole-volume factor arises.

For a spatially flowed observable whose original read is an entire
finite spatial slice, N(S) remains that large set with its fixed
collar. This theorem does not turn flow into finite spatial support.

The full real joint law is gauge covariant, so P preserves gauge
invariance. The holomorphic germ is its unique local continuation;
there is no gauge fixing in the argument. If a path convention is
not reflection covariant, use the reflected pair of channels and
their average Pbar=(P+mathcal R P mathcal R)/2. The same estimates
hold by the triangle inequality, using the union of the two fixed
halos; Pbar1=1 and mu Pbar=mu. For complex-valued observables,
mathcal R is the LINEAR geometric reflection and OS conjugation is
applied separately in the form E[overline(mathcal R F)H].

The exact OS-null buffered-round statement is proved separately in
turn82_auxiliary_channel_gauge.txt: it requires all original and
auxiliary reads, including the enlarged star, to remain strictly in
the relevant half. We import no stronger assertion here. In
particular P is not an algebra homomorphism; (4.5) must be retained.

6. ACTUAL GAIN AND REMAINING SCALING

This avoids the prior need to evaluate C_s at COMPLEX auxiliary
variables: C_s stays bounded on its complete REAL auxiliary space.
Consequently it works at small, zero and arbitrarily large currents.
It smooths even a discontinuous bounded original B and gives a
common analytic germ centered at every real original configuration.

The beta and read scaling remain explicit. On N relevant links with
equal imaginary angle size epsilon, (3.2) costs
exp[C(beta+1)N epsilon^2]. A volume-independent bound therefore
allows epsilon of order 1/sqrt[(beta+1)N], not a fixed per-link radius
for an arbitrarily large physical read. For a fixed number of link
directions the radius is of order beta^-1/2. This is stronger than a
total-l1 budget but is not automatically the native fixed polymer
background domain. Repeated P rounds have to retain their enlarged
reads; the present theorem alone supplies no all-scale source norm.

For canonical sources beta h_p the observable bound itself grows
with beta. One may apply the theorem to a specified bounded physical
probe (for example a bounded function of a magnetic energy) exactly,
but cannot silently replace beta h_p by a unit-bounded observable or
claim the same source-parameter radius for an unbounded family.

The interacting auxiliary law has not been continued or bounded by
a Gaussian. No correlation decay, continuum Cauchy property, physical
variance lower bound or positive-energy nontriviality follows merely
from the conditional smoothing estimate. It is a concrete actual-law
initial-source domain, with exact normalization and mixed contacts.
