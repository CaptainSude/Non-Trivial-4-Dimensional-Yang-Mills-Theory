Separately tagged signed localization/walk terms need the stronger joint

from a generic warning about Markov kernels.

No frozen proof/status file is changed. No original-law or continuum gap

1. WHAT CAN BE CONSTRUCTED EXACTLY ON THE ORIGINAL WILSON LAW

On a finite lattice let G be compact, H normalized product Haar, and
  rho_beta(U)=exp[-beta S_W(U)],  Z_beta=integral rho_beta dH.
At finite real beta the continuous Wilson action is bounded, so rho_beta
is strictly positive and bounded above/below. For finite sets of links
this gives ordinary Borel conditional kernels; no weak-field theorem is
needed for their existence.

Split links into a changed interior x and retained exterior e. Write
S_W(x,e)=S_int(x;e)+S_ext(e), assigning to S_int all plaquettes that read
x. Then the exact conditional law is
  p_e(dx)=exp[-beta S_int(x;e)] H_int(dx)/Z_int(e).          (1.1)
It has full Haar support at every exterior e. The conditional heat bath
K((x,e),d(y,e'))=p_e(dy) delta_e(de') preserves the full Wilson law and
is reversible. Indeed its joint density is
  mu_ext(de) p_e(dx) p_e(dy),                              (1.2)
which is symmetric in x,y.

Equation (1.2) is a concrete exact construction, but p_e is the FULL
Wilson conditional. It is not a constrained-minimum reference density
restricted to a weak chart. The source's reference denominator alone
does not identify those two distributions.

More generally, let a_h(x,e)>=0 be an exact positive partition of unity
on the original state, with all determining variables retained. For
each h,e choose any defined probability q_h,e(dy), for example an
explicit weak-chart reference quotient on its admitted exterior. The
joint unnormalized measure
  J_h(de,dx,dy)=a_h(x,e) rho_beta(x,e) H(de,dx) q_h,e(dy)    (1.3)
has original marginal sum_h J_h(de,dx)=rho_beta H. Its new marginal is
  nu_h(de,dy)=m_h(de) q_h,e(dy),
  m_h(de)=integral_x a_h(x,e) rho_beta(x,e) H(de,dx).        (1.4)

This retains the OLD branch mass exactly. It does not replace m_h by
the reference normalization. If a branch or exterior is inadmissible,
its actual complementary branch must be retained; an undefined quotient
is not an implicit probability kernel. Sequential normalized kernels
give the same construction over finitely many dates, provided every
selection is made at the declared point in the chronology.

Consequently one can define a mass-preserving alternative R_ref now.
What is not supplied by this definition is the equality
  native post-basic-step density = the marginal in (1.4), (1.5)
with the native cuts, variables, Jacobians, gauge factors and vacuum
scalars. Nor does (1.3) establish the native complete-history estimates
for its m_h. Those are precisely substantive attachments, not issues
that disappear on normalizing q.

2. A SHARP OBSTRUCTION TO A STATIONARY WEAK-REGION RESET

Fix e and a measurable weak region D with p_e(D^c)>0. Consider the
accepted-reference kernel
  K_e(x,dy)=a(x)q_e(dy)+(1-a(x))delta_x(dy),                (2.1)
where 0<=a<=1 and q_e(D)=1. Every x in D stays in D.
If p_e is invariant under (2.1), then
  p_e(D)=(p_e K_e)(D)
        =p_e(D)+integral_(D^c) a(x) p_e(dx).
Thus a=0 p_e-almost everywhere on D^c.                  (2.2)

This applies to a genuine finite Wilson conditional whenever D^c has
positive Haar measure. It is independent of how a depends on the old
configuration. The same conclusion holds for any invariant kernel for
which D is absorbing: stationary outflow from D^c must be balanced by
inflow from D, and here there is no such inflow.

In particular an independence Metropolis correction of a proposal
q_e supported on D cannot accept proposals from a point with q_e(x)=0:
the reverse proposal factor vanishes. It preserves p_e by refusing
exactly those proposed one-way healings.

This does NOT contradict native R, which is meant to change the current
representation, not keep the entire current Wilson distribution fixed.
It rules out a proposed shortcut: use a weak-chart redraw, enforce
stationarity, and then treat the same function of the new field as
the unchanged original physical observable. To preserve original
expectations under a nonstationary redraw one must transport the
observable, as follows.

3. THE EXACT INSERTION AFTER A REFERENCE REDRAW

For the actual joint (1.3) and a bounded original F(x,e), define the
complex numerator measure nu_h^F by pushing F J_h to (e,y). Then
  nu_h^F(de,dy)
    = [integral_x F(x,e) a_h(x,e)rho_beta(x,e)H(de,dx)]
                                                   q_h,e(dy).
Relative to nu_h, its density is
  f_h(e,y)=E_original[F(X,e) | e,h].                      (3.1)
The right side is independent of the redrawn y. Set it arbitrarily
on a nu_h-null set. It obeys |f_h|<=||F||_infinity.

The numerator is generally NOT F(y,e)nu_h(de,dy).
For example if a branch is selected by an old bad-field event, that
event may have conditional probability one in (3.1), even while the
new reference is supported entirely in a weak region. Keeping its
ancestral history label prevents confusing those two events.

At an ordinary T step, by contrast, a specified coarse observable f
has exact original pullback f(M(U)); a general fine F transports as
T(F rho)/T rho. These are different directional statements. A
conditional expectation cannot be inverted as an operator on every
original observable: it can discard conditional variance. Keeping
the actual joint variables preserves the numerator identity, not
an identity F=f(M(U)).

4. POSITIVE PAYOFF: TURN54'S TAGGED BOUND FOR BOUNDED ORIGINAL F

Here the attachment is stated precisely enough to distinguish an
actual native joint measure from an arbitrary coupling of equal-mass
marginals.

Fix a COMPLETE continued native history h through terminal date k,
including its original/ancestral tag, all later factors, and all
remaining external data e. Suppose the actual normalized insertions
and legitimate changes of variables have identified a finite
POSITIVE joint measure J_h^e on the original and retained variables.
Its specified terminal projection pi has the native marginal
  nu_h^e=pi_*J_h^e.                                      (4.1)
This is an equality of the actual construction; it is not inferred
from equality of two scalar integrals.

For any bounded original insertion F, the measure
  nu_h^{e,F}=pi_*(F J_h^e)
satisfies the measure inequality
  |nu_h^{e,F}| <= ||F||_infinity nu_h^e.                  (4.2)
Proof: for a measurable terminal set B, the total variation of the
pushforward is at most the pushforward of |F|J_h^e, which is bounded
by ||F|| times nu_h^e. Equivalently its Radon–Nikodym derivative
f_h^e is a conditional posterior with |f_h^e|<=||F||.
Singular gauge slices cause no problem for this finite-measure
argument. No division by a small branch mass is used in the bound.

TURN54 Section 7, at its stated native inputs, bounds the complete
tagged component with its fixed weaker account. For a tag which is
an ACTUAL measurable restriction of this positive history law, let
J_(k,X;tag_i)^e denote the full continued component measure, including
its allowed native history sum. Once its marginal identity (4.1)
is attached, the positive mass version of TURN54 gives immediately
  |integral F dJ_(k,X;tag_i)^e|
    <= C exp[-c N_i/(M R_i)]
       exp[-2lambda_0 c_beta P_k-kappa_c d_k(X)]
       ||F||_infinity.                                  (4.3)
Use the precise history summation/supremum convention of TURN54;
its census must not be counted again in summing (4.3).

There is no extra locality, analyticity, or derivative requirement
on f_h^e for (4.3). The conclusion is simply a positive-mass bound.
Even if the source formulation first states the operator estimate
on a smaller terminal test class, its positive mass estimate
(test 1) together with the ACTUAL joint measure proves (4.3).

This is weaker than pointwise domination of the old joint density
by a native envelope, but it is enough for this scalar inserted

envelope on every eliminated variable merely to prove (4.3).

Crucial scope:
  * The terminal marginal must be the marginal of the actual J_h^e.

  * All future factors reading old private variables must already

Thus a verified positive reference insertion identity could close
the bounded-insertion part together with the mass identity. One

theorem for every bounded F merely to discard the tagged histories.

There is a decisive distinction for a separately retained SIGNED

For such decorations the sufficient bound is on pi_*|J|, or on

5. BOUNDED SOURCES: WHAT THE PAYOFF DOES AND DOES NOT GIVE

For any real original observable X, however large its physical
rescaling, F_t=exp(i t X) has modulus one. At the positive-history
scope of (4.1)–(4.3), the discarded numerator is bounded uniformly
for real t.
It also applies to finite products of such characteristic functions.

For bounded original F_1,...,F_m with bounds B_l, leave the actual
baseline kernels, cutoffs and coupling fixed and insert
exp(sum_l z_l F_l) in the OLD density. Positivity and absolute
convergence give for any tagged numerator its coefficient-sum
majorant on |z_l|<=r_l:
  sum_n |[z^n] N_tag(z)| product_l r_l^(n_l)
      <= exp(sum_l r_l B_l) J_tag(1).                    (5.1)
The same TURN54 estimate applies to J_tag(1). This is an exact
bound on discarded source terms, not a claim that the complete
renormalized analytic density class is closed under those sources.
If B_l grow with the cutoff, (5.1) does not give a fixed uniform
complex disc at no cost.

Boundedness alone is not tightness of X. Conditional expectation
contraction gives |E(exp(i t X)|terminal)|<=1, not uniform continuity
at t=0. For example take the exact finite beta=0 SU(2) Wilson
law (product Haar), and let U_p be the holonomy of one nondegenerate
plaquette. Its four distinct Haar links make U_p Haar distributed.
The observable
  X_n=n(1-Re Tr(U_p)/2)
is gauge invariant.
This family is not tight: the nonnegative parenthesis is positive
almost surely. Redraw the displayed field to identity with a
normalized reference delta measure and keep the actual joint
law. Its current field is perfectly tight, and every original
characteristic posterior is bounded by one, but these facts do
not make X_n tight. This is an arbitrary wrong scaling, used
to diagnose the missing implication, not the physical scaling
that YM should use.

To obtain continuum control, one still needs information about
the actual original observable on the retained good histories:
for example its comparison with a tight coarse observable, or
uniform characteristic-function continuity/moment estimates.
The posterior in (3.1) is not automatically that coarse observable.
Likewise E(F_1 F_2|terminal) is not generally the product of the
two separate posteriors; discarded conditional fluctuations matter
for covariance and nontriviality.

6. WHY NORMALIZATION ALONE DOES NOT REPRODUCE THE NATIVE LEDGER

The explicit construction (1.3) gives exact original mass and
numerators for any normalized q. Its terminal marginal remembers
the actual m_h, which contains the original integrals and every
old selector. Replacing it by a tractable native expression is
precisely the evaluation that needs verification.

Three concrete alternatives are:

  A. Identify the native normalized reference insertion and
     changes of variables, including all scalars, with (4.1).
    Then the positive complete-history bounds immediately give
    (4.3) and (5.1) for bounded old insertions. Separately signed
    decorations still require the absolute-joint condition.

  B. Define the explicit alternative R_ref and prove the needed
     large-field/continuation estimates anew for its exact
     m_h and resulting complete density. This is a possible
     new construction, not an import of native bounds solely
     because its reference q resembles the native reference.

  C. Use the genuine Wilson heat bath (1.1). This needs no
     original-law identity audit, but the weak-reference
     suppression/localization estimates must be proved for
     the full conditional; stationarity does not supply them.

An arbitrary mass-one correction to an unmatched native output
chooses a different marginal or introduces the missing likelihood
ratio. Its numerator then contains that ratio. A small native
tag mass by itself cannot bound the ratio on the tagged set.
The support calculation (2.2) shows concretely why stationary
acceptance/rejection is not a free way around this issue.

7. CURRENT AUDIT CONCLUSION

The positive bounded-insertion payoff removes one unnecessary
extra demand: after an ACTUAL positive joint representation is
verified, no local analytic control of every posterior is needed
for the positive-history discarded-numerator bound. Terminal
positive mass is enough, with full chronology and normalization
as specified above. The signed-decoration counterexample shows
exactly why this cannot be inferred for every terminal expansion
term without its joint absolute or inserted estimate.

The present files do not yet supply (4.1) for the whole basic R
operation attached to the original Wilson density. TURN54's
tagged estimate applies to its admitted native continuations.
The equality of those continuations with the constructed original
joint law is still the decisive attachment. The source's ordinary
T and terminal reverse-FP identities cover particular steps only.

This narrows the original-insertion obligation. It does not
replace it with a generic assertion of mass preservation, nor
does it establish the renormalized physical-observable bounds
needed for a nontrivial continuum theory.
