FORCED CHART: EXACT GAUGE CURVES AND PHYSICAL CURRENT MATCHING
18 October 2026. Working proof for independent audit.

Scope. This is a real-endpoint argument on ONE eligible determining hierarchy.
The scalar and bond primitive operators, uniform analytic averaging estimates,
and true-pair Ward reduction are the explicit package of Raw I4.5. No nearby
nonlinear minimizer is assumed. Eligibility of later mixed hierarchies is a
separate question. In particular this proof does not supply an absent geometry
theorem. The nonlinear mean/axialization dictionary in Section 2 is the point
that must be checked against the exact averaging convention being consumed.

1. NOTATION, PRIMITIVE INPUTS, AND STATEMENT

Use d for the covariant SITE-to-bond derivative at the fixed real carrier W,
and d* for its adjoint. This distinguishes the gauge derivative from the
bond-to-plaquette derivative in the physical current. Put

 Delta_s=d*d, G'=(Delta_s+Q'^* a' Q')^-1,
 T=Q'G'^2Q'^*, C=T^-1,
 R=I-G'Q'^* C Q'G', S=G'^2Q'^* C.                    (1)

All pairings and adjoints use the source lattice measures; scale multipliers
are included in a'. These are the scalar primitives in Raw I13545ff and the
explicit formulas I23930--23962, quoting CMP99b(3.18)--(3.25). At a real W,
G' is self-adjoint and R is an orthogonal projection. Elementary algebra gives

 Q'G'R=0, R G'Q'^*=0, Q'S=I,
 R Delta_s G'R=R, R Delta_s S=-R Q'^* a'.             (2)

The last identity is important: no second derivative estimate for S is needed.

The scalar weak norm is

 ||lambda||_g = |lambda|_(0)+|nabla_W lambda|_(-1),

and the source norm for a scalar u is |u|_(-2). The actual primitive kernel
bounds, composed in the same weighted kernel algebra, give constants K_G,K_S,
K_Q,K_R, independent of depth and volume, such that

 ||G'R u||_g <= K_G |u|_(-2),
 ||S v||_g <= K_S |v|_coarse,
 |R Q'^*a'v|_(-2) <= K_Q |v|_coarse,
 |R u|_(-2) <= K_R |u|_(-2).                         (3)

Here |v|_coarse is the supremum of the dimensionless scalar datum over the
determining sites. The S bound uses the EXISTING scalar inverse-block kernel
C=(Q'G'^2Q'^*)^-1, not the bond inverse q_0. It follows by composing its order
-4 kernel with G'^2Q'^*, of order +4, and differentiating the left G' once.
This is one explicit place where that scalar primitive belongs to the input.

Let J=Jphys(W) be small in the actual source current norm. The true-pair
reduction Raw I15541--15674 gives H^J=H and

 R d*H=0, L(P)=P-H D(P),
 Sphys(exp(i eta L(P))W)
   =Sphys(W)+<J,P>+1/2<P,(Delta-Theta)P>+V(P).       (4)

L is the averaging linearization of I13982--14084. It is locally invertible
on a fixed smaller field ball because L'=I-H D'(P)=I+O(N(P)). Its exact identity is

 Q_j(W;eta L(P))=ell_j Q_j P                         (5)

on every determining layer. The nonlinear Q_j on the left is the source's
adjusted average, not an ordinary average silently substituted for it.

Consider the forced fixed point of forced_global_chart_2026-10-18.txt with
real b and real forcing f satisfying

 f-J in range Q*.                                   (6)

At sigma=1, the claim is that this fixed point P satisfies

 R d*P=0, R d*L(P)=0,

and is a constrained critical point of the actual action (4). Consequently
its bounded current companion is the actual physical current increment.
The assertion is uniform for sufficiently small |b|+|f|, with ceilings fixed
by the primitive constants. Equality extends to complex b by holomorphy.

2. EXACT CONSTRAINT-PRESERVING GAUGE CURVES

The nonlinear constraint is dealt with before using a linearized gauge
identity. Mean-one gauge functions are not assumed to form a subgroup.

Let A=L(P), and let v_A be the mean-normalized hierarchical axial normalizer
used to define Q_j(W;eta A). The exact source definitions are CMP98(58)--(63),
(77)--(81), and (88)--(92), reproduced in Raw I24239--24380. At one level, with
R_x=Ad W(Gamma_yx), the normalization operation is

 m_W(v)(y)=v(y) exp[ sum_x w_x log(v(y)^-1 R_x v(x)) ],
 sum_x w_x=1.                                      (7)

The weights are the normalized block counting weights. Higher means are
composed with the source averaged backgrounds, exactly as CMP98(78)--(80).
Write N_W(v) for the collection of these means on the determining layers.
The zeroth mean is v itself on the raw layer. Thus N_W(v_A)=1.

The recursive construction is literal: at one stage the tree equations give
R_x v(x)=v(y)(R_{W,y}V_1)(Gamma_yx); the mean-one equation fixes v(y) by (62).
At the next stage lift the coarse gauge k covariantly constantly down each
previous block. This lift has R_x k_lift(x)=k(y), and (7) gives exactly
m_W(k_lift v)=k m_W(v). Hence the successive normalized axializations compose
to a global v_A with the stated higher mean conditions. This uses the source
tree lifts, not an assertion that the mean operation is a homomorphism.

For a small site field lambda define

 n_A(lambda)=(1/i) log N_W(v_A exp(-i lambda)).       (8)

This is a finite-dimensional real analytic map near zero, and n_A(0)=0.
Its logarithms are the same near-identity branches as the source averages.
If n_A(lambda)=0 and u=exp(i lambda), then v_A u^-1 is an admissible normalized
axializer of (exp(i eta A)W)^u, because

 ((exp(i eta A)W)^u)^(v_A u^-1)
                  =(exp(i eta A)W)^(v_A).           (9)

The right member is precisely the same hierarchical axial representative.
Uniqueness of the small normalized axializer, which follows directly from
the recursive formulas (60)--(63),(86)--(87), identifies the normalizer on
the left. Therefore ALL adjusted averages in (5) are unchanged. If A(lambda)
is the local logarithm of the gauge transformed field relative to W, then

 P(lambda)=L^-1(A(lambda)),   QP(lambda)=QP.          (10)

The scalar functions in (8) are fixed to one on the Dirichlet exterior.
On the raw layer, (8) forces u=1. These conditions preserve the source boundary
data. No ordinary/adjusted averaging substitution has occurred.

3. UNIFORM FIRST DERIVATIVE OF THE NORMALIZATION MAP

Put K_A=-Dn_A(0). Then

 K_0=Q',   |(K_A-Q')lambda|_coarse
                 <= K_m N(A)|lambda|_(0).           (11)

Here is the explicit estimate behind the second assertion, including its
depth dependence. For one mean in (7), write

 X_x=log[v(y)^-1 R_x v(x)], Z=sum_x w_x X_x,
 J_r(X)=(1-exp(-ad_X))/ad_X.

For the right variation v(x,t)=v(x) exp(t zeta(x)), its right logarithmic
derivative at the output is exactly

 Ad(exp(-Z)) zeta(y)
 + J_r(Z) sum_x w_x J_r(X_x)^-1
       [ R_x zeta(x)-Ad(exp(-X_x))zeta(y) ].         (12)

At X_x=0 this is sum_x w_x R_x zeta(x). On a fixed small logarithm ball,
J_r, its inverse, and Ad exp are analytic with uniformly bounded derivatives.
Subtracting their values at zero in (12) therefore gives operator row-sum
error at most C max_x|X_x|, measured from site sup norm to output norm.
The bound does not count the number of sites in the block, since sum w_x=1.

The identification of the increments at a general W needs care. Here is the
exact recursion, with W held fixed. Put W_p=ordinary-average^p(W), V_0=exp(i eta A),
and let V_{p+1} be the ADJUSTED one-step average of V_p relative to W_p, namely
CMP98(63),(89)--(91), reproduced at Raw I24282--24355. Let s_p be the mean-one
tree axializer of V_p W_p relative to W_p. Equation (60) says

 s_p(y)^-1 Ad W_p(Gamma_yx) s_p(x)
                   =(R_{W_p,y}V_p)(Gamma_yx).       (12a)

Write v^(p) for the first p successive means of the full fine-lattice
normalizer v_A, with v^(0)=v_A. Recursive axialization and the covariantly
constant tree lift give v^(p)=k_lift s_p, where k is the normalizer still to be
performed on the next lattice. Indeed m_{W_p}(k_lift s_p)=k, since m_{W_p}(s_p)=1.
As Ad W_p(Gamma_yx) k_lift(x)=k(y), the k factors cancel EXACTLY in
v^(p)(y)^-1 Ad W_p(Gamma_yx) v^(p)(x). Thus the X_x at stage p in the derivative
of the composed mean is precisely the logarithm of (12a). No background
Wilson loop occurs alone in this expression. In particular V_p=1, s_p=1 and
X_x=0 at A=0, for every eligible W, even when W has nonzero curvature.

The required generic-background amplitude estimate is explicitly recorded
in Raw I16539--16555 and I17677--17687, invoking CMP98 Proposition 6 and
(159),(161),(163),(164). The latter passage applies to exp(i eta A')U with
U G-valued, not just to U=1. In units in which this determining n-block has
side one, let r_p=ell_p/ell_n and a=ell_n sup|A|. The cited level estimates
give a stage-p relative logarithm O(a r_p). The intermediate conjugating
factors from (159) obey, by (163), O(a sum_{q<p} L r_q)=O(a r_p), so passing
between the ordinary relative form and the adjusted form in (92) preserves
this bound. All background conjugations are isometries because W is real.
The estimates are local on the blocks and contours read by these operations;
the eligible determining family supplies those blocks and their collars.

For additional clarity about relative products, if an averaged bond is
U_p(c)=exp(i B_p(c)) W_p(c), then along an oriented contour Gamma,

 U_p(Gamma) W_p(Gamma)^-1
    =product_{c in Gamma} exp(i Ad W_p(Gamma_before_c) B_p(c)),

with the corresponding transported negative logarithm on a reversed bond.
Its logarithm is bounded by C sum_c |B_p(c)| on the common small ball. The
unitary W_p factors cancel in this exact product; a term depending only on
the curvature or holonomy of W cannot survive. The adjusted contour (12a)
is the same relative-product construction with V_p in place of exp(i B_p).

The contour at stage p has at most dL stage-p bonds. Consequently

 max |X_x^(p)| <= C N(A) L ell_p/ell_n,  0<=p<n.    (13)

Indeed the whole nested contour lies in that determining block, on which
|A| <= C N(A)/ell_n; the preceding generic-background estimate reads
|log V_p| <= C ell_p sup|A|.
For the original compatible nested hierarchy, B^n(y) is contained in Omega_n;
points belonging to a still coarser layer have an even stronger field bound.
Thus this supremum estimate does not assume the block avoids coarser layers.
The fixed C allows the adjacent-scale comparison at a declared boundary
collar. Raw I17792--17825 gives the base-1 version of this geometric contour
argument; it is NOT the citation for its generic-W averaging input. That
input is the explicitly generic statement at I17677--17687 just identified.
Eligibility requires these contours and their collars to belong to the
declared determining family; it is not supplied by (13).

Compose (12) over the n stages. Each unperturbed stage is a normalized
average of isometries, of norm at most one. If eps_p is the right member
of (13), the product differs from the unperturbed product by at most

 exp(C sum_p eps_p)-1 <= C' N(A),

because sum_{p<n} L ell_p/ell_n <= L/(L-1). The unperturbed product is
precisely the source Q'_n, with the nested background transports in
CMP99b(3.18)--(3.19), reproduced at Raw I23906--23922. This proves (11).
It does NOT replace a product of n equal O(N(A)) errors by a constant: the
geometrically shrinking contour lengths in (13) are the needed mechanism.

Only this derivative has a uniform estimate. For the argument below, local
existence of each gauge curve is finite-dimensional. No depth-uniform radius
for an arbitrary nonlinear gauge-mean ball is needed or asserted.

4. CORRECTION OF THE GAUGE TANGENT AND EXACT LOCAL CURVES

Let u be any real site field in range R, and put lambda_0=G'u. Then Q'lambda_0=0.
By (3),(11),

 ||K_A S-I|| <= K_m K_S N(A).

If K_m K_S N(A)<=1/2, K_AS is invertible, with inverse norm at most two. Apply
the finite-dimensional analytic implicit-function theorem to

 n_A(t lambda_0+S alpha(t))=0,  alpha(0)=0.           (14)

Its derivative in alpha is -K_AS. Hence an exact local real analytic curve
exists. Its tangent is lambda_u=lambda_0+S alpha_u, where

 alpha_u=-(K_AS)^-1 K_A lambda_0,
 |alpha_u|_coarse <= 2K_m K_G N(A)|u|_(-2),
 ||lambda_u||_g <= K_G(1+2K_S K_m N(A))|u|_(-2).     (15)

The identities (9)--(10) apply to this exact curve. Its parameter interval
may depend on the finite regulator and u. This causes no loss in stationary
variation: a derivative at zero is all that will be used. In particular no
uniform bound for every mixed second derivative of G'u is being presumed.

5. THE GAUGE-FIXING DERIVATIVE IS ONTO

The exact infinitesimal gauge variation of the bond potential is obtained
by differentiating

 exp(i eta A(t)(b))
 =exp(i lambda(t)(b_-)) exp(i eta A(b))
                         exp(-i Ad W(b) lambda(t)(b_+)).

It has the form

 dot A=-d lambda_u+C_A lambda_u,                    (16)

where C_0=0 and the convergent BCH derivative gives

 |d* C_A lambda|_(-2) <= K_c N(A)||lambda||_g.        (17)

For clarity, C_A lambda is a local analytic function of eta A and the two
endpoint values of lambda, divided by eta, whose value at A=0 vanishes.
Every term therefore has at least one A and one lambda, with no negative
power of eta. Taking the covariant divergence differences one of those
factors. The resulting terms contain only A,nabla A,lambda,nabla lambda;
their scaled orders are respectively -1,-2,0,-1. Summing the absolutely
convergent derivative series on a smaller fixed logarithm ball proves (17).
No second derivative of A or lambda occurs in this remainder.

By (4), R d*L(P)=R d*P identically in P, so along the exact curve (10),

 d/dt [R d*P(t)] at zero =R d*dot A.

Use (2), (15)--(17) to obtain

 R d*dot A=-u+R Q'^*a' alpha_u+R d*C_A lambda_u
          =(-I+E_A)u,                              (18)

with

 ||E_A||_{range R,(-2)->range R,(-2)}
 <= [2K_Q K_m K_G
      +K_R K_c K_G(1+2K_S K_m N(A))] N(A).          (19)

All output terms lie in range R. Choose the field ceiling so that the right
side of (19) is at most 1/2, in addition to the ceiling before (14). Then (18)
is onto range R, and its inverse has norm at most two. Equivalently

 |R d*dot A|_(-2) >= (1/2)|u|_(-2).                 (20)

This is the required uniform co-norm estimate. The only apparent second-order
term in the correction has been evaluated by the exact identity R Delta_s S
in (2), rather than estimated by an unsupported scalar Hessian bound.

6. THE FORCED FIXED POINT IS PHYSICAL

The forced fixed-point equation gives, for every delta P with Q delta P=0,

 <delta P, f+(Delta_a-Theta)P+V'(P)>=0.              (21)

The Q*aQ term vanishes against these variations, and f-J=Q*z contributes
<Q delta P,z>=0. Equations (4),(21) thus say that P is stationary, on QP fixed,
for the genuine finite-dimensional functional

 Sphys(exp(i eta L(P))W)+1/2 <P,d R d*P>.

For the exact gauge curves of Sections 2--4 the physical action is constant,
by ordinary gauge invariance with the preserved boundary data. Since R is
an orthogonal projection at real W, stationarity gives

 <R d*P, R d*dot P>=0                              (22)

for every u in range R. By (18)--(20), the second entry ranges over all of
range R. Taking it equal to R d*P shows R d*P=0. By (4), R d*L(P)=0 too.
Returning to (21) removes the gauge-fixing term and proves criticality of the
actual constrained action. No prior critical point or minimizing configuration
has entered the proof.

The exact defect formula (4.4) in forced_global_chart_2026-10-18.txt is

 K_f-[D_W*D_W L(P)+F(W,L(P))]
 =d R d*L(P)+k_J(Lambda H D(P))
                        +Lambda^T m_J(Lambda H D(P)),

where Lambda=G'R d* and D_W in this display is the bond-to-plaquette derivative.
Both terms vanish: R d*L(P)=0 and R d*H=0. Thus

 J+K_f=Jphys(exp(i eta L(P))W).                     (23)

All terms are holomorphic in b on the common small complex data ball. They
agree for real b in a real open ball, hence (23) holds for complex b as well.
The same reasoning covers a holomorphic forcing representative f=J+Q*z(b).
At sigma=1 both the chart and current depend only on f modulo range Q*, since
GQ*=0 and P0^TQ*=0. At complex sigma that annihilation is not asserted; the
chosen representative defines the analytic extension actually used.

An equivalent and directly usable forcing criterion is

 T0(f-J)=0,  T0=P0G0,                              (24)

because Delta_a T0=P0^T and ker(P0^T)=range Q*. Conversely T0Q*=0.
Thus no explicit multiplier z has to be reconstructed. Equality (24) makes
both Gf=GJ and P0^Tf=P0^TJ, and therefore the forced field and the substituted
current agree exactly with their full-current counterparts at sigma=1.

7. EXACT DEPENDENCY AND LIMITS

The new argument is gauge-orbit stationarity plus the explicit onto estimate
(19). It needs the already identified scalar inverse-block primitive C, its
weak kernel bounds, the uniform regular-background averaging bound used in
(13), the exact normalized axializer/adjusted-average dictionary, and the
true-pair identity R d*H=0. It does not need an existing nonlinear minimizer,
a graph norm for the forced field, or a uniform second derivative of G'u.

The source dictionary in Sections 2--3 is stated explicitly so it can be
audited. If a consumer uses a DIFFERENT block map or a different gauge
normalization from CMP98(58)--(92), (9) alone is not enough: equality of its
actual data must be checked. The theorem applies to the adjusted Q_j of
Raw I13999--14003, for which that axialization is its definition.

For general determining families, the required nested contour/collar and
primitive-operator eligibility remain external geometry premises. Establishing
them is not an algebraic consequence of this physical matching argument.
