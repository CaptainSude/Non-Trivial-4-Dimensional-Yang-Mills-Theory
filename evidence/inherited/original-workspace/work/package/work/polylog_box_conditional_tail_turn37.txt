NORMALIZED BARE WILSON TAILS ON BOXES GROWING POLYLOGARITHMICALLY
19 October 2026. Root proof, turn37. Independent geometry and analysis audits.

RESULT AND LIMIT

The fixed-box conditional Wilson theorem can be made quantitative in
the box side. It then applies to R(g)<=C_R[log(1/g)]^b. Its actual
conditional normalization is retained, uniformly in the surrounding
lattice volume. The proof below uses finite-dimensional compact-group
geometry with explicit polynomial bounds; it does not import a
continuum measure, a new RG trajectory, or a source theorem.

This is a bare conditional-box theorem. It does NOT identify its
unconstrained Dirichlet minimum with a Bałaban nonlinear-average fiber,
nor prove an annealed good-boundary probability at all RG scales. The
physical diameter of these boxes still vanishes in a usual continuum
limit. They are not boxes of a fixed physical diameter containing
O(a^-4) fine sites.

1. STATEMENT

Fix G=SU(N_c), with N_c fixed, normalized faithful trace tau, and

 h(U)=1-Re tau(U)=||U-I||_tau^2/2.

Let Omega_R={0,...,R}^4, R>=2. Prescribe all boundary edges V, and
integrate the remaining edges with normalized Haar and density
exp[-S_V/g^2], where S_V is the sum of h(U_p) over the box plaquettes.
Pure boundary plaquette terms are constants in both integrals below.

Gauge transformations are the identity at all boundary vertices.
Fix the axial forest described in Section2, and write y for its free
chord variables. In a small chart put y_e=exp(i x_e). Let
||x||_infty=max_e ||x_e||_tau. Constants c,C below depend only on the
fixed group and dimension, and may change between formulas.

There are fixed c_0,c_1,c_2,C_1,C_2>0 such that, when

 delta:=max_(boundary edges) ||V_e-I||_tau <= c_0 R^-24,       (1.1)

S_V has a unique global minimum y_*(V)=exp(i x_*(V)) in forest gauge.
It is real analytic in V on this neighborhood, and

             ||x_*||_infty <= c_1 R^-8/4.                  (1.2)

For 0<g<=c_1 R^-8/4 and 1<=T with gT<=c_1 R^-8/4, define

 W={y=exp(i x): ||x-x_*||_infty<=gT}.

The normalized conditional law satisfies

 Prob_(V,g)(W^c)
 <= exp[C_1 R^4 log(2+R)-c_2 R^-8 T^2]
  + exp[C_2 R^4 log((2+R)/g)-c_2 R^-20/g^2].                (1.3)

One can take the minimum of the right side with one. No constant
depends on the enclosing lattice, and no unspecified fixed-R constant
is used in (1.3). The deliberately conservative powers suffice here.

COROLLARY. Put ell=log(1/g). Fix b>=0 and C_R>=2, and assume

 R<=C_R ell^b,       T=ell^a,       a>6b+1/2.               (1.4)

For sufficiently small g, depending only on these fixed parameters,
the chart conditions hold and

 Prob_(V,g)(W^c) <= C exp[-c ell^(2a-8b)].                 (1.5)

The exponent 2a-8b is strictly larger than one. Consequently this
failure probability is smaller than any fixed power of g as g->0,
uniformly for every allowed R and boundary V in (1.1).

2. EXACT FOREST GAUGE AND EXPLICIT LOOP FILLINGS

For each interior vertex (x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4), follow negative x_1 edges
to the boundary root (0,x_2,x_3,x_4). The forest consists of the edges
in these columns with x_1=0,...,R-2 and transverse coordinates strictly
between 0 and R. Every interior vertex belongs to one component with
one boundary root. All other boundary vertices are separate roots.
There are no forest edges contained in the topological boundary.

Recursively gauge each forest edge to the identity. As in the previous
fixed-box proof, this is an exact triangular product-Haar change of
variables: each step is multiplication or inversion in G. Interior
gauge variables integrate to one. Boundary edges remain fixed. The
remaining coordinate dimension n is at most C R^4.

For every free chord e, join its endpoints to their forest roots and
connect those roots by a boundary path. The resulting loop reads y_e
and the boundary-path product because every forest edge is identity.
It has a rectangular plaquette filling with at most C R^2 plaquettes
and a boundary connector of length at most C R:

 * A transverse interior edge gives the strip between its x_1 ray
   and the corresponding edge on x_1=0. Its area is at most R.
 * A transverse edge reaching a side face uses the same strip; the
   side ray is a prescribed boundary path.
 * A top x_1 chord, from x_1=R-1 to R, closes through x_2=0, using
   the x_1--x_2 rectangle. Its area is at most R^2 and its boundary
   connector has length at most 3R.

Orientation reversals do not affect the estimates. Discrete nonabelian
Stokes expresses the boundary product as a product of conjugated
plaquette holonomies. Bi-invariance and the triangle inequality give

 max_e ||y_e-I||_tau <= C R sqrt(S_V(y))+C R delta.          (2.1)

Indeed each rectangle has distinct plaquettes, so Cauchy--Schwarz
costs the square root of its area. The weaker C R^2 sqrt(S) bound
would also suffice for every exponent used below.

At identity boundary the derivative at the identity of the same loop
identity is the usual curl/filling identity. Thus, for an edge Lie
algebra field x vanishing on forest and boundary edges,

 max_e ||x_e|| <= C R ||d x||_2,
 ||x||_2^2 <= C R^6 ||d x||_2^2 <= C R^8 ||d x||_2^2.       (2.2)

The Hessian of S_I at zero is the curl quadratic form. Hence its
least eigenvalue is at least c R^-8. Its largest eigenvalue is bounded
by a fixed C because the number of plaquettes meeting an edge is fixed.
This also proves the required nondegeneracy without an unspecified
compactness constant depending on R.

3. UNIFORM CHART CONVEXITY AND A POLYNOMIAL GLOBAL GAP

Each plaquette action depends on at most four chord or boundary
variables. On a fixed exponential chart its derivatives through order
three are uniformly bounded. The bounded number of incident plaquettes
and the row/column sum bound for sparse block matrices imply

 ||D^2 S_V(x)-D^2 S_I(0)||_(2->2)
                    <= C(||x||_infty+delta).             (3.1)

This estimate concerns the product forest coordinates, in which forest
links equal I; it introduces no derivative of a global gauge map.

Choose r_R=c_1 R^-8 with c_1 sufficiently small. On ||x||_infty<=2r_R
and under (1.1), (2.2),(3.1) imply

             c R^-8 I <= D^2 S_V(x) <= C I.              (3.2)

At V=I, the weaker version of (2.1) implies

          S_I(y)>=c R^-4 max_e ||y_e-I||_tau^2.           (3.3)

There is a group-dependent constant c such that leaving the product
chart ||x||_infty<r_R/4 forces max_e||y_e-I||>=c r_R.
This includes leaving the fixed injectivity neighborhood itself.
Thus outside that chart S_I>=c R^-20.

Changing the boundary data changes only O(R^4) plaquette terms, each
by at most C delta. Also S_V(I)<=C R^4 delta^2. Consequently

 |S_V(y)-S_I(y)|<=C R^4 delta,
                   S_V(I)<=C R^4 delta^2.               (3.4)

Choose c_0 in (1.1) small enough compared with the fixed constants
in (3.3). Then outside ||x||_infty<r_R/4,

                    S_V(y)-S_V(I)>=c R^-20.             (3.5)

Compactness supplies a global minimum; (3.5) places it inside that
small chart. Strict convexity in the larger chart makes it unique.
Its Hessian is nonsingular, so the analytic implicit-function theorem
gives its analytic dependence on V. No quantitative IFT radius is
used to create the global gap; that radius was established directly.

Let U_R be the product chart ||x||_infty<r_R. It is convex in x, and
contains the minimum with the stated margin. Taylor's theorem yields

 c R^-8 |x-x_*|^2 <= S_V(exp(i x))-S_min
                                      <= C |x-x_*|^2    (3.6)

on U_R. Outside U_R the same argument as (3.5), with S_min<=S_V(I),
gives

                   S_V(y)-S_min>=c R^-20.               (3.7)

4. NORMALIZATION WITH ITS DIMENSION DEPENDENCE RETAINED

The product Haar density in this chart lies between exp(-C n) and
exp(C n) relative to Lebesgue measure, with constants independent of R.
The Euclidean ball |x-x_*|<=g is contained in U_R under the theorem's
conditions. Its volume is at least g^n(c/sqrt(n))^n. Using the upper
quadratic estimate in (3.6) proves

 Z_V(g)>= exp(-S_min/g^2) g^n
                              exp[-C R^4 log(2+R)].      (4.1)

Within U_R, being outside W forces |x-x_*|>=gT. Split the lower
quadratic exponential into two equal parts and integrate one over
all R^n. The resulting bound is

 integral_(U_R\W) exp(-S_V/g^2)dy
 <=exp(-S_min/g^2) g^n
       exp[C R^4 log(2+R)-c R^-8 T^2].                  (4.2)

The factor R^(4n) from the Gaussian integral is included in the
displayed dimension cost. Dividing by (4.1), and increasing C,
gives the first term of (1.3).

On the rest of the compact group product, its normalized Haar mass
is at most one. By (3.7) its integral is at most
exp[-(S_min+cR^-20)/g^2]. Divide by (4.1), retaining g^-n. This gives
the second term of (1.3). The minimum energy cancels exactly in both
ratios. No unnormalized tail is being substituted for a conditional
probability, and no ultraviolet vacuum normalization is altered.

5. THE POLYLOGARITHMIC RANGE

Under (1.4), R^4 log(2+R)<=C ell^(4b)log(2+ell), while
R^-8 T^2>=c ell^(2a-8b). Since a>6b+1/2, the latter power strictly
dominates the entropy and exceeds one. The first term of (1.3) is
therefore at most exp[-c ell^(2a-8b)].

For the second term the negative contribution is at least
c exp(2ell)ell^(-20b), whereas the positive contribution is at most
C ell^(4b+1)log(2+ell). It is smaller than the same stretched-log
bound for all sufficiently large ell. Finally
gT/r_R<=C exp(-ell)ell^(a+8b)->0, so the window and denominator ball
stay in the original chart. This proves (1.5).

There is also a range larger than polylogarithmic for which (1.3)
is meaningful, but no fixed physical-size continuum box is asserted.
The chosen logarithmic exponents are sufficient, not optimized.

6. EXACT CONDITIONAL USE AND REMAINING MATCHING PROBLEM

If the box is embedded in a larger bare lattice, every plaquette
depending on a nonboundary edge of the box is wholly in the box.
Fixing the exterior and all boundary links therefore gives exactly
the conditional compact Wilson law used above, up to an additive
action constant which cancels. The bound is uniform in every such
exterior whose boundary obeys (1.1), in the prescribed boundary frame.
A fixed boundary gauge change transports both the data and the event.

For vertex-disjoint boxes with good prescribed boundaries, the original
conditional interiors factor exactly. The normalized replacement and
coupling construction of normalized_box_replacement_research therefore
applies with the failure probability in (1.3) or (1.5). In particular
the replacement preserves the original exterior marginal; discrepancy
marks can be conditionally coupled below independent Bernoulli variables
with this common failure bound. This conclusion is conditional on the
specified good-boundary event, not a new unconditional rarity theorem
for the boundaries themselves.

At a native weak scale, the geometric growth R~polylog(1/g) alone is
thus no longer a reason to reject this bare conditional estimate.
There are still different substantive issues: the native local map
has nonlinear average constraints and an effective action with dated
terms; its conditional minimum and measure have not been identified
with the present Dirichlet problem. A uniform annealed estimate must
also include the actual exterior law and its bad-boundary contribution.
No source-normalization or YM reconstruction claim follows without
those steps.
