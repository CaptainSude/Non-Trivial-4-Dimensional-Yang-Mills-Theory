 <F,AF>_pi=kappa int|grad F|^2 dpi.                       (1.1)

3. NEW: ARBITRARY BOUNDED PROCESSING OF MANY PRIVATE THIN LOOPS

The full conditional Haar theorem is stronger than the first moments.
For loop j with a private sparse set I_j of m_j edges it gives

 ||Law(G_j | all links outside I_j)-Haar||L1
       <=epsilon_j
       =C(1+beta)^(3/4) exp[-c(m_j-1)/(1+beta)],         (3.1)

where G_j is the full SU2 holonomy. Constants are uniform in ALL
conditioned original links, and min(2,epsilon_j) may always be used.

Assume the I_j are pairwise disjoint and NO other loop G_k reads a
link in I_j. Distinct-time copies of a spatial loop satisfy this.
Let Z be any original-link observable not reading any I_j.
For every bounded measurable Phi on SU2^q,

 |E[Z Phi(G_1,...,G_q)]
       -E[Z] int Phi(g_1,...,g_q) product_j dg_j|
       <=||Z||infinity ||Phi||infinity sum_j epsilon_j. (3.2)

PROOF. Condition on all links outside I_1. All G_j for j>1 and Z
are then fixed. Apply the total-variation test inequality obtained
from(3.1) to the bounded function
g_1 ->Phi(g_1,G_2,...,G_q), uniformly in its other arguments.
The replacement integral removes every read of G_1 and leaves an
ordinary bounded test of G_2,...,G_q and Z. Its error has the stated
epsilon_1 bound. Repeat with I_2,...,I_q and add the errors.
No assertion that the PRIVATE EDGE VARIABLES are mutually independent
after unconditioning is made. Exact conditional expectations at each
stage retain the full Wilson normalizer. The intervening nonprivate
links in each holonomy can be arbitrary shared original variables.

Now put all G_j in the positive half and let
B=Phi(G_1,...,G_q), ||Phi||infinity<=1. Assume their reflected reads
avoid all positive private sets I_j, as for strictly separated
time-half supports. Set c=int Phi product Haar. Formula(3.2) gives

 |EB-c|<=epsilon_sum,
 |E[(Theta B)B]-c E[Theta B]|<=epsilon_sum.

Here Theta includes complex conjugation. Reflection invariance gives
E[Theta B]=overline(EB). Subtracting |EB|^2 proves

 0<=||B-EB||OS^2
     =E[(Theta B)B]-|EB|^2
     <=2 epsilon_sum.                                  (3.3)

The lower inequality uses the actual reflection positivity admission.
The absolute upper bound follows even before invoking RP.
Phi can depend on the regulator, be nonlinear, select time samples
adaptively as a function of this finite data, or include a very large
numerical rescaling followed by a bounded function. Its supremum
bound, not its derivative or Lipschitz constant, is used.

For physical loops with m_j>=c_ell/a, beta=O(log(1/a)), and a polynomial
number q(a) of distinct time slices, sum epsilon_j->0. Hence NONE of
these bounded finite-data processing rules gives a nonzero separated
reflected state. This includes attempts to normalize a small temporal
average and then apply a bounded phase or threshold.

The result does not cover thick/flowed loops, reading a continuum
cloud with no private edges, nonlinear native group averages, or
unbounded insertions with a growing norm. It is an actual-law
decision for the thin-loop route, not a general YM no-go theorem.

5. WHAT TEMPORAL FILTERING DOES TO SPATIAL SUPPORT

The actual transformed generator is

 L=-A=kappa Delta+2kappa grad log Omega . grad.           (5.1)

For F reading a spatial set S, its first derivative in time involves
grad_e log Omega for e in S. Existing total energy and Fisher bounds
do not control how these coefficients depend on distant links.
They therefore do not imply a cutoff-uniform quasilocality theorem.

Even strict preservation of the original support fails in an actual
finite SU2 plaquette Hamiltonian. This can be checked without an
uncontrolled weak-coupling expansion. On a fixed periodic spatial
graph with side periods at least4 consider the genuine strong-coupling
analytic neighborhood

 kappa^-1 H=K+epsilon sum_p(1-W_p), epsilon=8/g^4.

At epsilon=0 the normalized full ground state is1. Its eigenvalue is
simple and isolated. Bounded analytic perturbation, followed by elliptic
regularity on this fixed compact manifold, gives a C^k expansion for
any fixed k:

 log Omega_epsilon=(epsilon/3)sum_p W_p+O(epsilon^2).    (5.2)

Indeed E_Haar W_p=0 and K W_p=3W_p (four spin-one-half links).
Insert(5.2) into(5.1), for F=W_p:

 kappa^-1 L F
  =Delta F+(2epsilon/3)sum_q grad W_q . grad W_p
        +O(epsilon^2).                                (5.3)

For adjacent plaquettes p,q sharing one edge, the q summand reads
the edge opposite that shared edge, which lies outside p. It is not
constant in that edge. At a shared link equal to I, write the
remaining p and q products as A=a0 I+i a.sigma and B=b0 I+i b.sigma.
The shared-edge contribution is +/- a.b/4; varying an exclusive
opposite q edge can change b while leaving p unchanged.
In a sufficiently large cubic graph that opposite edge is in no
other plaquette which also shares a p edge. Hence this exterior
dependence cannot cancel in the first-order sum. Choosing a nonzero
a gives a nonzero effect, stable for sufficiently small epsilon>0.

Thus P_t W_p depends on links outside p for all sufficiently small
positive t in such an actual model. This disproves strict support
preservation, not quasilocality. It is a rigorous strong-coupling
example and is NOT continued to weak coupling by assertion.

For a fixed contractible lattice cube and an unrenormalized
gauge-invariant Lipschitz observable, a complete cube tree gauge also
gives a simple separate limitation. The tree-gauged chord matrices
are products of finitely many transported plaquettes, so

 dist(U^G,I)^2<=C_cube sum_(p in cube) h_p.

The constant is fixed for that fixed lattice cube. Therefore
E_pi|F(U)-F(I)|^2<=C_cube Lip(F)^2 g^2. L2 contraction gives the same
upper bound for the variance of every P_tF. This excludes a variance
floor for that uniformly Lipschitz, fixed-lattice-size class as g->0.
It does not cover growing physical cubes or physically rescaled
curvature observables, whose constants/derivatives grow with a^-1.

 int sum_ij |C_t,ij(x)|^2 dx
    =int [d^3p/(2pi)^3] (|p|^2/2)exp(-2t|p|)

       0<C_Q(t)<= [3q/(32pi^2 t^5)] ||f||L2^2.          (7.3)

Strict positivity follows because f is nonnegative and nonzero,

Thus (6.1) gives a finite energy bound

Ordinary equal-time Q(f) is not an L2 random variable: the ultraviolet

8. NEXT MATHEMATICAL DECISION

The bounded private thin-loop route is excluded by an actual-law
multi-read theorem, not merely by failure of one variance argument.
Temporal filtering retains the zero separated signal for that route.

For other physical probes, automatic filtered energy control shifts
the needed work to a separated original-law covariance estimate.
For renormalized local action-density insertions this is a different
and potentially shorter target than a uniform nonlinear spatial-flow
Jacobian bound. It is not already contained in partition stability
or in the constant-action source flow.

No variational shortcut changes this requirement. For a physical
real phase f, the trial exp(i t f)Omega has excess energy
kappa t^2 E_pi|grad f|^2. After subtracting its ground component,
its normalized excess Rayleigh quotient is this numerator divided
by1-|E_pi exp(i t f)|^2. A small unnormalized energy numerator with
a collapsing denominator supplies no low-energy excitation.

The next useful positive attachment would therefore be a
volume/cutoff-uniform estimate of the normalized separated local
source in(6.2), together with its physical renormalization and mixed
insertion compatibility. No such actual attachment is claimed here.
