SPATIAL-FLOW MAGNETIC PROBES WITH EXACT TIME SUPPORT -- TURN63
20 October 2026. gauge47.

RESULT AND SCOPE

Spatial-only Wilson flow gives an exact finite-lattice original
observable with positive-time support. A bounded radial function
of ONE flowed magnetic plaquette needs no color-transport string.
Its canonical free Maxwell benchmark has finite equal-time variance,
an explicit positive reflected norm, and a cutoff-uniform free
lattice approximation. Time averaging can be performed AFTER the
radial function, without introducing temporal parallel transports.

This narrows the old probe problem in two concrete ways: it removes
time-boundary leakage from global four-dimensional flow, and removes
the entire thin-connector issue from the simplest observable. It
does not prove the nonlinear continuum flow on the original Wilson
law, or its calibration at a fixed physical coupling.

The latter qualification is substantive. With a fixed reference
coupling g_*, the bare free law at g_a instead has a factor
(g_a/g_*)^2 in its variance, and the reflected radial norm collapses
as g_a tends to zero. A physical RG matching cannot be replaced by
flat bare-field Taylor expansion.

1. EXACT FINITE-LATTICE SPATIAL FLOW

Fix a finite spatial graph, either a periodic spatial torus or a
box with only its internal spatial links and plaquettes. At each
Euclidean lattice time t independently, let V_s(t) be Wilson
gradient flow of those spatial links driven by their spatial
plaquette action. Choose the normalization of flow time so its
flat transverse linearization is exp(s Delta_space); physical
flow time s corresponds to lattice time of order s/a^2.

The vector field is smooth on a finite product of compact groups,
so the real flow exists uniquely for every finite s. Gauge
invariance of the spatial action and the product metric implies
equivariance under arbitrary gauges g(t,x), independently at
every time slice. Uniqueness also implies commutation with the
reflection of the time labels and the corresponding spatial
graph convention.

Most importantly, V_s(t) reads ONLY the original spatial links
at time t. It reads no original temporal link and no other time
slice. There is spatial propagation within the chosen graph;
there is no claim of finite spatial propagation.

For SU(2), write the flowed oriented magnetic plaquette as

 P_(12,s,a)(t,x)=p_0 I+i p.sigma.

Choose a fixed positive normalization parameter g_*. Define

 X_(s,a)(t,x)=2 p(P_(12,s,a)(t,x))/(g_* a^2),
 B_(s,a)(t,x)=exp[-h |X_(s,a)(t,x)|^2],   h>0.            (1.1)

Conjugation of the plaquette rotates p by the adjoint orthogonal
action at its base vertex. Thus B is exactly gauge invariant,
0<B<=1, and is a deterministic function of the ORIGINAL links.
There is no logarithm, weak-field cut, or gauge selection in
its definition. The parameter g_* specifies an observable;
identifying it with a physical renormalized coupling is a later
estimate, not part of this exact definition.

On any original Wilson geometry admitting the time reflection,
B at t>0 belongs to the positive-time algebra, and

 E[(theta B)B]-(E B)^2 >=0.                              (1.2)

The same holds for a positive normalized time average

 O_a=sum_(t>0) w_a(t) B_(s,a)(t,x),  w_a(t)>=0,
                         sum_t w_a(t)=1,                (1.3)

with weights supported in [delta,T], delta>0. Reflection uses
the reflected times, graph, and plaquettes. Equations(1.1)--(1.3)
are finite-regulator statements, independent of a continuum
flow theorem.

A spatial box gives exact compact spatial support but changes
the flow kernel at its boundary. A periodic spatial torus has
no such boundary. The whole-space formulas below apply directly
to the whole-space Gaussian reference, or after a justified
large-torus limit. They are not silently substituted for a
confined-box nonlinear flow.

2. THE COLOR-SMEAR VARIANT AND AN IMPORTANT TIME ORDERING

One can instead transport magnetic plaquette colors along
SPATIAL paths from x_0 to x inside each time slice, using the
same flowed links, and define

 Y_a(t)=a^3 sum_x f(x)
               Ad_(W_s(t;x_0,x)) X_(s,a)(t,x).           (2.1)

For real smooth f this transforms by Ad g(t,x_0), so
exp[-h |Y_a(t)|^2] is gauge invariant. Its positive weighted
time average is again an original positive-half observable.

It is generally WRONG to first form sum_t w_a(t)Y_a(t) and
then take its color norm. The independent matrices g(t,x_0)
rotate its summands differently. Restoring covariance of
that alternative requires temporal parallel transport, whose
UV analysis is a different problem. Taking the radial function
at each time and averaging the resulting scalar avoids it.

The point probe(1.1) is simpler still: it omits every connector.
The variant(2.1) remains useful when a specified spatial
color smear is needed, but its nonlinear marked comparison
has more structure to control.

3. CANONICAL MAGNETIC MAXWELL COVARIANCE

Take n independent canonical Maxwell colors, n=3 for SU(2).
The spatial transverse heat flow sends linear magnetic
curvature F_12 to e^(s Delta_space)F_12. This statement is
independent of the longitudinal gauge choice: the curl
annihilates longitudinal modes.

At a fixed spatial point, write X_s^b(t) for this canonical
flowed curvature. Its covariance is

 E[X_s^b(t) X_s^c(u)]=delta_bc c_s(|t-u|),

 c_s(tau)=integral_R3 dk/(2pi)^3
       [(k_1^2+k_2^2)/(2|k|)]
             exp[-2s|k|^2-|k|tau]
          =(1/(6pi^2)) integral_0^infinity
                        k^3 exp[-2s k^2-tau k] dk.       (3.1)

This follows by integrating
1/(p_0^2+|k|^2) in p_0, giving exp(-|k|tau)/(2|k|),
and then doing the angular average of k_1^2+k_2^2.
In particular,

                 v_s=c_s(0)=1/(48pi^2 s^2).             (3.2)

It is finite already at equal time. Temporal smearing is
not needed to cure a remaining free magnetic self-variance
divergence at s>0.

For the linearization of(2.1), the transports are identity
at order zero. Replace the integrand in(3.1) by its product
with |f_hat(k)|^2. The resulting v_(s,f) is finite, and
c_(s,f)(tau)>0 for finite tau if f is nonzero. For a fixed
smooth spatial f, v_(0,f) is also finite; the unsmoothed
nonlinear transports, rather than this linear smear, caused
the TURN61 line problem.

For the point process, c_s(tau) is continuous and

 0<=v_s-c_s(tau)<=tau v_s mu_s,
                 mu_s=3sqrt(pi)/(4sqrt(2s)).             (3.3)

The value mu_s is the mean k under the probability density
proportional to k^3 exp(-2s k^2). Gaussian moment estimates
therefore give a version Holder-continuous in physical time
with any exponent below1/2 on compact intervals. In particular
the scalar time average below is a well-defined Gaussian
random variable. Time differentiability is not asserted.

This magnetic choice is material. Spatial smoothing of an
electric component F_0i still leaves a p_0^2 numerator; its
equal-time integral contains
integral dp_0 p_0^2/(p_0^2+|k|^2)=infinity. At separated
times one can separate contact terms and the reflection
parity, but it is not the finite magnetic calculation above.

4. EXPLICIT POSITIVE OS MARGIN

Let b_s(t)=exp[-h |X_s(t)|^2]. The exact Gaussian formulas are

 m_G=(1+2h v_s)^(-n/2),

 E[b_s(-t)b_s(u)]
  =[(1+2h v_s)^2-4h^2 c_s(t+u)^2]^(-n/2),  t,u>0.       (4.1)

Use h=1/(2v_s), n=3, and r_s(tau)=c_s(tau)/v_s.
Then m_G=2^(-3/2), and for a point at time T,

 D_s(T)=(4-r_s(2T)^2)^(-3/2)-1/8
                         >=3 r_s(2T)^2/64>0.            (4.2)

Jensen's inequality applied to the k probability distribution
gives

 r_s(2T)>=exp[-2T mu_s]
           =exp[-3sqrt(pi)T/(2sqrt(2s))].                (4.3)

Thus the margin is quantitatively positive at every fixed
s>0 and T>0. No estimate on the interacting law has entered.

For a nonnegative h_time with integral1 supported in
[delta,T], define O_G=integral h_time(t)b_s(t)dt. This
uses a time weight distinct from the radial parameter h.
By bounded Fubini,

 D_G(h_time)=integral dt du h_time(t)h_time(u)
       [(4-r_s(t+u)^2)^(-3/2)-1/8]
                     >=3 r_s(2T)^2/64>0.                (4.4)

The ordinary variance is finite because 0<O_G<=1.
No comparison of colors at different times is present in
this formula; the radial operation was already performed.
For the spatial color-smear variant, the same formulas
hold with v_s,c_s replaced by v_(s,f),c_(s,f).

5. A DIRECT GAUSSIAN LATTICE CHECK

For completeness, the flat infinite-time lattice Maxwell
calculation has an exact cutoff formula. Let

 hat k_i=(2/a)sin(a k_i/2),  omega_a(k)^2=sum_i hat k_i^2,
 E_a(k)=(2/a)asinh(a omega_a(k)/2).

Temporal Fourier integration of the lattice propagator gives

 C_a(ma;k)=exp[-E_a(k)|ma|]
                 /[omega_a(k)sqrt(4+a^2 omega_a(k)^2)].  (5.1)

Thus the spatially flowed magnetic point covariance is

 c_(s,a)(ma)=integral_BZ3 dk/(2pi)^3
  (hat k_1^2+hat k_2^2) exp[-2s omega_a(k)^2]
                         C_a(ma;k).                    (5.2)

The zero spatial momentum does not contribute to curvature.
The same formula is a spatial Fourier sum on a finite
periodic torus. Plaquette phases cancel in the same-point
curvature covariance.

On the Brillouin zone,

 (2/pi)|k|<=omega_a(k)<=|k|.

Using sqrt(4+a^2 omega_a^2)>=2, the integrand is bounded by
a fixed multiple of |k|exp[-8s|k|^2/pi^2]. Its R3 integral
is finite and of order s^-2, uniformly in a. Dominated
convergence gives

 c_(s,a)(tau_a)->c_s(tau),  v_(s,a)->v_s                 (5.3)

whenever tau_a is a lattice time tending to tau. Formula(4.1)
then gives the same positive Gaussian radial limit, including
time averages by bounded dominated convergence.

This is an actual cutoff-uniform FREE calculation. It does
not identify the nonlinear Wilson-flow observable with the
linear Gaussian one. The detailed companion derivation is
spatial_magnetic_gaussian_limit_turn63.txt, independently
reviewed in spatial_magnetic_gaussian_audit_turn63.txt.
It also gives the quantitative covariance discretization
error O(a^2 s^-3+|T_a-T|s^-5/2) for a^2<=s.

6. WHAT HAPPENS TO THE OLD LINE DIVERGENCE

The point probe has no line. If using the transported
variant, at the free transverse or spatially gauge-damped
reference level the equal-time scalar heat covariance is

 C_s^sp(x)=integral_R3 dk/(2pi)^3
                      exp(ik.x-2s|k|^2)/(2|k|).

For a straight segment of length L define the scalar bound
I_s(L)=integral_0^L du integral_0^L dv C_s^sp((u-v)e_1).
The kernel is nonnegative on that axis. For example, writing
F_D for Dawson's positive integral,

 C_s^sp(r e_1)
  =F_D(r/(2sqrt(2s)))/(4pi^2 r sqrt(2s)).

Its value at0 is1/(16pi^2 s), and it falls like
1/(4pi^2 r^2) at infinity. Integrating along the full line,
or setting k_1=0 in its Fourier transform, gives

 integral_R C_s^sp(u e_1)du=1/(8sqrt(2pi s)).

Consequently

                  I_s(L)<=L/(8sqrt(2pi s)).             (6.1)

The transverse matrix covariance is bounded by the scalar
covariance as a positive Fourier quadratic form. Hence this
also bounds the variance of a straight spatial transverse
line integral. A general fixed rectifiable spatial path has
the coarser bound L^2/(16pi^2 s).

These estimates remove the L/a Gaussian self-contraction
diagnostic at fixed physical s. They do not prove uniform
nonabelian path-ordered transport estimates. The ungauge-damped
flow leaves longitudinal potential modes unchanged; the
statement is made in the transverse/gauge-damped reference,
or for the complete invariant observable after a justified
gauge argument, not for an isolated arbitrary open line in
every gauge. No temporal line has been introduced.

7. TWO PRECISE LIMITATIONS OF THE CALIBRATION

7A. Bare and physical normalizations differ.

Definition(1.1) uses a fixed g_*. If its input is a canonical
free reference with U approximately exp(i g_* a A.sigma/2),
its leading curvature is the X_s used above. If instead one
uses the BARE free law with U approximately
exp(i g_a a A.sigma/2), its linearized X is

                         (g_a/g_*) X_s.

Let rho_a=g_a/g_*. Its radial reflected covariance is then

 [(1+2h rho_a^2 v_s)^2-4h^2 rho_a^4 c_s(2T)^2]^(-3/2)
                 -(1+2h rho_a^2 v_s)^(-3).              (7.1)

For fixed h,s,T it is O(rho_a^4) and tends to zero when
g_a tends to zero. Thus a comparison with the fixed physical
Maxwell benchmark cannot be obtained by simply linearizing
the original law at its vanishing bare coupling. One needs
the actual physical-scale RG calibration of this observable.
Conversely, normalizing automatically by g_a changes the
observable and does not prove that its nonlinear physical
normalization is correct.

7B. Removing the flow scale loses this pointwise margin.

At fixed tau>0,

 c_s(tau)->1/(pi^2 tau^4),   s->0,
 r_s(tau)~48s^2/tau^4.

With the normalization h=1/(2v_s), at reflected separation2T,

 D_s(T)~27s^4/(64T^8),                 s->0.             (7.2)

The positive margin is therefore a fixed-flow-scale margin.
For time averages supported away from0 it similarly vanishes
as O(s^4). This is not an objection to using fixed smearing
scales as observables of a theory, but it rules out silently
removing s while retaining a uniform point-probe lower bound.

8. COMPARISON WITH CONFINED FOUR-DIMENSIONAL FLOW

Both constructions are exact finite-regulator maps of the
original links, and both give bounded gauge-invariant probes.
The confined four-dimensional construction needs a whole
spacetime box lying inside the positive half, including its
chosen time boundaries. Spatial flow works independently at
every time and needs no artificial positive-time boundary.
Time translation and reflection act on the time labels in
the evident way. Spatial box dependence can still remain.

The linear magnetic spatial-flow benchmark has finite
equal-time variance despite the absence of time smoothing.
This is a genuine advantage for time-local observables.
It is not a general claim that all spatially flowed
composites have finite equal-time moments.

The nonlinear initial-data problem has not disappeared.
The equal-time four-dimensional potential has covariance
1/(2|k|), so its ultraviolet spatial regularity is below
order-1, and it fails the alpha>5/3 covariance hypothesis
of[C]. The finite lattice ODE exists for every input, but
at physical flow time s its basic derivative bound is only
exp(C s/a^2). That bound is too weak for the desired native
analytic/marked admission; it is not evidence that the
actual flow necessarily has that instability.

9. THE ACTUAL NEXT COMPARISON

For the simplest point or time-averaged probe O_a, its one
original expectation and its reflected product expectation
must be transported separately through the native positive
history representation. A reference redraw does not carry
the old observable. The conditional two-point quantity is
not generally the product of the two conditional one-point
quantities.

At fixed physical s, temporal support and normalization,
the concrete desired estimates are only

 |E_W O_a-m_G|<=epsilon,
 |E_W[(theta O_a)O_a]-q_G|<=epsilon,                      (9.1)

where q_G-m_G^2 is explicitly given in(4.2) or(4.4), after
the matching of g_* has actually been established. Since
both original and Gaussian tests lie in[0,1], these two
estimates would give a reflected norm at least
q_G-m_G^2-3epsilon.

The TURN62 single-date auxiliary count can bound a
specified positive bad-history contribution if that event
is properly identified. It does not by itself control all
fine-scale histories meeting this flow observable, or the
nonlinear map on their complement. The rough initial-data
and coupling-calibration failures above state concretely
why(9.1) has not followed from the present estimates.

The recommended minimal probe is therefore the pointwise
spatially flowed magnetic radial observable, optionally
averaged as a scalar over positive times. It has no string
renormalization problem in its definition, exact time support,
and an explicit uniform free reference. Its original-law
nonlinear comparison remains open. No YM4 existence claim
is made.
