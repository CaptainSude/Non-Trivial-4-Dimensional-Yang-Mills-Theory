TURN88 -- WHAT THE ACTUAL TRANSFER FORM CAN CONSUME FOR THE FLOWED SCALAR
gauge47. 20 October 2026. Companion calculation; no old files changed.

DECISION

The exact Wilson transfer/Dirichlet form and the available temporal
plaquette MGF do give a quantitative adjacent-slice bound for the declared
flowed scalar. It involves the scalar's derivatives along the ACTUAL
original Wilson slab's geodesic interpolation. Those intermediate laws
are not the Wilson slice marginal. This is an explicit finite original-law
consumer, but its l1 influence input is too strong already for a free
point-probe diagnostic. It must NOT become a mandatory physical target.
The exact correlated square before Holder/Minkowski is the relevant
weaker consumer.

Even a successful upper bound on that Dirichlet form would not itself
supply positive physical-band mass. The old variance-plus-increment
criterion first needs a variance floor, and a time-filtered coefficient
needs a nonzero spectral response away from zero. No available unmarked
energy inequality supplies those lower bounds for the physically
calibrated spatial-flow scalar. This conclusion uses the actual transfer
form, not a generic countermodel.

1. EXACT NORMALIZED WILSON SLAB, WITH THE GAUGE LINKS RETAINED

Use the finite nondegenerate periodic Wilson lattice with beta>0 and
time-homogeneous spatial potential. For simplicity the temporal coupling
is the same beta on every link. The construction and original denominator
are established in transfer_matrix_witness_turn64.txt Sections2--3:
 T=M C_beta P M on the physical Hilbert space,
 M=exp(-V_sp/2), C_beta=product convolution by q_beta,
 q_beta(W)=exp[-beta h(W)]/c(beta),
 Z_W=c(beta)^(N|E_sp|) Tr_phys T^N.                     (1.1)
P is the site-gauge projection, equivalently the integral of the temporal
links in a slab. Both copies of M and that projection are indispensable.

Let (U,V,g) be the two neighboring spatial fields and the temporal links
between them, with the marginal of the FULL normalized original Wilson
law, including the entire rest of the time circle. The gauge-transformed
field V^g can be chosen so that the temporal plaquette at spatial edge e
is U_e(V^g_e)^(-1), up to a conjugation/inversion that leaves h invariant.
For a real smooth gauge-invariant slice function F define
 d_N(F)=(1/2) E_W [F(V)-F(U)]^2.
Then exactly
 d_N(F)=Tr(T^(N-1)[F,[F,T]])/(2 Tr T^N).                (1.2)
In kernel notation the complete positive slab weight is
 K(U,V)K^(N-1)(V,U)/(Tr T^N), with its temporal-gauge variables retained
before integrating them out. It is not the independent product q_beta
conditional on arbitrary U or V; the remainder of the circle remains.

Choose the principal logarithm, except on the measure-zero antipodal
set, and put
 X_e=log(V^g_e U_e^(-1)), U_e(v)=exp(vX_e)U_e, 0<=v<=1.
In the Pauli/2 metric, if eigenangle theta is in[0,pi], then
 |X_e|^2=4theta^2<=2pi^2(1-cos theta)
                       =2pi^2 h_temporal,e.           (1.3)
The inequality follows from sin(theta/2)>=theta/pi.
Gauge invariance gives F(V)=F(V^g), so this path compares the actual
source at both endpoints. No temporal gauge fixing of the whole torus
or new normalization is required.

2. A FINITE ORIGINAL-LAW INFLUENCE INEQUALITY

The exact derivative formula is
 F(V)-F(U)=integral_0^1 sum_e <grad_e F(U(v)),X_e> dv,   (2.1)
where gradients are left-trivialized in the same metric. In particular
the Dirichlet form is the expectation of the square of this integral;
all cross-link and density correlations remain in that square.

The original one-slab theorem of TURN64 gives
 E_W exp(t beta h_temporal,e)<=(1-t)^(-3/2), 0<=t<1.
For any real p>=1, use x^p<=(2p/e)^p exp(x/2), x>=0, to obtain
 ||beta h_temporal,e||_Lp
                         <=(2p/e) 2^(3/(2p)).         (2.2)
This is exponential Markov domination, not differentiation of an MGF
inequality to assert domination of higher moments. Combining(1.3),(2.2),
 ||X_e||_L(2p)^2
                  <=[4pi^2 p/(e beta)]2^(3/(2p)).      (2.3)
All these expectations are under the same FULL original slab law.

Take p>1 and q=p/(p-1). Define the actual path influence
 A_q(F)=integral_0^1 sum_e
                  ||grad_e F(U(v))||_L(2q,Wilson slab) dv.
Minkowski in(2.1) and Holder for each edge yield
 d_N(F)<= [2pi^2 p/(e beta)]2^(3/(2p)) A_q(F)^2.        (2.4)
This bound is valid at every finite regulator for the smooth finite
spatial-flow scalar, any convex spatial average of it, and any other
smooth gauge-invariant slice F. It does not require a selected history
or conditional independence. For example p=q=2 gives the explicit
coefficient (4pi^2/e)2^(3/4)/beta times A_2(F)^2.

The formal sufficient condition furnished by this bound would be
 A_q(F)^2<=C beta a_t                                  (2.5)
which would give d_N(F)=O(a_t) in the ORIGINAL Wilson law. Section2A
shows that the l1 sum can make this an inappropriate research target:
it loses a full spatial volume factor in a free point-probe example.
The exact square in(2.1) could exploit correlations that(2.4) discards.
No estimate of(2.5) is asserted. In particular(2.3) controls the temporal
innovation, not its product with the random source derivative.

For the point radial probe the terminal derivative is O(a_s^-2), and
its nonlinear pullback is the adjoint Y in
turn88_spatial_flow_influence_gauge.txt Section5. The actual temporal
path here changes the full initial spatial configuration. Neither its
intermediate law nor its weighted adjoint can be replaced by an
independent Gaussian, a product link law, or the unflowed Wilson slice
law. The pointwise energy-sublevel modulus fails by that note's exact
finite-pair construction, while the known coherent-window suppression
shows that this failure alone does not decide the annealed A_q.

2A. THE L1 CONSUMER LOSES THREE POWERS ALREADY IN A FREE POINT TEST

This paragraph tests the bound, not the original nonlinear Wilson law.
Consider the linear spatial Maxwell flow on a fixed cubic physical torus
of side L, with one color curvature at a POINT. Remove harmonic potential
zero modes when defining the Gaussian; the curl annihilates them. For link coordinates
theta_e, use a normalization g_c>0 and write
 X_c=sum_e l_e theta_e^c,
 l_(y,2)=[H_(s,a)(y+a e1)-H_(s,a)(y)]/(g_c a^2),
 l_(y,1)=-[H_(s,a)(y+a e2)-H_(s,a)(y)]/(g_c a^2),        (2A.1)
with harmless changes of orientation/basepoint. H_(s,a) is the normalized
lattice heat kernel. These are the exact coefficients of lattice curl
after heat flow; longitudinal gauge components cancel. If K_s^L is the
physical periodic heat kernel, then uniformly in y,
 l_(y,2)=(a^2/g_c)[partial_1 K_s^L(y)+o(1)],
 l_(y,1)=-(a^2/g_c)[partial_2 K_s^L(y)+o(1)].            (2A.2)
The same convergence holds for Riemann sums of absolute values and squares.
For justification, H_(s,a)/a^3 has the torus Fourier coefficients
exp[-s lambda_a(k)], lambda_a(k)=4a^-2 sum_i sin^2(a k_i/2).
On the Brillouin zone lambda_a(k)>=(4/pi^2)|k|^2, and the difference
multiplier divided by a is at most |k_i|. Summable Gaussian Fourier
majorants give the uniform convergence in(2A.2), including the tails.

Both following constants are strictly positive at finite s,L:
 C1=int (|partial_1 K_s^L|+|partial_2 K_s^L|),
 C2=int (|partial_1 K_s^L|^2+|partial_2 K_s^L|^2).
Thus
 sum_e |l_e|=[C1+o(1)]/(g_c a),
 sum_e l_e^2=[C2+o(1)]a/g_c^2.                          (2A.3)
No norm comparison with a hidden dimension constant is being used.

For the three-color bounded radial POINT probe B=exp(-alpha|X|^2),
 each link gradient has norm
 |grad_e B|=|l_e| Z(X),
 Z(X)=2alpha |X|exp(-alpha|X|^2).                        (2A.4)
The random radial factor is the SAME at every edge. In a calibrated
Gaussian reference whose flowed component variance tends to v>0, its
L^(2q) norm tends to a strictly positive finite constant. On the linear
interpolation between adjacent free time slices, the covariance of X(v)
converges to that same variance uniformly in v in[0,1], since the
positive-s flow covariance is continuous at temporal separation0.
Consequently the analogue of A_q has square of order1/(g_c^2 a^2),
whereas E sum_e |grad_e B|^2 is of order a/g_c^2. The ratio is a^-3.
In the customary normalization beta g_c^2 comparable to1, the proposed
condition(2.5) would ask beta/a^2 to be O(beta a), which is false.

That customary running normalization is NOT a license to change the
fixed g_* in our original source. For the literal fixed-g_* source in
an uncalibrated bare Gaussian with g_a^2 comparable to1/beta, instead
put g_c=g_* in(2A.1) and X=(g_a/g_*)Z_can. As g_a->0,
 ||Z(X)||_L(2q) is comparable to g_a/g_*.
Then A_q^2 is of order g_a^2/(g_*^4 a^2), and the squared-gradient
sum is of order g_a^2 a/g_*^4. The SAME volume-factor loss remains.
For logarithmic beta this A_q still violates(2.5): its ratio to beta a
is of order1/(beta^2 g_*^4 a^3). This bare Gaussian is precisely the
degenerating calibration discussed in TURN63; it is not an original-law
prediction or a candidate nonzero reference channel.

This exact diagnostic is for the radial point function. Our current
boundary-channel source is a FIXED SPATIALLY SMEARED phi. Its link
derivative is a sum over different output points with different random
radial factors. No lower bound of type(2A.4) for that entire smear has
been proved here. Therefore this section does not assert impossibility
of(2.5) for that phi; it demonstrates that(2.4) can be grossly wasteful
already in the simplest fixed-scale flowed source. The next step should
retain the correlated square or actual conditional innovations, rather
than impose the l1 bound as a necessary interface.

3. WHY THIS STILL DOES NOT GIVE A PHYSICAL-BAND LOWER BOUND

For F centered in the same finite-period law, write v_N=Var_W F and
G_N(m)=Cov_W(F_0,F_m). The exact spectral expansion of T gives
 d_N=v_N-G_N(1),
 G_N(m)>=v_N(1-d_N/v_N)^m, 1<=m<=N.                    (3.1)
The Jensen statement is for v_N>0; constant F is trivial. This is the
OLD TURN64 theorem. Thus a uniform v_N>0 together
with(2.5) would give a separated norm floor. Both required source
estimates remain unproved for the fixed physical spatial-flow scalar.
The action MGF and flow dissipation are upper energy estimates. They
contain no lower variance estimate for this bounded nonlinear source.

Moreover, even(3.1) does not exclude spectral weight converging to zero
energy. The concrete filtered target in turn88_filtered_probe_tail.txt
is the norm of (1-exp(-delta H))v after an infinite-time stationary
scalar reconstruction. Its lower bound requires a nonzero difference
response. An upper bound on d_N cannot supply that lower response.
The explicit Gaussian filter benchmark is a reference calculation,
not an original-Wilson spectral lower bound.

At finite thermal period the exact spectral ratios lambda_j/lambda_i
in(3.1) may exceed1; their logarithms are energy differences of both
signs. One may use(3.1) directly, but must not replace them by a positive
vacuum Hamiltonian without the additional infinite-time limit. The
buffer lemma of TURN82 bounds physical translation derivatives of an
ALREADY existing OS vector from above. It cannot create its norm.

4. THE DIFFERENTIAL GROUND-STATE FORM IS A DIFFERENT EXACT CONSUMER

The controlled temporal limit at FIXED spatial lattice yields
 H_a=[g^2/(2a_s)]K+[4/(g^2 a_s)]S_sp,
 with positive ground state Omega_a. In that actual law the exact
ground-state transform gives, for a smooth slice F,
 <(F-EF)Omega,(H_a-E0)(F-EF)Omega>
                   =[g^2/(2a_s)] E_(Omega^2)|grad F|^2. (4.1)
Hence a uniformly bounded physical first energy moment would follow
from E_(Omega^2)|grad F|^2=O(a_s/g^2). This is the old explicit target
of TURN65--69. The proved action/Fisher estimates in
hamiltonian_energy_turn66.txt bound E S_sp and the score of Omega;
they do not bound the source-dependent gradient on the right of(4.1).

The exact isotropic finite-Wilson Hamiltonian is -a_t^-1 log(T/lambda0),
not the differential operator in(4.1). Even where a logarithmic energy
form exists in the actual GROUND limit, its centered spectral measure rho
gives d_ground/a_t=int(1-exp(-a_t E))/a_t d rho(E)
<=int E d rho(E)=E_H, not the reverse. This is explicitly a ground-state
statement; a finite thermal form would require its own energy-difference
moment assumptions. Thus no upper Hamiltonian energy bound follows by
dividing an arbitrary discrete Dirichlet upper bound by a_t. Conversely a proved
ground-state form upper bound still requires a variance floor to yield
a nonzero separated state and further calibration to exclude zero energy.

These are distinct SAME-law consumers. Transferring the old Omega^2
energy theorem to an isotropic Wilson slice, or inserting an isotropic
Wilson expectation into(4.1), would silently replace the measure.

5. EXACT CURRENT CONCLUSION

The available original energy estimate does something concrete: it
controls every temporal X_e moment in(2.3), and therefore gives the
valid but potentially wasteful bridge-weighted bound(2.4). Section2A
shows why its l1 sufficient condition is not the recommended target.
What the energy estimate does not
control is precisely the random derivative of the fixed flowed scalar
on that bridge, nor its equal-time or filtered reflected variance.

No generic counterexample is needed for this diagnosis: the full
normalized transfer kernel displays the residual circle factor, the
exact form displays the marked gradient, and the physical filter
displays the missing nonzero spectral coefficient. A direct lower bound
on that one coefficient could bypass the sufficient derivative norm
altogether. No original-law physical-band lower bound has been proved
in this note.
