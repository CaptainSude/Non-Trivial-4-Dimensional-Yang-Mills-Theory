1. THE THREE REFERENCE PROPERTIES, WITH NO OUTPUT HESSIAN

Use a fixed finite four-dimensional cubic torus and a product of compact
convex coordinate sets K_i in fixed dimension. The initial reference nu
has the complete weighted-convex action and conditional estimates of
full_omega_spatial_consumer_20261023, Sections1--2. Write the coordinate
set as I disjoint-union Y. Integrate I and retain y in K_Y.

The proof of that consumer uses the following properties of a reference
law, uniformly for every update set B and every exterior configuration:

 (A1) joint protected marginal TV under one changed exterior block j:
      TV((nu_B^eta)_S,(nu_B^eta')_S)
                 <=min(1,C s exp[-nu d(j,S)]),
      for EVERY S subset B, s=|eta_j-eta'_j|;

 (A2) |nu_B^eta x_i-nu_B^eta' x_i|
                 <=C s exp[-nu d(i,j)];

 (A3) nu_B^eta |x_i-nu_B^eta x_i|^2<=sigma^2.

The weighted Hessian is a sufficient producer of (A1)--(A3), not an
additional ingredient in the subsequent block-coupling proof.

Let nu_Y be the EXACT marginal of nu. If B subset Y is updated and
Y\B is fixed, its conditional law is the B marginal of the original
reference law with I union B free. Thus apply the original estimates
with free set I union B and protected set S subset B. Its coordinate
means and variances are exactly those of the same original conditional.
Consequently nu_Y satisfies (A1)--(A3) with the SAME constants and
spatial rate. The size of the eliminated set does not enter.

This argument applies after ANY finite succession of coordinate
eliminations: the final reference is the corresponding original
marginal, by Fubini. It never differentiates a marginal Hamiltonian or
asserts weighted accretivity of a Schur complement or singular Hessian.

The block-consumer proof also applies to an arbitrary remaining subset
of the lattice. Use update sets B_z=Y intersect cube_ell(z), allowing
repeated sets and empty updates. Each retained site belongs to exactly
N translated cubes, as before. Every birth-cost bound only decreases
when coordinates are omitted, and the same N identical-condition erasures
remain. This includes disconnected retained sets. For the usual small
cubic tori use a whole-volume update; its size is bounded by the fixed
block size. No arbitrary retained-subset regularity is required.

2. A PERTURBED CONDITIONAL BOUNDARY COMPARISON

Let mu be the EXACT normalized tilt of nu by a real interaction

 V=C_V+sum_X psi_X,   v_X=osc(psi_X),
 delta=sup_i sum_(X contains i) exp[kappa diam X] v_X<delta_*.
                                                               (1)

Constants may be extracted so 0<=psi_X<=v_X; C_V retains the exact
extracted scalar. The finite-volume absolute sum of v_X is finite.
All partial or fractional insertions of these terms satisfy (1).

Fix y,y' differing only at j in Y, and let mu_I^y,mu_I^y' be their
conditional laws on the eliminated coordinates I. Put s=|y_j-y'_j|.
For a bounded F depending on internal S subset I, the block mechanism
gives the normalized conditional comparison

 |mu_I^y F-mu_I^y' F|
 <=C_ell osc(F) (1+s) exp[-lambda d(j,S)].                    (2)

Its right side may always be capped by osc(F). The proof is useful:
it does not assume smoothness of psi or differentiate a posterior ratio.

Use the block generator L_y on I, with the actual mu_I^y conditionals,
and its semigroup P_t^y. Set w_i=exp[-lambda d(i,S)]. The previous
coupling theorem gives

 Lip_(d_w)(P_t^y F)<=exp(-gamma t) Lip_(d_w)F,
 gamma>=N/2,   d_i(u,v)=1_(u!=v)+|u-v|.                      (3)

The exact comparison identity is

 mu_I^y F-mu_I^y' F
   =integral_0^infinity mu_I^y'[(L_y-L_y')P_t^y F]dt.          (4)

It follows by differentiating mu_I^y' P_t^y F and using invariance of
mu_I^y' for L_y'. All finite conditional normalizers remain in the two
heat-bath kernels. The finite-run convergence and measurable coupling
arguments are precisely those used for the original consumer.

For cubes not containing the external site j, reuse the near/far block
couplings from that proof. Their total weighted output cost is at most
C N w_j(1+s). There are at most N additional translated cubes containing
j; their actual update set is cube intersect I, so j itself is excluded.
For these, use an arbitrary coupling of the two block laws. Their
coordinate means differ by at most C s exp[-nu d(i,j)]+C delta N,
and their centered coordinate second moments are uniformly bounded
by the small bounded block tilt. Thus each such cube costs at most
C N w_j(1+s), because delta N<=1 and its weights are within a fixed
factor of w_j. Together,

 sum_B coupling E sum_(i in B) w_i d_i(X_i,Y_i)
                              <=C N^2 w_j(1+s).              (5)

Insert (5),(3) into (4) and use Lip_(d_w)F<=osc(F). This proves (2),
with fixed C_ell. The same proof gives for any internal profile
a_i^F<=A exp[-nu_F d(i,P)]

 |mu_I^y F-mu_I^y' F|
                 <=C A(1+s)exp[-lambda' d(j,P)],              (6)

with lambda' below the consumer and source rates. If F also depends
on y, its direct change is added separately. These estimates are in
the hybrid metric: the factor 1+s does NOT vanish as s tends to zero.

3. EXACT SEQUENTIAL NORMALIZER INCREMENTS

Write a reference density as exp[-H_0(x,y)] on the same product cut,
and enumerate the interaction terms in any fixed deterministic order.
The finite-volume reference and perturbed conditional integrals are

 Z_0(y)=integral_(K_I) exp[-H_0(x,y)]dx,
 Z_l(y)=integral_(K_I) exp[-H_0(x,y)-sum_(k<=l)psi_k(x,y)]dx.

The exact correction to the retained marginal is

 L(y)=-C_V+log Z_final(y)-log Z_0(y),
 dmu_Y/dnu_Y=exp[L(y)]/nu_Y(exp L).                           (7)

The integrals in (7) are raw finite masses, not independently normalized
substitutes. Define the insertion increment

 ell_l(y)=log Z_l(y)-log Z_(l-1)(y).

Interpolating ONLY the coefficient of psi_l gives the exact identity

 ell_l(y)=-integral_0^1 mu_(l,t),I^y psi_l dt,
                    -v_l<=ell_l(y)<=0,                      (8)

where mu_(l,t) has all preceding insertions and t psi_l. No division by
an exponentially small global event is used. Its interaction norm is
still <=delta, so (2) applies uniformly in l,t and all exterior y.

If j is outside the ORIGINAL joint read X_l of psi_l, changing y_j
does not change the inserted function itself. Apply (2) to its internal
read X_l intersect I, integrate t, and cap by v_l. If that internal
read is empty, the increment has no such remote dependence. For j in
X_l, use its direct oscillation bound v_l. In either case,

 |ell_l(y)-ell_l(y')|
 <=min(v_l,C_ell v_l(1+s)exp[-lambda d(j,X_l)]).              (9)

All cuts remain fixed. This is an endpoint normalized-law estimate
for merely bounded measurable local insertions, rather than a formal
derivative of their normalized expectations.

4. SUPPORTED EFFECTIVE INTERACTIONS ON THE RETAINED COORDINATES

Fix one actual point y* in K_Y and let D=max_(j in Y)diam(K_j), finite.
For each original X=X_l set

 A_(X,n)=Y intersect {j:d(j,X)<=n},
 y^(X,n)=y on A_(X,n), and y* elsewhere;
 y^(X,-1)=y*.

The support is deliberately built around the ORIGINAL complete read X,
including its eliminated coordinates. Define

 Psi_(l,n)(y_(A_(X_l,n)))
       =ell_l(y^(X_l,n))-ell_l(y^(X_l,n-1)).                  (10)

Stop once the finite torus is covered. Coordinate replacement stays in
the original product K_Y. Hence the identities are exact:

 L(y)=C_eff+sum_(l,n) Psi_(l,n)(y_(A_(X_l,n))),
 C_eff=L(y*).                                                (11)

The scalar C_eff is the actual retained-reference value of the raw
normalizer ratio. It may be extensive and may depend on any still-held
outer data. It cancels only when normalizing this particular mu_Y;
the corresponding outer mass must still contain exp C_eff. Nothing
has reset a partition function to one in the original positive law.

For n>=1, change the nth shell one coordinate at a time and use (9):

 ||Psi_(l,n)||_infinity
 <=C v_l(1+D)|X_l|(1+n)^(d-1)exp(-lambda n).                  (12)

For n=0 use ||Psi_(l,0)||<=v_l. Empty supports give zero increments.
The crude geometric bounds suffice:

 |A_(X,n)|<=C|X|(1+n)^d,
 diam A_(X,n)<=diam X+2n.

For any fixed polynomial power p>=0 and
0<kappa'<min(kappa,lambda/2), with strict exponent reserve, (12) gives

 sup_(j in Y) sum_((l,n):j in A_(X_l,n))
       |A_(X_l,n)|^p exp[kappa' diam A_(X_l,n)]
                                      osc(Psi_(l,n))
                         <=C_(p,kappa,kappa',ell)(1+D)delta. (13)

To check the incidence sum, j in A_(X,n) implies some i in X lies in
B_n(j). Sum the original interaction bound at these at most C(1+n)^d
sites. Powers |X|^(p+1) are absorbed by the spare kappa-kappa' in the
original diameter weight, since |X|<=C(1+diam X)^d on this lattice.
The remaining n sum has a fixed polynomial factor times
exp[-(lambda-2kappa')n]. It converges uniformly in volume.

Thus (11) is an exact local value expansion of the marginal correction,
small whenever C(1+D)delta is below the next fixed threshold. It does
not claim exponential decay in support VOLUME, nor smoothness of Psi.
Its dependence on D is explicit. The previous consumer's cutoff-free
constants do not make this newly produced interaction norm cutoff-free.

Neither observation is a scale-uniform RG theorem. The estimate

7. PRECISE OBSTRUCTION TO ORDINARY FIRST-DERIVATIVE CLOSURE

A small oscillation interaction alone cannot give ordinary parameter
derivative bounds. Already on a fixed one-variable strongly convex
reference, choose a bounded nonconstant h(x) and

       V(x,y)=delta sin(y/epsilon) h(x).

It has uniformly small bounded-interaction norm. But the normalized
expectation of h has derivative of order delta/epsilon at parameters
where cos(y/epsilon)=1 and the reference variance of h is positive.
The simpler delta 1_(y>0)h(x) even gives a discontinuity. This is not a
claim about the native ratio, which is Lipschitz. It demonstrates the
missing implication: a VALUE norm does not control a localized SCORE.

The known first ratio has a localized first-background bound for each
ell_b. Its shell coefficients Psi_(b,n) have small value tails, but
the naive derivative bound obtained by differentiating both truncated
ell_b terms does not decay in n for a nearby derivative coordinate.
Summing those derivative bounds over all n is therefore not licensed.
One needs a mixed local response, a suitable finite-difference modulus,
or another exact score representation before asserting the original
ordinary derivative profile after a new background variation.
