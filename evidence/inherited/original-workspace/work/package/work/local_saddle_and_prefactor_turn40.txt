LOCAL NONLINEAR SADDLE RESPONSE AND THE COMPLETE LEADING PREFACTOR
19 October 2026. public_argument_audit. Turn40 companion.

RESULT

The fixed-L KKT gap of gaussian_coarse_kernel_turn40 extends to the local
nonlinear constrained saddle at uniformly small data. The minimizing fine
field and its Lagrange multiplier have sup bounds independent of enclosing
volume, and their first and second datum derivatives have exponential
anchored kernels. This improves the R^2 sup bound obtained by the earlier
Euclidean argument. On the weak-data polylogarithmic regime in which the
prior low-energy coverage proof applies, this is the genuine compact-fiber
minimum; small sup data alone is not claimed to prove global uniqueness
on arbitrarily large boxes.

The exact complete Gaussian prefactor also has a useful invariant formula.
The constraint-coordinate determinant and the tangent Hessian determinant
combine into the determinant of the LOCAL nonlinear saddle matrix. Its
inverse is uniformly exponential. The first derivative and the spatial
Hessian of the leading vacuum prefactor are therefore uniformly local.
This is a theorem about the classical minimum and its full leading
Haar/coarea Gaussian prefactor, not the full finite-g effective action,
an arbitrary selected conditional law, or a repeated RG construction.

1. SPACES, PARAMETERS AND LOCALITY PREMISES ALREADY PROVED

Use the same complete fixed-crossing box, block-local G0 forest, ordinary
first-L averaging rows and exterior strip as gaussian_coarse_kernel_turn40
and constrained_effective_action_turn40. L and the compact group SU(N_c)
are fixed. X is the fine chord coordinate space, Y the COMPLETE framed
coarse data space. At the identity, H is the Wilson Hessian, Q=DF_0(0),
and QE=I for the proved finite-range right inverse E.

Let e denote the retained-link logarithms in the fixed exterior frame.
Use the actual relative logarithmic ordinary map F_e(x). Its rows have
bounded read and uniformly bounded local analytic jets. The Wilson action
S_e(x) is the sum of all plaquettes reading a free edge. The coarse datum
is v. If coarse chord parameters q are used instead, retain the actual
local formula v=v(q,e) from the specified coarse forest section. Setting
forest edges to identity is a coordinate insertion; no long, datum-varying
coarse gauge restoration is made here.

Every variable and parameter coordinate is assigned its actual fine/coarse
location. At fixed L the operator graphs have bounded degree and polynomial
volume growth, uniformly including their finitely many boundary types.
Derivatives with respect to a retained-edge parameter read only the local
action/constraint rows involving that edge. The relative-coordinate datum
conversion v(q,e) has the same bounded local-read property. All constants
below can depend on these fixed local conventions.

The flat saddle matrix

                  M0=[H Q^t; Q 0]                     (1.1)

has a uniformly bounded inverse in counting l2 and exponentially weighted
row and column norms, by the independent local-cochain/KKT theorem. In
particular ||M0^-1||_(infty->infty)<=C_L. This stronger norm follows from
the proved exponential kernel and bounded lattice growth; it is not an
inference from l2 invertibility alone.

2. VOLUME-INDEPENDENT SUP NORM SADDLE

Write z=(x,lambda), and define the exact stationarity/constraint map

 G(z;v,e)=(grad_x S_e(x)+DF_e(x)^t lambda,
                                      F_e(x)-v).       (2.1)

At the identity G(0;0,0)=0 and D_zG(0;0,0)=M0. Its full Jacobian is

 M(z,e)=[ L(x,lambda,e)  DF_e(x)^t;
                         DF_e(x)       0 ],
 L=S_e''(x)+sum_a lambda_a F_e,a''(x).                 (2.2)

The multiplier term is essential. It has bounded range and norm because
the multiplier enters locally. On a sup ball of radius epsilon, including
e, the finite local jet bounds give

 ||M(z,e)-M0||_row + ||M(z,e)-M0||_column
                                  <=C_L epsilon,      (2.3)
 ||G(0;v,e)||_infty<=C_L (||v||_infty+||e||_infty).

The transpose in (2.2) requires bounded column incidence as well as row
incidence, both available for the actual finite-read action/constraint.

Set R(z;u)=G(z;u)-G(0;u)-M0 z, with u=(v,e), and solve

 z=-M0^-1 G(0;u)-M0^-1 R(z;u).                        (2.4)

Choose the fixed z-ball radius sufficiently small that the Lipschitz
constant of the second term is below1/2, and then choose a fixed data
radius delta so that the first term maps the ball into its inner half.
The contraction theorem proves a unique branch z_*(u) there, satisfying

 ||x_*(u)||_infty+||lambda_*(u)||_infty
                   <=C_L (||v||_infty+||e||_infty),    (2.5)

with constants independent of R. The branch is real analytic, by the same
analytic fixed-point equation. The nearby constraint Jacobian remains
surjective. Its tangent Hessian is positive, by the constrained convexity
proof in constrained_effective_action Section3 (equivalently continuity
from the uniform gap, retaining the multiplier term).

Thus x_* is the unique local constrained minimum in the original small
chart. To identify it with the GLOBAL compact-fiber minimum, use the
separate low-energy coverage argument of constrained_effective_action
Section4. In particular, for ||q||,||e||<=gZ with R,Z fixed powers of
log(1/g), its requirement gR^3Z small holds. The global minimizing branch
there is the same local branch by uniqueness. No statement that arbitrary
fixed small data imply global uniqueness for all volumes is needed.

The nonlinear chart variables also improve: since B=(I-EQ)x, the actual
saddle satisfies ||B_*||_infty<=C_L||(v,e)||_infty. This is compatible with
the larger fixed integration window already used; the window need not be
shrunk and the old good event need not be changed.

3. LOCALITY OF THE EXACT DATUM RESPONSE

At the saddle put M_*=M(z_*,e). It is symmetric at real data, finite range,
and by (2.3) a small perturbation of M0 in l2 norm as well as row/column
norms. Its spectrum therefore remains uniformly away from zero. The same
finite-propagation series as in gaussian_coarse_kernel Section4 gives

 |(M_*^-1)_(a,b)|<=C_L exp[-m_L dist(a,b)],             (3.1)

and uniformly bounded exponentially weighted row and column sums. This
argument applies directly in (x,lambda) variables; no nonlocal projected
hard-window covariance formula is being used.

For a coordinate datum u_r, implicit differentiation gives

                  z_r=-M_*^-1 G_r.                    (3.2)

G_r, with z held fixed, has a bounded local footprint about r. Hence

                   |(z_r)_a|<=C_L e^-m_L dist(a,r).    (3.3)

For two parameters,

 z_rs=-M_*^-1{G_zz[z_r,z_s]+G_zr z_s+G_zs z_r+G_rs}.   (3.4)

All partial tensors on the right have bounded local coefficients. Define
the three-root tree distance tau(a,r,s) as the minimum total length of a
tree connecting those sites (bounded read enlargements are absorbed into
the constants). Products of (3.3), convolution with (3.1), and an unused
exponential fraction give

                |(z_rs)_a|<=C_L e^-m'_L tau(a,r,s).    (3.5)

Finite-dimensional local sums are bounded independently of the box:
select the connecting tree, extract part of its exponential, and sum the
remaining tree kernels by their uniform l1 norms. The same induction gives
anchored tree decay at every FIXED derivative order, with order-dependent
constants. No common all-arity factorial estimate is asserted.

4. EXACT CLASSICAL COARSE HESSIAN

First keep e fixed and use the COMPLETE framed v as datum. The envelope
identity and the sign convention of (2.1) give

 D_v S_min=-lambda_*,
 D_v^2 S_min=-(M_*^-1)_(lambda,lambda).                 (4.1)

Indeed grad S=-DF^t lambda and DF x_v=I. Differentiating G with respect
to v gives z_v=M_*^-1(0,I). Thus the signs in (4.1) follow directly.
The exact nonlinear classical coarse Hessian is exponentially local
uniformly on the data ball. At identity this is the positive semidefinite
coarse precision of the Gaussian theorem; no inverse coarse covariance
decay is inferred.

If q is used, the actual datum conversion must be differentiated:

 S_min,qr = -v_q^t (M_*^-1)_(lambda,lambda) v_r
                         -lambda_* dot v_qr.           (4.2)

Here v_q and v_r denote its first derivatives, and v_qr its second
derivative. The last term is local and must not be omitted at nonzero
exterior/datum. Explicit derivatives of S_e and F_e enter for exterior
parameters; they are local vertices, so (3.3)--(3.5) prove the corresponding
joint Hessian locality without pretending they vanish.

5. EXACT COAREA/TANGENT DETERMINANT IDENTITY

At a fixed saddle use the original coordinates x=B+E t, with B represented
in an orthonormal basis U of ker Q. Let

 A=DF_e(x_*) E,
 T=D_B x=U-E A^-1 DF_e(x_*)U,
 H_*=D_B^2[S_e(x(B))]|_(B_*)=T^t L T.                 (5.1)

The equality for H_* includes constraint curvature. Since
DF D_B^2x+F''[T,T]=0 and grad S=-DF^t lambda, its second term is
+lambda dot F''[T,T], exactly the multiplier term of L in (2.2).

Let c_E=|det[U,E]|, the original constant coordinate volume factor, and
form the square n-by-n change of variables

                    Bmat=[T, E A^-1].                 (5.2)

Then DF Bmat=[0,I] and |det Bmat|=c_E/det A on the chosen positive real
branch. Apply the congruence diag(Bmat,I) to M_*. In variables consisting
of tangent, constraint and multiplier, its lower constraint/multiplier
block has the off-diagonal identity. Elimination gives

 det M_* (det Bmat)^2 =(-1)^m det H_*,  m=dim Y.

Consequently

 c_E^2/[ (det A)^2 det H_* ]
                         =1/[(-1)^m det M_*].          (5.3)

The inertia is n positive and m negative eigenvalues: H_* is positive on
the tangent and DF is surjective. Thus (-1)^m det M_*>0 throughout this
small branch, and the real logarithm below is unambiguous.

The COMPLETE prefactor of the constrained Laplace formula therefore is

 J(B_*;q,e)/sqrt(det H_*)
   = j_C(x_*) / {j_J(v) sqrt[(-1)^m det M_*]}.           (5.4)

This retains the actual coarse Haar factor j_J and the fine chord Haar
factor j_C. It removes the auxiliary E-dependence by an exact identity,
not by discarding a determinant. At the flat point it agrees with the
Gaussian factor det(QQ^t)^-1/2 det(H_N)^-1/2.

6. LOCALITY OF THE COMPLETE LEADING VACUUM PREFACTOR

Define, retaining any source-independent additive constant,

 V1(q,e)=(1/2)log[(-1)^m det M_*]
                         -log j_C(x_*)+log j_J(v).     (6.1)

This is exactly the field-dependent leading prefactor term in
-log a_e(q), in addition to S_min/g^2 and the explicit Gaussian volume
constant. At nonzero q,e the entire matrix M_* is used.

For any two local datum coordinates r,s,

 D_rs log[(-1)^m det M_*]
   =Tr(M_*^-1 M_*,rs
                     -M_*^-1 M_*,r M_*^-1 M_*,s).      (6.2)

Although M_* is finite range, its DATA derivatives need not have compact
support: x_* and lambda_* respond exponentially. Equations(3.3)--(3.5)
give precisely the needed replacement. M_*,r has a bounded finite-range
matrix stencil with coefficients decaying exponentially from r; M_*,rs
has that stencil with a three-root tree weight about r,s. These bounds
include all explicit exterior derivatives as well as the implicit saddle
responses.

In the first trace of (6.2), the matrix indices of M_*,rs are nearby.
Sum its tree weight over this local vertex and use bounded M_*^-1 entries.
In the second trace, the two inverse kernels connect the two derivative
vertices; sum the resulting connected exponential graph. Both are bounded
by C_L exp[-m''_L dist(r,s)] after reducing the exponent. The same argument
gives a uniform bound for the first derivative of the trace logarithm.
No extensive trace is bounded merely by its dimension: the marked anchors
are retained before summing.

For the Haar terms, write log j_C(x_*) as a sum of its local link functions.
Its second derivative consists of h''(x_*) x_r x_s and h'(x_*) x_rs.
Equations(3.3)--(3.5) give the same exponential bound after the link sum.
The original log j_J(v) is local in the specified datum coordinates and
has bounded local derivatives. Hence

 |D_r V1|<=C_L,
 |D_rs V1|<=C_L exp[-m''_L dist(r,s)],
 sup_r sum_s exp[m'''_L dist(r,s)] |D_rs V1|<=C_L.       (6.3)

An extensive constant V1(0,0) can be retained in the vacuum normalization;
it is not present in its marked derivative bounds. This proves spatial
locality of the COMPLETE leading determinant/Haar contribution, without
separately bounding a coordinate Jacobian and a tangent determinant in
incompatible gauges.

7. RELATION TO THE ACTUAL FINITE-g EFFECTIVE ACTION

In the real weak-data/polylog regime of constrained_effective_action40,
the exact positive numerator satisfies

 -log a_e(q)=S_min(q,e)/g^2+V1(q,e)
               -(dim ker Q)/2 log(2pi g^2)-log(1+Err).  (7.1)

This companion proves volume-independent classical response and the
leading prefactor locality in (7.1). It does NOT infer local derivative
bounds for log(1+Err) from its scalar value bound. The actual finite-g
spatial two-source theorem in spatial_fluctuation_route40 is a separate
fixed-order argument at q=0,e=I; its sublogarithmic absorption cannot be
silently extended to arbitrary nonzero datum or repeated effective steps.

The determinant identity is coordinate exact on this known branch. It
does not turn an exceptional coarse fiber into a chosen regular conditional
law, remove missing compact branches, establish a physical-scale source
norm, or identify the full interacting coarse action with a nearest-neighbor
Wilson action. Those distinctions survive the genuine locality gains here.
