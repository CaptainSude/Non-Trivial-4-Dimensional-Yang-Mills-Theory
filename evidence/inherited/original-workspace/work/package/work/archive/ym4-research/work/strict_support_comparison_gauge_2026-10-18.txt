STRICT SUPPORT OF THE SMALL COMPARISON GAUGE: WORKING DERIVATION
18 October 2026. This is a proof calculation, not a publication artifact.

Purpose: improve the scalar q-block domain gain in Notebook42 to a
strict-support family bound. The fixed q is chosen before the field
radius. All constants below may depend on q and the fixed grouping J,
but not on preceding fine refinement or volume.

Parameter order: fix the carrier exponent and J, then q, then the
radius ceiling. Comparison operators do not require J to grow with q:
an operator of range O(1) in an intermediate physical scale has range
O(ell^-k) in final units. The multilevel range extensions sum a geometric
series. Thus a common positive physical decay exponent survives; q may
enlarge amplitudes and shrink the admissible radius. The direct-prefix
constant C_a in (3) is uniform in the endpoint ratio. This avoids a
circular choice J(q), followed by q depending on J.

1. SUPPORT ALGEBRA INPUT AND ITS SCOPE

Use the complete physical Banach fibers of cl-substitution: potentials
X=C^(1,alpha), scalar forcing Y=C^alpha, gauge parameters Z=C^(2,alpha).
For each output block, sum strict input-support coefficients with a
connected carrier joining the output to the input slots. Norm the
COMPLETE physical fiber before taking the weighted support sum. The
weight is exp(a |carrier|); a physical tree-decay reserve can be retained.
Products join carriers at their common output root. Linear propagation
joins the input and output roots by a chosen lattice path.

If ||K_xy|| between these complete local fibers is exponentially
summable, the resulting lifted linear map is bounded after choosing J
large enough that the path-cardinality weight costs less than its
physical decay exponent. The already established cl-substitution proof
applies, including smooth physical partitions of unity and their collars.
No enumeration of fine coordinates is used. A scalar weighted norm
alone would not prove this statement: complete localized operator bounds
are required. The scalar operators here have them by the canceled-symbol
and contour-shift arguments of ng-scalar-bounds, ng-biharmonic-bounds.

The multilevel maps G_A-Q0 and b_j(U)-1 have bounded final-physical range,
analytic coefficients, and a summable geometric spacing factor. Their
lifted norms are analytic majorants with a fixed radius independent of
fine refinement. The generator X_A-D is a local analytic multiplication/
shift operator from Z to X with norm O(||A||_X). The apparent eta^-1
cancels in eta^-1(Ad(exp(i eta A))-I); this cancellation is done BEFORE
lifting. These are the same complete-fiber products as in ng-generator-
bound, rather than entrywise bounds on a matrix of fine coordinates.

Consequently the operations below lift by explicit Neumann series and
products. This construction does not assert that every bounded analytic
map has a localized lift.

2. REMOVE THE RANGE-SPACE ISSUE BEFORE LIFTING

Write F_A=G_A-Q0, Ts_A=(I+H_b F_A)^-1 and T=G'R0.
The known identities are Q0 T=0 and R0 D^T D T=R0.
Define on the WHOLE scalar space Y

 N_A = R0 D^T [ (X_A-D) Ts_A T + D (Ts_A-I) T ] R0,
 Z_A = (I+N_A)^-1.

N_0=0 by its displayed expression, before any regrouping of supports.
Its lifted norm is at most C_a ||A||_support on a fixed smaller ball.
For C_a ||A||<1/2 the Neumann series for Z_A converges in the same
support algebra. No support coefficient is required individually to
belong to range R0. On scalar realization, N_A=R0 N_A R0, and I+N_A
restricted to range R0 equals M_A=R0 D^T X_A Ts_A T.

Put K(A)=X_A Ts_A T Z_A R0, P(A)=Ts_A T Z_A R0.
Both have bounded analytic lifted operator norms, uniformly on a fixed
small support ball. Scalar realization gives

 R0 D^T K(A)y=y,   G_A P(A)y=0       for y in range R0.

3. INTEGRABLE FORCING, WITHOUT A SUPPORTWISE CANCELLATION CLAIM

For a small supported input family A_in, let y_in be its lifted linear
image R0 D^T A_in. Solve directly in the support Banach space

 dA/dt = -exp(-t) K(A)y_in,       A(0)=A_in.              (1)

Choose a fixed operator-domain radius r and ||A_in|| small enough that
||A_in||+C ||y_in||<r/2. Picard continuation and

 ||A(t)-A_in|| <= C(1-exp(-t))||y_in||

keep the solution in the domain for all t>=0. Equivalently set
tau=1-exp(-t): a bounded analytic ODE on the fixed interval [0,1].
Uniform Picard iteration gives analytic dependence on A_in; the endpoint
is a strict supported family with norm at most C_a ||A_in||. The integral
tail is at most C exp(-t)||A_in||, in the SUPPORT norm itself.

Now realize the families as actual fields. The two scalar identities
above give

 R0 D^T A(t)=exp(-t)y_in,  G_A P(A)y_in=0.

Thus (1) is exactly the constraint-preserving gauge flow ng-flow, since
its instantaneous gauge generator is exp(-t)P(A(t))y_in. This verification
takes place AFTER realization. It does not assume that the error identity
holds for an arbitrary chosen support assignment.

Integrating the corresponding group-valued gauge ODE in its unital local
matrix algebra yields a strict supported endpoint g-1, and likewise its
inverse, of norm <=C_a||A_in||. The generator has an integrable Z-norm,
so differentiation in physical space and the complete Z fiber are
included. At real inputs it is the actual unitary gauge. Complex inputs
are the holomorphic continuation on the same small ball.

4. TRANSFERS AND THE COMPARISON GAUGE

The ordinary-to-corrected transfer solves
 theta = omega - [K_induced(A,theta)-theta].
Its bracket has zero theta differential at the joint origin and a
localized analytic majorant: finite-physical-range multilevel averages,
the supported H_b, and the local gauge action. The contraction therefore
lifts on a smaller support ball exactly as cl-substitution. The reverse
transfer uses the finite-range E_pt and the same multilevel b_j map.
Both transfers have strict supported gauge endpoints O(||A||).

Apply these transfers and (1) to the two same-graph minima in
csg-comparisons. The rescaling between their physical norms costs only
fixed q-dependent constants. Uniqueness on the flat slice identifies the
real endpoints; analytic continuation identifies their germs. Composing
the supported endpoint gauges, restricting them to current sites, and
inverting in the local group algebra gives the actual comparison gauge
gamma_(M,q)(w) of mrg-block-gain with a strict family

 ||gamma_(M,q)-1||_support <= C_(q,a) b.                 (2)

This uses the explicit comparison construction. Existence of an orbit
relation by itself would not give (2).

5. THE IMPORTANT ZERO-NOISE REPLACEMENT

The exact retained q-chart and the SAME noise-independent gauge (2)
give a strictly supported analytic map Bhat(w,t). On fixed larger
background/noise balls its norm is <=K_q b+C_q rho. Analytic difference
estimates in the noise Banach variable, on fixed fractional margins,
give a strict family for Bhat(w,t)-Bhat(w,0) with norm <=C_(q,a)||t||.

Scalar realization of Bhat(w,0) is EXACTLY the direct ordinary prefix
a_(M,ell^q)(w). Represent the zero-noise part by that prefix's existing
strict family, instead of retaining the possibly much larger family
obtained by separately multiplying the comparison-gauge factors.
Therefore one valid exact supported representation satisfies

 ||Bhat||_support <= (C_a/ell^q)b_out+C_(q,a)rho.        (3)

Choose q first so theta=C_a/ell^q is smaller than the fixed inverse
polarization and complete-block substitution constants (in particular
theta<1/16e), then the common fixed radius ceiling. Require the noise
term C_(q,a)rho to obey the same smaller fractional bound relative to
b_in, and b_out/b_in<2. A mere scalar one-quarter margin would not pay
the Banach polarization factor in a full support substitution.
Equation (3) gives a fixed fractional margin inside the input ball b_in.
This is a strict-support estimate, stronger than scalar domain gain.
For retained noise rho<=C g(log gamma)^p and b=c g(log gamma)^s,
s>p makes this condition hold for all sufficiently large gamma.
There is no uniformity assertion as q tends to infinity.

6. COMPACT GAUGES AND INFINITE SCALAR IDENTITIES

For compact coarse w, every nonzero coefficient of gamma(w)-1 meets
supp(w). The retained physical tree reserve therefore gives exponential
decay of gamma-1 and its physical differences away from supp(w). The
same is true of each prefix potential. Truncating log gamma by smooth
large-box cutoffs gives allowed compact gauges and convergence in l1
of their transformed current fields to the exact gauge transform.
On a sufficiently small germ all gauge paths stay in the input domain.
The analytic-activity total is l1-Lipschitz on strict sub-balls, so its
compact-gauge identity passes to this limit. Analytic continuation in a
scalar multiple of w extends the endpoint scalar identity throughout
the common ball on which both endpoint maps have a strict margin.

Thus this construction addresses the extra infinite-gauge compatibility
condition in aqb-descent-section for locally gauge-invariant totals.
It does not by itself prove the quantum kernel identity, mixed-sector
matching, positivity of a continuum limit, or existence of YM4.

7. SAME-EXPONENT CARRIER COMPRESSION (INDEPENDENT ELEMENTARY LEMMA)

For a connected input J-cell animal H, let pi_L H be its image in
coarse cells of side L J. Then in dimension d

 |pi_L H| <= 3^d [1+(|H|-1)/L].                         (4)

Color coarse cells by their coordinates mod 3. In one color, selected
points of H in distinct cells have lattice distance at least 2L.
A depth-first tour of a spanning tree of H has length 2(|H|-1).
Read the first visits to the selected points in tour order: each segment
between consecutive selected points has length at least 2L. Hence that
color contains at most 1+(|H|-1)/L selected cells. Sum over 3^d colors.

At a fixed large L, (4) pays an output carrier exponent using only a
fraction of the input exponent. Fixed polynomial attachment/mark factors
in |H| can use another fraction. This supplies the elementary geometric
reserve for a same-exponent q-block marked expansion; the actual cluster
expansion and its new-carrier costs still have to be established.
