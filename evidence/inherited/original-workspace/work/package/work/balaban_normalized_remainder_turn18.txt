3. TWO POSITIVE TAYLOR LEMMAS, WITH DIFFERENT VARIABLES

3A. Fixed FIELD radius.
Let F_g(B) be holomorphic on ||B||<rho_* with norm <=M, uniformly in g and
the regulator. No coupling analyticity is needed. If P_N,g is its Taylor
polynomial in B through degree N, radial Cauchy gives

 ||F_g(gx)-P_N,g(gx)||
 <= M (g||x||/rho_*)^(N+1)/(1-g||x||/rho_*) .             (3.1)

On g||x||<=rho_*/2, integration against a uniformly controlled Gaussian
moment majorant gives C_N M g^(N+1). An explicit prefactor g^-2, as in the
Wilson action, lowers that exponent by two. A retained cutoff complement
requires its own tail estimate; (3.1) cannot be applied there by fiat.

Thus B109's fixed-field-radius regular core is potentially useful. The
Taylor coefficients in (3.1) may still depend arbitrarily on g; identifying
them with a finite perturbative YM calculation is a separate step.

3B. Small ACTIVITY amplitude on an already admitted regular kernel.
Fix the maps, cutoffs, reference measure, geometry and other activities.
Suppose the original absolute connected expansion allows ||W||<delta_0
with spare budget and gives a normalized localized map F(W;v), bounded by
M(v) on that ball. Here v belongs to the admitted common source domain.
For ||W||=epsilon<delta_0, apply Cauchy to zeta -> F(zeta W;v). Then

 ||F(W;v)-sum_(n=0)^N D_W^n F(0;v)[W^n]/n!||
 <= M(v) (epsilon/delta_0)^(N+1)/(1-epsilon/delta_0).      (3.2)

The convergent connected expansion supplies the logarithm and its branch;
no global estimate of the partition denominator by exp(C volume) is used.
The original proof's uniform rooted majorants give the same uniformity in
(3.2). This conclusion is relative to the ORIGINAL activity class and
reserved budget. It does not authorize an amplified angular source which
has not been shown to belong to that class.

3C. Running radius is quantitatively weaker.
If the only bound is ||F_g||_(a_g)<=M_g with a_g=g ell_g and ell_g a fixed
power of log(g^-2), the corresponding bound contains (g/a_g)^(N+1).
For M_g=Ca_g^2 this is a g^2 times logarithmic estimate. Fixed field-order
Taylor truncation therefore does not produce every desired g-power from
this envelope alone.

4. GAUGE-INVARIANT COUNTEREXAMPLES TO TWO INVALID IMPLICATIONS

These are counterexamples to norm implications, not to the actual YM RG
trajectory or to B109's stronger fixed-field-domain hypotheses.

4A. Moving-ball envelope.
On a fixed local current-lattice carrier let

 I(U)=1-(tr U(partial p)+tr U(partial p)^-1)/2.

The trace is normalized. This is a holomorphic class function on the
complexified group, invariant under the full local gauge action. It ignores
the independent J coordinate, so simultaneous (U,J) invariance holds. On a
weak plaquette domain ||log U(partial p)||<=c a, |I(U)|<=C a^2; its quadratic
jet is nonzero in a suitable nonzero Lie-algebra direction. One may sum the
finite orientation orbit if lattice covariance is desired.

For fixed integer m define

 W_g(U,J)=a_g^2 [I(U)/(C a_g^2)]^m.                     (4.1)

Its original-domain supremum is <=a_g^2, it vanishes at (1,0), and its field
Taylor series begins at degree 2m. Choose 2m>N. Every term through field
degree N is zero. On U=exp(i g A) with a fixed generic local A,

 W_g(exp(i g A),0)=c_A g^2 ell_g^(2-2m)(1+O(g)), c_A!=0. (4.2)

For every p>2, the ratio of (4.2) to g^p diverges as g decreases. This is
already a fixed finite-graph example, so no refinement subtlety is needed
to refute the claimed implication. It obeys the gauge and independent-pair
conditions; the failure is exactly the moving analytic radius.

4B. Real-g smoothness or separate analyticity without a common bound.
Let Phi be a fixed invariant analytic function of norm <=1 on a fixed
field domain. For epsilon_j ->0 and a fixed even integer m>N, put

 F_j(g,U)=g^2 sin^m(g/epsilon_j) Phi(U).                 (4.3)

It is entire in g and is uniformly bounded by g^2 on the real interval.
Its Taylor coefficients through order N vanish, but at g=pi epsilon_j/2
its size is g^2. Thus neither a C-infinity statement nor separate complex
analyticity plus a real-interval bound supplies a regulator-uniform
coupling Taylor remainder. A common complex-g majorant would fix this;
none is inferred here from B109's wording.

5. EXACT FINITE-CUTOFF NORMALIZED INTERPOLATION

Let nu be a fixed finite-dimensional positive reference measure. Assume
S_t and the completed packets Delta_(a,t) are differentiable, with positive
finite Z_t=integral exp(-S_t) dnu for real 0<=t<=1. All fine Wilson variables
can be retained, so this formulation does not require a history kernel or
a global logarithm of a signed history expansion. Write mu_t for the
normalized law, R_t=partial_t S_t and kappa_t for connected cumulants.

For every finite nonempty set of labels A,

 d/dt kappa_t(Delta_(a,t):a in A)
 = sum_(a in A) kappa_t(partial_t Delta_(a,t),
                       Delta_(b,t):b!=a)
   - kappa_t(R_t,Delta_(a,t):a in A).                   (5.1)

Proof: differentiate the logarithm of the normalized generating integral
with independent nilpotent variables z_a, z_a^2=0. Its body is one. The
derivative subtracts the normalized expectation of R_t; coefficient
extraction gives (5.1). This proof uses no complex zero-free disk.

If S_t=S_0+tR and all packets are t-independent, iteration yields

 partial_t^n kappa_t(Delta_A)=(-1)^n kappa_t(Delta_A,R,...,R).

Consequently its exact Taylor remainder through order N-1 is

 (-1)^N/(N-1)! integral_0^1 (1-t)^(N-1)
                    kappa_t(Delta_A,R repeated N) dt. (5.2)

For R=sum_X R_X, expand the last expression into the sum over X_1,...,X_N.
The complete alternating radii, angular integrals, vacuum subtraction,
lower-field calibration and spectator conventions remain INSIDE each
cumulant before taking its absolute value. Any t-dependent calibration
produces the first sum in (5.1); it cannot silently be held fixed.

If the interpolation changes a measure, cutoff or coordinate map, its
Jacobian, boundary variation or observable pullback belongs in this
identity. The formula alone does not identify a native signed history
remainder with one globally small positive action R. A same-space complete
action interpolation is a substantive attachment when one is needed.

6. WHY AN ACTION POWER IS NOT A PER-COPY POWER

For one regular marked insertion B119's normalization reduces the expansion
to clusters connected to that insertion. With n packet copies and one
distinguished R vertex the analogous connected family may connect ALL n
copies through that ONE vertex and ordinary propagation. A bound epsilon_R
on the R vertex gives epsilon_R, not epsilon_R^n. Thus arbitrary fixed
perturbative order in R is not, by itself, the copied-norm estimate needed
by the reflection/heat consumer. Taking an n-th root erases a fixed power
g^p as n grows.

Nor does small LOCAL action imply a volume-uniform local response. Let a
positive two-state law be supported on the all-plus and all-minus spin
configurations of V sites, with equal weights. The local perturbation
R=-epsilon sum_x sigma_x has per-site norm epsilon, but

 <sigma_0>_(S_0+R)=tanh(epsilon V).

Its initial response is epsilon V. This logical counterexample isolates
the need for a connected, rooted spatial sum. It is not a gauge-theory
counterexample. A bound on TOTAL |R| would avoid this volume issue, but
would still multiply the potentially divergent packet norm and would not
give a small factor for every copy.

The missing estimate can be stated without prescribing a proof. For the
complete dyadic packet differences and each fixed necessary action order N,
one needs a rooted bound of the form

 sup_a sum_(A contains a) [tau^p/epsilon(r)]^|A|
   sum_(X_1,...,X_N) |kappa_t(Delta_A,R_X1,...,R_XN)|
       <= C_N epsilon_R^N,                            (6.1)

uniformly in the cutoff, volume, labels and interpolation. The packet-
derivative terms of (5.1) need the same estimate. A source formulation with
an amplified nilpotent-copy norm is equivalent for the intended use.
The original source theorem gives bounds when its INPUT norm is small;
it has not supplied the epsilon(r)^|A| cancellation of (6.1).

Here I is the fluctuation factor after the static value is factored out:
its integrand is exp(V_core+t Delta W). If that value has not been factored

This convention is necessary for the displayed sign and complete ledger.

After the original connected localization, every term on the right has at

9. SCOPE CORRECTION: CORRELATION EXISTENCE IS WEAKER THAN THE COPY CONSUMER

The per-copy bound (6.1) is the requirement of the particular bounded-heat/
operator-norm route, not a necessary condition for existence of a reconstructed
theory. Under the corrected OS-II E0' formulation being used by the parent,
an alternative target is a common sequence of distributions G_(n,j) on S^0
with a fixed seminorm scheme p_(s n), and constants A,B,s independent of n,j:

 |G_(n,j+1)(F)-G_(n,j)(F)|
        <= A^n (n!)^B epsilon_j p_(s n)(F),
                         sum_j epsilon_j < infinity.              (9.1)

Together with the corresponding initial growth bound, (9.1) gives the limit
and the same type of growth bound by an ordinary telescoping sum. Covariance,
symmetry and reflection positivity pass to the limit on their applicable
test domains if they hold for the common approximants. All other precise
reconstruction/domain hypotheses must still be checked. No operator-norm
gain or epsilon_j^n is inferred or needed for this particular convergence
step. Thus one action-remainder smallness per FULL n-point correlation can
be useful; the contrary inference would overstate Section6.

B119's normalized distinguished-insertion expansion is more directly suited
to this weaker target: keep one action-remainder insertion distinguished,
then differentiate an ordinary common source functional n times. A genuine
uniform source disk of radius rho, with the required rooted and spatial-test
majorant, would give n! rho^-n times the one-insertion error. Factorial
combinatorics of fixed-degree composite packets can still fit (n!)^B with a
fixed B. This does not require the amplified copy radius of (6.1).

What is not yet proved is precisely the uniform source-functional estimate
for the COMPLETE renormalized packets: its constants must have the form
A^n(n!)^B, with one fixed linear seminorm-order rule, and its small remainder
epsilon_j must survive packet-radius/contact renormalization uniformly in
the regulator and volume. Differentiating a scalar-source disk whose packet
embedding costs r_j^-D produces r_j^(-D n), not an admissible bound (9.1)
by itself. External test-function flatness at coincident packet CENTERS
does not automatically remove divergences internal to each point-split
packet, even when the centers stay separated. Those internal cancellations
and calibrations must be retained before absolute values. B119's one-mark
normalization provides part of the mechanism but not this uniform-in-n,
uniform-in-r source theorem. Sections3--7 neither prove nor rule out (9.1).
