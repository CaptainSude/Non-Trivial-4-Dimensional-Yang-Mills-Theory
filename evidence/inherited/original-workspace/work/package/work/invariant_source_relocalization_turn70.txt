                    sum_(p,X) A_(p,X)(U)=C.           (1.2)

2. A COMMON TWO-BACKGROUND HOMOTOPY

Let U,V both lie in an inner coherent weak domain D_bw, where
C0 b_w<b_s for a fixed group/chart constant C0. For each link
set

 X_e(U,V)=log(U_e V_e^-1),
 U_e(t)=exp[t X_e(U,V)] V_e,   0<=t<=1.                (2.1)

The logarithm is the near-identity branch. The common pair
domain can be fixed so that the whole path lies inside a
strict smaller part of D_bs. This costs a fixed radius factor,
independent of carrier size and volume.

Under the joint endpoint transformation

 U_e -> h_-(e) U_e h_+(e)^-1,
 V_e -> h_-(e) V_e h_+(e)^-1,

one has exactly

 X_e -> Ad(h_-(e))X_e,       U(t) -> U(t)^h.           (2.2)

The velocity in the left tangent frame is X_e. If a transformed
pair is also in the admitted inner domain, its path is again
in the outer domain and (2.2) holds throughout. Thus no
uncontrolled excursion under a large gauge is needed.

Choose a fixed physical location x_e for each read link and
put f_e=f(x_e). Differentiate (1.2) with respect to that link
at U(t). Its derivative is zero. The fundamental theorem of
calculus therefore gives the EXACT identity

 A_f(U)-A_f(V)
   =sum_(p,X) sum_(e in X) [f(x_p)-f_e] H_(p,X,e)(U,V),

 H_(p,X,e)
   =int_0^1 d_e^L A_(p,X)(U(t)) . X_e(U,V) dt.        (2.3)

Every H_(p,X,e) is individually invariant under JOINT local
gauge transformations of U,V. The derivative is a covariant
cotensor at the initial vertex and X_e transforms dually.
Its support is the same geometric X with two typed copies
of its link slots. It reads no second-background data outside X.

This support fact is important: the older general two-background
observation for a nonlocalized TOTAL did not localize its
background reads. Here strict support is already provided by
the actual incoming invariant catalogue (1.1).

Take k_w<k_s and b_w sufficiently below b_s. Relative Cauchy
on the fixed chart margin bounds

 ||H_(p,X,e)||_bw
        <=C b_w/(b_s-C0b_w) ||A_(p,X)||_bs.           (2.4)

With a bounded number of links per cell and connected complete
carriers,

 |x_p-x_e|<=C J ell |X|.

Thus, for 0<alpha<=1, the factors |X| from the edge sum and
|X|^alpha from the profile difference are paid by the fixed
positive exponent reserve. Consequently the invariant
two-background representative in (2.3) obeys

 ||A_f(U)-A_f(V)||_(inv;k_w,b_w)
                    <=C M ell^alpha [f]_(C^alpha).   (2.5)

The constant may depend on the fixed radius/exponent margins,
group, read-cell geometry and fixed J, but not on volume or
preceding classical depth. This is the desired profile gain
for the difference, with an exact total identity.

3. WHY FREEZING THE SECOND BACKGROUND IS NOT A DESCENT

Setting V=I in the TOTAL identity (2.3) recovers the wanted
scalar A_f(U)-A_f(I), but does not make its individual
two-background carriers invariant in U alone. A gauge
transformation must also send V_e=I to h_-h_+^-1.

Already the one-link function

             1-(1/2)Tr(U_e V_e^-1)

is jointly gauge invariant. At V=I it becomes a nonconstant
single-link function, which is not locally gauge invariant.
An arbitrarily small transformation at one endpoint changes
its value while both configurations remain in the weak chart.
Global compact color averaging does not correct this:
the one-link trace is already invariant under conjugation.

It is therefore invalid to cite (2.5), freeze V=I, and
declare its individual carriers locally invariant.

There are two legitimate uses of (2.3).

* If V is an INDEPENDENT physical background, retain both
  arguments and their joint transformation law.

* If an actual covariant local or quasilocal anchor V=R(U)
  has a controlled complete support catalogue, compose
  the two-background carriers with it. The exact identity
  becomes A_f(U)-A_f(R(U)); its second term remains.

The actual TURN47 covariant KKT saddle is an example of a
controlled anchor at its stated first-step weak-domain
inputs. It lies in a constrained coarse fiber and is not
a flat connection for general coarse data. Its substitution
therefore supplies a coarse/classical residual, not the
vacuum A_f(I). No enlargement to all native domains or
arbitrary-depth KKT closure follows from this observation.

4. SHORT COVARIANT FLOW: AN INVARIANT COMMUTATOR AND A RESIDUAL

There is also a single-background version for a finite
step, without an independent anchor. Let W_e(U) be a
holomorphic, finite-read, gauge-covariant left tangent
vector field, W(I)=0. Its finite-read coefficients and
relative Taylor tensors have uniform analytic bounds
on a fixed weak domain. The dimensionless Wilson
gradient vector field is one example. The flow obeys

             dU_e(t)/dt=W_e(U(t))U_e(t),
             U(0)=U,   U(t)=Phi_t U.                  (4.1)

Use DIMENSIONLESS lattice time here. A physical Wilson
flow with coefficient proportional to a^-2 would
rescale this time; no fixed physical time is declared
small uniformly in a.

The finite local analytic bounds and W(I)=0 give a
positive t0, independent of volume, such that for
0<=t<=t0 the flow from an inner weak domain stays in
the outer domain. The flow is gauge covariant by
uniqueness. Differentiating the same constant total
along it gives

 A_f(U)-A_f(Phi_t U)
   =-sum_(p,X) sum_(e in X)[f(x_p)-f_e]
       int_0^t d_e^L A_(p,X)(Phi_s U)
                           .W_e(Phi_s U) ds.         (4.2)

The contraction under the integral is individually
gauge invariant. Equation(4.2) keeps the EXACT residual
A_f(Phi_t U); it is not an identity modulo an unknown
field term.

For completeness, strict support and fixed-radius
bounds can be obtained without a noncovariant Taylor
expansion in the background link angles.

Write Phi_t(U)_e=exp[z_e(t,U)]U_e. The relative vector
z_e transforms by the initial-vertex adjoint. Its ODE
has finite-read background coefficients and covariant
relative Taylor tensors. Its analytic Picard trees
have the following uniform majorant on one smaller
relative ball: every vertex contributes a fixed
constant per incident leg, every read set has fixed
size, and time integration supplies the usual rooted
tree coefficients. The scalar ODE majorant with
analytic right side C(b+w)/(1-w/r) has a small solution
w(t)=O(bt). Assign each tree to the union of its
literal reads; these are connected to its output
link. Taking t0 smaller accommodates a fixed positive
carrier exponent. Marking any read vertex gives the
corresponding rooted bound, as in the covariant tree
proof of TURN47.

Now expand A_X(exp z U) in RELATIVE z at fixed U.
Its derivative tensors are covariant; contraction
with these trees yields invariant scalar diagrams.
For at most D|X| scalar relative coordinates, the
absolute Cauchy composition bound is of the form

       ||A_X||_bs (1-Cbt/r)^(-D|X|)
                  <=||A_X||_bs exp(C1 t |X|),

on a fixed strict margin. A small t0 pays C1 t|X|
from the exponent reserve. The extra derivative,
edge choice and source difference in (4.2) cost a
fixed power |X|^(1+alpha), paid from another fixed
part of the same reserve. All background reads of
W and of the flow trees remain in the output union.
No mere spatial-decay estimate is substituted for
this support accounting.

Thus (4.2) has an individually invariant, connected
carrier catalogue satisfying

 ||A_f-A_f o Phi_t||_(inv;k_w,b_w)
               <=C t M ell^alpha[f]_(C^alpha),
                         0<=t<=t0.                  (4.3)

The residual also has a controlled invariant
catalogue at this finite time. Its ordinary norm
is bounded by C M||f||_infinity, not by (4.3).
These are one-step bounds at fixed margins.

Iterating (4.2) formally telescopes, but the identity
always retains its last residual. Neither its
vanishing nor a uniformly bounded infinite-time
carrier sum follows from (4.3).

5. A SHARP OBSTRUCTION TO A COMMON LOCAL FLAT ANCHOR

Here is a precise reason not to close the previous
residual by assuming a uniformly local flat limit.

Take periodic d-dimensional lattices of side N,
d>=2. Suppose an anchor R is analytic near the
all-identity field and satisfies

 R(I)=I,
 R(U^h)=R(U)^h for local gauges near identity,
 every plaquette of R(U) is I.                       (5.1)

Only these local identities are used. In particular,
the argument does not need a global choice of large
gauge transformations, nontrivial topological sector,
or a continuum approximation.

In link Lie-algebra coordinates let P=D R(I). The
linearized lattice coboundary operators are d0
from vertices to links and d1 from links to
plaquettes. Gauge covariance and flatness imply

                  P d0=d0,        d1 P=0.            (5.2)

If R is not translation covariant, average P over
all lattice translations. The averaged linear
operator still satisfies(5.2), and any uniform
absolute locality bound is preserved. It is enough
to prove the obstruction for this average.

Write its convolution kernel as

          (P a)(x)=sum_z K_N(z)a(x-z),

including orientation and color indices. Choose
the centered periodic representative of z. Its
Fourier multiplier P(k) satisfies

 P(k)q(k)=q(k),
 image P(k) is contained in span q(k)
                           in spatial indices,     (5.3)

for every nonzero k, separately on color inputs.
Here q_i(k)=exp(i k_i)-1; the second statement is
the exact kernel of d1(k), not a small-k expansion.

Let k1=(2pi/N)e1 and k2=(2pi/N)e2.
At k1, gauge covariance requires

      P(k1)(e1 tensor c)=e1 tensor c

for any color c. At k2, flatness forces the
e1 component of P(k2)(e1 tensor c) to vanish.
For unit c,

                    ||P(k1)-P(k2)||>=1.              (5.4)

The Fourier representation and
|exp(iu)-exp(iv)|<=|u-v| give

 ||P(k1)-P(k2)||
  <=(2pi/N)sum_z (|z1|+|z2|)||K_N(z)||.

Therefore every such anchor obeys the exact lower bound

       sum_z (|z1|+|z2|)||K_N(z)|| >= N/(2pi).        (5.5)

In particular there is no volume-uniform exponential
locality estimate for its derivative. Such an
estimate would give a uniform finite first moment,
contradicting(5.5). A finite-range anchor with
uniform kernel norm is excluded as well.

The conclusion applies to the full coherent
coarse-link weak domain with its local gauge action.
It does not exclude the massive constrained-fiber
inverses of TURN47: their external coarse field is
retained, and they are not flat anchors on all
coarse connections.

It also does not exclude an auxiliary independent
transforming anchor. Joint covariance supplies a
different linear identity involving derivatives
in BOTH backgrounds. Freezing that background at
I forfeits the local gauge identity, exactly as
Section3 explains.

6. THE FINITE-FLOW ENDPOINT CHECK

The linearized covariant Wilson gradient flow
about I has, up to its fixed positive time
normalization, the Fourier operator

        exp[-t d1(k)^*d1(k)].

It is the identity on longitudinal/gauge
directions and damps transverse directions by
exp[-t |q(k)|^2]. On the smallest nonzero
momentum, |q(k)|^2 is of order N^-2.
Reducing that transverse mode by a fixed
factor therefore requires times of order N^2.

Its infinite-time limit is the longitudinal
projector for nonzero k (plus the constant
flat modes at k=0). That limit has exactly
the locality obstruction of Section5.
Thus a common finite-time covariant flow
does not have a volume-uniform contraction
to a flat endpoint on this full weak domain.

This check does not say a particular weighted
null family must decay as slowly as the
worst mode. Extra cancellation in that
family could improve the residual. Such
family-specific cancellation would have to
be proved; it is not supplied by covariance
of the flow or by its short-time locality.

7. WHAT THE GENERAL INVARIANT-TOTAL QUOTIENT DOES NOT SUPPLY

There are three different hypotheses, which cannot
be interchanged:

 (i) the total is invariant;
 (ii) an incoming invariant carrier catalogue exists;
 (iii) a SMALL invariant catalogue for a modulated
       null total exists with the profile gain.

The two positive constructions here start at(ii).
They prove a difference statement with a retained
anchor/flow residual. They do not prove(iii) for
the single-background vacuum-subtracted total.

The cycle example in gauge_localization_turn46.txt
already shows that a generic support-preserving
projection from arbitrary noninvariant carriers
can cost at least [c/(b)]^|X|. Its invariant total
does not control that cost at an unspecified
small exponent reserve. That example must not
be promoted into an obstruction for the stronger
input(ii), or for an allowed support enlargement.

Conversely, a carrier-dependent tree gauge may
stay in the weak chart for a small carrier and
may be excellent for estimating a classically
smoothed endpoint. It cannot simply replace the
COMMON path in (2.3) or (4.2): the zero derivative
of the TOTAL was used at the same field for every
summand. Different carrier paths do not preserve
that cancellation by themselves.

These general constructions alone narrow the
proof requirements: one must control the retained
residual or use a family-specific decomposition,
rather than silently freeze a jointly transforming
anchor. The actual leading source coefficient has
such an additional decomposition, proved in
Section9. It does not provide a universal invariant
projection for other quotient representatives.

9.1 Exact finite-dimensional ingredients.

Use the actual fixed-window selected map and its
moving relative constrained chart. Its frozen
ambient fluctuation coordinate is xi=(xi_r), with
one fixed-dimensional color space at each r.
The normalized product color-ball measure has

 E xi_r=0,    E[xi_r xi_s^t]=v_T delta_(rs) I_r.

The variance v_T is common to the color blocks.
All baseline windows and g-independent chart/
covariance data are frozen under the source.

For the scalar-vertex convention of
regular_source_modulation_turn70.txt, f_p below
means the LITERAL four-corner average of the
prolonged vertex source on the original plaquette.
On aligned grids it is equivalently the same
multilinear extension evaluated at its center.
The values f_r,f_a are that same scalar extension
evaluated at their fixed geometric anchors.
Convexly splitting each such evaluation into
current vertex labels preserves the exact
identities and coefficient norm, since its
weights sum to1. No different physical source
is substituted in this section.

At the constrained saddle define the actual
constraint derivative Q, its inverse Gram matrix
G, and the physical tangent projector

 G=(Q Q^t)^-1,
 P=I-Q^t G Q.                                         (9.1)

The rows of Q are the independent constraint
coordinates, indexed by a. They have fixed
dimension and fixed geometric locations x_a.
The ambient color blocks r likewise have fixed
locations x_r. Transpose is bilinear for the
holomorphic continuation. For real data (9.1)
is the orthogonal projector, and its rank is
constant on the admitted chart.

These are precisely the moving operators of
TURN47, not an invented replacement projection.
They have covariant complete-support matrix
catalogues at that theorem's stated weak-domain
and any-leg bounds. The following norm argument
retains those inputs. For later steps, the same
claim requires that this actual covariant
catalogue input be available there; a merely
invariant TOTAL is not enough.

For the literal source density labelled by its
original plaquette p, write

 J_p(g)=g J_(p,1)+g^2 J_(p,2)+O(g^3),
 J_(p,1)=sum_r xi_r . L_(p,r),
 J_(p,2)=(1/2)sum_(r,s) xi_r . H_(p,rs) xi_s.          (9.2)

The tensors include the full relative chart and
whitening K, including the second derivative of
the chart. They are not bare Wilson Hessians.
Their covariant source/leg/read catalogues come
from differentiating the actual covariant
physical-plaquette composition at fixed g-order.

Constrained stationarity and the EXACT physical
whitening give the pointwise tensor identities

                 sum_p L_(p,r)=0,
                 sum_p H_(p,rs)=P_(rs).              (9.3)

The first is stronger than zero expectation.
The second follows from
S(U_g)-S(V)=(g^2/2)xi^t P xi+O(g^3).
It includes all chart terms; deleting them would
invalidate (9.3).

With these literal weights f_p, the order-g^2 birth coefficient is

 A_E(f)=E J_(f,2)+E[J_(f,1) V_(1,E)].                 (9.4)

This is the exact coefficient of the SAME

9.2 First-jet term.

Let f_r=f(x_r). The first identity in (9.3)

 J_(f,1)=sum_(p,r)(f_p-f_r) xi_r . L_(p,r).            (9.5)

Each coefficient is covariant at its ambient

9.3 Hessian trace and the weighted rank identity.

Let F be the diagonal AMBIENT operator with
block F_r=f_r I_r. Let C be the diagonal
CONSTRAINT operator with block C_a=f_a I_a,
where f_a=f(x_a). They commute with the
respective block gauge transformations.

By the second identity in (9.3),

 E J_(f,2)
  =(v_T/2)[
     sum_(p,r)(f_p-f_r) tr H_(p,rr)+Tr(FP)].          (9.6)

There is an exact algebraic identity

 Tr(FP)=Tr F-Tr C
                    -Tr[G(QF-CQ)Q^t].               (9.7)

Proof: use P=I-Q^tGQ and cyclicity. The second
trace term is Tr(GQFQ^t); insert
QF=(QF-CQ)+CQ. Since G=(QQ^t)^-1,
Tr(GCQQ^t)=Tr C. No commutation of C with
Q or G is assumed.

In components, the last term of (9.7) is

 sum_(a,b,r) (f_r-f_b)
                tr[G_(ab) Q_(br) Q_(ar)^t].          (9.8)

The color contraction is invariant at every
leg. Its complete support is the connected
union of the G,Q,Q carrier reads, including
the row anchors a,b and ambient anchor r.
The profile difference is now attached to
actual positions in that union.

Combining (9.4)--(9.8) yields the exact
single-background identity

 A_E(f)=C_count(f)+R_E(f),                            (9.9)

 C_count(f)=(v_T/2)[sum_r dim(r)f_r
                                  -sum_a dim(a)f_a],

 R_E(f)
  =(v_T/2)sum_(p,r)(f_p-f_r)tr H_(p,rr)
   -(v_T/2)sum_(a,b,r)(f_r-f_b)
                       tr[G_(ab)Q_(br)Q_(ar)^t]
   +E[sum_(p,r)(f_p-f_r)
                         (xi_r.L_(p,r)) V_(1,E)].

Every term displayed in R_E has a profile
difference and an invariant contracted
catalogue. C_count is independent of the
background field, but is not automatically
the WHOLE source vacuum: R_E(f)(0) remains
part of that vacuum. Thus

 A_E(f)(0)=C_count(f)+R_E(f)(0).                       (9.10)

For constant f, all differences vanish and
(9.9) reduces to v_T rank(P)f/2 exactly.

10. WHAT THIS NEW ACTUAL REPAIR CLOSES

Equation(9.13) closes the specific
invariant-representative defect of the
leading ACTUAL selected source birth
whenever its moving chart/projection and
incoming E have the established covariant
complete-support catalogues. It is an
explicit finite-step construction from
those inputs, not a universal projection
theorem for arbitrary invariant totals.

It preserves the original source,
all weighted saddle first jets, and
the complete source vacuum. It does
not require a local flat anchor and
therefore does not conflict with (5.5).

There remain separate questions:
classical transport in the invariant
inhomogeneous quotient; availability
of the stated covariant input class
at every required scale and native
history; and the actual full-law
separated physical correlation.
None is solved merely by (9.13).
