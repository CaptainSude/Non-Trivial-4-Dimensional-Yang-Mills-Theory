TURN96 -- EXACT INTEGRATED SU(2) BOUNDARY CURRENTS
21 October 2026. mixed96. Scoped calculation for root; no frontier edit.

RESULT

The one-sided real response idea has a concrete smallest Wilson test.
Integrating raw links DOES cancel a motion of the marked plaquette that
is merely a change of Haar coordinates. The cancellation transfers the
response to the exterior staple score. That score is generally nonzero,
even after integrating all the raw links and contracting all gauge
indices. Two exact formulas below distinguish these statements.

The two-raw-link model is an actual finite Wilson conditional fiber,
not an arbitrary positive surrogate. It matches the noncommuting raw
plaquette geometry in turn96_old_holonomy.txt. Its nonzero first response
is proved by an exact Taylor coefficient, not numerical evidence. It is
NOT asserted to be an admitted first-R history at a chosen native scale.
It therefore supplies a concrete test of a proposed cancellation, not a
counterexample to a native theorem or a uniform original mixed estimate.

1. ONE-LINK FIBER: MARK AND WEIGHT SCORE TOGETHER

Use the real quaternion realization SU(2)=S^3. Condition the original
finite Wilson law on all links except e. If its six oriented staples
are v_1,...,v_6 in S^3, put v=sum v_p, s=|v|, r=beta s, n=v/s.
The exact conditional density is

 q_v(du)=z(r)^(-1) exp(beta u.v) du,
 z(r)=2 I_1(r)/r,     E u=m(r)n,     m(r)=I_2(r)/I_1(r).

At r=0 use z=1,m=0. The coupling-free complete star source is
S_e=6-u.v. Therefore

                  E_q S_e=6-s m(beta s).                (1.1)

For any differentiable physical exterior variation v(t), including
the change of the normalized conditional density, this gives

 d E_q S_e=-(m(r)+r m'(r)) ds.                           (1.2)

Equivalently, direct differentiation gives
 E_q dot S_e+beta Cov_q(S_e,u.dot v).
The equality with (1.2) keeps the weight score; it is not differentiation
of the mark at a fixed conditional law. Here m'(r)=Var_q(n.u)>0 for
finite r, so both terms have a definite, computable combination.

A common gauge rotation of all the staples has ds=0, and (1.2) vanishes.
A genuine relative exterior variation need not have ds=0. For example
take five staples equal to I and the sixth exp(i alpha sigma_3). Then
s=(26+10 cos alpha)^(1/2) and

 d_alpha E S_e=5 sin(alpha) (m(r)+r m'(r))/s >0
                                            for 0<alpha<pi.    (1.3)

The six opposite parallel links generating these staples can be chosen
independently on a sufficiently large ordinary four-dimensional lattice;
set the transverse links of their plaquettes to I. Other plaquettes
involve only the fixed exterior and drop from this conditional law.
Thus (1.3) is an actual Wilson boundary current. Gauge averaging changes
the staple frames, not the scalar s, and does not remove it.

There is a beta-independent finite bound in this constant-star channel:

                  |d E S_e| <= (5/2) |dot v|.            (1.4)

Proof. The spherical radial coordinate y=1-n.u has density proportional
to exp(-r y) y^(1/2)(2-y)^(1/2), 0<y<2. It is a Gamma(shape 3/2,
rate r) density tilted by a decreasing nonnegative factor, so
E y<=3/(2r), hence m>=1-3/(2r). Integration of the spherical radial
density, or differentiation of z, gives
 m'=1-m^2-3m/r. For r>=3/2, the last expression is decreasing as a
function of m>=0, and substitution of 1-3/(2r) gives
 r m'<=9/(4r)<=3/2. For r<=3/2 use Var(n.u)<=1, giving r m'<=3/2.
Together with 0<=m<=1 this proves (1.4). The r=0 endpoint follows by
continuity. No native Gaussian approximation is used.

For a weighted star w=sum f_p v_p the exact mean is
sum f_p-m(r)n.w. Its derivative also contains dot w and the transverse
derivative of n. The protected combination (1.2) applies when w=v (or
a fixed scalar multiple). It does not bound a general weighted mark on
arbitrarily balanced raw staple data merely by invoking stationarity.

2. TWO RAW LINKS: AN EXACT CHANGE-OF-VARIABLES CANCELLATION

Let Q,R in SU(2) be opposite parallel links, with R oriented so their
common plaquette is

 P_t=Q V_t R V_t^(-1),        V_t=exp(eta t T),
 h_t=1-Tr(P_t)/2,             T=i sigma_3.

Let A,B be fixed SU(2) exterior staple matrices. The conditional law is

 d nu_t(Q,R)=Z_t^(-1) exp{ beta Tr(P_t)/2
                      +kappa Tr(AQ)/2+lambda Tr(BR)/2 } dQ dR. (2.1)

This is a realizable Wilson fiber with the SAME homogeneous beta on
all plaquettes. Each raw link has five other oriented staples. Choose
their sums to be (kappa/beta) A and (lambda/beta) B. Such sums are
available for |kappa|,|lambda|<=2 beta: negative signs are absorbed in
A or B. For the positive amplitudes, three group elements can form
an equilateral quaternion triple summing to zero, while the remaining
pair can be chosen with sum c A for every 0<=c<=2. For example multiply
the pair cos(theta) I+sin(theta) C and -cos(theta) I+sin(theta) C
by suitable unit quaternions, with C^2=-I. All ten opposite parallel
exterior links are distinct. The two transverse common-plaquette links
are V_t, and no other Q/R plaquette reads them. Terms containing only
exterior links are conditional constants.

Change variables R'=V_t R V_t^(-1). Haar measure is unchanged, the
marked h becomes the FIXED h(QR'), and only the exterior B staple moves:

 B_t=V_t B V_t^(-1),
 d nu'_t proportional to exp{ beta Tr(QR')/2
                        +kappa Tr(AQ)/2+lambda Tr(B_t R')/2 }.

Consequently the exact normalized response is

 d_t E_{nu_t} h_t |0
       = (lambda eta/2) Cov_{nu'_0}( h(QR'), Tr([T,B]R') ). (2.2)

In the original variables the same derivative is
 E dot h-beta Cov(h,dot h), with the other factors fixed. Equation(2.2)
is its precise Haar integration-by-parts cancellation. The beta-weight
score and the moving-mark derivative cannot be bounded separately and
then declared canceled. They combine into the exterior-staple torque.
If lambda=0, or [T,B]=0, the full response vanishes exactly.

All traces in (2.1)-(2.2) are closed after the exterior staples are
included. An average over a common gauge frame conjugates T,B,R'
together and leaves the scalar score Tr([T,B]R') unchanged. The fact
that a bare adjoint vector has zero Haar mean does not zero this scalar.

3. THE REMAINING INTEGRATED TORQUE IS GENUINELY NONZERO

The nonzero response can be established without evaluating the whole
two-link integral. At kappa=lambda=0 write S=QR'. Then S has the central
Wilson law q_beta and Q is independent Haar. Schur orthogonality gives

 int dQ Tr(AQ) Tr(B_t Q^(-1)S) = Tr(A S B_t)/2.

Since E S=m(beta) I, expansion at the zero exterior-staple amplitudes
gives the EXACT mixed Taylor coefficient

 d_kappa d_lambda log Z_t |_(0,0)
                      = m(beta) Tr(A B_t)/8.             (3.1)

Single first coefficients vanish. Also
 E h_t=1-d_beta log Z_t, with kappa,lambda held fixed. Therefore

 d_kappa d_lambda d_t E h_t |_(0,0,0)
                  = -m'(beta) eta Tr(A[T,B])/8.          (3.2)

Take A=i sigma_1, B=i sigma_2, T=i sigma_3. Then
Tr(A B_t)=-2 sin(2 eta t), and (3.2) equals

                         eta m'(beta)/2 >0.             (3.3)

For kappa=lambda=epsilon, analyticity of the compact group integral and
its positive real denominator implies

 d_t E h_t |0=eta epsilon^2 m'(beta)/2+O_beta(eta epsilon^4). (3.4)

The absence of odd total powers follows from (Q,R')->(-Q,-R'), which
leaves QR' fixed and changes both staple scores' signs. At either
kappa=0 or lambda=0 the t dependence is identically absent. Thus for
each beta>0 and eta>0 the derivative is strictly positive for all
sufficiently small epsilon>0. This is an actual integrated scalar
boundary current, not merely a pointwise noncommuting trace derivative.

At large beta, m'(beta)~3/(2 beta^2). This also follows without importing
a Bessel asymptotic: beta(1-n.u) converges, with its first two moments,
to a Gamma(shape 3/2,rate 1) law by the radial density in Section1 and
dominated convergence after rescaling. The coefficient in (3.3) thus
has a beta^(-2) gain. The following direct estimate gives that same
order uniformly for bounded exterior-staple amplitudes; it does not
control arbitrary native surroundings with amplitudes of order beta.

3A. A UNIFORM REAL BOUND FOR THIS INTEGRATED TWO-LINK CURRENT

For every fixed K<infinity there is C_K<infinity such that, for
beta>=max(1,K/2), |kappa|,|lambda|<=K, all A,B in SU(2), and every real t,

          |d_t E_{nu_t} h_t|
                <=C_K |eta| |kappa lambda| beta^(-2).    (3.5)

This is a bound on the fully normalized actual fiber (2.1). It needs
no analytic norm of a raw source and no small-source Taylor truncation.
The K/2 threshold ensures the explicitly proved Wilson realization
|kappa|,|lambda|<=2 beta. The same analytic integral estimate holds for
all beta>=1, but values outside that realizability range are not being
called a six-plaquette homogeneous Wilson conditional fiber here.

Proof. After the above Haar change write S=QR', with c=Tr(S)/2.
Under the central Wilson law its scalar coordinate has density
proportional to exp(beta c)(1-c^2)^(1/2), -1<c<1. Integrate Q and the
direction of the vector part of S to form the positive radial weight
k_t(c). In the quaternion decomposition of B_t Q^(-1), write a for
its scalar part, so the vector part has length (1-a^2)^(1/2). The
directional integral is exactly

 k_t(c)=int dQ exp[kappa Tr(AQ)/2] exp(lambda a c)
             F(lambda^2 (1-a^2)(1-c^2)),
 F(x)=sinh(sqrt(x))/sqrt(x)=sum_(n>=0) x^n/(2n+1)!.       (3.6)

Its endpoint values follow by the series. Possible conventions for the
quaternion vector sign disappear in this directional integral. In
particular exp(-2K)<=k_t(c)<=exp(2K). The functions F,F',F'' are bounded
on [0,K^2]. Also |d_t a|<=2|eta|. Direct differentiation of (3.6) yields
uniform bounds for k, d_c k, d_t k and d_c d_t k. Every t derivative
contains at least one lambda. At kappa=0, integration of Q makes k_t
independent of t. Subtract that zero derivative BEFORE taking absolute
values, using |exp(kappa Tr(AQ)/2)-1|<=|kappa| exp(K). Hence

 |d_t k_t(c)|+|d_c d_t k_t(c)|<=C_K |eta| |kappa lambda|,
 |d_c k_t(c)|<=C_K,
 Lip_c[d_t log k_t(c)]<=C_K |eta| |kappa lambda|.          (3.7)

The normalized radial law is q_beta(dc) k_t(c)/q_beta(k_t), and
 h=1-c. Its exact derivative is Cov(h,d_t log k_t(c)). For any random
variable X and L-Lipschitz function b, the independent-copy identity
gives |Cov(X,b(X))|<=L Var(X). Thus (3.7) bounds the response by
C_K |eta kappa lambda| Var_{nu_t}(h).

The density ratio of this radial law to q_beta is at most exp(4K).
Centering first at the q_beta mean gives
 Var_{nu_t}(h)<=exp(4K) Var_{q_beta}(h)=exp(4K)m'(beta).
The elementary bound from Section1 implies
m'(beta)<=9/(4 beta^2) for beta>=1: for 1<=beta<=3/2 use variance<=1;
above 3/2 use its Bessel-identity calculation. This proves (3.5).

The use of radial angular integration is essential to this bound. A
pointwise estimate of the moving raw commutator has order |eta|, and
a separate absolute estimate of its beta weight score can be worse.
The normalized integrated response instead has the order in (3.5).
For kappa or lambda of order beta, the constants just proved can grow
exponentially with beta. Claiming (3.5) uniformly for those data would
be unjustified. The full native boundary-score attachment remains open.

4. CONSEQUENCE FOR THE ACTUAL MIXED SOURCE GATE

These computations support a one-sided approach: keep the rough old
mark under its positive integration and estimate the response of the
complete distant regular source through the boundary scores. Such an
approach need not first prove that the old mark belongs to an analytic
own-field class.

They also identify what cannot be omitted. The raw integration generally
leaves a scalar boundary current. In the complete native expression the
corresponding current includes all remaining interaction, constraint,
coarea, cut, old-record and continuation scores. For the desired
Cov(R_f,H_rest), it is the decay of the actual response connecting that
current to the distant original H_rest that must be bounded. Equations
(1.4) and (2.2) do not give its physical source-cell census or its decay
through the later history. The native erasure theorem is an integrated
catalogue comparison, so it does not itself identify a field-local
boundary path to which these calculations can be applied.

No arbitrary future-test requirement is introduced here. A useful
next target is the literal boundary score contracted with ONE actual
regular energy insertion on the first restarted branch, with its
conditional normalizer. The two-link calculation is a verification
example for that target: it must reproduce (2.2) and the positive
coefficient (3.3), rather than assert that all first currents vanish.
