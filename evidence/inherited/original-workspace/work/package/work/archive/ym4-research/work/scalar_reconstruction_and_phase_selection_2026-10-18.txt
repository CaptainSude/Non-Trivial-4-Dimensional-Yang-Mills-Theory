SCALAR RECONSTRUCTION, ENERGY BOUNDS, AND NONTRIVIAL PHASE SELECTION
18 October 2026. Working proof and exact scope audit.

CONCLUSION IN ADVANCE. The ordered scalar sector, once the two-frame
rotation/slab arguments are completed, has a route to a genuine scalar
Wightman theory and then to a nontrivial clustering vacuum phase. It does
not require a coincident Euclidean probability law. The phase-selection
step is NOT a generic ergodic-decomposition assertion: it uses a proved
generalized Hamiltonian bound and Driessler--Summers Theorem 2.3. The
operator bridge proving that bound is given below. This remains a scalar
sector construction, not identification of the full Yang--Mills theory.

1. EXACT RECONSTRUCTION INPUT; THE FACTORIAL EXPONENT IS UNRESTRICTED

Primary input: Osterwalder--Schrader, CMP42 (1975), pp284,287--288,
Section II and IV.1--2. Their S^0 consists of Schwartz tests flat on
all full collision diagonals. E0' requires a fixed linear seminorm
order sn and constants of factorial growth, allowing a fixed power
of n!. E0'' is a stronger full-Schwartz/product condition and is not
needed. The proof first constructs tempered Wightman distributions
using E0', Euclidean covariance and RP; symmetry gives locality.
The cluster axiom is the separate vacuum-uniqueness input. See also
their Introduction remark6 on subsets of axioms.

Our bound

 |S_n(F)| <= A^n (n!)^B p_{ceil(Cn)}(F),  F in S^0,       (1)

therefore has the required form. Choose an integer s>C+1 and absorb
norm conventions and A^n into another fixed factorial power. The
large numerical B in os_s0_whitney_extension is harmless. A bound
with finite order separately for each n would not suffice; (1) does.

If rotation_slab_operator_consumer Section7 produces S_new, use that
hierarchy consistently. It agrees with the original physical ordered
sector, is O(4)-invariant and symmetric, obeys (1), and is RP by the
new off-collision kernels and their cutoff limit. It need not equal
the first extracted S^0 distribution on equal-time contact sets.
There is no need to choose full Euclidean contact terms first.

Dropping only clustering in reconstruction gives a cyclic scalar
Hermitian Wightman representation (H,U,Omega,phi) with positive-energy
Poincare covariance and locality, possibly with a degenerate invariant
subspace. Uniqueness of this representation up to the usual cyclic
intertwiner is different from uniqueness of its vacuum vector.

2. THE HILBERT SPACE USED BY THE POINT-INSERTION BOUND

Let H_ord be the closure of strictly ordered Euclidean polynomial
vectors in the source-derived reflection Hilbert space. The bounded
insertion construction gives, for every tau>0, the full-point operator

 A_tau = A_full(tau e_0,tau e_0),
 ||A_tau|| <= C min(1,tau)^(-5).                         (2)

Here the two tau factors are the left and right time paddings. This
is the full four-dimensional point construction of Section7, not
only a transverse-smeared planar bound. Its exact insertion/padding
identities and mixed Gram identities are essential inputs.

The Hilbert space reconstructed from S_new is canonically H_ord.
Indeed its compact off-collision positive-time tests may be cut away
from internal equal-time surfaces in the NEW smooth kernel norm.
Dominated convergence of the corresponding reflected forms proves
Hilbert-norm density of ordered tests. This does not assert their
Schwartz density in the OLD S^0 space. Ordered Gram forms agree by
construction, hence their isometry extends to the completion.

Positive-time semigroups and spatial translations preserve H_ord.
They also preserve its orthogonal complement because the former are
selfadjoint and the latter include their inverses. The insertion
operators preserve H_ord: regularize the new point by a packet, keep
its two positive gaps, and apply it to an ordered vector; the result
is again an ordered vector, and the bounded point limit stays in its
closure. Thus (2) acts on the reconstructed space, rather than on an
unrelated larger contact-sector Hilbert space.

3. FROM THE EUCLIDEAN OPERATOR TO MINKOWSKI SMEARING

This is the bridge that cannot be replaced by a casual invocation of
Wick rotation of an operator norm.

Use U(x)=exp(i x^0 H-i x.P) for real Minkowski translations. Define
the bounded weakly continuous operator-valued function

 B_tau(x)=U(x) A_tau U(x)^*,
 ||B_tau(x)|| <= C min(1,tau)^(-5).                      (3)

For any Schwartz Minkowski test f, its weak operator integral exists:

 B_tau(f)=integral f(x) B_tau(x) dx,
 ||B_tau(f)|| <= C min(1,tau)^(-5) ||f||_L1.             (4)

Claim: on the full Borchers test domain D,

 <u,B_tau(f)v>
   = <exp(-tau H)u, phi(f) exp(-tau H)v>.                (5)

Proof of the identification. Use the full Borchers domain, allowing
Schwartz kernels of all n arguments, not only finite sums of products.
For a vector u defined by such a kernel, give its ordered field word
strictly positive imaginary-time gaps epsilon, and integrate the real
Minkowski arguments against that same kernel. The joint tube/Gram
construction gives vectors u_epsilon. The Wightman boundary-value
construction gives

 ||u_epsilon-u|| -> 0.                                  (6)

More explicitly, each norm and cross norm is a 2n-point boundary
value with positive imaginary gaps; its limit is the same Wightman
Gram form. This proves (6), including finite sums and mixed lengths.
Perform this separately for v.

With those external gaps positive, the exact point-insertion identity
identifies <u_epsilon,B_tau(x)v_epsilon> with the same ordered analytic
kernel having the central insertion separated by tau on both sides.
For real Euclidean external times this is the defining insertion
identity. The two sides then agree for real Minkowski external times
by uniqueness of the joint analytic continuation. This uses a JOINT
tube and its Gram vectors, not separate one-gap analyticity.

Now remove the external epsilon gaps while holding tau fixed. By (3)
and (6) the left side converges uniformly in real x, and therefore
after integration against every L1 test f. The right side converges
as a tempered distribution to the partially heat-damped Wightman
matrix element in (5). This last assertion also follows directly
from the spectral boundary construction: the two retained central
gaps multiply the intermediate-energy variables by exp(-tau E),
while all removed gaps tend to one on the forward spectral support.
The usual smooth extensions of these multipliers off that support
have uniform polynomial derivative bounds, so the stated tempered
boundary limit applies. Consequently (5) follows.

There is no hidden domain assumption in its right side. If h is a
Schwartz function of the energy, h(H)D is contained in D: its inverse
Fourier transform averages common time translations of the full
Schwartz kernel. The resulting convolution is again Schwartz. On
the positive spectrum, exp(-tau E) agrees with a Schwartz function
on the whole real line (choose a smooth bounded extension for E<0).
Thus exp(-tau H)D is contained in D.

Equation(5) is a bounded extension of the two-sided form. It does NOT
yet assert that phi(f)exp(-tau H) is bounded; the following spectral
argument supplies a one-sided bound for the required test class.

Domain convention. Let D_prod denote the usual finite-product
Schwartz-word domain and D_full the domain allowing arbitrary Schwartz
kernels of each word length. Finite sums of tensor products are dense
in every such kernel space by nuclearity of Schwartz space. Wightman
continuity gives simultaneous convergence of the represented vector
and its image under any fixed phi(f): the latter is the same continuity
statement for the kernel with f adjoined. Thus D_prod is graph dense
in D_full for phi(f), and their operator closures coincide. Working on
D_full above does not replace the Wightman field by a larger closure.

4. COMPACT ENERGY TRANSFER GIVES A GENERALIZED H-BOUND

Fix f in Schwartz with compact Fourier support. Choose Lambda with
supp(f_hat) contained in {|p^0|<=Lambda}. Translation covariance of
(3)--(4) implies compact energy transfer for the BOUNDED operator
B_tau(f):

 P_I B_tau(f) P_J=0 if (I-J) intersects [-Lambda,Lambda]
 is empty.                                               (7)

One elementary proof smears exp(itH)B_tau(f)exp(-itH) with a Schwartz
time function whose Fourier transform separates I-J from the support
of f_hat. The operator smearing vanishes by the scalar Fourier
support, while spectral projections express the same smearing by
the energy differences. Approximation by compact spectral intervals
gives (7). No unbounded-operator spectral theorem is used here.

Choose smooth functions h_n, n>=0, with sum h_n(E)=1 for E>=0,
0<=h_n<=1, and supp h_n in [n-1,n+2] (with the evident n=0 change).
Their inverse Fourier transforms are Schwartz. Hence h_n(H)D is
contained in D. By (5) and (7), for v in D,

 phi(f)h_n(H)v
   = exp(tau H) B_tau(f) exp(tau H)h_n(H)v,               (8)

where the right side is well-defined: its intermediate output is
supported in [0,n+2+Lambda]. To verify equality, pair against
exp(-tau H)u, u in D, use (5), and use density of that range. Thus

 ||phi(f)h_n(H)v|| <= C_tau,f exp(2 tau n)||v||.           (9)

The fixed factor exp(tau(4+Lambda)) is included in C_tau,f. In
particular each phi(f)h_n(H) extends to a bounded operator, and its
range lies in the graph closure of the original phi(f): approximate
v by vectors in D and use closability of the Hermitian field.

Take s>2tau. Equations(8)--(9), with the spectral factor exp(-sH),
give the norm-convergent operator series

 C_f = sum_n phi(f)h_n(H)exp(-sH),
 ||C_f|| <= C_tau,f sum_n exp[-(s-2tau)n] < infinity.      (10)

The finite partial inputs sum h_n(H)exp(-sH)v tend to exp(-sH)v,
and their images tend to C_f v. Closedness therefore proves

 exp(-sH)H subset Dom(closure(phi(f))),
 closure(phi(f))exp(-sH)=C_f.                            (11)

This is the required generalized H-bound. It suffices to fix tau=1
and s=3 once; the constants may depend on f and its energy-transfer
width. The energy weight exp(-3E) is continuous and strictly positive
on [0,infinity). Arbitrary f need not satisfy a one-sided bound for
this argument; compact Fourier support is exactly the required class.

4A. THE SAME ARGUMENT IN FACT GIVES A POLYNOMIAL H-BOUND

The preceding fixed-tau argument is already sufficient. Keeping the
tau-dependence in (4) improves it and makes the hypothesis particularly
transparent. Put P_m=1_[m,m+1)(H), m=0,1,.... The same bounded-band
argument, first with smooth spectral cutoffs and then their strong
limits, shows P_m H is contained in Dom(closure(phi(f))) and that
the output lies in the finitely enlarged band. Write phi_bar for
that closure. Equation(7) gives

 P_n phi_bar(f) P_m=0 unless |n-m|<=Lambda+2.

For an allowed pair use (8) at tau=(1+n+m)^(-1). Both exponential
spectral factors are now uniformly bounded, while (4) gives

 ||P_n phi_bar(f)P_m||
   <= C ||f||_L1 (1+n+m)^5.                             (15)

Consequently the block operator

 C_nm=P_n phi_bar(f)P_m (1+H)^(-5)P_m

has norm <=C_Lambda ||f||_L1 and has only a fixed number of nonzero
blocks in each row and column. The elementary block Schur estimate
proves that its matrix defines a bounded operator C with

 ||C|| <= C'_Lambda ||f||_L1.

For clarity, the Schur step uses orthogonal energy bands: for each n,
square the norm of the sum of its at most K_Lambda input blocks,
use Cauchy--Schwarz, sum over n, and count that each input appears at
most K_Lambda times. No summation of infinitely many uniform block
norms is involved. Apply closedness to the finite-energy truncations
(1+H)^(-5) sum_{m<=M}P_m v; their images are C sum_{m<=M}P_m v.
This proves the stronger conclusion

 (1+H)^(-5)H subset Dom(phi_bar(f)),
 ||phi_bar(f)(1+H)^(-5)|| <= C'_Lambda ||f||_L1.          (16)

The smooth-cutoff passage used above is justified in graph norm by
the fixed-tau band estimate (9): approximate a spectral projection
within a slightly larger compact interval and then approximate its
argument by D. The uniform bound and closedness identify the limits.
Thus (16) is a conclusion about the closure of the actual reconstructed
field, not merely an arbitrarily chosen closed extension of its form.

5. A PRECISE PHASE-DECOMPOSITION THEOREM NOW APPLIES

Primary input: Driessler--Summers, Helvetica Physica Acta59 (1986),
331--348, introduction p332 and Theorem2.3 p335. For a scalar Hermitian
Wightman field, allowing nonunique vacuum, their generalized H-bound
condition for compact-Fourier Schwartz smearings suffices for a central
decomposition into Wightman theories with clustering and unique vacua.
The theorem includes the field and Poincare representations and their
vacuum expectation values, not merely a decomposition of an abstract
commutative measure. An alternative hypothesis involving intrinsic
locality is unnecessary here.

All input roles are now explicit: reconstruction gives the tempered
scalar field, positive-energy covariance, locality, cyclic vacuum and
separable Hilbert space; (11) gives the additional regularity. The
separability follows from the countable dense Schwartz test algebra.
Apply that theorem to obtain a direct integral over a measure nu,

 H = integral^direct H_z dnu(z),
 U = integral^direct U_z dnu(z),
 Omega = integral^direct Omega_z dnu(z),                 (12)

and the corresponding disintegration of all field moments. Almost
every resulting field theory has a unique vacuum and clusters. We
do not infer this from positivity or an unqualified Choquet theorem.
We also do not claim that the original mixed hierarchy itself clusters.

6. KEEPING A NONTRIVIAL POSITIVE-ENERGY PHASE

A positive reflected scalar covariance alone is not enough for this
purpose: a spacetime-constant random scalar Z has such covariance,
O(4), RP and excellent factorial growth, but all its extremal phases
are constant fields with no excitations. Centering only against Omega
does not remove a larger zero-energy subspace.

The repaired witness is v_f=[O(f)] with f=partial_t psi, where psi is
smooth, compact, and supported at strictly positive Euclidean time.
The source/Maxwell comparison must prove ||v_f||^2>0 for this signed
profile. Its free reflected norm is positive; this precise extension
of the witness is proved in positive_energy_derivative_witness_2026-10-18.
That note uses the stronger f=partial_t partial_(x1)psi and establishes
v_(f_R)=-iR^2 H P_1 v_(psi_R). Its Sections4--6 have been independently
checked here: the strong mixed derivative/domain argument, dilation
factor, spectral projection conclusion, and phasewise disintegration
are valid. Thus the chosen witness also avoids the zero-P1 subspace.

Positive-time translation of psi differentiates strongly in the
reflection Hilbert space, because the two-point test pair remains
compact and separated. Its derivative identifies v_f with H v_psi,
up to the harmless convention sign. Consequently

 P_{ker H} v_f=0.                                      (13)

Since H>=0, (13) and ||v_f||>0 imply that for some finite rational
energy interval [epsilon,M] with epsilon>0,

 ||1_[epsilon,M](H) v_f||^2 >0.                         (14)

The direct integral (12) decomposes H and its spectral projections.
The witness vector is a limit of the same ordered analytic field
vectors on both sides; hence it too is decomposable. Thus (14) is
the integral of nonnegative phase norms. A set of positive nu-measure
has a nonzero positive-energy vector. Intersect it with the full-
measure set of phases satisfying every conclusion of Theorem2.3 and
choose one phase there. This phase is nontrivial and has a unique
vacuum and clustering. Its scalar field cannot be a constant multiple
of the identity.

No uniform mass gap follows: epsilon in (14) detects SOME spectral
weight, not a lower bound on the whole nonvacuum spectrum. No claim
of uniqueness of the selected phase is made. Because every phase is
translation invariant and integral f=0, phase-dependent constant
one-point values cannot reintroduce a vacuum component into this
witness.

7. WHAT THIS WOULD FINISH, AND THE REMAINING YM IDENTIFICATION

Once the actual two-frame slab/rotation input is complete, (1)--(14)
provide a route from the ordered Wilson F^2 sector to a nontrivial
four-dimensional scalar Wightman phase with a unique vacuum. The
finite-lattice source producer, normalization, and covariance lower
bound remain the construction inputs previously audited; they are
not replaced by these functional-analytic deductions.

This is still insufficient to identify a full Yang--Mills theory.
The missing construction can be stated as TWO concrete extensions:

(YM-local) Construct a compatible JOINT hierarchy, on the same
regulator sequence, for a countable family spanning the gauge-invariant
local curvature differential polynomials at each engineering dimension.
It must include the tensor fields, not just the scalar trace F^2,
their renormalization mixing, joint RP and covariance, and enough
all-order bounds to reconstruct them together on one Hilbert space.
Separate unrelated reconstructions of each observable do not supply
this. The scalar phase selection alone also does not select a common
phase for an unconstructed larger field family.

(YM-match) Prove that this joint renormalization matches the chosen
nonabelian Yang--Mills short-distance theory and its local identities.
The positive one-loop coupling drift and the single Gaussian F^2
witness provide only part of this input. They do not fix the missing
tensor/operator mixing or the higher short-distance relations. A
multi-source finite-prefix construction with the relevant local
counterterms is the specific missing source theorem, rather than
another scalar OS reconstruction theorem.

Jaffe--Witten's problem description Section4 asks for local operators
corresponding to gauge-invariant curvature differential polynomials
and the specified short-distance behavior. The actual current route
also fixes SU(2); extension to other compact simple gauge groups is
another scope enlargement if that formulation is targeted. Mass gap
is a separate spectral assertion and is not obtained by phase selection.

8. WHAT PHASE SELECTION DOES NOT PRESERVE AUTOMATICALLY

The disintegration theorem preserves Wightman axioms and the existence
of a positive-energy witness in some phase. It does NOT turn an
asymptotic identity proved only for the mixed vacuum expectations into
the same identity in every selected phase. Even a scalar mixture
phi_z=a_z phi_free has an averaged two-point normalization determined
by integral a_z^2 dnu(z); that normalization need not equal a_z^2 in
any specified phase. This example illustrates the inference failure,
not a claim that the present theory is such a mixture.

There are two precise ways to obtain a phasewise UV conclusion:

(i) Prove a decomposable bounded operator estimate, for example a
heat-damped short-distance/OPE remainder with norm <=epsilon(scale)
tending to zero. A direct-integral operator norm bound passes to
almost every fiber; a countable determining family and sequence of
scales keeps one full-measure set. The identity leading coefficient
then remains the same identity coefficient in each phase.

(ii) Prove the proposed asymptotic equality after insertion of EVERY
bounded central test Z in the decomposing algebra, with an integrable
uniform error. Then the equality of all central conditional averages
identifies the phasewise coefficient. Mere vacuum averages correspond
only to Z=1 and cannot do this.

Neither strengthening has been established by the current positive
two-packet witness or by the scalar source vacuum estimates. A positive
norm error with summable bounds could also provide an almost-everywhere
subsequence statement, but one must actually construct that nonnegative
error; a signed difference between interacting and free moments is not
such an error. Thus selection of a clustering scalar phase does not
by itself close (YM-match), even within the scalar subsector.
