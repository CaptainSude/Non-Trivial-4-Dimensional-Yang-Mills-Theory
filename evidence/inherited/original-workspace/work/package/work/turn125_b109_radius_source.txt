TURN125 -- A QUANTITATIVE DIRECT-SLOT RADIUS FROM ORIGINAL B109(3.13)
source102, 21 October 2026. Existing TURN125 producers and frozen files unchanged.

RESULT

The supplied original B109 makes the selected substitution explicit.
Its direct field, relative-potential/gradient and independent-current
conditions admit a strong-field radius bounded below by a fixed
constant times their actual available margins. For the half-radius
base pair in B109(3.13), and its actual old-input indices j<=k,
this yields r_direct>=c min(alpha0,alpha1).

This is NOT a lower bound for the full alpha2 of(3.14): the
reconstructed-minimum tests(iv) have not been included. The result
isolates the direct part of that missing radius estimate. All norms
are in one admitted representative and on complete finite stencils.

2. LOCAL CURRENT REMAINDER, WITH THE POWERS OF xi CANCELLED FIRST

For this task the gauge group is SU(2) and its complexification.
Use an operator matrix norm and the fixed finite-dimensional Lie
norm. At complex data every inverse transport is the holomorphic
inverse, not the Hermitian adjoint of a varying complex matrix.

Assume 0<xi<=1 and, on the complete required plaquette/derivative
stencils,

 ||partial U-I||<=a0 xi^2,
 ||U_b||,||U_b^-1||<=exp(xi a1_cap),
 ||A||<=r, ||nabla_U^xi A||<=v, xi r<=r_cap.             (2.1)

The caps are fixed sufficiently small constants. The link bounds
hold in a representative U=exp(i xi A0) Ubar with real unitary
Ubar and ||A0||<=a1_cap. No smallness of Jphys(U) is assumed.

Original gauge paper CMP99(1985),75--102, printed83--85,
(1.43)--(1.54), is supplied as balaban7.pdf. Fresh pixels viewed:
 work/turn125_primary/B99-radius-10.png and -11.png.
In unit physical scale, the remainder in(1.54), after dividing by
xi^2, is bounded by

                       C[a0 r+r v+r^3].                 (2.2)

The mechanism is essential to uniformity in xi, so it is recorded
explicitly rather than taking an absolute derivative of a product:

 (a) The terms involving background plaquette defect in(1.43)
     contain xi^2 a0, and changing the divergence transport inserts
     an additional xi A before the derivative xi^-1. They cost
     C a0 r after the current normalization xi^-2.
 (b) The quadratic plaquette term V2 is rewritten by(1.49)--(1.50)
     into the leading commutator [A_mu,A_nu] and terms with explicit
     xi factors. The covariant discrete product rule(1.52) gives
     C r v, including shifted gradients. In terms such as xi v^2,
     the elementary bound xi v<=C r on a fixed stencil reduces
     this to the same C r v. One must perform this algebra before
     taking suprema.
 (c) The remaining third-order plaquette product is O((xi r)^3).
     The divergence contributes xi^-1 and current normalization
     contributes xi^-2, leaving C r^3. Higher orders are dominated
     by the same expression on the fixed cap.

These are finite analytic matrix identities. The equality(1.49)
explicitly holds for complex Lie-algebra A. For complex U, the
same product/transport identities hold with inverse transports;
each finite path changes constants by at most exp(C xi a1_cap).
Thus (2.2) holds for the holomorphic extension with a different
fixed C. This is an estimate of the algebraic terms, not an
identity-theorem extension of an estimate known only for real A.

PROJECTED CURRENT. For SU(2), extend Im holomorphically by
Im_alg P=(P-P^-1)/(2i) on SL(2,C). Cayley-Hamilton gives
Im_alg P=pi0(P)/i, where pi0 removes the trace. Conjugation and
the algebraic covariant divergence commute with pi0. Applying
pi0/i to the raw matrix expansion therefore gives precisely the
projected current expansion B109(3.11), with the same bound up to
a fixed projection constant. In particular no extra derivative or
inverse xi appears from taking the actual Lie-algebra current.

Finally the exact covariant Hodge identity is

 c_U^*c_U+d_U R d_U^*=Delta_U^xi-P1+E_curv(U),          (2.3)

where c is the bond-to-plaquette derivative and d the scalar gauge
derivative. Each coefficient of E_curv is a finite transported
plaquette commutator divided by xi^2. Therefore

                    ||E_curv(U)A||<=C a0 r.             (2.4)

On the physical Landau family the gauge term vanishes before
introducing the selected continuation. The continuation then uses
Delta-P1 for arbitrary strong A, exactly as in(1.1). Combining
(2.2),(2.4), its F1 has the uniform bound

                 ||F1(U,A)||<=C[a0 r+r v+r^3].           (2.5)

For r from(1.3), v<=r, so

 ||J_sel-theta^3 J||
    <=2r+C(a0 r+r^2+r^3)<=C_J r,                       (2.6)

for fixed upper caps on a0,r. C_J is independent of xi, j, volume
and the number of old dates. Equation(2.6) is a statement about
the SELECTED independent-current slot. It does not identify it
with the physical current for arbitrary non-Landau A.

3. DIRECT FIELD AND RELATIVE-POTENTIAL SLOTS

In the same representative let

 U=exp(i xi A0)Ubar,
 max{||A0||,||nabla_Ubar^xi A0||}<=b1,

with real Ubar satisfying its original curvature and gauge-cube
tests. The product with exp(i xi A) keeps Ubar unchanged. The new
relative potential is the principal BCH expression

 A0_new=(i xi)^-1 log[exp(i xi A)exp(i xi A0)].           (3.1)

On fixed small caps it satisfies

 ||A0_new-A0||+||nabla_Ubar^xi(A0_new-A0)||<=C_B r.      (3.2)

Here is the uniform-spacing justification. Write the difference
as Psi_xi(A,A0), which is analytic in the two matrices, covariant
under conjugation and vanishes at A=0. Its derivative in A is
bounded uniformly, and its derivative in A0 is O(xi||A||), by the
convergent two-variable BCH series. Applying the covariant discrete
difference and telescoping these two arguments costs
C||nabla_Ubar A||+C xi r||nabla_Ubar A0||. Also
nabla_Ubar A-nabla_U A is bounded by C b1 r, since the difference
of their transports is O(xi b1) before division by xi. These bounds
prove(3.2) without a xi^-1 loss. All paths and first-difference
stencils are part of the stated read.

The plaquette product expansion, equivalently the first-order part
of the same B99(1.21)--(1.22),(1.47), gives

 xi^-2 ||partial(exp(i xi A)U)-partial U||
                    <=C(r+r^2+a0 xi r)<=C_K r.           (3.3)

The linear oriented plaquette sum is xi^2 c_U A, with finite
background-curvature corrections; its quadratic products have
factor xi^2. Thus (3.3) also follows before applying any absolute
xi^-1 derivative. The unchanged Ubar continues to meet its real
gauge-cube tests. Consequently (3.2)--(3.3) control the direct
parts(i)--(iii) of the target domain in the original representative.
No norm-preserving arbitrary complex gauge transformation is used.

4. AN EXPLICIT MARGIN THEOREM

Let the target direct allowances be kappa_target for normalized
complex curvature, a1_target for relative potential and its first
gradient, and j_target for the independent current. Suppose the
unchanged real Ubar already meets the target real-field conditions.
Define the positive margins

 m_K=kappa_target-xi^-2||partial U-I||,
 m_B=a1_target-max{||A0||,||nabla_Ubar A0||},
 m_J=j_target-theta^3||J||.                              (4.1)

With the fixed constants from(2.6),(3.2),(3.3), it suffices that

 r<=min{r_cap, m_K/(2C_K), m_B/(2C_B), m_J/(2C_J)}.      (4.2)

The factor2 keeps strict positive margins. All expressions are
holomorphic on that strong ball and the displayed bounds hold
uniformly on it. This theorem only concerns the direct slots.

For the actual homogeneous base of(3.13), its curvature and
relative-potential bounds are alpha0/2 and alpha1/2. By(1.4),
the input current ||J||<=alpha0 gives

              m_J>=(1-L^-3)alpha0.

Therefore one may choose, for fixed upper native caps,

                   r_direct=c min(alpha0,alpha1),       (4.3)

where c>0 depends only on the fixed local conventions and L, not
on xi,j,X or volume. This is the requested quantitative direct
strong-field radius. The inverse of this radius has only the
explicit inverse-margin cost; there is no hidden fine-mesh power.

If selected running radii in one matched homogeneous top patch are
alpha_nu=C_nu g_i ell_i^Q and r=C_in g_i ell_i^p0, then the direct
image comparison costs C ell_i^(p0-Q), after the actual date and
dilation are matched. The strict Q>p0 hierarchy pays that ratio.
This does not by itself place a mixed old scalar in its full domain.

5. WHAT THE DIRECT THEOREM DOES NOT CLAIM

The all-n reconstructed tests(iv) in B109(1.15)--(1.16) and the
mixed B119 counterparts involve actual regional minima and their
currents, with insets or copied collars appropriate to each paper.
They are not among(4.1). Thus (4.3) must NOT be relabeled
alpha2>=c min(alpha0,alpha1) for the FULL old scalar.

B109(1.17) provides a separate sufficient direct-to-reconstructed
statement on more strongly shrunken homogeneous domains. Applying
it to the same old scalar requires its exact constants, complete
gauge-cube/read hypotheses and actual reconstructed family; it is
not a consequence of the current estimate above. In particular the
half-radius base in(3.13) is not automatically small enough for that
separate sufficient criterion merely because a fixed B3 exists.

The new estimate does not assert absolute root-summed localization,
an arbitrary mixed-history radius, old-A holomorphy, or control of
all inherited E/R/B contributions. It supplies the local direct
radius in the reduced proof obligation of the earlier TURN125
registered-current and substitution notes. Their exact source
normalization and complete mixed-source bookkeeping are unchanged.
