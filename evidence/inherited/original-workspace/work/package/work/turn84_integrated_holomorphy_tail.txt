TURN84 -- GAUSSIAN ELIMINATION, OLD SMOOTH CUTS AND MARKED HOLOMORPHY
20 October 2026. tail47. Independent pressure test of the raw-entry target.

RESULT

An analytic source-free integrated branch, even as an operator on EVERY
remaining new-record test, need not have analytic integrated coefficients
for an ORIGINAL analytic action insertion whose variable was eliminated
earlier. Sections1--3 give an exact positive finite-dimensional example:
Gaussian regular elimination, a fixed smooth partition, an exact coarse
constraint, and a normalized analytic fresh reference all appear. The
source parameter itself has a common analytic disc; the failure concerns
the restarted EXTERNAL field parameter. The full sourced sum over both
complementary branches remains analytic. No source is changed or retuned.

There is also a fully nondegenerate Gaussian variant. Every marked
coefficient is entire at each positive smoothing width, but no bound
uniform in that width exists on any fixed complex neighborhood. Thus
Gaussian integration alone does not supply the uniform native source norm.

This is NOT an exhibited Bałaban history. The freshly transformed cut in
Large-I(1.59) has a stronger fixed-slot property that rules out the example
at that primitive. The unresolved question is preservation of that property
for an ORIGINAL marked read across a stopped/restarted block. Section5
states the exact native implications and the narrow check that is needed.

1. AN EXACT POSITIVE GAUSSIAN MODEL WITH AN ORIGINAL ACTION MARK

Let X,Y be independent standard real Gaussian variables, with density
phi(x)phi(y). The original quadratic action is(X^2+Y^2)/2. The marked
local action is

                     F(X)=X^2/2.

Put
 b(x)=exp(-x^2)-1/sqrt(3),
 chi(y)=0 for y<=0, and chi(y)=exp(-1/y^2) for y>0.

The function chi is C-infinity, lies in[0,1], and is flat but not real
analytic at0. The exact mean of b under phi is zero. For0<epsilon<=1/4,
define the fixed positive partition

 p_+(x,y)=1/2+epsilon chi(y)b(x),
 p_-(x,y)=1-p_+(x,y).                                  (1.1)

Because |b|<1, both weights lie strictly between1/4 and3/4. They sum to1
pointwise. Equivalently introduce one independent uniform seed and accept
the plus branch iff the seed is at most p_+(X,Y). This is a literal positive
partition of the original Gaussian law, not a signed correction. The cuts
are independent of the source parameter. They need not have the precise
shape of a native ball cutoff; the example tests an implication about
positive smooth partitions and Gaussian integration, not the exact native
cut geometry.

Take the coarse variable to be Y, impose the exact coarse constraint
Y=z, and perform the regular Gaussian elimination of X. Its conditional
law is still phi(x)dx. The common exterior density phi(z) may be retained;
it is nonzero and entire and does not affect the following failure. Define

 A_+(z,s)=int phi(x) p_+(x,z) exp(s F(x)) dx,
 A_-(z,s)=int phi(x) p_-(x,z) exp(s F(x)) dx.             (1.2)

These integrals are defined for real z and Re s<1. Direct Gaussian
integration gives, with the branch of the square root positive at s=0,

 A_+(z,s)=1/2(1-s)^(-1/2)
  +epsilon chi(z)[(3-s)^(-1/2)
                      -(1/sqrt(3))(1-s)^(-1/2)],         (1.3)
 A_-(z,s)=1/2(1-s)^(-1/2)
  -epsilon chi(z)[(3-s)^(-1/2)
                      -(1/sqrt(3))(1-s)^(-1/2)].         (1.4)

In particular,

 A_+(z,0)=A_-(z,0)=1/2,                                 (1.5)
 A_+(z,s)+A_-(z,s)=(1-s)^(-1/2).                         (1.6)

Thus both unmarked branch masses are constant and the complete original
source partition is analytic. Its normalization at s=0 is exactly1.
The source family is uniformly analytic in s on every fixed disc|s|<1,
even before adding the complementary branch. The issue is not a shrinking
source radius, an uncontrolled original moment, or a missing denominator.

2. FIRST AND MIXED ORIGINAL JETS ARE NONANALYTIC

The elementary Gaussian moments are

 E b=0,
 E F=1/2,              E F^2=3/4,
 E[F b]=-1/(3sqrt(3)), E[F^2 b]=-2/(3sqrt(3)).             (2.1)

The raw plus-branch coefficients, using two independently declared source
variables s,t for the same original action if desired, are therefore

 A00=1/2,
 A10=A01=1/4-epsilon chi(z)/(3sqrt(3)),
 A11=3/8-2epsilon chi(z)/(3sqrt(3)).                      (2.2)

Here A11 is the coefficient of st in A_+(z,s+t), namely the integral
of the ORIGINAL product F^2. It is not A10 A01/A00.

All derivatives of the chi correction at z=0 vanish, yet the correction
is nonzero for every z>0. Consequently A10 and A11 have no holomorphic
extension to any complex neighborhood of0 agreeing with their real values.
Identity of the holomorphic Taylor series with its value on a neighborhood
would otherwise make the correction vanish there.

Normalizing the branch does not cure this. Its original-action mean and
variance are exactly

 E_+F=1/2-2epsilon chi(z)/(3sqrt(3)),
 Var_+(F)=1/2-2epsilon chi(z)/(3sqrt(3))
                         -4epsilon^2 chi(z)^2/27.        (2.3)

Likewise centering the original action by its unconditional Gaussian mean
leaves A_+(F-1/2)=-epsilon chi(z)/(3sqrt(3)). There is no discarded source
vacuum that would restore analyticity: the troublesome scalar coefficient
depends on the restarted external field z.

One can also eliminate any concern that unbounded X^2 causes the failure.
Let F_b(x)=1-exp(-x^2), and replace the original X density by the normalized
Gaussian times exp[-lambda F_b(x)], with fixed lambda>0, as small as desired.
This is a Gaussian regular integral with a bounded analytic interaction;
F_b is a literal action-coupling insertion. Set
 b_lambda=exp(-x^2)-E_lambda exp(-X^2)
and use(1.1) with b_lambda. Again A00=1/2, while

 A10(z)=1/2 E_lambda F_b
                    -epsilon chi(z) Var_lambda(F_b).   (2.4)

The variance is strictly positive. This coefficient is nonanalytic,
although F_b lies in[0,1] and its source generating function is ENTIRE,
with bound exp(|s|). Multiplying F_b by its fixed calibration lambda does
not change the conclusion. The explicit quadratic formulas(1.3)--(2.3)
already suffice for the main counterexample.

3. NORMALIZED ANALYTIC REPLACEMENT DOES NOT RESTORE THE OLD JET

After X has been eliminated, introduce a FRESH variable V with normalized
Gaussian reference q_z(dv)=phi(v-z)dv. This is independent of the source.
For bounded Borel tests G of the remaining new record define

 (T_+^0(z)G)=A00(z) int q_z(dv)G(v)
                         =1/2 int phi(v-z)G(v)dv.        (3.1)

As an operator on ALL bounded new-record tests, this is holomorphic in z.
For complex z=a+ib its variation bound is

 |T_+^0(z)G|<=1/2 exp(b^2/2)||G||_infinity.               (3.2)

The Gaussian contour shift has exactly mass1. The bound follows directly
from |phi(v-z)|=exp(b^2/2)phi(v-a); no tiny branch normalization is used.

Keep the passive original source during the SAME frozen replacement.
Its first marked operator is

 (T_+^F(z)G)=A10(z) int q_z(dv)G(v).                     (3.3)

Taking G=1 proves that it is nonholomorphic. Similarly the exact mixed
operator uses A11, with the same q_z and exactly one old mass. Thus even
operator-valued holomorphy and uniform variation control for EVERY NEW
test do not imply the old marked estimate. X is no longer a literal
integration variable of(3.1). If X were retained, the joint density
phi(x)p_+(x,z) would visibly fail the hypothesized operator holomorphy
against all OLD X tests; the example does not contradict that stronger
statement.

Summing the plus and minus histories with the SAME source and the SAME
future factor restores(1.6), including every mixed coefficient. This is
an exact marked partition-of-unity cancellation. It would be invalid
to use that identity if the future factors differ between the branches,
or only one branch belongs to the fixed output catalogue being bounded.
Source-free cancellation of E b=0 is weaker and does not suffice.

4. NONDEGENERATE GAUSSIAN ELIMINATION: ANALYTICITY WITHOUT A UNIFORM NORM

Instead of the exact coarse constraint, keep a real Gaussian variable
Y=z+sigma Z with Z standard and sigma>0. The old smooth cut remains a
fixed function of the REAL integrated Y. Let

 c_sigma(z)=int phi_sigma(y-z) chi(y)dy.                  (4.1)

For each sigma>0 this is entire in z. The proof uses the fixed real
integral and the absolute Gaussian bound, not an evaluation chi(z).
On compact complex z sets the kernel and all its derivatives are
dominated by an integrable Gaussian; chi is merely bounded.
The example's marked formulas hold with chi replaced by c_sigma,
while A00=1/2 is unchanged. In particular all integrations can be
nondegenerate Gaussian and every finite-regulator marked coefficient
can be analytic.

For real z, approximate-identity convergence gives c_sigma(z)->chi(z).
Suppose these functions were uniformly bounded on ANY fixed complex
disc about0. Montel would give a locally uniformly convergent holomorphic
subsequence. Its real values would equal chi. It would vanish on the
negative real interval, hence everywhere on that disc by the identity
theorem, contradicting chi(z)>0 on its positive real interval.
Therefore there is no such uniform complex bound. The same conclusion
holds for A10 by(2.2). Yet the unmarked replacement operator(3.1)--(3.2)
is independent of sigma and has its uniform bound unchanged.

The elementary estimate |c_sigma(a+ib)|<=exp[b^2/(2sigma^2)] displays
a possible upper-bound price. This is not asserted to be the exact
growth. Shrinking the continued domain proportionally to sigma may
restore a bound; that is an EXTRA radius/smoothing comparison. It does
not follow from the source-free marginal. Native constraints remove
Gaussian directions altogether, so smoothing in every old cut direction
must not be assumed from the presence of some fluctuation Gaussian.

6. RARE-TAIL SWITCHES AND COALESCENCE ON THE CORRECT TEST ALGEBRA

The failure can be confined to a Gaussian large-field tail, while keeping
the exact source-free sibling aggregation and a residual positive energy
reserve. This tests a switch that vanishes on an inner ball; no reversal
of an inner/outer native cutoff convention is used.

Choose a smooth function a_R(x) in[0,1], zero for|x|<=R and one for
|x|>=2R, with R>0. Put q_R=E a_R(X)>0 and let E_R be expectation for
the normalized positive density a_R(x)phi(x)/q_R. Define

 b_R(x)=exp(-x^2)-E_R exp(-X^2),
 p_+(x,y)=a_R(x)[1/2+epsilon chi(y)b_R(x)],
 p_-(x,y)=a_R(x)[1/2-epsilon chi(y)b_R(x)],
 p_0(x,y)=1-a_R(x).                                    (6.1)

These are nonnegative and sum to1. Every chi-dependent modification is
supported in the old large-field tail. The sibling masses at Y=z are
q_R/2, whereas their original action first jets are

 A10_+(z)=q_R E_R F/2
          +epsilon q_R chi(z) Cov_R(F,exp(-X^2)),        (6.2)
 A10_-(z)=q_R E_R F/2
          -epsilon q_R chi(z) Cov_R(F,exp(-X^2)).

The covariance is strictly negative: with independent X,X' under E_R,
it is half the mean of
 [F(X)-F(X')][exp(-X^2)-exp(-X'^2)],
which is nonpositive and strictly negative on a set of positive measure.
Thus the nonanalyticity is still present for every finite R.

Nevertheless every fixed raw action moment is exponentially small.
For integer k>=0, with(-1)!!=1, one use of the quadratic reserve gives

 int phi(x) a_R(x) F(x)^k dx
 <= exp(-R^2/4) E[exp(X^2/4)(X^2/2)^k]
 =sqrt(2)(2k-1)!! exp(-R^2/4).                           (6.3)

Each p_+ or p_- is at most a_R, so(6.3) bounds its one/two raw jets
before normalization. It also bounds any such real positive operator
with a bounded remaining test, multiplied by that test's supremum.
No division by q_R, repeated primitive-credit extraction, or restoration
of the spent exponent occurs. This directly separates real raw smallness
from exact background holomorphy. The example does NOT claim the exact
shape, geometry or exponent of a native large-field family.

Here is the precise coalescence requirement. On a COMMON old record x,
let p_l(x,z) be sibling weights with sum_l p_l=a(x), and let Q_l^z be
their FULL future/reference kernels, pulled back to a common remaining-
test algebra A. If for every G in A the value Q_l^z G(x) is independent
of l, then for every same original source product O(x,z),

 sum_l int mu_z(dx) p_l(x,z) O(x,z) Q_l^zG(x)
                =int mu_z(dx) a(x) O(x,z) Q^zG(x).       (6.4)

This is a pointwise partition identity before integration. It applies
to1,F,H,FH simultaneously and retains the old mass once. Equality of
the Q_l on A is sufficient, not claimed necessary; a more specialized
weighted cancellation can also suffice if it is actually proved. The
relevant A must include the future outside/private tests in the desired
operator estimate, not just the constant function1. Gauge-equivalent
kernels could agree on an invariant A without agreeing on every test,
but that equality also has to be established on the actual reads.

Normalization alone only gives Q_l1=1. For an explicit failure take
the two siblings(6.1) and let their fresh normalized kernels be
q_+=N(1,1), q_-=N(-1,1), independent of x,z. For ANY remaining test G,
their coalesced UNMARKED operator is

             q_R [q_+G+q_-G]/2,                         (6.5)

which is constant and analytic in z. The coalesced OLD ACTION operator is

 q_R E_R F [q_+G+q_-G]/2
 +epsilon q_R chi(z) Cov_R(F,exp(-X^2))[q_+G-q_-G].       (6.6)

For G=1 the last term vanishes. For the bounded analytic test G(v)=sin v,

                   q_+G-q_-G=2 exp(-1/2) sin(1) !=0,    (6.7)

so the operator in(6.6) is nonholomorphic. Adding the central branch
p_0 with any fixed analytic future kernel does not cancel this term.
Every fresh q is positive, normalized and analytic. Thus even summing
all sibling OLD masses with their exact source can prove too little
when a future factor distinguishes their fresh records. The full
original sourced total mass, tested by1, remains correct.

Conversely, when q_+=q_- on the required algebra, the chi term cancels
exactly and(6.3) leaves the complete rare-tail source bound after summing.
This is the useful positive version of the test: aggregating a genuinely
common raw continuation can remove a nonanalytic partition WITHOUT
requiring the rare event to have probability close to1 or dividing by
its tiny mass. Native application must identify that common continuation,
the actual source/read coordinate alignment and the required test algebra.
