JOINT BAD-TILE AND CLUSTER BOUNDS IN THE ACTUAL GROUND LAW -- TURN67
20 October 2026. gauge47. Submitted for independent audit.

This note extends the proved local action MGF to simultaneous bad
regions. No independence of regions is assumed. The law is the actual
finite-spatial Hamiltonian ground law, not a native RG reference law.

1. MODEL, BOARD, AND INPUTS

Let mu=Omega^2 dU be the normalized positive ground law of

 H=g^2 K/(2a)+4S/(g^2 a),  S=sum_(spatial p)(1-Tr U_p/2),

on a nondegenerate periodic three-dimensional SU2 spatial lattice.
Retain the even-period assumption N_i even in every direction,
including any direction tiled by a single block. Its periods N_i
and tile lengths l_i>=2 satisfy

 M_i=N_i/l_i=2^k_i,  k_i nonnegative integers.

Let n=N_1N_2N_3, V=l_1l_2l_3, and K_board=M_1M_2M_3=n/V.
Tile labels form the periodic board product_i Z/(M_i Z).
The closed tile B_b has vertices in product_i[b_i l_i,(b_i+1)l_i],
with the natural periodic interpretation. Its strict interior excludes
the boundary planes. Plaquette sets below are sets, not multisets.

From hamiltonian_marked_derivative_turn67.txt we use two proved
facts for THIS same normalized ground law:

 log E_mu exp(tS/g^2)<=(9n/2) artanh(t/2),  0<=t<2,     (1.1)

and spatial reflection positivity in every integer-coordinate site
plane. The RP proof conditions on the two fixed planes' links in
the finite anisotropic Wilson law, factors the two reflected halves,
then passes first to its temporal ground state and next to the
controlled fixed-spatial temporal limit. It applies to functions
of a CLOSED half, including its planar boundary links.

No spatial reflection positivity of a nonlinear flowed measure is
an input. The present estimates concern the unflowed ground law.

2. MULTIFUNCTION CHESSBOARD FROM REPEATED RP CAUCHY--SCHWARZ

We give the full argument, because the one-function inequality alone
does not imply a joint bad-event estimate.

For a function f on the base tile B_0 define its canonical reflected
copy theta_b f as follows. In coordinate i, place the copy by

 x_i -> b_i l_i+x_i                 if b_i is even,
 x_i -> b_i l_i+(l_i-x_i)           if b_i is odd.

The induced pullback on links includes the correct link inversion
when an oriented edge is reversed. Since M_i is even unless it is1,
the convention is consistent on the periodic board. Dimensions with
M_i=1 need no reflections or dissemination in that direction.

For a site reflection in x_j=c l_j, the tile index changes to

                  b'_j=2c-1-b_j modulo M_j.

Its parity flips. Hence the reflection maps theta_b f EXACTLY to
theta_b' f, with all other indices unchanged. This identity, rather
than a mere translation of a possibly nonsymmetric f, is used below.

Take a finite alphabet of nonnegative bounded continuous base-tile
functions f_1,...,f_q. Initially replace each by f_l+epsilon,
epsilon>0, so all normalization factors are strictly positive:

 z_l=[E_mu product_(b on board)theta_b(f_l+epsilon)]^(1/K_board).

Set g_l=(f_l+epsilon)/z_l. A homogeneous labeling by l then has
expectation exactly1. For an arbitrary labeling sigma of all board
sites with alphabet{1,...,q}, put

             Z(sigma)=E_mu product_b theta_b g_(sigma(b)).

There are finitely many labelings. Choose one maximizing Z, and
write its maximum M. We know M>=1 from a homogeneous labeling.

Cut the board into the two closed halves of any permitted site
reflection. Write the mixed product as F_+ theta(F_-), where F_+
and F_- read one closed half after the reflected identification.
Shared planar links cause no problem: each factor is assigned to
the half containing its tile, and both half functions may read
those same boundary links. The RP bilinear form gives

 Z(sigma)^2 <= Z(sigma_+) Z(sigma_-).                    (2.1)

Here sigma_+ is obtained by copying the positive half's labeling
into the other half by reflection, and sigma_- copies the negative
half. Both are among the labelings over which M was maximized.
At a maximizing sigma, (2.1) and Z(sigma_+),Z(sigma_-)<=M imply

                       Z(sigma_+)=Z(sigma_-)=M.         (2.2)

We now turn one maximizing pattern into a homogeneous one, without
assuming it was homogeneous initially. Start with the label l at
tile0. There is a monochromatic rectangle of side1 in every board
direction. Suppose its length in direction j is m<=M_j/2, beginning
at coordinate0. Reflect in the right boundary x_j=m l_j and retain
the half containing that rectangle. The rectangle fits in that
half, because m<=M_j/2. Its old and reflected copies give a
monochromatic rectangle of length2m in direction j; all its other
lengths remain unchanged. By(2.2) the new pattern still maximizes Z.

Repeat until direction j is full, then perform the same doubling
in the remaining directions. Dyadic M_j ensure that every step
has the indicated half-geometry. A dimension of size1 is skipped.
The result is a homogeneous labeling by l and still has value M.
Its value is1 by normalization. Thus M=1 and every pattern has
Z(sigma)<=1.

Undoing the normalization and then taking epsilon down to0
(bounded convergence on this finite board) proves

 E_mu product_b theta_b f_(sigma(b))
  <= product_b [E_mu product_c theta_c f_(sigma(b))]^(1/K_board).
                                                               (2.3)

Zero disseminated expectations are handled by the same limiting
argument; no division by them is performed. Unoccupied sites are
assigned the alphabet function1. Formula(2.3) is the required
multipoint chessboard bound.

The normalized-maximizer proof was independently derived and
checked with history47. Standard primary context: Biskup,
Reflection Positivity and Phase Transitions in Lattice Spin Models,
Theorem5.8 and proof, printed pp44--46:
 https://arxiv.org/pdf/math-ph/0610025 .
Our dyadic rectangle construction above states the actual geometry
and is sufficient for the present gauge-field application.

3. STRICT-INTERIOR JOINT MOMENTS AND BAD-TILE PROBABILITIES

Let S_b^int sum h_p over plaquettes whose every vertex is strictly
inside B_b. These copies are disjoint and

                      sum_b S_b^int<=S.                (3.1)

All plaquette orientations are included, so the reflected copy
theta_b S_0^int is exactly S_b^int.

For arbitrary numbers0<=t_b<2, use(2.3) with the finite alphabet
of functions exp(t_b S_0^int/g^2), including1 where t_b=0.
By(3.1) and(1.1), each disseminated expectation is at most
exp[(9n/2)artanh(t_b/2)]. Consequently

 log E_mu exp[sum_b t_b S_b^int/g^2]
              <=(9V/2) sum_b artanh(t_b/2).             (3.2)

In particular define the bad event

                   A_b={S_b^int>=g^2 L^2}.

For every prescribed set A of DISTINCT board sites, exponential
Markov with t_b=1 on A and0 outside gives

 mu(intersection_(b in A) A_b)<=q_int^|A|,

             q_int=min{1,3^(9V/4) exp(-L^2)}.           (3.3)

This is the proposed joint rare-tile estimate with the complete
original ground-state normalizer. It is not a product-law claim.
Distinct thresholds L_b may be used, with the corresponding
product of the unclipped factors, or all clipped factors via
omitting the factors above1 and using monotonicity of events.

Strict-interior tests do not inspect plaquettes on tile boundaries.
No complete large-curvature field decomposition is inferred solely
from(3.3). Section4 supplies a precise variant that includes them.

4. COMPLETE CLOSED TILES: A BOUNDED-OVERLAP VARIANT

Let S_b^cl sum h_p over all plaquettes whose every vertex lies
in the CLOSED tile B_b. Every spatial plaquette belongs to at
least one such tile. It belongs to at most two: it has extent1
in each of its two tangent directions, so its containing tile in
each such direction is unique; only its third, constant coordinate
can lie in a boundary plane shared by two tiles. Periodic duplicates
are collapsed because plaquette lists are sets. Therefore

                       S<=sum_b S_b^cl<=2S.            (4.1)

The shared boundary reads are allowed in the closed-half RP
argument of Section2. We do not assert disjointness or independence
for these functions. Apply(2.3) to exp[t_b S_0^cl/(2g^2)] and use
the upper bound in(4.1):

 log E_mu exp[sum_b t_b S_b^cl/(2g^2)]
              <=(9V/2) sum_b artanh(t_b/2), 0<=t_b<2.  (4.2)

Thus for A_b^cl={S_b^cl>=g^2 L^2},

 mu(intersection_(b in A) A_b^cl)<=q_cl^|A|,

             q_cl=min{1,3^(9V/4) exp(-L^2/2)}.          (4.3)

This variant tests every plaquette, paying the explicit factor2
in the threshold exponent. It does not turn small plaquette
curvature into a globally weak connection or determine a gauge.
It also does not imply a lower bound on a physical fluctuation.

5. CONNECTED BAD-CLUSTER TAILS: NO INDEPENDENCE NEEDED

Either choose the strict-interior events with q=q_int, or the
closed-tile events with q=q_cl. Let G_board be any specified
simple adjacency graph on the board, with maximum degree D>=1.
Examples are nearest-face neighbors, D<=6, or touching tiles,
D<=26. Dimensions of size1 or2 can only reduce these degrees;
self-loops and repeated neighbors are removed.

For a vertex b let C_b be its bad connected component, with
C_b empty when b is not bad. The number of connected vertex sets
of size k containing b is at most

                             D^(2(k-1)).               (5.1)

To verify(5.1), impose a fixed ordering of neighbors, choose a
canonical spanning tree of each such set, and perform its
depth-first traversal from b. This gives a walk of length2(k-1)
which visits exactly that set. Different sets have different
visited sets and thus different walks. There are at most
D^(2(k-1)) such walks. For k=1 the bound is1.

If |C_b|>=k, it contains a connected set of exactly k vertices
including b: grow a spanning tree until k vertices have been
selected. Joint bound(3.3) or(4.3) and the union bound give

             mu(|C_b|>=k)<=q (D^2 q)^(k-1).            (5.2)

There is no sum over larger cluster sizes and no conditional
independence statement. The estimate holds for1<=k<=K_board;
for larger k the probability is zero.

For D^2 q<1 it follows, in particular, that

 E_mu |C_b|<=q/(1-D^2 q),

 E_mu exp(t|C_b|)
  <=1+(exp(t)-1)q/[1-exp(t)D^2 q],
                    0<=t<log[1/(D^2 q)],              (5.3)

and

 mu(some bad component has size>=k)
                  <=K_board q(D^2 q)^(k-1).            (5.4)

The graph-radius bound from b follows by replacing k with r+1.
Formula(5.4), rather than a rooted probability without a volume
factor, is what controls existence somewhere on the whole torus.

There is also a simple all-bad-count bound. If N_A is the number
of bad sites in any prescribed finite board subset A, expansion
of product_b[1+(exp(t)-1)1_(A_b)] and the joint estimate give

            E_mu exp(t N_A)<=[1+q(exp(t)-1)]^|A|, t>=0. (5.5)

This is an upper Laplace comparison, not stochastic domination
of the full bad-site process and not an assertion of independence.

6. PHYSICAL SCALE AND EXACT LIMITS OF THIS RESULT

For fixed tile side lengths, V is fixed. Choosing L large enough
that D^2 q<1 gives exponentially decaying component-size tails
uniformly in the ambient torus and in g. Choosing
L(g)=log(1/g) as g->0 makes q super-polynomially small in g
at fixed V. The finite-board estimates remain the precise claims.

For an illustrative fixed-physical-volume regulator sequence with
K_board<=C a^-3 and D^2 q<=exp(-c0)<1 uniformly, take
k_a=ceil[(4/c0)log(1/a)]+1. Equation(5.4) gives an upper bound
of order a on the existence of any bad component of size k_a.
On its complement every connected component of CLOSED bad tiles
has physical diameter in the spatial torus metric at most
C l_max a k_a, which tends to0.
Face or touching adjacency is kept as specified. If one wants
to connect all geometrically touching bad regions, touching
adjacency, with D=26, is the appropriate version.

For a concrete logarithmic weak-coupling sequence
g_a^2 comparable to1/log(1/a), one may take L(g_a)=log(1/g_a).
Then q_cl tends to0, the uniform c0 condition eventually holds,
and g_a^2 L(g_a)^2 tends to0. Thus all plaquettes outside the
closed bad tiles obey a vanishing unrenormalized curvature
threshold, while the bad-tile components have the preceding
small-diameter high-probability bound. This still gives no
control on the dynamical influence of those components.

This conclusion concerns the actual unflowed Hamiltonian ground
field at that fixed tile resolution. Many small bad components
may still occur, and their effect on nonlinear spatial flow is
not bounded by their diameter alone. The thresholds themselves
are g^2 L^2 and are not a physically renormalized curvature scale.

The events are not Bałaban history labels. They do not include his
derived minimizer, auxiliary-field, reconstruction, gauge-choice,
or age/maturity predicates. They provide neither native history
admission nor an all-history physical transport theorem.

Similarly, RP of mu supplies no multipoint estimate for the
nonlinear flowed pushforward, and these unmarked probabilities
do not bound derivative-weighted bad events. Such a weight can
favor a rare component. The marked entropy/transport issue in
hamiltonian_marked_derivative_turn67.txt remains.

The new result is genuine joint full-ground-law local
concentration and its connected-cluster consequences. It does
not prove the nonlinear physical observable derivative target,
its variance floor, or a nontrivial continuum YM4 existence theorem.
