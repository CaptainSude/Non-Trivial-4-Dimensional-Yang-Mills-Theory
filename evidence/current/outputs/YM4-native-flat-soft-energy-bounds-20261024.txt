RESULT AND SCOPE

This supplies the flat operator item(H1) and the elementary flat heat
bound from native_affine_multiplier_and_heat_operator_interface_20261024.
It uses the actual flat ordinary/corrected averages, the newly registered
uniform classical quadratic kernels and the native Wilson lower bound.
It does not identify the full native determinant with a different archive
formula, bound nonzero-source derivatives, prove coarse Schur gaps, or
compute a signed logarithmic coefficient.

Work first on the infinite current lattice and its declared periodic
periodizations. K=L^p, mu=1/K. All pairings are counting pairings.
D is the scalar-to-bond forward difference, d_1 is bond-to-plaquette
curl, and q_W=d_1^*d_1. Write Delta_0=D^*D and
Delta_1=q_W+DD^*. Delta_1 is the componentwise scalar lattice Laplacian.
The reference energies are R_s=Delta_0+mu^2 I and R_v=Delta_1+mu^2 I.
This distinction avoids calling the vector curl form alone an elliptic
reference. Color only multiplies fixed dimensions.

The matched classical inputs are

 q_W <= q_m <= C q_W,    ||q_m||<=R_q,                       (1)

with uniform exponential quadratic rows and spatial moments. The lower
bound is the exact native curl-average contraction; the upper bound and
the twist estimate below follow from the uniform classical kernels and
Ward identity. These have independent producer/audit notes.

1. THE TWO ACTUAL FLAT AVERAGES

Let Q_0 be the arithmetic average on centered disjoint K-blocks:

 Q_0 f(Y)=K^-4 sum_(r in B_K) f(KY+r).

Its counting Hilbert norm is K^-2. Let J=K^2 Q_0^*, Pi=JJ^*,
P=I-Pi. J is an isometry and Pi is the orthogonal block-constant
projection. The corrected flat ordinary bond average A is, componentwise,

 (A v)_alpha(Y)=sum_(s=0)^(K-1)
                        Q_0(translation_(s e_alpha) v_alpha)(Y).       (2)

Indeed the native ordinary differential has the two endpoint means;
subtracting the exact coarse gradient of their mean leaves the straight
string average. Literal primitive composition gives(2), because balanced
block digits and ordinary string digits enumerate their K-block/segment
once each. Thus this is the actual Cbar(0), not a new large-block map.
The translated Q_0 operators each have norm K^-2, whence

                         ||A||<=K^-1=mu.                    (3)

This is diagonal in bond direction. Its rows have physical range bounded
by a numerical constant, with nonnegative scalar coefficients.

2. SCALAR BLOCK POINCARE AND THE SOFT SCALAR ENERGY

The one-dimensional telescoping estimate on an interval and tensorization
over a K-cube give

                   ||P f||^2<=C K^2 ||D f||^2.              (4)

Only internal block differences are needed: the average is constant on
those edges. There is no derivative of a discontinuous block indicator.
Orthogonality gives mu^2||f||^2<=C||Df||^2+mu^2||J^*f||^2.
Consequently the actual flat soft scalar

                       T=Delta_0+mu^2 Pi

satisfies

                            c R_s<=T<=R_s.                 (5)

The constants are uniform in K and volume, including K=1. The infinite
operator has the same estimate by finitely supported approximation.

3. A DIRECT VECTOR MASS ESTIMATE

Apply(4) to each component of v. Since mu J^*=K Q_0,

 mu||v|| <= C||nabla v||+||K Q_0 v||.                      (6)

The difference between this simpler scalar average of each component
and the ACTUAL A can be controlled without a new Fourier alias lemma:

 ||A-K Q_0|| applied to v
 <= sum_(s=0)^(K-1) K^-2 ||(translation_(s e_alpha)-I)v_alpha||
 <= K^-2 sum_s s ||nabla v|| <= (1/2)||nabla v||.            (7)

The displayed last constant can be enlarged for vector components.
Therefore

             mu^2||v||^2<=C(||nabla v||^2+||A v||^2).       (8)

Choose a fixed a>=R_q+1, as in the archived soft completion. The native
flat coarse Hessian q_(m+p) has the same bound R_q by(1). Define

 V=q_m+DD^*+A^*(aI-q_(m+p))A.                              (9)

At flatness the actual multiplier is zero and q_(m+p)D_c=0, so the
ordinary and corrected constraints agree inside C^*q_(m+p)C. Thus(9)
is the actual flat soft vector appearing in the affine interface; its
classical term remains q_m, rather than a substituted Wilson action.

Equations(1),(8) imply V>=Delta_1+A^*A>=c R_v. Equations(1),(3)
give the upper bound V<=C Delta_1+C mu^2 I. Hence

                            c R_v<=V<=C R_v.                (10)

No separate estimate for a pinned ghost inverse is used. The soft
completion and its exact block average are essential here.

4. PHYSICAL EXPONENTIAL TWISTS

Conjugate fine fields by W_theta(x)=exp(theta.x/K), and coarse fields
by exp(theta.Y), initially as kernel identities. Take real theta with
|theta| sufficiently small; complex theta works on a smaller strip.
The entries of Q_0, J and A have bounded physical displacement. Their
scalar entries are nonnegative, so domination by the untwisted positive
operators, and the mean-value bound for the exponential weight, give

 ||J_theta||<=C,       ||J_theta-J||<=C|theta|,
 ||A_theta||<=C mu,    ||A_theta-A||<=C mu|theta|.            (11)

For real theta use (A^*)_theta=(A_(-theta))^*, and likewise for J.
For complex theta the Hermitian adjoint identity instead uses
-conjugate(theta); equivalently continue the real transpose formula
holomorphically. Do not use the Hermitian adjoint at theta itself.

Uniform exponential rows make q_m(k) analytic in a fixed strip.
The Ward relation q_m(k)d(k)=0 implies q_m(0)=0 by approaching zero
along each coordinate axis. Positivity at both signs of a real momentum
forces every first derivative at zero to vanish. Uniform second moments
therefore imply, with delta(k)=sum_alpha|exp(ik_alpha)-1|^2,

 ||q_m(k+i mu theta)-q_m(k)||
       <=C(mu|theta|sqrt(delta(k))+mu^2|theta|^2).           (12)

Near zero use Taylor's formula and the double zero; away from zero,
bounded analytic first derivatives suffice since sqrt(delta) is bounded
below. DD^* and Delta_0 have the same type of estimate.
Since R_s and R_v are scalar Fourier multipliers in their fibers,
(12) divided by delta+mu^2 is bounded by C(|theta|+|theta|^2).

The coarse q_(m+p) changes by O(|theta|) in operator norm. Its two A
legs each cost O(mu), so the complete correction in(9) changes by
O(mu^2|theta|). Similarly mu^2 Pi changes by O(mu^2|theta|).
It follows that

 ||R_s^-1/2(T_theta-T)R_s^-1/2||<=C|theta|,
 ||R_v^-1/2(V_theta-V)R_v^-1/2||<=C|theta|.                 (13)

Together with(5),(10), a Neumann inverse gives a fixed physical strip
and uniform bounds on

 R_s^1/2 T_theta^-1 R_s^1/2,
 R_v^1/2 V_theta^-1 R_v^1/2.                              (14)

The square roots in(13)--(14) are the UNTWISTED positive reference
energies. Uniform constants do not rely on their commuting with block
operators. This proves the energy-sandwiched inverse part of(H1).

On the infinite block-periodic lattice these bounds also yield uniform
final-block operator decay for energy legs, via the block Fourier strip.
For the ordinary gradient and mu I legs, their twisted versions have
uniform R^-1/2 bounds directly from their symbols. If square-root R
legs are used instead, delta(k+i mu theta)+mu^2 has positive real part
comparable to delta(k)+mu^2 on a smaller fixed strip; its analytic
square-root ratios and their inverses are therefore uniformly bounded.
This supplies the required twisted-leg bounds before Fourier inversion.
The internal fine fiber has K^4 entries; no entrywise sum over this
fiber is taken. For finite tori one can periodize the infinite weighted
block kernels. Nothing here equates unrelated finite winding kernels
with these specified periodizations.

5. THE ELEMENTARY FLAT TRACE BOUND

Consider a torus of side NK in fine units, i.e. N per final direction.
The quadratic forms(5),(10) imply by the finite-dimensional min-max
principle (not by an operator-monotonicity claim for exp) that for
W=T or V,

 N^-4 Tr exp(-u K^2 W)
  <= C exp(-c u){K^4/(1+K^2 u)^2+N^-4},   u>=0.           (15)

To see the lattice factor, diagonalize the componentwise Laplacian.
For a one-dimensional cyclic factor of n=NK sites, its heat sum is
at most C(1+n/sqrt(1+K^2u)), by the elementary lower bound on
4 sin^2(pi j/n), splitting j at n/2. Taking four factors and using
(a+b)^4<=8(a^4+b^4) gives(15). The fixed color and bond dimensions
are absorbed in C. The separate exp(-cu) comes from the mu^2 mass.
The same estimate per physical volume follows in the infinite limit.

This is an undifferentiated flat trace bound. It does not substitute
for the rooted source insertions, coarse Schur factors or cancellation
estimates needed for the determinant's Maxwell coefficient. In
particular one cannot multiply (14) by the K^4-dimensional fiber to
obtain a uniform differentiated trace bound.
