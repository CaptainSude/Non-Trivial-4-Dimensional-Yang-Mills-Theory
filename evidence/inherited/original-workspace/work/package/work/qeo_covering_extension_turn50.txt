1. THE COMMON ALGEBRA

Let I={1,...,N}, p>=1, r>0, and work in

 J=C[t_1,...,t_N]/(t_1^(p+1),...,t_N^(p+1)).

Use the coefficient norm ||P||_r=sum_alpha |P_alpha| r^|alpha|.
Truncation deletes terms, so this norm is submultiplicative.
For each c let phi_c depend only on t_c and ||phi_c||_r<=A, A>=1.
Let ell_c be a one-variable linear functional satisfying

 ell_c(phi_c)=0, |ell_c(t_c^j)|<=D r^j, 0<=j<=p.

The tensor functional ell=otimes_c ell_c obeys
|ell(P)|<=D^N ||P||_r.

Assume the EXACT identity in J

 Phi=(product_c phi_c)
          integral product_(nonempty S subset I)(1+k_S(omega;t)) dnu(omega)
      + L.                                                    (1.1)

The finite complex measure nu has total variation at most one and
does not depend on t. Each k_S depends only on variables in S.
All coefficient functions are measurable and have the specified
uniform majorants. The remainder obeys ||L||_r<=lambda^N.

Expanding the product, any term indexed by a family F of error sets
with union F different from I is annihilated: an uncovered variable c
appears only in its untouched factor phi_c. Multiplication followed by
coordinatewise jet truncation does not alter this observation.

This is the covering cancellation. It uses neither positivity nor
independence of the cell variables. It does require the full
factorization (1.1), the support statement, and the tensor functional.

2. CHECK OF QEO1 B6

Suppose 0<s<1, kappa>0,

 ||k_{c}||_r<=kappa,
 ||k_S||_r<=kappa^N for |S|>=2,
 N kappa^(1-s)<=1/2.

The last condition implies kappa<1. Give a singleton cost one and a
larger set cost N. Every covering family has total cost at least N.
Put a=kappa^(1-s). Extract kappa^(sN) from each covering term and
sum the remaining costs over ALL families. The bound is

 |ell(Phi-L)|
   <=D^N A^N kappa^(sN)(1+a)^N(1+a^N)^(2^N-1-N).

For N=1 the logarithm of the last product is at most a<=1/2.
For N>=2 it is at most

 N a+(2^N-1-N)a^N
   <=1/2+(2a)^N
   <=1/2+N^-N<=3/4<1.

Thus the product is at most exp(1), as asserted in the paper.
Adding |ell(L)|<=(D lambda)^N and taking N-th roots gives the
slightly sharper intermediate estimate

 |ell(Phi)|^(1/N)<=D[A exp(1/N)kappa^s+lambda],

and hence the displayed B6 bound with exp(1) in place of exp(1/N).
The case D=0 is immediate.

The condition N kappa^(1-s)<=1/2 cannot be suppressed. In QEO1 the
reference cell count is chosen with the charge; a separate chessboard
transfer is then needed to obtain arbitrary distinct-product orders.
B6 alone is not a uniform theorem for all N at a fixed kappa.

3. A SUPPORT-WEIGHTED VERSION, UNIFORM IN THE NUMBER OF CELLS

Retain (1.1). Suppose 0<kappa<=1 and 0<theta<1. Let a_S>=0 satisfy

 ||k_S(omega)||_r <= kappa^|S| a_S,
 sup_(c in I) sum_(S contains c) kappa^((1-theta)|S|) a_S <= B, (3.1)

uniformly in omega. B is independent of N and of the chosen finite
cell set. Then

           |ell(Phi)|^(1/N)
                  <=D[A exp(B)kappa^theta+lambda].            (3.2)

Proof. A covering family has sum_(S in F)|S|>=N. Its norm is bounded by

 kappa^(theta N)
       product_(S in F)[kappa^((1-theta)|S|)a_S].

Sum over all families, using 1+x<=exp(x):

 sum_F product_(S in F)[kappa^((1-theta)|S|)a_S]
  = product_S[1+kappa^((1-theta)|S|)a_S]
  <= exp(sum_S kappa^((1-theta)|S|)a_S)
  <= exp(NB).

The final inequality follows by summing (3.1) over c: every nonempty
set is counted |S|>=1 times. Multiply by D^N A^N, integrate against
the total variation of nu, add (D lambda)^N, and take roots.
For nonnegative x,y, (x^N+y^N)^(1/N)<=x+y. This proves (3.2).

No number-of-cells smallness condition is needed. The replacement is
the substantive local support summability (3.1).

Example of an adequate geometric input. Suppose only connected sets
on a fixed bounded-degree cell graph occur, each has a_S<=C0^|S|,
and the number of connected sets of size k containing a cell is at
most Cgraph^k. If q=C0 Cgraph kappa^(1-theta)<1, then

 B<=sum_(k>=1)q^k=q/(1-q).

More general exponentially decaying spanning-tree majorants can be
used directly in (3.1). No such geometric majorant follows merely
from the cardinality of a defect set.

4. THE EXACT SOURCE OBSERVABLE REQUIRED

For p>=4, suppose X_c are real random variables with the requisite
joint moments and define the NORMALIZED finite jet

 Phi(t)=[ E exp(g^-1 sum_c t_c X_c
                       -v/(2g^2) sum_c t_c^2) ]_(degree<=p per cell).

With only finite moments this notation means: truncate the formal
exponential coordinatewise FIRST, and then take its coefficientwise
expectation. An untruncated exponential moment is not required.
If all joint exponential moments exist, it also is the actual
generating function's indicated Taylor jet.

If phi_c(0)=1 and c_{m,c}=phi_c^(m)(0), set
ell_c=(partial_(t_c)^m-c_{m,c}) evaluated at zero, m=2 or 4.
Then ell_c(phi_c)=0 and

 ell(Phi)=E product_c[g^-m H_m(X_c;v)-c_{m,c}],
 H_2(x;v)=x^2-v,
 H_4(x;v)=x^4-6vx^2+3v^2.

One may take D at least max_c(|c_{m,c}|,m! r^-m).
This follows by differentiating a separate source variable for each
cell. A single constant source obtained by setting all t_c equal
does not recover this tensor functional or its full coefficient norm.

The denominator defining E is the actual full partition function
at the SAME bare parameters. A source-dependent normalization or
an independently retuned cell reference changes Phi. A complement
estimate must control L in this same normalized jet, not just its
zero-source mass.
