1. PRIMITIVE CAUSALITY

The regular formula uses E_r+R_r^old in the fluctuation difference only.
Its h_r^reg is the annular log ratio at this same s=t=0 regular input.
The q raw identity instead uses h_r^full at E_r+all R, with s=1. Keeping
these distinct makes the primitive estimate independent of the new R''
birth. The static R_old term is not moved into regular E. Source (3.26)
and (3.28), followed by the extraction described on p279, give exactly
this allocation. Thus the new beta and regular E can be bounded before
invoking the source bound for the new R birth.

One ordering clarification should be explicit. The primitive regular
integral and its annular ratio are first constructed on a provisional
output domain with radii a fixed fraction of alpha(g_r), determined by
the entering coupling. The one-step preparation is on that domain before
the next counterterm is extracted. After beta gives |Delta gamma|<=D,
the actual alpha(g_{r+1}) is comparable with alpha(g_r), and the provisional
fraction contains a suitable fixed fraction of the actual output domain.
No future coupling containment is used to define the integral which proves
that containment. The fixed-source preparation margins and the freedom
to enlarge the final weak threshold permit this ordering.

The finite shrinking phase radii make every primitive chart fit. The
finite decreasing carrier exponents pay the primitive support reserves;
one does not need a same-a primitive theorem. Restricting the canonical R
bound to the phase ball and normalizing by its squared radius costs only
fixed powers of zeta. These belong to C_a. The q-endpoint theorem is used
directly from the original endpoint and restores the canonical norm; it
does not propagate the smallest phase radius/exponent into the next block.

The fixed-domain forcing is O(b_phase^2) after vacuum subtraction and
global-color averaging. Its beta is O(1), independent of the phase norm
K_a. beta(T_1 E)=0 is exact. Hence D can be chosen first above this forcing
bound, and Gamma last to absorb delta K_a and the annular errors. There
is no circular requirement D>=C D generated by the phase estimates.

2. ENDPOINT INDUCTION

On an unfinished block, deleting at most its q terminal primitive steps
from the previous completed-block drift gives the needed affine past-flow
comparison with a larger fixed additive slack. This does not use positivity
of the unfinished block. After the next primitive bound establishes weak
containment, the source conditional construction supplies its actual R'
and R'' birth. The static-history sum can then include that birth. Repeating
this q times provides the actual R_{j+q} before it is used in Z_q.

The endpoint terms T_qR_j and -R_{j+q} must both remain in Z_q. Their
normalized norms are O(rho), and no scalar R counterterm is silently moved
to gamma. beta of the complete endpoint bracket is legitimate even when
its separate young-R pieces have only partition covariance.

With theta<1 fixed, K>=2 C_F/(1-theta), then finite phase K_a, then D, then
Gamma is an acyclic choice. The slowly changing canonical radii contribute
only 1+O_q(1/gamma), absorbed into a theta still below 1. The errors delta,
rho, eta, and tau/b^2 all tend to zero. The two endpoint estimates therefore
restore the norm and give a fixed positive decrement d_G/2. No additional
all-age quantum ancestor estimate is used. I found no algebraic circularity
in this induction once the established fixed-q/source-prefix inputs are
kept in the explicit order above.

3. STOPPING ARITHMETIC

For A>H>Gamma+2qD, let N be the first aligned endpoint with gamma_N<H.
Every preceding endpoint is >=H. All intermediate steps stay >=H-qD,
so they remain in the weak domain. The final endpoint obeys

 H-qD <= gamma_N < H,
 (A-H)/D < N <= q[1+(A-H)/d].

The lower bound follows from A-gamma_N<=DN; the upper bound follows by
summing the completed aligned decrements. These are valid without monotone
primitive steps, continuity in A, or prescribed terminal matching. Thus
A_l -> infinity implies N_l -> infinity. A compact terminal-coupling
subsequence is automatic.

4. SIMULTANEOUS FINITE REGULATOR CHOICE

There is a stronger construction than assuming a regulator-independent
infinite-lattice recursion. It uses only the uniform estimates on actual
compatible finite tori. Given A, set

 n_max = q ceil(1+(A-H)/d).

Choose an aligned integer L_A tending to infinity and larger than every
fixed collar and rounded source block possible for gamma<=A+qD. For
example, an appropriate ell-power above A suffices for all sufficiently
large A, since the largest source separation scale is a fixed power of
log(A+qD). Start the ACTUAL finite recursion on a torus with

 M_A = L_A ell^{n_max}

sites per side. It has enough levels for n_max primitive steps, with at
least L_A sites per side at every level. The uniform induction forces the
first stopping endpoint N to occur by n_max. Its coupling and all source
coefficients may depend on this torus; that causes no problem.

After this actual finite run is constructed, assign physical mesh

 a_A=ell^{-N}.

The resulting physical torus has side

 M_A a_A=L_A ell^{n_max-N} >= L_A -> infinity,

while a_A->0 by the lower stopping bound. These are simultaneous UV and
infinite-volume candidate regulators. No finite-volume state is claimed
to equal an infinite-volume state, and no continuity or horizon shooting
is needed. If a fixed physical torus were required instead, a separate
matching or infinite-volume coefficient construction would still be needed;
the present full-space existence objective permits these expanding tori.

This supplies candidate regulator sequences only. It does not establish
their physical observable convergence, reconstruction, or nontriviality.
