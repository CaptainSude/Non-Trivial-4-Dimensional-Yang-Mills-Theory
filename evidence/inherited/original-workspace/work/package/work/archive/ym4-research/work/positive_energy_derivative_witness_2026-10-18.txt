SIGNED DERIVATIVE WITNESS: POSITIVE ENERGY AND NONZERO MOMENTUM
18 October 2026. Working extension of the sign-packing witness.

Scope. This strengthens random_sign_packing_nontriviality without changing
its source estimates. It proves strict positivity of a signed Maxwell
benchmark, then identifies the resulting OS vector. It rules out an
explanation by vacuum-sector fluctuations. It is relative to the same
source/early-Gaussian modules and the existing OS reconstruction; it is
not a mass-gap or vacuum-uniqueness theorem.

1. A COMPACT TEST WITH ZERO INTEGRAL

Choose nonzero nonnegative smooth functions

 eta in C_c^infinity((tau,2tau)), tau>0,
 chi in C_c^infinity(R^3),
 psi(t,x)=eta(t)chi(x).

The first choice f_1=partial_t psi already annihilates every zero-energy
vector, as shown below. For a stronger witness use

             f=partial_t partial_(x1) psi.             (1)

Then f is real, smooth, compactly supported in the strict positive-time
halfspace, sign-changing, and integral f=0. Its reflected test is
(theta f)(t,x)=f(-t,x); this includes the correct derivative sign and
must not be replaced by an independently chosen positive lobe.
The two supports are separated by at least 2tau in time. Multiply psi
by a fixed constant if a prescribed unit C1 ceiling for f is desired.
The lower bound below changes by that fixed constant squared only.

2. STRICT MAXWELL REFLECTED POSITIVITY FOR THIS SIGNED TEST

The actual free benchmark is the scalar quadratic energy
E=(1/2)sum_(mu<nu,color)F_(mu nu)^2 in the normalized trace metric.
Away from coincidences its connected Euclidean kernel is

                 K_E(x)=C_M |x|^-8, C_M>0.            (2)

This follows directly from the already used curvature Wick formula.
For the standard component normalization of broad_packet_actual_gaussian,
D(x)=1/(4pi^2|x|^2) for each potential component. At x=(t,0,0,0), the
six diagonal curvature covariances are three copies of -1/(pi^2 t^4)
and three copies of +1/(pi^2 t^4); all off-diagonal entries vanish.
Wick's factor dim(g)/2 gives C_M=3 dim(g)/pi^4. Euclidean covariance
and homogeneity give(2). Only C_M>0 is needed below, so harmless trace
convention changes do not affect the argument.

For sigma>=0 let D_sigma be the Euclidean scalar propagator with mass
squared sigma. Its heat-kernel formula, for x!=0, gives

 D_sigma(x)=int_0^infinity (4pi s)^-2
                exp[-|x|^2/(4s)-sigma s] ds.

Integrating sigma^2 first yields

 int_0^infinity sigma^2 D_sigma(x) d sigma
   =(1/(8pi^2))int_0^infinity s^-5 exp[-|x|^2/(4s)]ds
   =192/(pi^2 |x|^8).

Consequently the separated kernel has the positive representation

 K_E(x)=int_0^infinity rho(sigma) D_sigma(x)d sigma,
 rho(sigma)=(C_M pi^2/192) sigma^2.                    (3)

This identity is used only at positive separation; it makes no claim
about a unique extension of |x|^-8 at its ultraviolet contact point.
For t>0 the spatial Fourier transform of D_sigma is
exp(-omega t)/(2omega), omega=sqrt(|p|^2+sigma). Thus for any real
positive-time compact smooth f,

 K_Maxwell(f,theta f)
 =int_R3 dp/(2pi)^3 int_0^infinity d sigma
       rho(sigma)/(2omega) |F(omega,p)|^2,              (4)
 F(omega,p)=int_(t>0)dt dx f(t,x) exp(-omega t-ip.x).

The time support gap makes every differentiation and integral here
absolutely convergent; the high-energy tail has exponential decay.
For f_1=partial_t psi, integration by parts gives
F_1=omega Psi. For the stronger choice(1), it gives

             F(omega,p)=i omega p_1 Psi(omega,p).      (5)

In particular there is no sign cancellation in its reflected norm:

 K_Maxwell(f,theta f)
 =int dp d sigma/(2pi)^3
       rho(sigma) omega p_1^2/2 |Psi(omega,p)|^2>0.    (6)

To see strictness quantitatively, hat chi(0)=int chi>0, so |hat chi(p)|
has a positive lower bound in a small ball around zero. The Laplace
transform of nonzero eta>=0 is positive and bounded below on every
fixed compact interval of positive omega. Restrict(6) to a small
positive-volume p region with p_1 bounded away from zero, and to a
compact sigma interval inside (0,infinity). Every factor is then
bounded below by a positive constant. Denote the resulting fixed
shape constant by K_f>0. The same proof without p_1^2 proves the claim
for f_1.

3. THE INTERACTING SOURCE/PACKING ESTIMATES ARE UNCHANGED

Use the fixed shape f from(1), with its reflected shape theta f, in
the broad packet at witness scale s. All earlier source arguments are
holomorphic/multilinear in arbitrary signed smooth profiles. Their
error bounds use absolute C1 norms, support size and separation;
none required the profile itself to be nonnegative. The initial
gradient, tensor-hat phase error, regular marked comparison, R deletion,
Gaussian quadrature and pre-witness mixed tail therefore retain their
proved bounds with new fixed shape constants.

In particular the exact leading factor remains

       q_s^2 g_s^4 K_f,   q_s=-Gamma'_s,               (7)

not q_s g_s^4 and not an additional derivative-dependent power of
rho. The shape f(x/rho) is an amplitude-one rescaled shape; its
dimensionless derivatives are already in the definition of f.
The degree-minus-eight continuum kernel cancels the two four-volume
rescalings exactly as for the old positive packet.

For random-sign packing, the sums of disjoint copies of f and theta f
still have sup norm O(1), Lipschitz norm O(R_N^-1), independently of
their count. The original fixed-cell h bound, exact h12-h1*h2 identity,
and causal re-terminalized late-vacuum estimate therefore give

 Cov_bare(O(f_R),O(theta f_R))
       =q_s^2 g_s^4 K_f+o(g_s^4),                     (8)

with the same parameter order as random_sign_packing_nontriviality.
Here rho=Gamma_w^B and the witness band Gamma_w are fixed before
removing regulators, R=rho ell^{-(N-s)}, and a subsequence fixes N-s.
Choose the band sufficiently large that the absolute error is at most
(q_*^2/2)g_s^4 K_f. The continuum limit then has

             ||v_(f_R)||_OS^2 >= c_w>0,               (9)

where c_w can be chosen as q_*^2 K_f/[2(Gamma_w+C_q)^2].

Covariance and reflected norm: work with the centered scalar field,
or subtract the exact finite-regulator mean in the OS vector. The
reflected connected covariance is then exactly its squared OS norm.
In the continuum, translation invariance makes the one-point function
constant, and integral f_R=0, so this mean subtraction is zero for
the present test. If exact finite-mesh zero mean is wanted, replace
the two derivatives in(1) by lattice differences of psi at that mesh;
periodic sums telescope in each direction. Their supports remain in
the strict positive halfspace at sufficiently fine mesh and they
converge in every required smooth-test norm. This optional convention
is unnecessary for the connected identity(8).

4. WHY THIS VECTOR HAS GENUINE POSITIVE ENERGY

Let v_chi=[Phi(chi)] be the one-field OS vector for a positive-time
test chi, using the reconstructed centered scalar field. This use of
one-field vectors and their reflected pairings is entirely inside the
off-collision test domain. Let

 T(a)=exp(-aH),  U(y)=exp(i y.P),
 T(a)v_psi=v_(psi(t-a,x)),
 U(y)v_psi=v_(psi(t,x-y)).

Since psi has positive distance from the time-zero plane, these test
translations exist in a neighborhood of zero. Smooth-test convergence
and continuity of the separated S2 pairing imply convergence of their
difference quotients in OS norm. Therefore

 v_psi in D(H P_1),
 v_(partial_t psi)=H v_psi,
 v_(partial_(x1) psi)=-iP_1 v_psi,
 v_f=-i H P_1 v_psi.                                 (10)

These are strong derivative statements, not formal integration by
parts with an uncontrolled point field. Joint-generator domains follow
by the same test-difference argument for the mixed derivative and the
strong commutation of H and P_1.

For psi_R(x)=psi(x/R) and f_R(x)=f(x/R), the physical-unit version is

                 v_(f_R)=-i R^2 H P_1 v_(psi_R).      (11)

For the simpler f_1 packet the factor is R and only H appears.
Let E_H({0}) and E_(P1)({0}) be the joint spectral projections. From
(11),

 E_H({0})v_(f_R)=0,  E_(P1)({0})v_(f_R)=0.            (12)

Thus(9) is NOT a fluctuation among zero-energy vacuum vectors, even
if that subspace is larger than the span of the chosen Omega. It also
cannot live entirely at zero spatial momentum. Its joint spectral
measure has positive mass with E>0 and p_1!=0. Since it is a finite
nonzero measure, some bounded region with E>=1/k and |p_1|>=1/k has
positive mass. This does not imply a lower bound for the spectrum of
H on ALL nonvacuum vectors, and hence does not prove a mass gap.

5. VACUUM-PHASE DECOMPOSITION

Suppose the reconstructed theory is decomposed by a VALID vacuum-phase
or central-decomposition theorem into positive-energy covariant sectors,
with translations, vacuum vectors and smeared fields decomposable:

 H=integral^direct H_xi d pi(xi),
 H_generator=integral^direct H_xi,generator d pi(xi),
 P=integral^direct P_xi d pi(xi),  Omega=int^direct Omega_xi.

The direct-integral spectral calculus applied to(11) gives, almost
everywhere,

 v_(f_R),xi=-iR^2 H_xi P_(1),xi v_(psi_R),xi,
 ||v_(f_R)||^2=int ||v_(f_R),xi||^2 d pi(xi)>0.         (13)

Therefore a positive-measure set of sectors has a nonzero witness
orthogonal to its entire zero-energy and zero-P1 subspaces. Restricting
to the field-generated cyclic subtheory in such a sector retains the
witness. This is the precise preservation claim needed when extracting
a vacuum phase. It is not inferred merely from a positive total
connected covariance of a test with nonzero integral.

Equivalently, in a decomposition into translation-invariant states
with appropriate reflection-positive reconstructions, each phase's
one-point value is c_xi integral f_R=0. The between-phase variance of
one-point functions vanishes, so the total reflected covariance is the
average of the phase reflected norms, with no vacuum-mixture term.

CAUTION: an arbitrary ergodic decomposition of a translation-invariant
Euclidean measure is NOT asserted here to preserve RP or the other
quantum axioms. Those properties must come from the chosen valid phase
decomposition theorem. Formula(13) is the consequence once that theorem
applies; it cannot replace its hypotheses.

6. WHAT LORENTZ COVARIANCE SAYS ABOUT ZERO SPATIAL MOMENTUM

Positive energy and the cone H>=|P| alone allow a spatially constant
quantum system with H>0 and P=0. Thus f_1=partial_t psi by itself
does not exclude that possibility before Lorentz covariance is known.
Our stronger mixed derivative(1) excludes it directly by(12).

In a SEPARABLE Hilbert space carrying a Lorentz-covariant joint
translation spectrum, the spectral projection of

       S={(E,p): E>0, p=0}

is zero. Indeed a boost of rapidity r in direction1 maps S to the ray
S_r={(E cosh r,E sinh r,0,0):E>0}. Distinct rapidities give disjoint
rays. If E(S) were nonzero, covariance would make every E(S_r)
nonzero, providing uncountably many orthogonal nonzero subspaces,
contrary to separability. This rules out a normalizable state whose
entire positive-energy momentum measure sits on the rest line.

It does NOT remove rest-energy points from the topological spectrum:
ordinary massive mass shells contain (m,0), although their exact
point spectral projection is zero. No stronger statement is needed
for the present witness, which already has p_1!=0 without that argument.
