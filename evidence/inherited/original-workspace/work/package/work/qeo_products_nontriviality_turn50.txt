1. A COMPLETE GENERAL LEMMA, WITH SEPARATION ALLOWED

Let a probability space carry a measure-preserving Z^d action T_y,
and let U(y)G=G composed with T_-y. Let I_lat be its invariant sigma-
algebra. Assume G is real and belongs to L^p for every finite p.
Fix a finite separation distance R>=0 and u>=0. Suppose that for
every even J and every tuple y_1,...,y_J with pairwise distances >R,

  | E product_(j=1)^J U(y_j)G | <= u^J.                 (1.1)

Then

  || E[G | I_lat] ||_infinity <= u.                    (1.2)

For any smaller invariant sigma-algebra I contained in I_lat,
the same bound holds for E[G|I]. No ergodicity of the lattice action,
mixing, Gaussian domination, field space, or reflection positivity
is needed. The source's B5 is R=0.

Proof. Let C_n be a cube in Z^d, N_n its cardinality, and
A_n=N_n^-1 sum_(y in C_n) U(y)G. Fix J=2k. At most

  binomial(J,2) v_R N_n^(J-1)

ordered tuples in C_n^J have a pair at distance <=R, where v_R is
the number of lattice points in the radius-R ball. This includes
repeated indices. By Holder, every one of their expectations has
absolute value at most E|G|^J. The other tuples satisfy (1.1).
Since A_n is real,

  E|A_n|^J
   <= u^J + binomial(J,2) v_R N_n^-1 E|G|^J.            (1.3)

The commuting unitary mean-ergodic theorem gives A_n -> P_lat G in
L^2. Concretely, the cube average is a product of d commuting one-
parameter Cesaro averages, each converging strongly to its invariant
projection; their product projects onto the joint invariant space.
Choose an almost-surely convergent subsequence. Fatou and (1.3) give
E|P_lat G|^(2k)<=u^(2k) for every k. If u>0, Markov at u+epsilon
and k->infinity proves (1.2); if u=0, k=1 already proves it.
Finally P_I=P_I P_lat and conditional expectation contracts L-infinity.

Only arbitrarily high EVEN orders are needed. It suffices to assume
the product bounds for all sufficiently large even orders. Bounds
C_J u^J give the same conclusion only if the relevant J-th roots
of C_J tend to at most one (or otherwise with the corresponding
root loss). A factorial loss in J cannot be ignored.

This strengthening matters for local probes: their translated
supports may overlap for nearby lattice translates. Such tuples can
be discarded by the same negligible-count argument. A uniform bound
for separated copies of a fixed probe is enough.

2. CONDITIONAL FOURTH CUMULANTS: EXACT ALGEBRA AND LIMIT ORDER

B5 p.8 assumes E[X|I]=0. For a common deterministic v and g>0 set

  G_2=g^-2(X^2-v)-c_2,
  G_4=g^-4(X^4-6vX^2+3v^2)-c_4.

If Section 1 applies to G_2 and G_4 with bounds u_2,u_4, write
d_m=E[G_m|I]. Then |d_m|<=u_m and direct substitution gives

  g^-4 kappa_4(X|I)=c_4+d_4-3(c_2+d_2)^2.              (2.1)

The v and v^2 contributions cancel exactly; they must be the SAME
v in the two Wick polynomials. Consequently

 |g^-4 kappa_4-(c_4-3c_2^2)|
       <=u_4+6|c_2|u_2+3u_2^2.                        (2.2)

For 0<g<=1, u_4,(1+|c_2|)u_2,
|c_4-3c_2^2-b|<=C g^sigma imply

  |kappa_4-g^4 b| <= (8C+3C^2) g^(4+sigma).             (2.3)

The constant in B5/T18 is correct: the first two error contributions
cost C and 6C, comparison with b costs another C, and
g^(2 sigma)<=g^sigma bounds the square term.

Conditional centering is essential. Parent centering E X=0 is not
enough. Without E[X|I]=0 the displayed fourth-moment combination is
not the fourth cumulant. If centering is unavailable, one must control
conditional moments of degrees 1,2,3,4 and use

  kappa_4=m_4-4m_1m_3-3m_2^2+12m_1^2m_2-6m_1^4.

The same-parent argument in A3/T18 (pp.22,107) is valid as an implication:

* Fix a parent weak limit mu realized by a particular cutoff sequence.
* Pass to a further subsequence on which the bounded c_2,i,c_4,i
  converge. This does not change that parent mu.
* For every fixed finite set of translates, pass the polynomial
  product bound using the stated joint moment convergence and test-
  approximation estimates. It is enough that the finite torus admits
  that set eventually; no simultaneous cutoff threshold for all sets
  is required.
* Apply the lemma to the resulting continuum G_2,G_4 on the fixed
  lattice lambda Z^d. Continuous translation invariants form a smaller
  sigma-algebra; lattice ergodicity is unnecessary.
* Intersect the two full-measure component sets with the centering,
  Gaussian/moment, and independently obtained OS component sets of
  this SAME mu.

The lattice spacing lambda may depend on a fixed admitted coupling.
It must be fixed while taking that coupling's regulator limit. One
cannot silently replace it by a lattice diverging with the regulator.

A positive PARENT fourth cumulant alone is an invalid substitute.
For example mix two stationary ergodic Gaussian fields having different
variances V. Conditional means are zero and every component has
kappa_4=0, but the parent has kappa_4=3 Var(V)>0.

3. WHAT Q4 ACTUALLY REQUIRES

Q4 pp.18--19, T07 p.89, T12 p.96, T13 p.98, T14 p.100,
T15 p.101, and T17 pp.105--106 expose a useful chain of inputs.
It is substantially stronger than a one-source pressure bound.

T07 specifies independent cell marks t_c and an EXACT normalized
source identity. For orthogonal reference curvatures the Wick
generating function is

  Phi(t)=E exp(sum_c t_c X_c/g - v sum_c t_c^2/(2g^2)).

The relevant quantity is

  [product_c (partial_(t_c)^m-c_m)] Phi(0), m=2,4,       (3.1)

NOT a one-cell derivative and NOT the derivative of log Phi.
It has degree up to four in EACH mark and total degree 4|C|.

The reference construction uses a fixed but possibly large number
N_* of reflected source cells. T12 controls one-cell normalized free
factors. T13 requires genuinely mixed derivatives and spatial decay
for two separated marked cells, including differentiated field legs.
T14 supplies a positive small-field measure with its ORIGINAL
normalizer and a small COMPLEX marked complement. T15 applies the
finite covering lemma only after this full jet factorization exists.

The elementary covering mechanism is sound: an error family failing
to cover one cell is killed by that cell's annihilating functional.
Assigning singleton cost one and larger sets cost N forces cost >=N
for every survivor. The assumption N kappa^(1-s)<=1/2 then bounds
the leftover product sum. This algebra does not establish the required
one-cell, mixed-cell, complement, or normalization estimates.

T17 then uses rectangular chessboard/trace inequalities and a
TWO-reference-volume normalized pressure defect to transfer the fixed
reference product bound to arbitrary finite sets of cells in arbitrary
large outer volumes. It explicitly pays an inverse charge power in
the normalization. This transfer is why a fixed N_*-cell estimate can
supply all the product orders required by B5.

Thus one needs either:

  (i) direct mixed-product estimates for arbitrarily many separated
      cells with exponential constants uniform in the number of cells;
  or
  (ii) a proved reference-to-all-volumes transfer theorem supplying
       those estimates from a fixed reference family.

The QED3 plan records tasks for (ii). It does not make (ii) available
for the present YM4 selected kernel. Positivity of that kernel is not
reflection positivity or a chessboard theorem. Our bad-label estimates
do not bound the marked complex complement in T14. The correct tuning
sequence, normalizer, and parent law must be common to all branches.

The stated model-specific Q4 estimates are not fully provable from the
107-page cards alone: those cards refer to the longer manuscript for
their determinant telescope, differentiated marked bounds, complex
complement and normalized chessboard comparison. This is a proof-status
limitation, not a demonstrated counterexample to Q4.

4. B7 IS SPECIFIC TO THE ABELIAN THREE-DIMENSIONAL DIRAC RESPONSE

B7 p.10 and T02 p.83 use the doubled three-dimensional Dirac
representation and a real abelian connection:

  Q_(4,M)(a)=-(1/4)Tr[(M+gamma.partial)^-1 i gamma.a]^4
           =(240 pi M^5)^-1 integral |curl a|^4
                + O_a(M^-7).

The scaling arithmetic is consistent. Unitary dilation with
a_l(x)=l a(x/l) gives Q_(4,m)(a_l)=l^8 Q_(4,ml)(a).
Since b=24 Q_4, this yields

  b(dd* beta_l)
    =l^3/(10 pi m^5) integral |curl d* beta|^4
        +O_beta(l m^-7),
  beta_l(x)=l^2 beta(x/l).

Thus the claimed positive leading integral dominates for large l,
IF the large-mass expansion with that error has been proved.
The leading numerical coefficient agrees with expanding the constant
magnetic-field factor x coth x: its quartic term is -x^4/45, and
the proper-time integral gives 1/(240 pi M^5). This consistency
check does not prove the uniform local expansion and its O(M^-7)
remainder for every compact varying field.

Those remainder, mass-derivative, local gauge reduction, and trace
justification steps are listed as proof work in T02. The plan itself
does not provide their full derivation. No nonabelian/d=4 sign theorem
is claimed there, and none follows by changing the dimension or the
group in the displayed coefficient.

5. THE PRECISE YM4 NONTRIVIALITY GAP

The TURN49 theorem controls one ORIGINAL CONSTANT ACTION source,
with frozen baseline windows, in a normalized FIELD quotient. It
does not bound its scalar vacuum cumulants. It supplies neither the
independent local source functions (3.1), their arbitrary mixed jets,
nor a lower bound for a renormalized local observable.

Even knowledge of a complete scalar generating function for a total
sum would not determine separated moments. A two-site translation-
invariant example is (O_1,O_2)=(1+Z,1-Z), Z uniform on {-1,1}, versus
(O_1,O_2)=(1,1). Both have the same constant sum 2 and every exponential
moment of that sum agrees; their local variances and cross moments
differ. Both observables are nonnegative. Knowing only the diagonal
t_1=t_2 of a generating function loses the needed marked information.

For a usable YM criterion one must first specify:

* real gauge-invariant physical probes X_i(y), their lattice
  calibration/counterterms, translated support, and continuum limits;
* a fixed translation-invariant parent law from the SAME regulator
  sequence, with joint moment convergence and uniform integrability
  of each finite mixed polynomial family;
* conditional centering, or degree 1--4 conditional-moment control;
* products (3.1) with a uniform exponential bound in |Y|, or a proved
  transfer mechanism furnishing it;
* a signed/nonzero comparison coefficient in the ACTUAL YM model,
  with the error smaller than that coefficient;
* identification with the original measure and the needed positivity/
  symmetry statements for those same components.

None of these marked observable estimates follows just from alpha_j
remaining close to the original constant source coefficient.

There is also a structural caution. QED's X=F(f) is LINEAR in a
gauge-invariant abelian field strength. For a simple nonabelian group,
a color-fixed linear smearing of F is not gauge invariant, and
Tr F=0. Wilson loops or Tr(F^2) are nonlinear observables with their
own renormalization.

A nonzero fourth cumulant of a nonlinear observable does NOT by itself
establish an interacting underlying field. If Z is a centered unit
Gaussian, X=Z^2-1 has variance 2 and fourth cumulant 48. A free
Gaussian theory already has such nonlinear composites. A fourth-
cumulant test must say precisely which field is being tested and
which free/composite comparison it excludes. Likewise a same-support
fourth cumulant should not be called a separated physical witness
without an argument excluding contact-only or null-state explanations.

A useful generic alternative targets a separated OS norm directly.
Suppose a limiting parent has RP components, O is a real observable
supported strictly at positive times, and O^- is its reflected copy.
If Section 1 gives in those components

  |mu_xi(O)|<=epsilon, |mu_xi(O^-)|<=epsilon,
  |mu_xi(O^- O)-c|<=u,   c>u+epsilon^2,                (5.1)

then

  mu_xi(O^- O)-mu_xi(O^-)mu_xi(O)>0.

This is the positive squared OS norm of the centered O state, so
that state is nonzero and orthogonal to the vacuum. The three bounds
in (5.1) can be obtained by applying the separated-product lemma to
O, O^-, and O^-O-c. This is a rigorous conditional criterion, not an
estimate currently available for YM4. It gives a more directly
physical nontriviality target than a composite's one-point cumulant.

6. AN ACTUAL CONSEQUENCE FOR OUR EXISTING LABEL ESTIMATES

Fix the homogeneous flat datum and the translation-covariant weak-
chart law used in TURN49, with a fixed finite list of label types
per current block. Suppose the admitted periodic volumes exhaust
Z^4 and the constants in our existing joint estimate are uniform:

  Pr(B_i=1 for every i in S)<=p^|S|,                  (6.1)

for every set S of distinct ambient color-ball labels. Here B_i
is the bad indicator. These are the weak-chart-normalized augmented
laws; no outside-chart mass is included.

Their periodic label extensions form probability measures on the
compact product space {0,1}^(Z^4 x types). A diagonal subsequence of
finite cylinder probabilities has a probability limit nu. Translation
invariance passes to nu. For each FIXED finite cylinder its labels are
distinct modulo the finite period eventually, so (6.1) passes by
bounded cylinder convergence. The periodic extension is not asserted
to satisfy (6.1) for infinite-lattice labels that coincide modulo its
period. This needs no field compactness, Gaussian moment theorem, or
continuum scaling argument.

Fix a finite pattern S and put G_S=product_(i in S) B_i. For translates
y_1,...,y_J separated by more than twice the pattern diameter, the
translated patterns are disjoint. Hence

  E_nu product_j U(y_j)G_S <= p^(J |S|).

Apply Section 1 with u=p^|S|. In almost every translation-ergodic
component nu_xi,

  nu_xi(B_i=1 for all i in S)<=p^|S|.                 (6.2)

There are countably many finite label patterns, so (6.2) holds for
all of them on ONE common full-measure component set. In particular
each component has bad-label density at most p and the same rooted
connected-bad-component tail obtained by bounded-degree counting.

This is a genuine component-level extension of the already proved
unconditional bad-label moments. It does NOT claim that arbitrary
GOOD-conditioned bounds automatically pass to components; their
denominators would require a separate argument. Nor does it imply
Bernoulli domination, factorization, or connected activity decay.

The variables in (6.2) are auxiliary labels of a weak-chart law at a
fixed scale. This is not a statement about the full Wilson measure,
previous-scale nonlinear history constraints, continuum fields, or
any nonzero gauge-invariant physical witness.

FINAL MATHEMATICAL SCOPE

The transferable new mathematical tools are the separated-product
conditional-expectation criterion, its exact conditional-cumulant
algebra, and the componentwise retention of our existing bad-label
moment bounds. The QED3 plan provides a precise target for independent
marked sources and their normalized full-measure transfer. It does
not close those YM4 interfaces, and the single constant original-
action source cannot replace them.
