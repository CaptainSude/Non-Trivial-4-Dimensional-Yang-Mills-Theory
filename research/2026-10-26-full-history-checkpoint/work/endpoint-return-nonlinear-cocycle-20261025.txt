1. INPUTS AND DOMAIN

Let Lambda_j be nested finite compatible lattices. The chosen ordinary
one-step group average M_j and its chosen internal endpoint transporter
P_j(V;y,z) satisfy, on the domains used,

 M_j(V^g)=(M_jV)^(r_jg),
 P_j(V^g;y,z)=g(y)P_j(V;y,z)g(z)^(-1),
 V^g(e)=g(e_-)V(e)g(e_+)^(-1).

Here r_j is restriction to the actual next coarse vertices, not scalar
block averaging. These are the properties recorded for the literal B109
endpoint mean in the repository's infrared-cancellation notebook,
ccs-primitive-section, and the native affine interface / ordinary moving-map
notes dated 2026-10-24. The present proof uses these properties as inputs;
it does not independently verify B98(104)-(108),(130)-(135).

Fix a finite length t. Work on an open set D of pairs (W,V) for which all
maps in the recursion below are defined, every relative endpoint product
has a single specified analytic logarithm, and all applications of the
ordinary gauge identity stay on that branch. A sufficient local D exists
at any pair (W,W) whose finite ordinary trajectory lies strictly inside
these analytic charts: at the diagonal all relative products equal I,
all correction gauges equal I, and finite continuity gives an open
neighborhood. No radius uniform in t, volume, or source localizers is
asserted by this observation. If W itself varies complexly, D must be a
joint open set in (W,V), not separately chosen charts.

2. DECLARED EXACT RECURSION

Set W_0=W, C_0=V and b_0=I. Recursively define

 W_(j+1)=M_j W_j,
 ell_j(y)=|B_j(y)|^(-1) sum_(z in B_j(y))
                 Log[P_j(C_j;y,z) P_j(W_j;y,z)^(-1)],
 a_j(y)=exp(ell_j(y)),
 C_(j+1)=(M_j C_j)^(a_j^(-1)),
 b_(j+1)=(r_j b_j) a_j.                                  (1)

The endpoint P_j is exactly the chosen native endpoint mean; it is not
replaced by a single axial path or arbitrary large-block mean. The finite
block set and weights are those of the chosen ordinary convention.
On the diagonal V=W one has C_j=W_j, ell_j=0, a_j=b_j=I.

THEOREM A. Let M^(j)=M_(j-1)...M_0. On D,

                      M^(j)V=C_j^(b_j).                  (2)

Consequently the actual ordinary endpoint is retained by the pair (C_j,b_j).
For a bond (y,y'), formula (2) reads

 M^(j)V(y,y')=b_j(y) C_j(y,y') b_j(y')^(-1).              (3)

Proof. The convention gives (U^g)^h=U^(hg). The assertion at j=0 is
immediate. Suppose (2) holds at j. Ordinary gauge covariance gives

 M^(j+1)V=(M_j C_j)^(r_j b_j).

By (1), M_jC_j=C_(j+1)^(a_j), so the right side equals
C_(j+1)^((r_jb_j)a_j)=C_(j+1)^(b_(j+1)). This proves (2).
No logarithm of a product and no cocycle property of the endpoint mean
has been used.

The ordered product is explicitly

 b_t=(r_(t-1)...r_0 b_0)
       (r_(t-1)...r_1 a_0) ... (r_(t-1)a_(t-2)) a_(t-1),

with the b_0 factor equal I. Each factor is a value at the same final
coarse vertex. No factor may be commuted through another.

3. JOINT GAUGE COVARIANCE

Apply one gauge g to both W and V. Write g_j=r_(j-1)...r_0g.
Assume the transformed pair remains in the declared logarithm domain;
then analytic matrix logarithms commute with similarity on this domain.
Inductively W_j'=W_j^(g_j), C_j'=C_j^(g_j), and

 ell_j'=Ad_(g_(j+1)) ell_j,
 a_j'=g_(j+1)a_jg_(j+1)^(-1),
 b_j'=g_j b_j g_j^(-1).                                 (4)

To verify C covariance, use
(M_jC_j')^((a_j')^-1)=(M_jC_j)^( (a_j')^-1 g_(j+1))
and (a_j')^-1g_(j+1)=g_(j+1)a_j^-1. The b recursion then
proves its transformation law. Thus a complex background does not break
the algebra, provided the SAME joint analytic branch domain is supplied.
This is simultaneous covariance of evaluated configurations. It says
nothing about covariance under independent source weakening operations.

4. MATCH TO THE ALREADY REGISTERED MOVING-REFERENCE TANGENTS

Keep W fixed and vary V(s)=exp(i s v)W. Let Q_j be the left-relative
differential of M_j at W_j, D_j the gauge generator at W_j, and define

 Z_j v=(1/i) d/ds ell_j(W_j,exp(i s v)W_j)|_(s=0),
 (B_j phi)(y)=|B_j(y)|^(-1) sum_z Ad_(P_j(W_j;y,z)) phi(z).

Here the notation ell_j(W_j,...) means the single-step logarithmic
endpoint mean, before inserting the recursion. Endpoint covariance gives

 Z_j D_j=r_j-B_j,
 Q_j D_j=D_(j+1)r_j,
 Qbar_j=Q_j-D_(j+1)Z_j,
 Qbar_jD_j=D_(j+1)B_j.                                  (5)

For example, differentiation along exp(i s phi) gives the endpoint
relative increment phi(y)-Ad_(P_j(W_j;y,z))phi(z). Its average proves
the first formula, and ordinary covariance proves the second.

Let c_j be the left-relative tangent of C_j and write
b_j(s)=I+i s z_j+O(s^2). Direct differentiation of (1) yields

 c_0=v,   c_(j+1)=Qbar_j c_j,
 z_0=0,   z_(j+1)=r_jz_j+Z_jc_j.                        (6)

If q_j=Q_(j-1)...Q_0v is the ordinary tangent, differentiation of (2)
gives q_j=c_j+D_jz_j. Inserting this into (6), and using (5), gives

                  z_(j+1)=B_jz_j+Z_jq_j.               (7)

This is exactly the moving-reference scalar correction recurrence in
the archived ccs-Z-recursion. Thus the declared nonlinear recursion
realizes that tangent identity without replacing it by the derivative
of the fixed-identity corrected nonlinear map.

5. ELEMENTARY UNIFORM PRODUCT BUDGET, CONDITIONAL ON ENDPOINT READS

Use an induced matrix operator norm and suppose the actual corrections
in (1) obey ||ell_j||_infinity<=epsilon_j throughout one joint parameter
domain. Restriction has supremum norm one. Therefore

 ||b_t||_infinity <= exp(sum_(j<t) epsilon_j),
 ||b_t^-1||_infinity <= exp(sum_(j<t) epsilon_j),
 ||Ad_(b_t)|| <= exp(2 sum_(j<t) epsilon_j).              (8)

A product telescope additionally gives

 ||b_t-I||_infinity, ||b_t^-1-I||_infinity
                              <= exp(sum epsilon_j)-1. (9)

For example, expand a product of (I+(a_j-I)); the sum of norms is bounded
by product exp(epsilon_j)-1. The inverse order has the same bound.
If epsilon_j<=C delta_j and the sum delta_j stays small on a logarithmic
prefix, (8) rules out a generic C^t loss for this coarse frame product.
It does not prove the assumed epsilon_j estimates for the actual downward
noisy construction, nor bounds on its fine return gauges.

6. SCOPE: WHAT THIS DOES NOT IDENTIFY

This note gives a literal endpoint identity for the explicitly declared
correction (1), using the native endpoint mean P_j. The B98 relative Q,
its axial return, and its subsequent Landau return have NOT been shown
to equal this chosen nonlinear continuation. Matching first derivatives
in (5)-(7) is insufficient: different nonlinear maps can have identical
first jets. One must either establish that identification on the full
required domain or justify a new continuation for every unmarked factor
and normalizer by exact physical identities and verified bounds.

There is no independent-current substitution in this proof. The native
selected companion, all source localizers, complete old/current domains,
noise contours, and normalized read costs remain separate requirements.
All original real noise variables are fixed; no complex change of noise
integration variables has been performed. No original mask, coarea factor,
scalar factor, complement, contact, root count, or normalizer is altered.
