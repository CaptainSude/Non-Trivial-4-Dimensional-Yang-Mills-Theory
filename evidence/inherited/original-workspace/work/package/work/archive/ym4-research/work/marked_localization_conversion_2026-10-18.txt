BIRTH SPECIALIZATION IN A LOCALIZED FINITE-JET NORM
18 October 2026. Research proof. No public artifact changed.

RESULT

The actual homogeneous physical specialization of a source bulk birth
R_X admits a strict supported decomposition on a comparable current-field
ball, with exponential DIAMETER weight and arbitrary fixed polynomial
support weight. Every fixed number k of current-field derivatives costs
only C_k r_i^(-k). There is no exponential cost in the number of fine
coordinates, or in the volume of the birth MR_i blocks.

The construction uses shell differences of complete classical maps,
not a Taylor expansion in every fine coordinate. Its physical current
locality is proved below from the already constructed joint (A,m)
strict-support fixed point. The source geometry is important: d_i(X)
still refers to the fixed source M-blocks, even when X is constrained
to be a union of the much larger MR_i blocks.

For the actual one-step retained chart, the same construction localizes
the centered observable G(B,t)=f(F_M(B,t))-f(F_M(B,0)). Its linear and
quadratic fluctuation pieces have the fixed real jet norms requested
by the second-background response calculation. Two background
derivatives plus one fluctuation gradient require a fifth mixed
analytic derivative; the fifth-derivative term comes with g and is
bounded by C r_i^(-4) when g/r_i is bounded. The norms are L^8 under
any actual reference law with uniform coordinate moments through order
32. This statement supplies the localized marked-observable input;
it does not prove uniform convexity, score bounds or stability of all
descendants under repeated conditional expectation.

1. CONVENTIONS AND SOURCE GEOMETRY

Use M_ref=ell^k for the homogeneous refinement and use mathfrak M for
the FIXED large localization-block side called M in the primary papers.
These are different parameters. The current lattice spacing is one
AT THE BIRTH SCALE i. M_ref is the preceding ultraviolet refinement
at that birth, not the number of subsequent steps since the birth.
All regulators are finite compatible periodic tori; constants below
are uniform in M_ref and volume. The primitive ell, mathfrak M, gauge
group and fixed exponents may enter constants.

CMP109 p257 defines D_i as connected unions of fixed mathfrak M-blocks.
The number d_i(X) is the least length, divided by mathfrak M, of a
tree in X meeting every one of those blocks. CMP119 (2.30)--(2.31)
restricts bulk R domains to unions of mathfrak M R_i blocks, but keeps
X in D_i and keeps d_i(X). CMP122-II p390 before (1.100) expressly
replaces the fresh-bulk linear size by d_k(X). There is no division
of that bulk decay length by R_i.

In particular, choose a deterministic anchor x_X and let Z_X be all
current cells needed to restrict the source field to X and its fixed
collar. If D_X=max_{x in Z_X}dist(x_X,x), then

  D_X <= C mathfrak M [1+d_i(X)].                         (1.1)

The number of fixed source blocks is also at most C[1+d_i(X)]. A tree
of length t can be covered by C(1+t) unit graph balls; each meets a
bounded number of unit cubes. Conversely the domain is connected.
The standard connected-set count is exponential in its block count.
Thus, after spending a fixed part of a sufficiently large source
exponent kappa_s, for every fixed P one has

  sup_x sum_{X: x_X=x} (1+D_X)^P exp(-kappa_s d_i(X)/2)
                                                    <= C_P.   (1.2)

The same bound holds on compatible tori, counting incidences as usual.
Requiring X to be a union of larger blocks restricts the sum and
does not worsen it. No factor exp(C R_i^4) is introduced.

The source input for a birth is

  |R_X(U,J)| <= epsilon_i exp(-kappa_s d_i(X))=:A_X,       (1.3)

on its independent source domain, with the stated gauge invariance.
It may be vacuum-subtracted at the cost of two. One may take the
published epsilon_i=g_i^K or any established sharper birth envelope.
The argument is linear in epsilon_i.

2. STRICT SUPPORTS FOR THE NONLINEAR PHYSICAL CURRENT

PROPOSITION 1. Fix any finite cardinality reserve a and sufficiently
small classical spatial exponent kappa_cl>0. On a fixed complex coarse
ball the complete physical field

 T(B)=(A_ord(B),J_phys(U_ord(B)))                         (2.1)

has coherent strict families T_{v;S}(B_S), zero at B=0, with

  sup_v sum_S exp(kappa_cl tau({v} union S)+a|S|)
                 ||T_{v;S}||_{E_v;b} <= C_a b.           (2.2)

E_v is the joint maximum of the full C^(1,alpha) potential norm and
the supremum of the physical current on a fixed physical collar of v.
The fine coordinate count stays inside this Banach output norm.

Proof. Theorem cl-fixed-point-theorem already constructs a JOINT
strict family for (A_*,m), with the first component in its complete
physical C^(1,alpha) space, the second in current supremum norm.
The exact physical Euler identity is

  E(A_*)=Q^T m + D R(A_*)^T m,
  J_phys(exp(i eta A_*))=T_{eta A_*}^{-T} E(A_*).           (2.3)

Here R is the corrected-constraint remainder and T_X the left
exponential differential. The physical adjoint Q^T is local in a
fixed physical collar with uniform fine supremum output bound.
The primitive V(A,m)=D R(A)^T m is precisely the finite-range analytic
vertex already controlled in cl-fixed-point and cl-local-cauchy;
the input needed is holomorphy in its physical absolute-row norm.
Thus support substitution gives families for both terms on the right
of the first identity. Multiplication by the pointwise analytic
matrix T_{eta A}^{-T} preserves the support algebra uniformly for
eta<=1. It introduces no outside derivative.

The ordinary gauge transfer conjugates J at its basepoint. That gauge
and A_ord are the uniformly holomorphic complete-field maps used in
the proof of pgh-support-proposition: their dependency range is a
fixed number of physical cells. Their local Taylor vertices therefore
have geometric majorants independent of fine dimension. Substitute
the joint (A_*,m) families and use the rooted support gluing inequality.
All resulting sums are absolute. Choosing one global coefficient
assignment before restricting collars proves coherence. The norm is
O(b) because J(0)=0. This proves (2.2). QED.

Consequently, for B,B' equal on the n-neighborhood Z^n of a source
region Z, with both fields bounded by b, one has

  ||T(B)-T(B')||_{E(Z)} <= C b exp(-kappa_cl n).           (2.4)

Indeed any strict support S producing a difference has a point outside
Z^n, so tau({v} union S)>n for every output v in Z. Bound that
coefficient difference by twice its norm and sum (2.2), then take
the maximum over v in Z. There is NO |Z| factor in this step.

3. THE SOURCE BALL AND A SHELL CONSTRUCTION

Write r_i for the common physical input radius, comparable to
min(alpha_0,i,alpha_1,i). The homogeneous source-domain inclusion
proved separately from CMP109 pp262--263 is used in the following
precise form: an independent pair (A,J), with the complete physical
C^1 potential and current supremum smaller than c_src r_i, gives
(exp(i eta A),J) in the source domain on every required collar.
This is a convex product ball in (A,J), so it also contains straight
segments between smaller pairs. It is the only source-domain input
needed below. Its constant must be uniform as r_i shrinks.
This input is now derived in work/source_response_inputs_2026-10-18.txt
from the primary source's explicit reconstruction bounds; it is not
being assumed solely from the fixed-radii Notebook41 statement.

Put f_X(B)=R_X(T(B))-R_X(0). Choose b_i=c r_i, with c fixed so small
that (2.2), and straight segments between the resulting pairs, lie
in a fixed fraction of the independent source ball on ||B||<2b_i.
Banach Cauchy on that ball yields

  ||D R_X|| <= C A_X/r_i.                                (3.1)

Choose integer D_X including the collar. Set H_{X,n} to the current
graph ball centered at x_X with radius D_X+n, and let P_H B retain
B inside H and set it to zero elsewhere. Define

  f_{X,0}(B)=f_X(P_{H_{X,0}}B),
  f_{X,n}(B)=f_X(P_{H_{X,n}}B)-f_X(P_{H_{X,n-1}}B), n>=1.
                                                               (3.2)

On a torus the balls eventually fill the graph and further terms
are zero. Every coefficient depends STRICTLY only on B in H_{X,n}.
The coarse truncation P_H need not be smooth: the minimizer theorem
takes arbitrary coarse supremum data and returns a complete smooth
fine field. No sharp fine field has been differentiated.

By (2.4), (3.1) and b_i/r_i=O(1),

  ||f_{X,n}||_{2b_i} <= C A_X exp(-kappa_cl n),            (3.3)

with an increased constant also covering n=0. The sum over n equals
f_X(B) exactly. On finite tori it is a finite telescoping identity;
the displayed bounds also give regulator-uniform absolute sums.

Let a=(a_1,...,a_k) be an ordered tuple of fixed-dimensional current
scalar coordinates. On ||B||<b_i, Cauchy gives

  ||partial_a f_{X,n}||_{b_i}
       <= C_k A_X r_i^(-k) exp(-kappa_cl n),              (3.4)

and that derivative is zero unless every a_j is in H_{X,n}.
Repeated coordinates are allowed. A fixed k uses a fixed C_k; no
all-degree coordinate expansion is being summed.

THEOREM 2 (uniform rooted diameter-jet conversion).
For every fixed p>=0 and integer k>=0, there exists mu>0 and C_{p,k},
independent of r_i, M_ref, R_i and compatible volume, such that

 sup_z sum_{X,n:H_{X,n} contains z}
   (1+|H_{X,n}|)^p exp(mu diam H_{X,n})
   sum_{a_1,...,a_k in H_{X,n}} ||partial_a f_{X,n}||_{b_i}
                         <= C_{p,k} epsilon_i r_i^(-k). (3.5)

For k=0 the innermost sum means the single function norm. Finite sums
over 0<=k<=k_* obey the corresponding sum of bounds. One may keep the
birth label X on the pieces or add pieces with equal H; both preserve
the estimate. Individual shell pieces need not be gauge invariant;
their total retains the source scalar's gauge invariance.

Proof. A graph ball in four dimensions obeys
|H_{X,n}|<=C(1+D_X+n)^4 and diam H_{X,n}<=2(D_X+n).
There are at most C_k |H|^k scalar-coordinate k-tuples, with a fixed
color/orientation factor. Choose delta,mu>0 such that

  2mu+2delta < kappa_cl/2,
  C mathfrak M(2mu+2delta) < kappa_s/4.                  (3.6)

When z in H, insert the elementary bound
1<=exp(-delta dist(z,x_X)) exp(delta(D_X+n)).
Pay the fixed polynomial (1+D_X+n)^(4(p+k)) with a further
exp(delta(D_X+n)) and a fixed constant. Use (1.1) to pay all
remaining D_X exponentials with kappa_s d_i(X)/4. The n sum is
bounded by a convergent geometric series using (3.6). The remaining
sum of source domains anchored at x_X is bounded by (1.2), leaving
sum_x exp(-delta dist(z,x)), uniformly bounded on periodic tori.
Insert (3.4). This proves (3.5). QED.

Thus k=2 supplies an exponentially summable quadratic response kernel,
and polynomial spatial moments such as the Maxwell second moment are
bounded by C epsilon_i r_i^-2. Gauge invariance of the total yields
its usual Ward identities; tensor/scalar extraction still requires
the relevant complete spatial symmetries. The theorem alone does not
identify the coefficient or its sign.

4. ACTUAL ONE-STEP FLUCTUATION INSERTIONS

Fix the primitive ell and use the exact unrooted scalar chart F_M(B,t)
of Notebook42, retaining all original kernel weights. Define

  G_X(B,t)=f_X(F_M(B,t))-f_X(F_M(B,0)).                   (4.1)

There are c_1,c_2>0, fixed independently of i,M_ref and volume, such
that this is holomorphic and bounded by 2A_X on
||B||<c_1 r_i, ||t||<c_2 r_i. This first uses a conservative common
one-step ball; it does not assert the larger fixed-block ball here.

The joint map (B,t) -> T(F_M(B,t)) has strict complete-output families
with bound C(||B||+||t||) on a fixed outer ball. To see this, root
and unroot by the actual finite-range contour maps, use the actual
local pivot chart, and use the classical strict supports for the
stationary prefix and T. Group the retained t cells and their coarse
B cell in an ell-block; its coordinate count is fixed at fixed ell.
The local Banach-valued Taylor majorants and cl-substitution apply
to the joint field. Spatial lengths change by fixed ell factors only.
Hence (2.4) holds for this joint map, with a fixed positive exponent.

Apply (3.2) to joint truncations of B AND t, and subtract the same
truncated expression at t=0. This gives strict functions G_{X,n}
with G_{X,n}(B,0)=0 and

  ||G_{X,n}|| <= C A_X exp(-kappa_1 n).                  (4.2)

The support geometry and rooted summation are those of Theorem 2,
now in fixed ell-blocks with both species as spectators in the support.
This is an actual local fluctuation substitution, not an independently
postulated source-current extension.

For real t=gx, with g||x|| on the retained domain less than a fixed
fraction of c_2 r_i, write exactly

  G_{X,n}(B,gx)=g L_{X,n}(B,x)+g^2 Q_{g;X,n}(B,x),
  L_{X,n}=D_t G_{X,n}(B,0)[x],
  Q_{g;X,n}=int_0^1(1-s)D_t^2G_{X,n}(B,sgx)[x,x] ds.    (4.3)

For |J|<=2 background derivatives, Cauchy and (4.2) give

 ||partial_B^J L|| <= C_J A_X e^-kappa_1 n
                                      r_i^(-|J|-1)||x_H||,
 ||partial_B^J Q_g|| <= C_J A_X e^-kappa_1 n
                                      r_i^(-|J|-2)||x_H||^2.
                                                               (4.4)

Use any fixed coordinate norm for x_H; below its Euclidean norm is
convenient. One x-gradient of L has the same coefficient and no x.
One x-gradient of Q has both terms

  O(A_X e^-kappa_1 n r_i^(-|J|-2)) ||x_H||,
  O(g A_X e^-kappa_1 n r_i^(-|J|-3)) ||x_H||^2.           (4.5)

The second term uses a fifth mixed derivative when |J|=2. It is not
omitted. Since r_i<=1 and g/r_i is bounded in the retained regime,
both (4.4)--(4.5) are bounded by C A_X e^-kappa_1 n r_i^-4
times 1+||x_H||+||x_H||^2. Coordinate sums for J and the x-gradient
cost only a fixed additional polynomial in |H|.

COROLLARY 3 (localized real marked-observable input).
Assume the actual retained support obeys g||x||_infinity<=c r_i,
with c chosen inside the fractional t-domain of (4.1), and take B
inside the specified fractional B-domain. Suppose further that the
actual reference measure on those retained coordinates has
sup_a ||x_a||_{L^32}<=C_32, uniformly in regulator. Then the joint
supported family (4.3), all |J|<=2 background derivatives and one
x-gradient of each have rooted L^8 norms

 sup_z sum_{J,H containing z and J}
     |H|^3 exp(mu diam H)
       [||F_{J,H}||_8+||grad_x F_{J,H}||_8]
                              <= C epsilon_i r_i^-4,     (4.6)

for F=L and F=Q_g, with fixed positive mu. The gradient can use its
Euclidean norm, or the stronger sum of component L^8 norms, by
spending another polynomial support factor in Theorem 2.

Proof. Minkowski gives || ||x_H||_2 ||_32<=C|H|^1/2, and therefore
|| ||x_H||_2^2 ||_8<=C|H|, without independence. Insert these in
(4.4)--(4.5). Sum the finitely many J coordinate slots and gradient
components. Theorem 2 allows any fixed polynomial support weight,
which pays these extra powers. Its geometric sums use a possibly
smaller fixed mu but never a volume-dependent constant. QED.

The moment premise in this corollary is exactly a premise about the
actual real law. A uniform Gaussian coordinate-moment estimate suffices;
uniform convexity/locality methods may supply it for a perturbed law.
The support premise is separate: moment bounds alone do not keep the
analytic Taylor segment sgx inside the birth ball. For an older mark,
establishing it may require the distinct startup-radius mechanism.
No complex-coupling disc or full source decoupling-polydisc norm is
needed to establish this birth input.

5. RELATION TO FIXED-BLOCK DOMAIN GROWTH AND REMAINING WORK

Theorem 2 constructs a genuine rooted supported current-field family
on a fixed fraction of the birth radius. It meets the finite-order
diameter norm required by the real second-background response route.
The joint version supplies the centered actual one-step observable,
including its fifth-derivative contribution. These results remove the
initial source-to-physical-jet localization gap for homogeneous births.

All spatial sums in Theorem 2 are on the BIRTH-scale unit lattice.
Section 4 makes one primitive step from that scale, so its geometric
rescaling costs only fixed ell factors. Uniformity in M_ref refers to
the prior ultraviolet refinement, not arbitrary birth age. At a much
later scale j, one current cell contains h^-4 birth-scale cells with
h=ell^(i-j). Such an anchor multiplicity must be retained and, where
appropriate, canceled by the canonical h^4 scaling of a curvature
quadratic observable. Theorem 2 does not establish that historical
rescaling or a sum over old births at their later common scale.

Notebook42's fixed-block gain proves a larger field domain modulo a
controlled holomorphic gauge. It does not by itself propagate the
above localized norm through a quantum conditional expectation.
Two distinct further tasks remain: the real measure must satisfy the
uniform moment/convexity and background-score bounds used in the
response estimate, and that estimate must preserve a finite collection
of derivative norms for every descendant, with sufficiently small
per-step amplification. The birth estimate C epsilon_i r_i^-4 pays
a FIXED power at entry; it must not be charged anew at every step.

For full analytic source-prepared closure, independent-current and
decoupling extensions remain a separate issue. Our shell functions
are physical current-field functions and generally fail individual
gauge invariance. Their sum is the correct gauge-invariant scalar.
This is deliberate and adequate for the indicated real derivative
norm, but it is not an assertion that the pieces meet every hypothesis
of the published E-sector preparation theorem.
