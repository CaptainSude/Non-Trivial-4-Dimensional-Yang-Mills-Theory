Callan--Symanzik analysis based on perturbative calculations will be
discussed in another paper. Printed259 separates the two tasks:
Theorem1 proves the effective-action bounds CONDITIONALLY on all

a choice of bare coupling for each cutoff with the final coupling
fixed at a prescribed small g, all intermediate couplings in that
weak interval, and the additional quantitative bound(0.31).
Its proof is explicitly deferred to a separate paper. The short
remark on printed260 says the SU(2) restriction simplifies the
calculation; that remark is not a proof for another group.

4. THE PRECISE MISSING INPUT FOR OUR NORMALIZATION

Writing X_j=g_j^-2, define the exact signed native increment by
b_j=X_j-X_(j+1), using the actual completed Wilson coefficient,
normalizers, current cut and inherited effective action. A useful
replacement theorem must control these ACTUAL increments uniformly
in grade, UV depth and the allowed history of the homogeneous flow,
with the correct positive leading coefficient and a quantitatively
smaller remainder. It must also establish the parameter dependence
needed to tune the bare coupling to the prescribed final value.
For example a uniform asymptotic estimate b_j=b_*+o(1), b_*>0,
with the needed continuity/solvability and exact cutoff matching,
would be a plausible route; no such estimate is proved here.

The scalar bound |b_j|<=C E0 supplied by a local action catalogue
does not fix the sign and does not by itself tune arbitrary depth
to a nonzero fixed terminal coupling. The standard perturbative
Yang--Mills beta coefficient, even in another rigorous scheme,
does not establish a uniform remainder for Balaban's nonperturbative
recursive coefficients with our actual cutoffs and scalar ledger.
Changing a cutoff, a coupling convention or a marginal extraction
requires an explicit comparison, not an appeal to universality.

Accordingly the homogeneous action, large-product mean and marked
source results from this pass remain conditional on the declared
weak trajectory. This check changes none of their finite conclusions
and closes no continuum-existence gate.
