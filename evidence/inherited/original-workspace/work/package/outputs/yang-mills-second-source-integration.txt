RESULT

Two opposite parallel raw links sharing one plaquette give an actual
eleven-plaquette Wilson fiber. Its two integrations can be performed in
sequence while keeping both the changed action and the old action source.
The second conditional law is explicitly not a Wilson one-link law.

For all exterior staples in a fixed sufficiently small neighborhood of
a common unit quaternion, the full conditional mean of the ORIGINAL
eleven-plaquette action, after subtracting its flat-exterior value, agrees
with its actual classical conditional minimum with a relative error
O(beta^-2). The constants are uniform over that neighborhood and over
beta>=beta_0. In particular, the same O(beta^-2) protection holds for the
quadratic exterior coefficient after the second interacting integration.
The external fields in the exponent have size approximately 5 beta;
they are not the bounded-amplitude regime of TURN96 Section3A.

This is a two-link weak-exterior theorem, not a full block induction or
a cutoff-uniform correlation estimate. The generated action is retained

1. THE ACTUAL WILSON FIBER AND THE CHANGED SECOND ACTION

Choose two opposite parallel links of one elementary plaquette on an
ordinary finite four-dimensional Wilson lattice, with periods at least
four. They share exactly that plaquette. Each belongs to five additional
plaquettes, giving eleven distinct plaquettes in their union. Condition
on every other original link and take the constant profile on this union.
An additive source from exterior-only plaquettes can be restored as an
exterior-measurable summand; it is not part of the fiber energy below.

If the shared plaquette is Q A R^{-1} B^{-1}, put x=Q and y=B R A^{-1}.
Both are unit quaternions. Left/right multiplication preserves Haar, and

 (1/2)Tr(Q A R^{-1} B^{-1})=x dot y.

Each private plaquette is linear in the appropriate quaternion variable.
There are unit exterior staples a_i,b_j in S^3, i,j=1,...,5, such that,
with a=sum_i a_i and b=sum_j b_j, the original touched action is exactly

 S_t(x,y)=11-x dot y-a dot x-b dot y
        =(1-x dot y)+sum_i(1-a_i dot x)+sum_j(1-b_j dot y). (1.1)

Here t denotes the actual exterior staple data after the indicated Haar
coordinate change. Correlations among those data caused by the original
exterior geometry do not affect any assertion below: it is a theorem for
all data in the stated neighborhood, hence also for the realizable ones.
The conditional probability and normalized original-source mean are

 Z_beta(t)=int exp[-beta S_t(x,y)] dx dy,
 A_beta(t)=Z_beta(t)^(-1) int S_t exp[-beta S_t] dx dy.
                                                               (1.2)

All Haar measures are normalized. Write z(s)=2 I_1(s)/s, z(0)=1, and
m(s)=z'(s)/z(s)=I_2(s)/I_1(s). The FIRST integration is exact:

 Z_beta(t)=exp(-11 beta) int exp(beta b dot y)
                                  z(beta|a+y|) dy,              (1.3)
 J_beta(t,y)=E[S_t(x,y)|y]
           =11-b dot y-|a+y|m(beta|a+y|).                        (1.4)

The actual second probability is

 rho_beta,t(dy)=exp(beta b dot y)z(beta|a+y|)dy /
                     int exp(beta b dot y)z(beta|a+y|)dy,
 A_beta(t)=int J_beta(t,y)rho_beta,t(dy).                         (1.5)

The new effective energy is

 E_eff,beta(t,y)=11-b dot y-beta^(-1)log z(beta|a+y|),

and the old source is J_beta=partial_beta[beta E_eff,beta], not merely
E_eff,beta. The logarithmic Bessel term and its beta derivative cannot be
discarded in the second integration.

For a differentiable exterior path the exact response, wherever written
with r=|a+y|>0, is

 d_t A_beta=E_rho[d_t J_beta]
  +Cov_rho(J_beta, beta dot b dot y+beta m(beta r) dot r).         (1.6)

Thus the action score and the normalizer are both present. Near the flat
data considered below |a| is close to five, so r is bounded away from zero
for EVERY y in S^3. Globally (1.3)--(1.5) still have their smooth continuous
interpretation at r=0.

If F is integrable and measurable outside these two raw links, then

 Cov_original(F,S_t)=Cov_original(F,A_beta(t)).                   (1.7)

This is the conditional-expectation identity in the same original Wilson
measure. It permits subtraction of the constant A_beta(flat). It does
not make arbitrary history ownership or deciding labels exterior
measurable.

2. PRECISE NEAR-FLAT THEOREM

Let n=(1,0,0,0). Use tangent coordinates

 a_i=(sqrt(1-|u_i|^2),u_i), b_j=(sqrt(1-|v_j|^2),v_j),

 D(t)=min_(x,y in S^3) S_t(x,y),

There exist epsilon>0, beta_0>=1, and finite C, depending only on this

 (i) the minimizer is unique and smooth in t, D is smooth, D>=0;

     uniformly beta>=1 after enlarging C if necessary;

     |T_beta(t)-D(t)|<=C beta^(-2)D(t).                        (2.2)

Consequently, for beta sufficiently large,

 (1-C beta^-2)D(t)<=T_beta(t)<=(1+C beta^-2)D(t).                (2.3)

No typical-field statement is made here. The neighborhood is an actual

3. UNIQUE MINIMUM, THE CLASSICAL QUADRATIC FORM, AND ITS ZERO MODES

At t=0,

 S_0(x,y)=(1-x dot y)+5(1-n dot x)+5(1-n dot y)
        >=(5/2)(|x-n|^2+|y-n|^2).

Its unique minimum is (n,n), with value zero. In the six tangent variables
(xi,eta) of x,y, the Hessian is

 H=[[6 I_3,-I_3],[-I_3,6 I_3]],

with eigenvalues 5 and 7, each of multiplicity three. In a sufficiently
small fixed chart its Hessian remains uniformly positive under small
t. The implicit function theorem gives a unique smooth critical point
there. The displayed global gap at t=0, compactness, and a small uniform
perturbation exclude any global minimum outside that chart. This proves
(i), including a uniform gap away from a smaller minimum neighborhood.

Put U=sum_i u_i, V=sum_j v_j. Minimizing the quadratic expansion gives

 D^(2)(t)=1/2 sum_i|u_i|^2+1/2 sum_j|v_j|^2
           -[6(|U|^2+|V|^2)+2 U dot V]/70.                   (3.1)

Indeed the quadratic energy before minimization is

 1/2 |xi-eta|^2+1/2 sum_i|xi-u_i|^2

and H^{-1}=(1/35)[[6 I_3,I_3],[I_3,6 I_3]]. The kernel of (3.1)
consists exactly of u_i=v_j=w for one common w. These are the common
staple rotations. On a slice fixing a_1=n, the form is positive definite.

More exactly, every summand in (1.1) is nonnegative. Hence D(t)=0 iff

4. PARAMETER-UNIFORM LAPLACE PROOF FOR THE ORIGINAL SOURCE

We include the argument to specify the beta derivative control that is
actually needed. Smooth parameter-dependent Morse coordinates z in R^6
near the unique minimum put the energy exactly into the form

 S_t=D(t)+|z|^2/2.

One construction starts with centered local coordinates w at the smooth
minimum, writes S_t-D(t)=w^T K(w,t)w/2 by the integral Hessian formula,
and sets z=K(w,t)^(1/2)w. The uniformly positive matrix square root is
smooth, and its derivative at w=0 is invertible uniformly in t. The
inverse function theorem gives a common coordinate neighborhood. The
product Haar density in these coordinates is a positive smooth B(z,t),
with B(0,t) uniformly bounded away from zero and with uniformly bounded
derivatives of every fixed finite order.

Insert a fixed radial cutoff chi(z), supported in that neighborhood and
equal to one in a smaller ball. The complement has S_t-D(t)>=delta>0
uniformly. Its integrals, and two t derivatives, are bounded by a fixed
polynomial in beta times exp(-delta beta). This applies to both the
partition function and its energy-excess numerator; it also covers
derivatives of the moving local chart/cutoff when returned to x,y.

After z=beta^(-1/2)w, radial symmetry removes the odd Taylor terms of B.
The standard Gaussian moment identities, obtained by one-dimensional
integration by parts, give in dimension six

 e^(beta D)Z_beta
  =(2 pi)^3 beta^-3 [ B_0+(Delta_z B)_0/(2 beta)+O_C2(beta^-2)],

 e^(beta D) int (S_t-D)exp(-beta S_t) dx dy
  =(2 pi)^3 beta^-4 [3 B_0+2(Delta_z B)_0/beta+O_C2(beta^-2)].
                                                               (4.1)

Normalized-Haar constants are already in B. Taylor remainders of order
four, with two t derivatives, are bounded by C|z|^4. Gaussian fourth
moments control the remainders in (4.1); the cutoff error is exponential.
The positive lower bound on B_0 permits division with two parameter
derivatives. The ratio is

 A_beta(t)-D(t)=3/beta+
               [(Delta_z B)_0/(2 B_0)]/beta^2+O_C2(beta^-3).

This proves (2.1), with c_1=(Delta_z B)_0/(2 B_0). In particular, the
beta-independent one-loop log-determinant makes no beta^-1 contribution
to the flat-subtracted original source. The universal dimension term
3/beta cancels when its flat value is subtracted. There is no inference
from a beta-undifferentiated partition-function error: the energy
numerator was estimated directly.

An independent flat-point calculation by block97 makes the scalar

5. WHY THE REMAINDER IS RELATIVE TO D, WITHOUT A PER-FIBER CONSTANT

Both D and A_beta are invariant under a simultaneous orthogonal rotation

A_beta is exactly equal to A_beta(0) on the common-staple zero manifold.

Choose a smooth orthogonal rotation sending a_1 to n near a_1=n. It
puts the remaining nine staples into small tangent coordinates w in
R^27, while fixing the first staple to n. On this local slice define

 E_beta(w)=A_beta(w)-A_beta(0)-D(w).

From (2.1), ||E_beta||_C2<=C beta^-2. Exactly E_beta(0)=0. The subgroup

Therefore grad_w E_beta(0)=0 for every beta, independently of the
asymptotic expansion. Taylor's theorem yields

 |E_beta(w)|<=C beta^-2 |w|^2.

The positive definite classical Hessian (3.1) on this slice gives
D(w)>=c|w|^2 after shrinking the neighborhood. Combining the two proves
(2.2). This argument is uniform over the common-staple rotations; it
does not introduce a spurious cost along their exact zero directions.

In these quotient coordinates one also gets a genuine response estimate

 |grad_w[A_beta(w)-D(w)]|<=C beta^-2 |w|,                   (5.1)

because subtraction of A_beta(0) does not affect a derivative. Thus the
conditional-source response agrees with the actual classical response
