ROOTED-FOREST BOX GEOMETRY: EXPLICIT RECTANGLES AND A POLYNOMIAL POINCARE BOUND
19 October 2026. balaban_inputs. Independent audit of root's turn37 lemma.

VERDICT

The requested geometry is valid for the full open cubical box, with all
boundary vertices anchored and all boundary edges fixed. In fact each
needed filling is a simple coordinate rectangle using DISTINCT elementary
plaquettes. This proves the stronger estimates

 max_chord dist(y_e,1) <= C R sqrt(S_V(y)) + C R delta,
 ||x||_2^2 <= C R^6 ||d x||_2^2 at V=1,                     (0.1)

in dimension four, with unscaled link/plaquette counting norms. Therefore
the proposed weaker R^2 and R^8 estimates also hold. No native effective
R kernel, continuum limit or uniform growing-graph Laplace expansion is
asserted by this geometric result.

1. EXACT GRAPH AND FOREST

Take vertices {0,...,R}^4 with integer R>=2. A boundary vertex has at least
one coordinate 0 or R. Every boundary vertex is anchored; boundary edges
are edges contained in the cubical boundary. The free/nonboundary edges
have at least one interior endpoint. With unit nearest-neighbor edges
and R>=2, an edge with two boundary endpoints is contained in a common
boundary face, so these conventions agree.

For each transverse t=(x2,x3,x4) in {1,...,R-1}^3 include the edges

           (k-1,t)--(k,t),             k=1,...,R-1,

in the forest. The component is rooted at (0,t). All other boundary
vertices are isolated forest roots. Thus every forest component contains
EXACTLY ONE anchored vertex, and every interior vertex is covered.
There is no edge from (R-1,t) to (R,t) in the forest: including it would
put two boundary anchors in the same component and would be invalid.

A gauge transformation equal to identity at all boundary vertices sets
the forest links to identity recursively. Boundary links remain the
prescribed V. Write y_e for the transformed nonforest free links.
All paths below are interpreted with their induced orientations; reversing
an edge replaces its holonomy by its inverse and preserves its distance
to identity.

2. EXPLICIT FILLINGS FOR EVERY FREE CHORD

There are precisely the following nonforest free-edge cases.

(A) Transverse interior edge, direction mu in {2,3,4}.
Both endpoints are interior and have the same x1=s, 1<=s<=R-1.
Their forest roots lie at x1=0 and are joined by the corresponding one
boundary edge. The two forest paths, e, and that boundary connector bound
the x1--xmu rectangle of width s and height one. It has s<=R plaquettes.

(B) Transverse edge from an interior vertex to a side boundary, direction
mu in {2,3,4}, with xmu reaching 0 or R. The boundary endpoint is its own
anchored root. Join the interior root to the corresponding side-face
point at x1=0 by one boundary edge, then run in the x1 direction along
that side face to the boundary endpoint. The connector has length s+1.
Together with the one interior forest path and e it bounds the same
width-s, height-one coordinate rectangle, with s<=R plaquettes.

(C) Top x1 edge, from (R-1,t) to (R,t), t strictly interior transversely.
The latter endpoint is an anchored boundary root. The forest path plus
e runs along the entire line from (0,t) to (R,t). Take the boundary
connector from (R,t) along the top face to x2=0, then down the side face
x2=0 to x1=0, then along the bottom face back to (0,t). Its length is
R+2t2<=3R. The resulting loop bounds the x1--x2 rectangle

                    [0,R] x [0,t2]

with x3,x4 fixed. It has R t2<=R^2 plaquettes.

Bottom x1 edges are already forest edges. All remaining x1 edges either
belong to the forest or are fixed boundary edges. These cases exhaust
every free chord, including those with one boundary endpoint.

Each filling plaquette is NOT wholly in the cubical boundary. In cases
(A),(B), the x1 and xmu coordinates vary and all other coordinates are
strictly interior. In(C), x1 and x2 vary and x3,x4 are strictly interior.
Thus every filling plaquette belongs to the action depending on the free
links. Pure-boundary action constants need not be included in S_V.

3. NONABELIAN PRODUCT ESTIMATE

Use a faithful unitary representation and its normalized Hilbert--Schmidt
distance d(U,1)=||U-1||. It is invariant under conjugation and obeys

                d(UV,1)<=d(U,1)+d(V,1).

Any fixed bi-invariant group distance is comparable to it on this compact
group, with constants depending only on the group/representation.
For a rectangular plaquette disk, successively peel boundary plaquettes
(or use a spanning tree in its dual grid). The perimeter holonomy is a
product of conjugates of the elementary plaquette holonomies or their
inverses, with EACH elementary plaquette appearing once. The connecting
transports need not be small: conjugation invariance is enough. Hence

 d(U_boundary(rectangle),1)
        <=sum_(p in rectangle) d(U_p,1)
        <=sqrt(number of p) [sum_(p in rectangle)d(U_p,1)^2]^(1/2).

In the forest gauge the same perimeter holonomy is y_e times its fixed
boundary connector C_e(V), up to orientation. If

 delta=max_(boundary edge b) d(V_b,1),

then d(C_e(V),1)<=3R delta. The Wilson action S_V containing every
plaquette that reads a free edge is a fixed positive multiple of
sum_p d(U_p,1)^2 in this representation. Since each rectangle has at most
R^2 distinct plaquettes, the preceding inequalities prove

                   d(y_e,1)<=C R sqrt(S_V(y))+3R delta.     (3.1)

The total action may also include fixed boundary plaquettes; this only
enlarges its right-hand side. If a convention subtracts the actual
minimum action from S_V, (3.1) does NOT follow with that centered action:
the displayed proof uses the nonnegative uncentered plaquette sum.

4. LINEARIZATION AT IDENTITY BOUNDARY

Set V=1 and y_e(t)=exp(t x_e), with x zero on the forest and boundary.
Linearizing the exact rectangular perimeter identity gives

                 x_e = sum_(p in rectangle_e) +/- (d x)_p.

The Lie algebra is a vector space at this order; the transports all equal
identity. Therefore

              |x_e|^2 <= R^2 sum_p |(d x)_p|^2.

There are at most C R^4 free chords. Summing this bound gives

                  ||x||_2^2<=C R^6 ||d x||_2^2.             (4.1)

The Hessian on this anchored forest slice is thus strictly positive with
a polynomial lower bound c R^-6 in these unscaled counting norms.
The weaker c R^-8 claimed in the candidate follows immediately. Constants
also include the fixed positive invariant quadratic form from the chosen
faithful Wilson representation. No monotonicity or continuum Poincare
theorem is used.

5. SCOPE QUALIFICATIONS

The boundary must be anchored at EVERY fixed exterior vertex; the forest
above satisfies this exactly. Prescribed nonidentity boundary values are
allowed in(3.1), and their connector contribution remains C R delta.
The linear estimate(4.1) is at V=1. Obtaining a uniform Hessian bound near
a nonzero boundary-dependent minimum requires a separate perturbation
estimate with its R dependence; it is not part of this audit.

The claim is for a full embedded cubical box. Holes, periodic local
identifications, irregular deleted subcomplexes, or a forest joining two
anchored vertices are different geometries. They are not covered.
