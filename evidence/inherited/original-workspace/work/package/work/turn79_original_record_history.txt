RESULT

The original-record route is viable for a fixed elementary Wilson trace,
but its missing step cannot be replaced by keeping ALL old variables real.
There is an exact obstruction to such a replacement at the native coarse
constraint, proved in Section3. It is an obstruction to an all-observable
finite-total-variation real-record representation on a complex external
domain, not a counterexample to the desired fixed one/two-plaquette bound.

There is an actual finite-block advance: the stronger nested-domain
statement on Large-I printed191 proves sup-norm-preserving pullback of an
already-admitted ORIGINAL source through one uninterrupted accepted
strip-integration sequence, before its integrations. It applies to the
complete source and its product, retaining the old record; it is not an
estimate for a newly chosen trace of the entrance field. Section5 proves
this statement directly from the native maps.

Two further facts narrow the remaining step. A native background-removal
shift has a bounded analytic group-matrix effect with no inverse power of
g, although its Lie-algebra coordinate displacement contains H/g. Also,
an original coordinate is not redrawn when a new reference coordinate is
introduced: its inverse record is kept, and an uninterrupted regular
ancestry moves through proper scales. The unresolved map is the complete
composition of the ACTUAL constraint-flattening/minimum reads with the
tiny rejected-branch background shifts and retained finer determining
rows. Final unmarked holomorphy does not supply that composition.

This note does not change the TURN78 real posterior/deletion result or
claim a full-history complex trace bound. It gives an exact native reason
why the reconstruction is necessary, a quantitative primitive bound, and
the literal first inclusion that a successful proof must establish.

2. THE ACTUAL OLD/NEW RECORD AND WHAT CAN STAY REAL

In an admitted positive branch the old source is inserted before its
first read is eliminated. A basic R step has the old integration and a
normalized reference draw. They are different coordinates. Retaining
the old one as a passive record gives the exact original numerator, as
in TURN60/74/78. It does not identify the source with the fresh reference.

Literal old group variables still integrated over a background-independent
raw Haar domain can remain real. On those coordinates a closed SU(2)
plaquette has 0<=1-Tr(U_p)/2<=2. This is the real/private mechanism already
used in TURN73 and the positive posterior estimate in TURN78.

The word 'original' is essential. After (1.53) and constraint flattening,
holding the new B_j real does not hold the original V_j real for complex
V^(j). Conversely, fixing V_j real would generally make the relative
coordinate or its cut depend on the external complex field. The fixed
real-domain representation used for (1.73) has already resolved this
issue for the unmarked integrand. Its inverse map must still be used for
the original source. One cannot keep both coordinate systems real merely
by renaming their integration variables.

An original variable is eliminated once in the chronological integration
DAG. Along an uninterrupted regular inverse ancestry, a j-field is
reconstructed from its fluctuation and genuinely coarser data. Global
waiting dates do not introduce additional independent copies of that
same old variable. But this observation alone is not a strict-age proof
for the whole source: (1.1)--(1.3) also read retained finer/raw determining
rows, and a rejected-branch inverse contributes (1.2). Those side reads
must be included. One may not replace the actual proper-layer labels by
the global date at every occurrence.

3. A PRECISE OBSTRUCTION TO AN ALL-REAL COMPLEX LIFT

The native regular transformation has an exact coarse constraint

                         M(U)=V.                         (3.1)

For real old fields U, M(U) is group valued. The flattening in Large-I
printed187 is precisely one way to continue this constrained integral
analytically when V is complex. A real constrained configuration cannot
satisfy (3.1) when V is not in SU(2).

There is a stronger operator statement, which also rules out replacing
the constraint by a finite complex measure on ALL-REAL old fields while
retaining its action on all analytic insertions. Let chi_l be the SU(2)
character of its irreducible representation of dimension l+1 and put

                 f_l(U)=chi_l(M(U))/(l+1).

On real U, |f_l(U)|<=1. The literal coarse constraint gives the normalized
conditional insertion chi_l(V)/(l+1). Choose the arbitrarily small
complex external displacement V_t=diag(exp(t),exp(-t)), t>0. Then

 chi_l(V_t)/(l+1)=sinh((l+1)t)/[(l+1)sinh(t)] -> infinity. (3.2)

If a finite-total-variation complex measure mu_t on real old U represented
this normalized constrained operator for every such insertion, its total
variation would bound the left side of (3.2) for every l, a contradiction.
All statements concern one finite regulator and a small admissible complex
direction. A chosen unconstrained coarse row can be used; gauge fixing
does not remove the underlying averaging identity.

This argument is not a proposed counterexample to Yang--Mills bounds or
to an elementary original plaquette. Its test degree increases with l.
A fixed-degree trace may have a uniformly bounded complex reconstruction.
What is ruled out is obtaining that result as a consequence of a universal
finite-TV positive/complex old-record kernel with every old field real.
The exact coarse constraint itself requires the regular reconstruction.

4. A BACKGROUND-SHIFT PRIMITIVE HAS NO INVERSE-g TRACE LOSS

At a fixed real integration coordinate b the native switched BCH map has
the group form, with the appropriate orientation sign,

 exp(i g Phi_H(b))=exp(i g b) exp(+- i s(b)H),
                         0<=s(b)<=1.                    (4.1)

The sign is immaterial here. For complex H and real b,

 ||exp(i g Phi_H(b))||, ||exp(-i g Phi_H(b))||
                                      <=exp(||H||).      (4.2)

This is immediate from unitarity of exp(i g b) and the matrix exponential
bound. In particular, the coordinate displacement O(H/g) is NOT an
O(H/g) distortion of the link matrix. It has become O(H). The switch is
a bounded real number in this estimate, so its lack of complex analyticity
in b causes no difficulty for analyticity in H. The native real chart and
its unchanged cut are retained, rather than inferred from a periodic
exponential inequality alone.

For a product of four link factors which are unitary except for such
analytic perturbations with total matrix exponents L_p,

                 |1-Tr(U_p)/2| <=1+exp(L_p).             (4.3)

The reversed-link factors use the corresponding inverse bound. A genuine
site gauge transformation on the FULL old plaquette cancels exactly in
the trace. Separate nonmatching frame substitutions do not. All closing
links and the original inverse gauge record have to be present before
using this cancellation.

The flattening in (1.3) is not removed by (4.1). On an actual admitted
native chart on which C and Dtilde have their uniform local derivative
bounds, a perturbation g Delta b=O(H) changes
g C b-h Dtilde(g C b) by O(H), with its native fixed constants.
This gives the same absence of a naked inverse-g loss through ONE such
flattening. The minimum/current composition and all its external domains
remain to be checked. In particular, it is not legitimate to cancel
exp(-iH) against the background in (1.2) as if C were the identity and
Dtilde absent. Nor is failure of that simple cancellation evidence of a
large error: (4.2) is already the useful small bound.

Applied to the actual H_Z^(j) in (1.2), this isolated correction costs
exp[C epsilon_j exp(-R_j)] per affected link, rather than
exp[C epsilon_j exp(-R_j)/g_j]. On its given external analytic domain
this bound is uniform in the real switched integration variable. The
Jacobian, transformed action and all old normalizations are still part
of the native measure; (4.2) is an observable estimate, not a measure
replacement or a new source-dependent normalization.

4A. REPEATED TINY H VALUES ARE NUMERICALLY SUMMABLE

There is a useful consequence of the literal bound (1.2), stronger than
merely saying that each shift is small. Let gamma_j=g_j^{-2}, and retain
the actual weak-trajectory envelope

 gamma_j>=G+d(N-j), d>0,  epsilon_j<=C gamma_j^{-1/2}(log gamma_j)^p,
 R_j>=c(log gamma_j)^r, r>1.                              (4.4)

If a proper-j record participates in at most a fixed number of these
background-removal changes per chronological date, its multiplicity is
at most m_j<=C(N-j+1)<=C'(1+gamma_j). This is an explicit multiplicity
condition, not an inference that an arbitrary decorated read appears
only once. A complete source-read reconstruction has to verify it.

Because exp[-c(log x)^r] times any fixed power of log x decays faster
than every inverse power of x, (1.2) gives, for every fixed K>2,

 sum_j m_j ||H_j|| <= C_K sum_j gamma_j^(1-K)
                 <= C'_K [G^(1-K)+G^(2-K)/(d(K-2))].      (4.5)

In the first inequality K is an arbitrary chosen power, after absorbing
the fixed threshold factors into its constant. The second follows from
the decreasing envelope and the integral test. Hence the upper bound
tends to zero with G, uniformly in N. The same argument pays any fixed
polynomial price in gamma_j from ONE native derivative estimate by first
choosing a larger K. The constants may depend on that fixed price.

If these are the actual perturbations in a simultaneous group-product
representation, (4.2) bounds their combined matrix distortion by exp of
the sum in (4.5). Genuine common gauge factors cancel before this bound.
Thus repeatedly summing already-admitted H corrections is not the
numerical obstruction, and no H/g price should be inserted.

What (4.5) does NOT do is put an inadmissible partially composed argument
back into the domain on which (1.2) was proved. A finite bootstrap would
have to establish, at each old chart, that all regular response plus H
side-read perturbations stay inside its strict margin. The comparison is
with that old chart's actual radius and full current/constraint norm,
not only with the final chart's radius or a scalar link norm. The bound
above supplies the small H budget once this domain/multiplicity issue
is addressed; it does not assume that issue away.

5. ACTUAL ACCEPTED-BLOCK PULLBACK, BEFORE INTEGRATION

Consider ONE basic-R component and an uninterrupted string of accepted
strip integrations n=0,...,m-1 in Large-I(1.60). Keep every actual free
noise coordinate in a separate real slot xi_n; keep earlier passive
records xi_old as well. Let F_0(z_0,xi_old) be the ORIGINAL source
expressed in its entrance coordinates, holomorphic in z_0=(U,J) on D_0
and bounded there, uniformly in those real records, by M_F. This is an
entrance norm, not an assertion that every older original source already
has this norm. TURN78's first-partial trace supplies such an entrance
when this is its actual admitted mixed domain/read.

Use the maps Phi_n of (1.4), with their actual native cuts. For z_m in
D_m define recursively z_n=Phi_n(z_(n+1);xi_n). The explicit domain
statement on printed191 gives z_n in D_n by finite backward induction.
Consequently

 F_m(z_m,xi_old,xi_0,...,xi_(m-1))=F_0(z_0,xi_old),
                         sup|F_m|<=M_F.                 (5.1)

The composition is holomorphic in z_m. This is a POINTWISE statement
before integrating any of these retained noises. It costs no factor
rho_n^{-1} or product of inverse source radii. The proof is domain
inclusion and composition, not a deduction from the analyticity of the
final unmarked logarithm. Apply it simultaneously to a second source H_0:

       sup|H_m|<=M_H,       sup|(FH)_m|<=M_F M_H.         (5.2)

The latter is the transported complete product, including the mixed
scalar coefficient. It is not the product of two conditional means.
Neither source normalization nor the native integration weights have
changed. If one integrates with a native absolute majorant afterwards,
the source has precisely the entrance multiplier M_F or M_F M_H.

This theorem uses the complete declared source read. It does not shorten
the determining rows or change the local frames. A source whose entrance
formula is a new plaquette of z_0 rather than the original F_0 is a
different observable; (5.1) would bound it but would not identify it with
the original source. The earlier inverse record is part of F_0.

For an external localization family s the same finite induction works
IF EACH actually used Phi_n(s) has the same domain-inclusion property on

5A. THE SMOOTH SWITCH MUST NOT BE COMPOSED AT A COMPLEX ARGUMENT

There is an important limit to the one-map observation in Section4. If
one first writes Phi_H1(b_1) using a merely smooth switch s_1(b_1), and
later substitutes b_1=Phi_H2(b_2) with a nonreal value, the resulting
s_1(Phi_H2(b_2)) has no specified holomorphic meaning. Analyticity in H
at fixed REAL b does not justify such a substitution. This can obstruct
a naive simultaneous original-record formula even when each integrated
unmarked operation has a valid analytic continuation.

Within the uninterrupted accepted block above, this problem does not
arise from the NEW switch(1.59): the accepted alternative of (1.55) is
the one integrated in (1.60), whereas the switched rejected alternative
is removed from Z and does not undergo the following strips in that
basic-R block. Also the old A_j variable and the new relative B_j from
(1.53) are distinct slots. Keeping earlier inner operator variables as
their own real coordinates does not substitute a new B into an old A.
This is a concrete benefit of the literal chronology.

For a stopped component and a later global operation the inspected text
does not give a complete decorated inverse-record dictionary. Printed191
says that the stopped terms remain boundary terms and their domains are
generalized for later steps. That is a statement about the integrated
coefficient. To use a direct-record proof across this boundary one must
show that every earlier switched coordinate stays a distinct real latent
slot, with later changes acting only on its analytic background argument;
or prove a source-specific cancellation/alternative contour identity.
One cannot instead feed a complex new coordinate into its old smooth
switch. A separate real latent coordinate with an additional constraint
is not automatically an already admitted native complex integral.

Thus repeated-switch failure has not been exhibited as an unavoidable
actual native history, but neither is the needed absence of such a
substitution proved by the one-primitive H estimate. This is an explicit
cross-block chronology condition, with a clear source-specific reason.

This is a source-read condition, not a demand to reconstruct unrelated
old variables elsewhere in the volume. It includes the original
determining rows and their entire footprints, not only the final carrier.
The weakest needed quantitative consequence is a direct bound on the
INVARIANT CLOSED trace. Uniform bounds on all four open matrices in a

Even a specified growth factor charged to an UNUSED part of the same
complete-history ledger would suffice for the marked-operator consumer.
A bounded endpoint argument, separate
per-stage analyticity, or holomorphy of the final source-free coefficient
does not establish (6.1).

The first accepted-block substitutions are now covered by Section5. The
first NOT covered substitution is a stopped/restarted older coefficient's
original inverse record, or a composed localized Phi_n on circles not

There is useful structure for a proof of (6.1), but not a completed one:
regular proper-scale responses may carry their native scale reduction;

The missing input is thus narrower than a new full posterior theorem:
it is an admitted ORIGINAL finite-degree-record composition estimate for

families. Keeping the record real solves the wholly raw part but cannot
