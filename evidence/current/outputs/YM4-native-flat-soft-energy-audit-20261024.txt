1. Actual averages and mass estimates

The corrected native flat average is the componentwise straight-string
mean. Literal primitive composition has exactly the centered balanced
block digits and the ordinary segment digits, giving producer(2).
Its counting operator norm is at most K times K^-2=K^-1. This is an
operator estimate; no internal K^4 fiber is counted again.

Scalar block Poincare uses only internal block differences and proves
the mass estimate for Delta_0+mu^2 Pi. The vector proof is also sound:
translation by s unit steps differs from the identity by at most s
times the corresponding difference norm. Multiplying by ||Q_0||=K^-2
and summing s=0,...,K-1 gives a constant independent of K. Applied
componentwise, this compares the actual A with K Q_0 and gives(8).
It avoids any unproved inverse for a pinned or mean-zero ghost.

For a>=R_q+1, the coarse correction aI-q_(m+p) is at least I.
The already established q_m>=q_W then makes V>=Delta_1+A^*A.
Equation(8) supplies the vector mass. The upper estimate follows from
q_m<=C q_W and ||A||<=mu. The distinction between R_s and R_v is
essential: the elliptic vector reference includes DD^*, in addition
to the curl form. The producer makes this explicit.

2. Twists and inverse bounds

The averages and their adjoints have bounded physical displacement.
Entrywise domination of their nonnegative scalar coefficients proves
the O(|theta|) relative changes, retaining the actual mu factor for A.
The uniform analytic quadratic rows and Ward identity give a double
zero of q_m at momentum zero, and hence(12). Away from zero its
uniform strip bound gives the same inequality. Dividing by
delta(k)+mu^2 is uniformly bounded, since
mu sqrt(delta)/(delta+mu^2)<=1/2.

The complete coarse correction changes by O(mu^2 |theta|), because
both A legs keep their mu factor. The scalar projection term has the
same mass scale. This proves the energy-sandwiched perturbation(13).
The lower energy bounds give the Neumann inversion(14), without
requiring the reference energy to commute with block operators.

3. Heat trace and regulator scope

The heat-trace proof correctly uses ordered eigenvalues from the
quadratic-form lower bound, not operator monotonicity of the
exponential. The lattice heat sum in four directions is bounded by
C[1+(NK)^4/(1+K^2 u)^2]. After division by N^4 this is exactly the
displayed K^4 term plus the separate N^-4 zero-mode contribution.
The soft mass supplies exp(-c u). The estimate is valid at u=0,
for K=1, and on compatible finite tori of the declared periodization.

The producer begins with infinite-lattice kernels and their specified
periodizations. It does not identify unrelated finite-volume native
winding kernels with them. Thus there is no hidden regulator passage
in its finite heat sum. This limitation must remain when the result
is used in a beta comparison.

4. Verdict

At the stated infinite-lattice/periodized scope, the proof establishes
the flat soft scalar and vector energy bounds, their physical-weight
inverse bounds, and the undifferentiated heat trace. The two details
in Section2 above are routine clarifications, not new hypotheses.
The proof does not supply moving-reference derivatives, localized
source vertices, differentiated coarse Schur estimates, the complete
Gaussian determinant identification, or its signed logarithmic
coefficient. Multiplying a bounded inverse by its K^4-dimensional
fiber is expressly not an allowed replacement for those estimates.
