Let the even spatial periods N_i be divided into tile lengths l_i>=2

1. CANONICAL REFLECTED PLACEMENTS

In one coordinate, place the base interval[0,l] into tile b by

 T_b(u)=b l+u                 if b is even,
 T_b(u)=(b+1)l-u              if b is odd.

Take the product map in all three coordinates, including the
induced link orientation reversal/inversion. Denote the resulting
placement of a scalar block function f by theta_b f.

Reflection R across the site plane x_i=j l_i maps tile index b_i
to2j-b_i-1 modulo M_i. If M_i>=2 it is even, so this reflection
reverses its parity and

             R(theta_b f)=theta_(R b) f                 (1.1)

exactly. Thus a finite alphabet of block functions is closed under
all half-board reflections: they permute the labels and do not
create new types of function. Coordinates with M_i=1 need no
reflection or dissemination.

This orientation convention is relevant for general block
functions. For the nonnegative plaquette action and its bad
events, the reflected version is also their literal reflected
geometric copy.

2. MIXED-PATTERN THEOREM

For bounded nonnegative base-block functions f_1,...,f_r define
their full dissemination values and norms by

 Z_l=E_mu product_(b in board) theta_b f_l,
 a_l=Z_l^(1/K).

For every assignment of a label alpha_b in{1,...,r} to each tile,

 E_mu product_b theta_b f_(alpha_b)
                         <=product_b a_(alpha_b).       (2.1)

PROOF, positive functions first.
Assume f_l>=epsilon>0. Put g_l=f_l/a_l; their disseminated
expectations are exactly1. For a label pattern alpha define

                   Z(alpha)=E_mu product_b theta_b g_(alpha_b).

The alphabet and board are finite, so a maximizing pattern exists;
write M=max_alpha Z(alpha)>0. Fix any site reflection along a
tile boundary. Separate the maximizing product into its two
closed-half products, called A and theta B. Neither product
reads the plane-boundary links, by strict interior.

Reflection positivity and Cauchy--Schwarz give

 M^2=|E_mu A theta B|^2
       <=(E_mu A theta A)(E_mu B theta B)
       <=M^2.                                           (2.2)

Both factors on the right are expectations of allowed label
patterns, by(1.1), so each is at most M. Since M>0, equality of
the endpoints forces BOTH reflected-half patterns to attain M.
Consequently reflecting EITHER half of a maximizing pattern
onto the other half preserves maximality.

Take the label at tile0 in such a pattern. Reflect the half
containing it across the right boundary of that tile. It now
occupies two adjacent tiles along direction1. Reflect the half
containing that pair across its right boundary, then continue.
At every stage the uniform-label rectangle in that direction
has width at most N_1/2; hence it lies in the chosen closed half.
The dyadic lengths make it possible to double until it fills
direction1. Repeat in directions2 and3. Every operation
preserves the maximal expectation by(2.2). The final pattern
has the chosen label on the entire board, so its expectation
is exactly1 by normalization. Therefore M=1.

Undoing the normalization proves(2.1) for positive functions.
For merely nonnegative bounded functions replace every f_l
by f_l+epsilon. Apply the result and let epsilon down to0.
Bounded convergence passes both the mixed expectation and the
full dissemination norms to their limits. This also handles
zero dissemination norms without dividing by zero.

This proof establishes the multipoint inequality itself.
It does not infer it from a series of unrelated one-function
bounds, which would not be sufficient.

3. ACTUAL JOINT ACTION MOMENTS

Let S_b be the reflected copy of the strict-interior tile action
S_B. For any0<=t_b<2, apply(2.1) to the finite alphabet
f_b=exp(t_b S_B/g^2). The copies of the strict-interior plaquettes
are disjoint and their union is a subset of all plaquettes.
The actual global MGF from the producer therefore gives

 log E_mu exp[sum_b t_b S_b/g^2]
       <=sum_b (9V_B/2) artanh(t_b/2).                   (3.1)

Indeed each dissemination is bounded by E_mu exp(t_b S/g^2),
whose logarithm is at most(9n/2)artanh(t_b/2), and the K-th
root replaces n by V_B. This is a simultaneous MGF inequality,
not factorization of the joint distribution.

4. JOINT BAD-BLOCK BOUND

For thresholds r_b>=0 and A any set of distinct tiles, let

                  B_b={S_b/g^2>=r_b}.

For any t_b in[0,2),

 P_mu(intersection_(b in A) B_b)
 <=exp[-sum_(b in A)
           {t_b r_b-(9V_B/2)artanh(t_b/2)}].             (4.1)

This follows either from(3.1) and Markov's inequality or from
(2.1) applied to the corresponding indicator alphabet, with
the remaining tiles assigned the constant function1.
The bound can be capped at1. Optimizing each parameter separately
is legitimate; no independence has been asserted.

In particular for t_b=1 and a common threshold r,

 P_mu(intersection_(b in A) B_b)
               <=[exp(-r)3^(9V_B/4)]^(|A|).             (4.2)

The scale here is a fixed tile in current lattice units. Its
dependence on V_B is retained, and this is the original ground
law at that spatial cutoff, not its deterministic flow pushforward.
It does not by itself bound derivative-weighted bad blocks.

5. CLOSED-TILE EXTENSION: SHARED BOUNDARY LINKS ARE ALLOWED

The same multipoint proof applies to functions reading the CLOSED
tile. The two half-products in(2.2) can then share links lying in
the reflection planes. This is allowed by the actual site-RP
proof: condition on these links first, and the two conditional
half integrals are functions of the common boundary record.
Their pairing is an integral against the same positive boundary
weight; Cauchy--Schwarz remains valid. Reflecting one half simply
duplicates its boundary dependence, exactly as the corresponding
allowed reflected label pattern does. No boundary variable is
integrated twice and no product-law assertion is being used.

Let S_B^cl contain every plaquette whose vertices lie in the closed
tile. In three spatial dimensions each plaquette varies in two
coordinate directions. In either varying direction its unit
edge interval belongs to only one tile interval. Its third
coordinate can lie on a tile boundary and then allow two tiles.
Therefore, including the induced periodic identifications,

              S<=sum_b theta_b S_B^cl<=2S.              (5.1)

The bound is also valid if a coordinate has only one tile.
In particular every plaquette is covered; seams are not omitted.
This overlap changes the useful MGF radius to0<=t<1:

 log E exp[sum_b t_b S_b^cl/g^2]
              <=sum_b (9V_B/2)artanh(t_b).              (5.2)

For distinct tiles A and threshold r,

 P(for every b in A: S_b^cl/g^2>=r)
             <=[3^(9V_B/4)exp(-r/2)]^(|A|),             (5.3)

using t_b=1/2. The constant in brackets may be capped at1.
These are genuine simultaneous bounds under the unflowed
Hamiltonian ground law.

6. CLUSTER AND PHYSICAL-DIAMETER CONSEQUENCE

Give the block board any fixed bounded-degree adjacency in which
one step moves by at most C_B tile diameters; face or touching
adjacency both suffice. Let its degree be at most D_B and
q denote the bracket in(5.3). The number of rooted connected
k-tile sets is at most A_D^k for a numerical A_D depending only
on D_B. For example ordered rooted tree enumeration supplies a
conservative A_D=4 max(1,D_B), enlarged if necessary.

A bad component at a given root with at least k vertices contains
a connected k-subset through that root. The simultaneous bound
and a union bound consequently give

 P(root bad component has size>=k)<=(A_D q)^k.           (6.1)

A component of physical torus diameter at least d_*>0 requires
at least c d_*/(a max_i l_i) vertices: a connected k-vertex set
has a spanning tree of k-1 adjacency edges, each of bounded
physical length C_B a max_i l_i. If the tile lengths and q are
fixed with A_D q<1, union over all K=O(a^-3) roots proves

 P(any bad component has physical diameter>=d_*) ->0.    (6.2)

This is a statement about spatial bad-action blocks in Omega^2.
It is not a statement of stochastic domination, conditional
independence, a physical observable variance floor, or control
of the derivative-weighted flowed law.
