2. PRECISE FINITE-CUTOFF PRESSURE INPUT

Fix a compact transverse profile f and a planar C1 shape phi supported
in a fixed square. Put phi_delta(z)=delta^-2 phi(z/delta), and let
O_a(phi_delta*f) be the finite-lattice scalar observable. Work first at
fixed lattice spacing a and then in a reflection-positive thermodynamic
Wilson limit. A finite periodic torus cannot be used for arbitrarily
many doublings without taking that thermodynamic limit first.

For the repeated source define the exact single-source normalizer

 d_a(z;phi_delta*f)=log mu_a(exp[z O_a(phi_delta*f)]), d_a(0)=0. (2.1)

It suffices that this logarithm exists on |z|<c delta^3, uniformly in a.
This does not ask for a zero-free many-source partition function. If
mu_a(exp[zO])=exp[E_UV,a(zphi_delta*f)] h_a(z), h_a(0)=1,
and the fixed-support source bound gives |h_a(z)|<=C on a disc of
radius c0 delta^3, Cauchy gives |h_a(z)-1|<=1/2 on a smaller such disc.
Thus (2.1) has the required analytic branch. Its covariance is exact
under Wilson translations and reflections, although the fixed-scaffold
E_UV coefficients need not have those covariances.

Let rho_delta=c delta^(7/2). Consider m copies, including reflected
and conjugate copies, separated in their full spacetime supports by
c_sep delta, lying in a time cylinder of length O(m delta), with
fixed transverse cross-section. Let z_l equal z or its conjugate as
required by reflection, |z|<=rho_delta. The needed array estimate is

 |mu_a[exp(sum_l z_l O_a(phi_l)-sum_l d_a(z_l;phi_l))
             exp(sum_b lambda_b O_a(psi_b))]|
       <= C_{a,psi,delta} exp(C_f m),                         (2.2)

on one fixed (at fixed a) polydisc in finitely many endpoint source
variables lambda_b. The endpoint profiles occupy fixed compact regions
outside the array, translated with its endpoints. C_f must NOT depend
on those endpoint profiles. C_{a,psi,delta} may depend on them and on a.

Why these dependencies suffice: after differentiating in lambda, the
endpoint polynomial factor has a prefactor independent of m. The
reflection-doubling root then removes that prefactor at fixed a.

The local source ingredients suggest a direct proof of (2.2):

(i) For every localized coefficient, subtract its separate single-copy
values. The remainder vanishes for carriers meeting zero or one copy.
On a carrier meeting r_X>=2 copies, the disjoint supports make the norm
of their sum at most C|z| M_a, not r_X times this norm, where
M_a=max(delta^-2,sqrt(a)delta^-3). The second-order Cauchy estimate and
the sum of individual remainders give C r_X |z|^2 M_a^2. Charge r_X
to the copies met by X, retain exp(-c delta/a), and perform the Section17
scale sum. The total cross-copy UV term is <=C m |z|^2 delta^-7<=Cm.
Singleton log h terms are O(1) per copy on the smaller disc.

(ii) A bare Wilson rectangular chessboard estimate avoids reliance on
the covariance of E_UV under an arbitrarily small chessboard cell.
Take one time-long cell containing the array and fixed transverse cell
sizes containing its tube. The normalized cell's disseminated source
partition can be estimated directly: global source stability contributes
exp(C V_total); the preceding cross-copy estimate and singleton log h
terms contribute exp(C times total clone count). Raising to the reciprocal
number of cells gives exp(C V_cell+Cm)<=exp(C_f m+C_delta).
The cell boundary must leave the reflected source copies separated by
c delta. Spatial faces can have fixed buffers. Small time end buffers
are allowed because their endcap cost is independent of m.

(iii) Endpoint sources factor over the other cells at the bare plaquette
level. At fixed a their cell factors have finite sup norms independent
of m; only O_psi(1) cells contain endpoints as m tends to infinity.
They therefore cost C_{a,psi,delta}. Differentiate the finitely many
lambda variables to obtain endpoint polynomials. The array-cell
cross-section is determined by f, not by the endpoint observables.

This last point matters: enclosing the array and arbitrary endpoint A
in one huge rectangle gives exp(C_A m), which is insufficient for a
uniform operator norm. Also, replacing the final complex array by its
absolute value produces d_a(2 Re z)-2 Re d_a(z); its divergent single-
source terms need not cancel. No such ordinary-L2 step is allowed.

The following checks complete (i)--(iii), relative to the actual global
source stability and fixed-support singleton bound of Section16.

Closed-cell RP: take a coordinate reflection through bare vertices.
There are two mirror planes on the periodic torus. No nearest-neighbor
plaquette meets both STRICT sides of those planes. Conditional on the
links lying in the mirror planes, write S=S_++theta S_++S_0. The Haar
integrals on the two open halves factor, and the RP form is an integral
of |integral F(U_+,U_0) exp(-S_+) dU_+|^2 against the positive mirror-
link measure exp(-S_0)dU_0/Z. Thus RP holds for every complex function
on the CLOSED half, including nongauge-invariant matrix-entry products.

The abstract chessboard Holder theorem uses only cyclicity and this
reflection Cauchy--Schwarz inequality. It applies to any even number
of cells in each coordinate, not merely powers of two, and does not
require the neighboring cell algebras to have disjoint boundary data.

https://repo-archives.ihes.fr/FONDS_IHES/I_Prepublications/FROHLICH/1978-1980/P_78_224/P_78_224_web.pdf

The array cell's dissemination gives many translated/reflected copies;

3. REFLECTION DOUBLING WITH EXACT SINGLE-SOURCE SUBTRACTION

Let the source support lie in a slab of width L=O(delta), with gaps of
size c delta at its faces. Write U_L for positive time translation and

 D_a(z)[A]=[ exp(z O_a(phi)-d_a(z;phi)) U_L A ].           (3.1)

Initially A is a finite polynomial in compactly supported positive-time
plaquette observables. At fixed a every such observable is bounded and
the vector is holomorphic in z on the single-source disc.

Reflection and a common translation turn ||D_a(z)A||^2 into the scalar
product of [A] with a doubled insertion whose two source coefficients
are z and conjugate(z), with the corresponding exact d's. The doubled
slab has reflection-conjugation symmetry. For its m=2^r repetitions set
a_r equal to the norm of the resulting vector with endpoint A, and
b=||[A]||. Repeating the identity and Cauchy--Schwarz gives

             a_r^2 <= b a_{r+1}.                       (3.2)

The endpoint-source estimate (2.2), followed by finite Cauchy
differentiation, bounds

             a_r^2 <= C_{a,A,delta} exp(C_f 2^r).        (3.3)

If b=0, (3.2) already gives a_0=0. Otherwise iteration yields
a_0<=b^(1-2^-r) a_r^(2^-r). Taking r to infinity at fixed a gives
a_0<=exp(C_f/2)b. The preliminary doubling changes only a fixed
constant. Hence D_a(z) is uniformly bounded for |z|<=rho_delta.

Cauchy at zero therefore gives

 || [ (O_a(phi)-mu_a O_a(phi)) U_L . ] ||
                 <= C_f delta^(-7/2).                 (3.4)

The derivative of (2.1) is the exact mean. Higher self-counterterms
disappear from (3.4); they were necessary to bound the holomorphic
exponential before differentiating it.

For a general planar alpha supported in a square of radius delta,
normalizing its C1 shape gives the scale-covariant estimate

 || B_delta(alpha f) || <= C_f delta^(2-kappa)
            (||alpha||_infinity+delta||grad alpha||_infinity),
                  kappa=7/2.                          (3.5)

Here B_delta inserts the centered field in |t|<delta and leaves a
positive time delta on either side. Fixed changes of the slab-width
constant change only C_f. Complex f and alpha follow by linearity and
real/imaginary decomposition.

5. A SHARP PLANAR POINT-INSERTION LEMMA USING C1, NOT L2

Assume H and P commute, H>=|P|, and
 V(z)=exp(-z_t H+i z_x P), q(z)=Re z_t-|Im z_x|>0.
Then V is a norm-holomorphic contraction on W={q>0}. Assume (3.5)
and the exact translation/padding identities for B.

Take a smooth nonnegative planar mollifier j_epsilon of mass one,
supported in a disk of radius epsilon. For q(xi),q(eta)>epsilon set

 A_f,epsilon(xi,eta)=V(xi-epsilon e) B_epsilon(j_epsilon f)
                                      V(eta-epsilon e).       (5.1)

Claim: uniformly as epsilon decreases,

 ||A_f,epsilon(xi,eta)|| <= C_f min(1,q(xi),q(eta))^-kappa.      (5.2)

Proof. Put q0=min(q(xi),q(eta)); choose r=c min(1,q0) with c fixed
small and epsilon<r. For unit u,v consider the holomorphic scalar
 h_epsilon(zeta)=<u,A_f,epsilon(xi+zeta,eta-zeta)v>.
For zeta=z+ib in a complex ball of radius r, the exact padding identity
expresses h as a matrix element of B_{2r+epsilon}((tau_z j_epsilon)f)
between two contraction vectors depending on b, but not on real z.

Choose a smooth radial density rho_r on C^2, supported in that ball,
with integral one. Holomorphic mean value gives
 h_epsilon(0)=integral rho_r(z+ib) h_epsilon(z+ib) dz db.
For fixed b, alpha_b(z)=rho_r(z+ib) satisfies
 ||alpha_b||_infinity<=C r^-4, ||grad alpha_b||_infinity<=C r^-5.
The real-z integral equals a matrix element of
B_{2r+epsilon}((alpha_b*j_epsilon)f). Convolution does not increase
these two norms. Applying (3.5) bounds that integral by
C_f r^(2-kappa) r^-4=C_f r^(-kappa-2). The b integration has area
O(r^2), proving (5.2). This uses a signed/complex linear source bound,
not a pointwise probability or an L2 duality assertion.

To identify the limit, fix d>0 below the neighboring cone margins.
For every smooth planar alpha supported sufficiently near zero,

 integral alpha(z) A_f,epsilon(xi+z,eta-z) dz
   =V(xi-d e) B_d((alpha*j_epsilon)f) V(eta-d e).        (5.3)

The right side converges in operator norm by (3.5), since the smooth
convolution converges in C1. Local scalar normal-family compactness
from (5.2) and uniqueness on the real square identify a unique weak
limit A_f(xi,eta). It is bounded by (5.2). The exact shift identity
factors it locally as two norm-holomorphic semigroups around a fixed
bounded A_f, proving norm holomorphy, rather than merely weak
holomorphy. Adjoint and arbitrary inner-gap splitting identities pass
from the regularized operators. Equation(5.3) also proves

 B_d(alpha f)=integral alpha(z) A_f(de+z,de-z) dz.       (5.4)

6. JOINT TUBE AND IMAGINARY-ANGLE GRAM KERNEL

For ordered complex points Z=(z1,...,zN) with each difference in W,

 |S_N(Z;f)| <= C_f^N min(1,min_j q(z_{j+1}-z_j))^(-kappa N). (6.1)

Repeated (5.4) identifies it with the original centered S0 moments on

 <Psi(Z;f),Psi(W;g)>=S_{m+n}(theta conjugate(Z),W;bar f,g), (6.2)

with the reflected group written in reversed order. Include the empty

7. CONDITIONAL COVARIANT EXTENSION OF THE ORDERED PHYSICAL SECTOR

Assume the actual SAME state supplies both frame hypotheses above.
Also apply the coordinate source-slab construction to fully localized
four-dimensional packets eta_delta=delta^-4 eta(./delta). Their local
source norm is M_a=max(delta^-4,sqrt(a)delta^-5). At a<=delta the

terms are O(delta^-8+delta^-9), and the coarse sum is
delta^-10 sum_{a>=delta}a<=C delta^-10. The singleton zero-free radius
is c delta^5, so the same argument gives the full-packet insertion
norm C delta^-5, with C independent of the moment order.

Since the spectral cone has |P_i|<=H for each spatial coordinate,
V(t,x)=exp(-tH+i x.P) is a contraction for
q_4(t,x)=Re t-sum_i |Im x_i|>0. Repeat Section5 in C^4: the radial
reproducing density has size r^-8; its real slices cost r^(4-5)r^-8,
and integration over four imaginary coordinates contributes r^4.
Thus the full-point insertion has bound C min(1,q_4)^-5. Its N-fold
product defines a real-analytic full-point kernel K_N whenever all
times are distinct, with

 |K_N(X)|<=C^N min(1,min consecutive time gaps)^(-5N).   (7.1)

It represents the original hierarchy on compact strictly time-ordered
tests. Smearing its transverse variables identifies it with the planar
kernel of Section6: both represent the same such moments, and both
are smooth in the remaining separated variables. Therefore the sharp
planar rotation identities hold pointwise for K_N whenever both
configurations have distinct times.

Claim: there is a unique O(4)-invariant real-analytic extension
K_N,new to every configuration of distinct FULL spacetime points,
obtained by rotating the points to have distinct times.

Proof. First take a generic configuration with no parallel chords.
Along a generic path in SO(4), the time projections cross only one
equal-time wall at a time. Inside a chamber the three (t,x_i) rotation
derivatives of K_N vanish by the planar identities. Their commutators
give all spatial rotation derivatives as well, so the value is constant
on each chamber. At a wall only one pair has equal time. Some spatial
coordinate x_i distinguishes that pair; all (t,x_i) projections are
then distinct. Choose a small fixed rotation in that plane which
separates every time throughout a neighborhood of the wall. Evaluate
K_N after this rotation. Planar angular invariance identifies this
analytic patch with the original time-ordered kernel on BOTH sides
of the wall. Thus the chamber constants agree. This also handles the
permutation change in their ordering, using scalar permutation symmetry.

For nongeneric distinct configurations, choose two separating rotations
and approximate the configuration by generic ones while retaining both
strict projection gaps. Equality at the generic configurations passes
by continuity, proving independence of the separating rotation in
general. On a neighborhood of any distinct configuration one fixed
rotation separates times, so the resulting extension is real analytic.
Translation, permutations, and all proper rotations follow from the
definition; one original coordinate reflection adds O(4).

Its growth is controlled without an uncontrolled order-dependent
analytic-continuation constant. Let d=min_{i!=j}|x_i-x_j|. A spherical
union bound over the N(N-1)/2 pair directions supplies a unit time
normal v with |v.(x_i-x_j)|>=c d/N^2 for every pair. Hence (7.1) gives

 |K_N,new(X)|<=C^N N^(10N) [1+d^(-5N)].                (7.2)

This has a fixed factorial envelope because N^(10N)<=C^N(N!)^10.
Define S_N,new on collision-flat Schwartz tests by ordinary integration
against K_N,new. The integral is absolute. Indeed flatness on the
closest pair diagonal and Taylor's formula of order 5N bound the test
by C^N d^(5N) times a seminorm with at most 5N derivatives. A polynomial
weight of degree 4N+2 makes the remaining 4N-dimensional integral
finite. Along the Taylor segment, for d<=1, that weight changes by
only a factor C^N. Thus the distribution order and weight order are
linear in N and the constants have fixed factorial growth. No choice
of a contact distribution is made by this absolutely convergent S0
definition.

Reflection positivity of S_new follows as follows. On compact positive-
halfspace off-collision supports, excise the INTERNAL equal-time
hypersurfaces with smooth cutoffs. Every resulting test splits into
strict time-order chambers, where S_new equals the original hierarchy,
so its reflected quadratic form is nonnegative. Against the NEW smooth
off-collision kernels these cutoffs tend to one by dominated convergence.
This is not a claim that these cutoffs converge in the old S0 topology.
It proves RP for all compact off-collision positive-halfspace tests.
The new linear-order S0 bound and the standard flat-diagonal/cutoff
approximation then extend RP to the declared positive S0 domain.

The constructed covariant hierarchy agrees with the ordered Wilson
sector. It need not equal the originally extracted S0 hierarchy on
