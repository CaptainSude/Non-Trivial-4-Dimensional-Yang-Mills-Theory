1. EXACT LINEAR CURL IDENTITY FOR THE NATIVE AVERAGE

B109 printed252--254 (0.3),(0.5)--(0.12) defines centered odd-L blocks,
the equivariant endpoint contour mean, and the anchored-log ordinary
average. Linearize at the identity. For a coarse edge from y to
y+L e_mu and a fine bond field a, write tau_y(r) for the arithmetic
mean of line integrals over the actual endpoint paths from y to y+r.
Then the literal differential is

 (Q_L a)_mu(y)=L^-4 sum_(r in B_L)
   [tau_y(r)+sum_(s=0)^(L-1) a_mu(y+r+s e_mu)
                                  -tau_(y+L e_mu)(r)].       (1)

The endpoint terms are psi(y)-psi(y+L e_mu), where
psi(y)=L^-4 sum_r tau_y(r). They therefore cancel EXACTLY in the
coarse curl. Telescoping the two straight strings gives

 (d Q_L a)_(mu nu)(y)=L^-4 sum_(r in B_L)
   sum_(s,t=0)^(L-1) (d a)_(mu nu)(y+r+s e_mu+t e_nu).       (2)

No selected axial-path identification is used: the endpoint means
cancel whatever their actual arithmetic path weights are. Orientations
are fixed positive orientations; reverse plaquettes follow by sign.

Iterate the ACTUAL primitive map p times. Its linear differential
is Q^(p), not an independently chosen direct large-block average.
Balanced base-L digits identify
r_total=r_0+L r_1+...+L^(p-1)r_(p-1) bijectively with B_K.
The ordinary digits in s_total and t_total similarly enumerate
0,...,K-1 exactly once. Thus (2) iterates to the SAME formula

 d Q^(p) a = Q_K^(2) da,
 (Q_K^(2)F)_(mu nu)(y)=K^-4 sum_(r in B_K)
            sum_(s,t=0)^(K-1) F_(mu nu)(y+r+s e_mu+t e_nu). (3)

This proof applies on the infinite lattice and on compatible tori,
with the prescribed periodic translations. It needs no affine flux.

Here is a uniform norm proof which avoids an overlap constant at
each stage. Let Q_(0,K)F(y)=K^-4 sum_(r in B_K)F(y+r). Distinct
coarse rows have disjoint fine scalar supports, so its counting
l2 operator norm is exactly K^-2. For a fixed plaquette component,

 Q_K^(2)=sum_(s,t=0)^(K-1) Q_(0,K) translation_(s e_mu+t e_nu).

Each translation is unitary on the fine counting space. Hence

                     ||Q_K^(2)||_(2->2)<=K^2 K^-2=1.        (4)

The map is diagonal in plaquette component and color, so the same
bound holds for the whole curl field. This is the four-dimensional
counting cancellation. The analogous bound in dimension d is
K^(2-d/2). It is not a volume count or an estimate for each entry.

2. CONSEQUENCE FOR THE ACTUAL CLASSICAL FLAT HESSIAN

At flatness the second variation of the classical minimum value is
the minimum of the fine Wilson quadratic form subject to the
linearized actual averaging constraint. The stationarity/implicit
minimum theorem identifies its minimizing lift; gauges affect neither
energy nor the constraint curvature in this flat quadratic statement.
With consistent trace normalization,

 <v,q_m v> = min_(Q^(m)a=v) ||d_fine a||^2.

Use (3)--(4) on every admissible lift. It gives

       ||d_current v||^2<=||d_fine a||^2,
       q_m >= q_W=d_current^*d_current.                     (5)

An overall fixed normalization common to both sides is harmless, but
must be the SAME Wilson convention. In d=4 no scale-dependent action
prefactor is inserted. Equation(5) is an actual native flat lower
bound, independent of m and compatible volume. It does not use a
claimed universal quantum coefficient or a choice of structural limit.

For the complementary upper bound, suppose the separate classical
kernel producer supplies uniform exponential quadratic rows. Ward
invariance q_m(k)d(k)=0 first implies q_m(0)=0. Positivity for real
momenta then forces q_m'(0)=0, since either sign of a nonzero Hermitian
linear jet would contradict positivity. Uniform second moments yield
||q_m(k)||<=C delta(k), with delta(k)=sum_mu|exp(ik_mu)-1|^2.
Since q_m kills the gradient vector, this is equivalent to

                    q_W<=q_m<=C q_W                         (6)

on the transverse subspace, and both vanish on gradients. The same
uniform Taylor data yields the physical complex-twist estimate

 ||q_m(k+i mu theta)-q_m(k)||
 <=C[mu|theta| sqrt(delta(k))+mu^2|theta|^2]                 (7)

for a fixed sufficiently small theta. Near zero this uses the double
zero and Taylor's formula; away from zero it uses the uniform analytic
strip and delta bounded below. The upper bound and(7), unlike(5),
explicitly retain the separate uniform classical-kernel premise.

3. THE EXACT COMMUTING AFFINE CANDIDATE

Take commuting Cartan-valued c_mu,B_(mu nu), with B_(mu mu)=0, and

 A_mu(x)=c_mu+sum_nu B_(mu nu)x_nu,  U_mu(x)=exp(i A_mu(x)).

Its plaquette is the constant commuting matrix exp(i F_(mu nu)),
F_(mu nu)=B_(nu mu)-B_(mu nu). The FULL Wilson Euler derivative
vanishes componentwise: the two plaquettes incident to a bond in
each transverse direction contribute opposite transported sine
terms. Their transports commute with the background curvature.
This is a Lie-algebra identity, valid against non-Cartan variations
as well as Cartan variations. It is a local infinite-lattice
identity, with no artificial boundary action added.

For x=y+r in one primitive block, every coordinate-order endpoint
path has transport exp(i r.A(y)) times a Cartan matrix independent
of y. Equivariance of the selected native group mean factors out
exp(i r.A(y)). Conjugation covariance puts its remaining value in
the Cartan subgroup, on the local branch. Thus

             P_L(U;y,y+r)=exp(i r.A(y)) p_L(B,r).           (8)

No logarithmic-arithmetic formula for the nonlinear selected mean is
assumed. The SAME p_L factors cancel between two translated endpoint
blocks. The path product in B109(0.12), relative to its center string,
therefore has logarithm

                 L sum_nu (B_(mu nu)-B_(nu mu)) r_nu.

The centered r mean is zero. Since B_(mu mu)=0, the center string
itself is exp(i L A_mu(y)). Consequently

 M_L(U)_mu(y)=exp(i L A_mu(y)),
 M_L^p(U)_mu(y)=exp(i K A_mu(y)).                            (9)

These equalities are local analytic-germ identities and therefore
formal Taylor identities at each lattice site. They are not an

fine curvature. The rooted candidate may be put into the actual
