COMPATIBLE FINE/COARSE TREE GAUGES MAKE PATH BLOCKING A PROJECTION
19 October 2026. Exact finite graph/group statement.

0. RESULT

For a coarse graph embedded by internally disjoint fine paths, compatible
rooted spanning trees can be chosen so that coarse path-holonomy blocking,
AFTER the exact based tree quotients, is a coordinate projection on fine
chord group variables. In a common logarithm chart it is a linear
coordinate projection with surjective derivative. There is no nonlinear
gauge or constraint chart hidden in this finite construction.

The map is path decimation, not the native symmetrized averaging map.
It does not identify their effective actions, locality estimates,
reflection properties, or continuum limits. All constants and paths
here belong to fixed finite graphs.

1. GRAPH HYPOTHESES AND NOTATION

Let G_f=(V_f,E_f) be a finite connected graph with one distinguished
root r. A finite connected coarse graph G_c=(V_c,E_c) has V_c subset V_f
and contains r. Each oriented coarse edge c=(u,v) is represented by a
simple fine path P_c from u to v. Distinct such unoriented edge paths
have disjoint edge sets and disjoint internal vertices. They intersect
only at the coarse vertices which are their endpoints. No internal
vertex of a path belongs to V_c.

These hypotheses describe a subdivision embedding of G_c into G_f.
For a standard coarse cubical subgrid in a fine cubical box, straight
coordinate paths have this property. Contractibility of the full
cubical complex is NOT needed for the graph construction; it is needed
separately for the positive Wilson Hessian/unique flat-orbit argument.

Choose one orientation per fine edge and use U_(yx)=U_(xy)^(-1).
Reversing a chosen edge orientation is harmless; Haar is inversion
invariant. All group products below follow the indicated path order.

2. THE COMPATIBLE SPANNING TREE

Choose an arbitrary rooted spanning tree T_c in G_c. For every coarse
chord c in E_c\T_c choose ONE fine edge e_c in its path P_c. Let

 F = union_(c in T_c) P_c
       union union_(c notin T_c)(P_c\{e_c}).                 (2.1)

Here F includes every embedded-network vertex, including the two ends
of each removed edge. It is a TREE on the entire embedded network.

Proof. The lifted T_c is a tree: subdividing tree edges does not create
cycles. Removing one edge from a coarse chord path splits it into two
possibly empty end segments. Each segment is attached at exactly one
endpoint to the lifted coarse tree and has no internal vertex shared
with any other path. Adding the two segments therefore preserves
connectedness and creates no cycle. Repeat independently over all
coarse chords. Every internal vertex is included, and the result is
connected and acyclic.

Extend F to a spanning tree T_f of G_f. This is always possible for a
forest in a finite connected graph, for example by adding edges joining
distinct current components until all vertices are connected. None of
the distinguished edges e_c can enter T_f: its endpoints are already
connected in F, so adding it would make a cycle. Equivalently, deleting
all e_c leaves G_f connected, because each deleted edge has a replacement
path in F. Thus

       F subset T_f,       {e_c:c notin T_c} subset E_f\T_f.   (2.2)

One important consequence is exact: the T_f path from r to any coarse
vertex v equals the lifted T_c path from r to v. That lifted path is
already in F, and uniqueness of paths in the extended tree prevents
it from changing when the tree is extended.

3. EXACT GROUP TREE QUOTIENTS

Let G be any compact group. The based fine gauge group consists of
vertex maps k:V_f->G with k_r=1, acting by

                  U_(xy)^k=k_x U_(xy) k_y^(-1).

Define h_v(U) as the ordered holonomy along the T_f path from r to v,
and h_r=1. Then

        W_e=h_x(U) U_e h_y(U)^(-1)                    (3.1)

equals1 on every tree edge. The variables W_e on E_f\T_f are exact
coordinates for the quotient by the BASED gauge group. The inverse is

        U_e=h_x^(-1) W_e h_y,     W_e=1 on T_f.       (3.2)

Thus the finite configuration space is globally parametrized by the
site variables h_v, v!=r, and the chord variables W_e. This is a smooth
global group-coordinate transformation when G is a Lie group; no local
logarithm or implicit constraint theorem is required.

Successive tree-leaf Haar integration gives exactly

 product_(e in E_f) d_H U_e
   =product_(v!=r) d_H h_v
       product_(e notin T_f) d_H W_e.                (3.3)

There is no field-dependent Jacobian in this GROUP formula. Local
exponential coordinates on the chord groups later contribute their
ordinary Haar densities and must retain them.

If gauges at the root are also allowed, they act on every W_e by the
SAME conjugation. We keep that residual compact action explicitly.
We do not identify the generally singular full conjugacy-orbit space
with a smooth Euclidean slice near the identity. Gauge-invariant
observables are simultaneous-conjugation-invariant functions of W.

4. BLOCKING COMMUTES WITH THE COMPATIBLE TREE QUOTIENTS

Define the raw path-blocking map by

                  q_c(U)=product_(e in P_c) U_e.     (4.1)

It is equivariant under the fine gauges restricted to coarse vertices;
the internal gauge factors cancel in each path product.

Let H_v(q(U)) be the coarse T_c path holonomy. By the final assertion
of Section2,

                  H_v(q(U))=h_v(U),  v in V_c.       (4.2)

The coarse tree-quotient variable on a coarse chord c=(u,v) is

       V_c=H_u(q(U)) q_c(U) H_v(q(U))^(-1).

After fine tree gauge, all edges of P_c except e_c equal1. Orient e_c
along P_c. Therefore the exact identity is

                   V_c=W_(e_c),  c notin T_c.        (4.3)

For c in T_c the entire path lies in T_f and its gauge-fixed value is1.
If the preselected orientation of e_c is opposite, the right side of
(4.3) is its inverse; one may simply orient its chord coordinate along
the coarse edge. Distinct coarse chords select distinct fine chords.

Consequently the induced group map on based quotients is the literal
coordinate projection

 G^(|E_f|-|V_f|+1) -> G^(|E_c|-|V_c|+1),
                  (W_e)_e -> (W_(e_c))_c.           (4.4)

It is surjective globally: assign the selected chord variables
arbitrarily and set the other chord variables to1. It is a smooth
submersion for Lie G. In a common small exponential chart W_e=exp(ix_e),
with the selected orientations, its logarithmic expression is

                          q_log(x)=(x_(e_c))_c.     (4.5)

Its derivative is the same coordinate projection at EVERY point in
that chart, with an isometric coordinate right inverse for the product
Euclidean norms. This is not a statement about the condition number
of a separate physical energy norm.

5. EXACT FINITE JOINT LAW AND OBSERVABLES

For a gauge-invariant positive finite density rho_f(U) relative to
product Haar, (3.3) removes only the based gauge variables. The remaining
EXACT joint density is

   rho_f(W_(selected),W_(other))
             product_selected d_H W product_other d_H W.       (5.1)

The notation means evaluation in tree gauge. It does not make the two
sets independent. Their effective marginal and conditional law are
obtained by integrating this actual density, with all original factors.
Unlike a fluctuation integral at an ALREADY FIXED coarse datum, (5.1)
is a joint law in which the coarse selected chord variables vary.

For the bare Wilson density on a finite cubical complex this formula
is exact on the FULL compact tree quotient. In a small chart, Haar
coordinate factors must be included with the action. A finite-graph
positive Hessian follows separately from flat curl injectivity after
tree gauge when the plaquette complex is simply connected and all
flat configurations are in the identity orbit. The graph projection
proof itself neither supplies a uniform gap under fine refinement nor
identifies that local chart with the full measure without tail bounds.

Every coarse loop holonomy pulls back to the fine holonomy obtained by
substituting its complete P_c paths, and its trace is exactly preserved.
Residual root conjugation acts simultaneously on the selected and
unselected chord variables; (4.4) intertwines this action. Thus trace
words, invariant polynomials of local logarithms, or a symmetry-stable
finite polynomial space can be treated without a nonlinear-gauge
ambiguity. Their Gram conditioning and low-reference calibration are
additional quantitative questions.

The true support of a pulled-back observable includes ALL fine edges
on its paths. A trace of a based loop may cancel root-path conjugations,
but arbitrary quotient coordinates and the tree-gauge action can read
long tree paths. Those reads cannot be erased from a locality estimate.

6. BOUNDARY AND TOPOLOGY QUALIFICATIONS

The proof above uses one based root, not arbitrary fixed physical
boundary links. With a boundary-fixed gauge group one may instead use
a rooted forest; extra relative boundary transports remain physical
coordinates. A single-tree quotient must not gauge away links or
boundary data that the actual measure holds fixed. Such a variant
needs its own compatible relative-forest statement.

Likewise a periodic box has noncontractible loops and harmonic flat
modes. The coordinate-projection graph statement still holds for a
subdivision embedding, but unique flat orbit and positive Wilson
tree-slice Hessian no longer follow. Those modes must be retained or
conditioned. Contractibility is being used only in that separate
action/concentration argument.

Internal path overlap is another real restriction. Edge-disjoint paths
with shared INTERNAL vertices do not in general form the subdivision
used in Section2; the union after the proposed deletions can contain
extra cycles. The stated internally-disjoint hypothesis avoids that
problem and is satisfied by the specified coarse cubical embedding.

7. EXACT UV OBSTRUCTION FOR THIN-PATH DECIMATION IN FOUR DIMENSIONS

The lack of a uniform-refinement action bound is not merely unproved:
there is a concrete commuting counterexample. Subdivide a straight
coarse edge c into M fine links. Take c to be a coarse chord so that
its holonomy is directly a selected quotient variable. Choose a fixed
nonzero Cartan generator H in a faithful unitary representation and a
small fixed real theta with exp(i theta H)!=1. Put

 U_e=exp(i theta H/M) on those M oriented fine links,
 U_e=1 on every other fine link.                              (7.1)

The coarse c holonomy is EXACTLY exp(i theta H); every other coarse
edge holonomy is1 because the embedded paths have disjoint edges.
Any coarse plaquette containing c has the fixed nonidentity holonomy
exp(+/-i theta H). Thus the prescribed coarse data are nonflat.

In a nonwrapped fine cubical box, an elementary fine plaquette contains
at most one charged link: the two parallel edges of a plaquette are
transversely separated, while the charged links all lie on one straight
line. Each fine link belongs to at most 2(4-1)=6 plaquettes. Hence at
most 6M fine plaquettes are affected, each with holonomy
exp(+/-i theta H/M). With the standard normalized Wilson plaquette term

             s(U)=1-(dim rho)^(-1) Re tr rho(U),

the elementary inequality 1-cos t<=t^2/2 gives

 s(exp(i theta H/M))
   <=theta^2 tr rho(H^2)/(2 dim(rho) M^2).

Consequently the four-dimensional classical Wilson action obeys

                       S_f(U)<=C_H theta^2/M.                 (7.2)

In four dimensions the conventional fine-spacing prefactor
eta^(d-4) is1, so no additional eta power repairs this estimate.
If physical boundary links are fixed, choose an interior coarse edge
and a sufficiently large fixed coarse box; the same construction leaves
the fixed outer boundary unchanged. The full compact target in this
note has no such obstruction to choosing the edge in any case.

Compatible tree gauge changes neither the action nor the coarse loop
values. In its quotient coordinates the selected chord equals
exp(i theta H), as in Section4. Thus the minimum classical action at
these fixed nonflat path-decimated coarse data is at most (7.2).
At the origin the corresponding effective Maxwell quadratic form,
obtained by minimizing the fine quadratic action under the linearized
path constraints, has a value at most C_H/M in this direction. If a
nonlinear constrained action Hessian is used, its same directional
bound follows by taking theta->0 at each fixed M.

Therefore NO uniform-in-M positive coarse precision or classical
coercivity comparable to the ordinary fixed coarse plaquette energy
can hold for this path-decimation map. This counterexample does not
evaluate the quantum entropy or rule out every possible construction
using decimation. It does rule out promoting the fixed-graph tree
projection into the uniform classical attachment needed by the native
averaging route. All finite-graph positive-gap statements above have
their declared graph-dependent constants.

8. WHAT THIS MAP DOES NOT PROVE

This is thin-path holonomy decimation. It is NOT Bałaban's native
symmetrized block averaging map. In particular it does not inherit
native minimizing extensions, matched free precisions, suppression of
thin-loop fluctuations, source domains or prescribed cutoff identities.
No change of names can substitute (4.4) into those proofs.

Nor does a fixed-graph coordinate projection give UV-uniform locality:
the path lengths and tree-coordinate reads may grow with refinement.
The blocked action is the exact marginal of (5.1), generally containing
nonlocal interactions; it is not automatically a Wilson action with a
renormalized scalar coupling. Reflection positivity, a local score norm,
the physical low cocycle and continuum nontriviality all remain separate.

The constructive result is nonetheless exact: for this specified bare
finite graph blocking, the joint-law variables and nonlinear gauge
quotient can be aligned so that the coarse map is a genuine coordinate
projection. A finite real conditional theorem may then be applied to
the ACTUAL joint law when its separate action/tail hypotheses are proved.
