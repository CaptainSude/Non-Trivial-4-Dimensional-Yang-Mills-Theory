RESULT

There is a concrete real-variable bypass of the old-mark analytic problem
at ONE conditional integration. A separated original Wilson action source
can be replaced, inside its covariance with the other original source,
by a local function of exterior staple defects. The complete original
normalizer is retained. The removed term is the explicit conditional value at a flat exterior; it is not the full Wilson expectation.
The flat-value-subtracted conditional source is pointwise comparable to the geometric
defect with relative error at most 1/(4 beta). A varying source profile
adds its star oscillation TIMES that same defect, rather than a separate
per-link constant.

This is a finite, original-law identity, not the missing all-scale native
bound. It does not apply to a history-owned raw mark merely because that
mark's original source lies far away: its ownership or branch label can
read the link being conditionally integrated. The theorem instead applies
before that history decomposition to the full original covariance.

The convexity argument in Section 2 was proposed by root and independently
checked here. Sections 1, 3--6 give the actual conditional source, global
normalization, local reads, and limits of this reduction. The refined
weak-field remainder in Section 4 has an independent elementary integral
proof. No shared frontier or previous producer was edited.

1. LITERAL ORIGINAL CONDITIONAL LAW AND ITS SOURCE

Take a finite periodic nondegenerate four-dimensional SU(2) Wilson lattice,
with periods at least four, normalized link Haar measure, beta>0, and

  mu(dU)=Z^{-1} exp[-beta sum_p e_p(U)] dHaar(U),
  e_p=1-Tr(U_p)/2,             S_q=sum_p q_p e_p.

The profiles q_p are deterministic real numbers. An integrated link e
belongs to six distinct elementary plaquettes. Hold every other link
fixed, and identify U_e with its unit quaternion u in S^3. With the proper
orientation of the other three links of each incident plaquette, there
are unit quaternions v_{p,e} such that

  e_p=1-u dot v_{p,e},
  V_e=sum_{p containing e} v_{p,e},    r_e=|V_e| in [0,6].   (1.1)

This includes inverse appearances of U_e because inversion is an
orthogonal linear operation on quaternion coordinates. The ACTUAL
conditional probability, including its normalizer, is

  q_e(du|outside)=exp(beta V_e dot u)du / z(beta r_e),
  z(s)=2 I_1(s)/s,             m(s)=I_2(s)/I_1(s),
  E_e u=m(beta r_e)n_e,        n_e=V_e/r_e.                  (1.2)

At r_e=0 the law is Haar, m(0)=0, and n_e can be any unit vector.
Formula (1.2) is the actual six-staple conditional law already derived in
TURN45/64/85. It is not the conditional law after fixing an arbitrary
native history or its deciding indicators.

Define the nonnegative geometric defect and its constituent deficits:

  D_e=6-r_e,
  delta_{p,e}=1-v_{p,e} dot n_e >=0,
  sum_{p containing e} delta_{p,e}=D_e.                    (1.3)

The last identity also holds at r_e=0 for any choice of n_e.
For a profile put

  qbar_e=(1/6)sum_{p containing e}q_p,
  omega_e(q)=max_{p containing e}|q_p-qbar_e|.

Writing S_{q,e}=sum_{p containing e}q_p e_p, direct conditional
expectation yields the EXACT identity

  E_e S_{q,e}
   =qbar_e [6-r_e m(beta r_e)]
      +m(beta r_e)sum_{p containing e}(q_p-qbar_e)delta_{p,e}.
                                                               (1.4)

Its profile correction is bounded by omega_e(q) D_e. In particular the
correction has no independent microscopic fluctuation or constant term.
For a constant-star profile it is zero exactly.

With c_beta from Section 2, an equivalent form is

 E_e S_{q,e}-c_beta qbar_e
  =m(beta r_e)sum_p q_p delta_{p,e}
           +(sum_p q_p)[m(6 beta)-m(beta r_e)].            (1.5)

Since m is increasing, this is nonnegative whenever all q_p>=0,
including nonconstant profiles and r_e=0. This positivity concerns the
conditional source function, not its covariance with a separated source
or its expectation uniformly in the cutoff.

2. GLOBAL FLAT-VALUE-SUBTRACTED COMPARISON, WITHOUT A GOOD-STAPLE EXCEPTION

Set

  c_beta=6[1-m(6 beta)],
  T_beta(D)=6m(6 beta)-(6-D)m(beta(6-D)),  0<=D<=6.

Then

  6-r m(beta r)=c_beta+T_beta(6-r),
  m(6 beta)D <= T_beta(D) <= D,
  0 <= D-T_beta(D) <= D/(4 beta).                          (2.1)

The last bound is valid for every beta>0; for small beta it is merely
loose. The first comparison remains meaningful for all beta, including
the beta=0 limiting identity T_0=0.

Here is a complete proof. The Bessel ratio, equivalently differentiation
of the S^3 tilted Haar integral, obeys

  m'(s)=1-m(s)^2-3m(s)/s,   m(s)=s/4+O(s^3).               (2.2)

For s>0 one also has m'(s)=Var_s(n dot u)>0 and m(s)>0.
For completeness, integrate the derivative of
exp(st)(1-t^2)^(3/2) over [-1,1]. Its endpoint values vanish, and the
result is s E_s(1-t^2)=3 E_s t. Since m=E_s t and
m'=E_s t^2-m^2, this gives (2.2) directly. The expansion at zero uses
E_Haar t^2=1/4 and symmetry.
Let g(s)=s m(s). Then

  g'(s)=s-[g(s)^2+2g(s)]/s,
  g''(s)=2-[2g(s)+3]g'(s)/s.                             (2.3)

First g'>0 by g'=m+s m'. Moreover g'<1 everywhere. It starts below one
near zero. At a hypothetical first point g'=1, the first equation gives
g+1=sqrt(s^2-s+1), and the second gives

  g''=2-[2sqrt(s^2-s+1)+1]/s <0.

This contradicts the nonnegative derivative at a first crossing from
below. The strict inequality follows from
s^2-s+1=(s-1/2)^2+3/4, and is also immediate when s<1/2.

Next g''>0 everywhere. It starts at g''(0)=1/2. At any zero of g'',
differentiating (2.3) and using the zero condition gives

  g'''=2[1-g'^2]/s >0.

A first zero reached from strictly positive g'' must instead have
g'''<=0. This contradiction proves convexity.

For 0<=s<=s_* convexity and g(0)=0 give
g(s)<=s g(s_*)/s_*. The upper slope bound g'<1 gives
g(s_*)-g(s)<=s_*-s. Taking s=beta r and s_*=6 beta and dividing by beta
proves the two-sided comparison in (2.1).

Finally 1-m(s)<=3/(2s). One direct proof uses z=s(1-n dot u): its density
on [0,2s] is proportional to

  z^(1/2) exp(-z) (1-z/(2s))^(1/2).

It is a Gamma(shape 3/2, rate 1) density tilted by a nonincreasing
nonnegative function. Its mean is therefore at most 3/2, by the elementary
negative covariance of an increasing and a decreasing function. Hence
s[1-m(s)]<=3/2. Combining this with (2.1) at s=6 beta gives D/(4 beta).

No asymptotic Bessel expansion or exclusion of r=0 was used in (2.1). The word centered below refers only to subtraction of this flat-exterior value. The transformed source generally has nonzero expectation under the original Wilson law.

3. THE DEFECT IS A LOCAL CLOSED-LOOP FUNCTION

The identity

  sum_{p<p'} |v_{p,e}-v_{p',e}|^2=36-r_e^2

gives

  D_e = 2/(6+r_e) sum_{p<p'} [1-v_{p,e} dot v_{p',e}].     (3.1)

Each dot product is one half the trace of the closed path formed from
one three-link staple and the inverse of another. Thus D_e is a bounded,
nonnegative, gauge-invariant function of exterior closed loops. It reads
no U_e and uses only the six plaquette stars around e. The formula also
holds at r_e=0. Immediate retracing in some paired paths causes no problem;
these are the literal products of the correctly oriented staple paths.

Although n_e is undefined at r_e=0, the profile term in (1.4) is continuous
and independent of that choice there. A formula without n_e is

  m(beta r_e)sum_p(q_p-qbar_e)delta_{p,e}
   =-[m(beta r_e)/r_e] sum_p(q_p-qbar_e)v_{p,e} dot V_e,    (3.2)

with m(beta r)/r continued at zero as beta/4. Also r m(beta r) is a smooth
function of r^2 at zero. Thus the complete conditional insertion is
well-defined on ALL original real configurations. No inverse-staple
singularity or manually chosen axis is introduced.

This is only real regularity. Uniform holomorphy on every native complex
external domain does not follow from (3.2); the continued conditional
normalizer has complex zeros. The new argument deliberately does not
require that holomorphy for its finite original-law covariance identity.

3A. ACTUAL CONDITIONAL RESPONSE WITH NO BETA LOSS

There is also a uniform response estimate for the CONSTANT-star source,
including the response of its actual conditional probability and normalizer.
As a function of the exterior staple sum V, write

  A_beta(V)=6-beta^(-1)g(beta|V|).

By Section 2, 0<g'(s)<1 for s>0 and g'(0)=0. Consequently

  |grad_V A_beta|=g'(beta|V|)<=1,
  |A_beta(V')-A_beta(V)|<=|V'-V|.                         (3.3)

The function is smooth at V=0 because g(s)=s^2/4+O(s^4).
For a differentiable path of actual exterior staples,

  |d A_beta/dt| <= |sum_p d v_{p,e}/dt|
                        <=sum_p |d v_{p,e}/dt|.           (3.4)

This is a genuine conditional-source response bound uniform in beta;
the conditional score and normalizer are included by the exact formula,
rather than bounded separately by their apparent beta factors. Subtracting
c_beta does not change (3.3). The same statement holds for a scalar
constant-star profile, multiplied by its absolute value.

Do not extend this bound unchanged to arbitrary varying profiles. Their
extra term (3.2) can have derivatives of order beta omega_e(q) near V=0.
Nor does (3.3) by itself bound the dependence of a multistep posterior on
its native exterior variables: the actual map from those variables to
the present original staples would still have to be controlled.

3B. ACTUAL CONDITIONAL MINIMUM AND ITS QUADRATIC SOURCE COEFFICIENT

The defect has a direct variational meaning on every real exterior:

  D_e=min_{u in S^3} sum_{p containing e}(1-u dot v_{p,e})
     =6-|V_e|.                                           (3.5)

At V_e!=0 the minimizer is n_e; at V_e=0 every u minimizes. Thus the
constant-star source after conditional integration differs from the
ACTUAL classical conditional minimum by the scalar c_beta and a relative
remainder bounded in (2.1). This is not a substitution of a fresh
reference-field action for the old source.

Around a flat exterior choose a common orthogonal quaternion frame
with v_{p,e}=(1-|x_p|^2/2,x_p)+O(|x_p|^3). Put xbar=(1/6)sum_p x_p.
Expansion gives

  D_e=(1/2)sum_p |x_p-xbar|^2+O(|x|^3).                   (3.6)

This is exactly the Schur complement obtained by minimizing the quadratic
star energy (1/2)sum_p |y-x_p|^2 over the varied link coordinate y.
For a constant-star profile, the quadratic coefficient after subtracting
the flat exterior value is multiplied by

  T_beta'(0)=g'(6 beta)=1-b'(6 beta).                     (3.7)

Equation (4.3) below therefore shows
0<g'(6 beta)<1 and 1-g'(6 beta)<=C beta^(-2) for beta>=1/6.
The actual conditional quadratic source changes only by O(beta^-2),
although the exception-free pointwise relative estimate in (2.1) is
O(beta^-1). This is a one-link compact-law statement. It does not identify
the quadratic extraction of a full native blocked map or prove stability
after overlapping conditional integrations or repeated coarse graining.

There is an equally explicit weighted quadratic jet. At the same flat
exterior, delta_{p,e}^{(2)}=(1/2)|x_p-xbar|^2, so (1.4) gives

 (E_e S_{q,e}-c_beta qbar_e)^{(2)}
  =(1/2)sum_p {qbar_e g'(6 beta)
             +m(6 beta)(q_p-qbar_e)}|x_p-xbar|^2.          (3.8)

The original weighted source evaluated at the UNWEIGHTED conditional
classical minimizer has quadratic term
(1/2)sum_p q_p |x_p-xbar|^2. Subtracting this from (3.8) and using
(2.1),(4.3) bounds the absolute quadratic error by

 [C beta^(-2)|qbar_e|+(4 beta)^(-1)omega_e(q)]
                           (1/2)sum_p |x_p-xbar|^2,      (3.9)

for beta>=1/6. The constant-star coefficient has O(beta^-2) change;
the profile variation has O(beta^-1) change. These statements concern
the literal quadratic Taylor coefficient at a flat original exterior,
not the size of the source on typical configurations or a completed
native marginal extraction. The reference minimum in this comparison
is the baseline unweighted action minimum; it has not been recomputed
using the source profile as a new action.

4. SHARPER CENTERED REMAINDER AWAY FROM ZERO STAPLE SUM

For every fixed r_0 in (0,6], beta r_0>=1, and r in [r_0,6],

  0<=[6-r]-[6m(6 beta)-r m(beta r)]
       <= C (beta r_0)^(-2) [6-r].                       (4.1)

Here C is absolute; the r_0 dependence is displayed. Thus the stronger
O(beta^-2) statement holds in a fixed nondegenerate-staple region, still
with the centered error proportional to D rather than a constant.

Proof independent of the convexity argument. Let
b(s)=s[1-m(s)]. In the Gamma representation from Section 2, b(s)=E_s z.
For s>0, differentiating the density at fixed z gives

  b'(s)=Cov_s(z, z/[4s^2(1-z/(2s))]).                     (4.2)

The upper integration endpoint contributes no boundary term because the
undifferentiated weight vanishes there. The differentiated square-root
singularity is integrable. This can also be justified first away from
the endpoint and then by dominated convergence on a compact interval
of positive s. Both arguments of this covariance increase, so b'>=0.
For s>=1,

  b'(s) <= (4s^2)^(-1) E_s[z^2/(1-z/(2s))] <= C/s^2.     (4.3)

For the second inequality the normalizing integral is bounded below by
its fixed portion 0<=z<=1. In the numerator split 0<=z<=s, where the
inverse square root is at most sqrt(2), and s<=z<=2s, whose integral
is bounded by C s^(7/2) exp(-s). This is uniformly bounded for s>=1.

Since

  D-T_beta(D)=[b(6 beta)-b(beta r)]/beta,

integrating (4.3) over u in [r,6] proves (4.1). This refinement is not
needed for the global, exception-free inequality (2.1).

5. EXACT GLOBAL SEPARATED-COVARIANCE REDUCTION

Let E(q) be the set of all original links in plaquettes with q_p!=0.
Suppose F is integrable and measurable outside EVERY link in E(q).
At finite volume all action sources considered here are bounded.
Define, under the SAME original Wilson law,

  D_beta,q(U)=(1/4)sum_e {
       qbar_e T_beta(D_e)
       +m(beta r_e)sum_{p containing e}(q_p-qbar_e)delta_{p,e}
                               },                        (5.1)

where only stars with some q_p!=0 need be included. Then

  E S_q = [1-m(6 beta)]sum_p q_p + E D_beta,q,
  Cov(F,S_q)=Cov(F,D_beta,q).                              (5.2)

Proof. Every plaquette has four edges, so S_q=(1/4)sum_e S_{q,e}.
For each summand separately, F does not read e. Conditional expectation
therefore preserves both its ordinary expectation and its pairing with F.
Use (1.4), subtract the deterministic c_beta qbar_e, and sum. The scalar
is correct because

  (1/4)sum_e c_beta qbar_e
        =(c_beta/6)sum_p q_p=[1-m(6 beta)]sum_p q_p.

This is a sum of SEPARATE conditional-expectation identities. It is not
a claim that simultaneous conditional draws on overlapping stars are
independent, nor a composition of heat-bath kernels, nor an equality
S_q=constant+D_beta,q pointwise.

In particular, if the original plaquette-link supports of f and q are
disjoint, F=S_f gives

  Cov(S_f,S_q)=Cov(S_f,D_beta,q).                          (5.3)

If additionally the complete star read of D_beta,q contains none of the
original source edges E(f), applying the same identity to the f source
gives the TWO-SIDED version

  Cov(S_f,S_q)=Cov(D_beta,f,D_beta,q).                     (5.4)

For fixed positive physical separation the additional read condition
holds at all sufficiently fine meshes. At a specified finite lattice
the exact link-read condition, rather than an arbitrary distance
constant, is the assertion used. The source-defect transform enlarges
the read only by one plaquette star around a source edge.

6. POINTWISE ERROR, WHAT IT DOES AND DOES NOT ESTIMATE

Let D_q=(1/4)sum_e qbar_e D_e. Equations (1.3),(2.1) imply pointwise

  |D_beta,q-D_q|
    <=(1/4)sum_e [|qbar_e|/(4 beta)+omega_e(q)]D_e.         (6.1)

For a smooth scalar physical profile sampled at plaquette centers,
omega_e(q)<=C a [q]_Lip, including stars meeting its support boundary
when q is extended smoothly by zero. Orientation-dependent sources
require their actual star oscillation; one cannot replace it by a spatial
gradient without the corresponding regularity assumption.

The pointwise error has no standalone per-edge constant. Consequently,
for a valid F in Section 5,

 |Cov(F,D_beta,q-D_q)|
 <=(1/4)sum_e [|qbar_e|/(4 beta)+omega_e(q)]
                         E[|F-EF| D_e].                 (6.2)

This retains the actual correlated geometric defect rather than first
bounding it by the number of fine links. Formula (6.2) is a legitimate
bound but is NOT cutoff-uniform as presently proved. Replacing the last
expectation by a crude absolute moment reintroduces the fine root census.
Pointwise comparison of T_beta(D) with D does not imply multiplicative
comparison or positivity of separated covariances with an arbitrary F.

No assertion D_e=O(a^4) on typical Wilson configurations is made. Smooth classical-field scaling cannot replace an estimate in the quantum measure.

This step provides an explicit, gauge-invariant, flat-value-subtracted
descendant of the original source in real variables. It avoids needing
an analytic norm for a history-dependent old mark at this step. It does
not yet give a closed RG class for the generated loop-defect functions:
after actual decimation the action is no longer a sum of elementary
Wilson plaquettes, so the next conditional law is not automatically
the same six-staple tilted Haar law. Iterating (5.1) as though the action
were unchanged, or changing only the source and declaring the lattice
coarser, would be invalid.

The proposed useful next gate is therefore specific: propagate the
explicit flat-value-subtracted exterior function (5.1), with its closed-loop form
(3.1) and relative remainder (6.1), through one actual admitted native
regular step; or construct a controlled decimation of BOTH the action
and this source. It is not enough to invoke holomorphy of a bare trace.

Neither (5.2) nor its two-sided version estimates the already
history-resolved Cov(R_f,H_rest) unless the claimed F measurability has
been established including every ownership, class and continuation read.
Conditioning on native indicators also changes (1.2). The present result
is a possible finite start of a route around that decomposition, not a
retroactive closure of its missing marked comparison.
