POSITIVE INSERTION WITH HEREDITARY PRIVATE RECORDS -- TURN62
20 October 2026

This is a finite-measure theorem. Its native Yang--Mills application
requires the source identifications recorded separately. It is not
a continuum existence theorem.

1. STATEMENT

Fix outer data o. Let L be a finite set of geometric component labels.
For each X in L let (E_X, nu_X) be a measurable space with a finite
positive measure. An element of E_X may contain all ancestral discrete
choices, gauge-tree choices, and the old continuous integration
variables private to X. No probability normalization is assumed.

For every eta subset L let H_eta be a measurable {0,1}-valued function
on the product of E_X for X in eta. All compatibility conditions,
including conditions involving several private records, may occur
in H_eta. Let V_eta be real and measurable on {H_eta=1}.

Suppose:

(a) Erasure is admissible:

 H_(eta union {X})(xi_eta,xi_X) <= H_eta(xi_eta).

Thus a record permitted with X remains permitted when X is erased.
This is a pointwise condition on surviving records, not merely
downward closure of the set of undecorated geometric families.

(b) The SAME measures nu_Y and record coordinates E_Y occur for
every surviving Y. In particular they do not acquire a normalization,
cutoff, denominator, or history multiplicity depending on eta.

(c) For X not in eta and on {H_(eta union {X})=1},

 V_(eta union {X})(xi_eta,xi_X) - V_eta(xi_eta) <= b_X,

where b_X is finite and independent of the private records.
This holds for every eta, not just for one initial family.

Put

 w_eta = integral H_eta exp(V_eta) product_(Y in eta) nu_Y(dxi_Y),
 Z = sum_eta w_eta,       0 < Z < infinity,
 lambda_X = exp(b_X) nu_X(E_X).

The empty-family exponent V_empty is retained. It may depend on o.
It is not set to zero by convention.

For a set L_S of component labels, let N_S=|eta intersect L_S| and
Lambda_S=sum_(X in L_S) lambda_X. Under the probability weights
w_eta/Z,

 E[(N_S)_q] <= Lambda_S^q             for every integer q>=0,
 E[exp(t N_S)] <= exp((exp(t)-1)Lambda_S)   for every t>=0.

Here (N)_q=N(N-1)...(N-q+1), with (N)_0=1.

2. PROOF

For a fixed family eta and X outside eta, assumptions (a)--(c)
give the pointwise bound, with the exponent evaluated only where
the left side is nonzero,

 H_(eta union X) exp(V_(eta union X))
 <= H_eta exp(V_eta) exp(b_X).

The right side is independent of xi_X. Integrating the private
record xi_X against the same finite measure nu_X gives

 w_(eta union X) <= lambda_X w_eta.

For distinct X_1,...,X_q in eta, repeated erasure gives

 w_eta <= (product_i lambda_(X_i)) w_(eta without {X_i}).

The intermediate inequalities are legal by (a) and (c).
For fixed distinct X_1,...,X_q, the map
eta -> eta without {X_1,...,X_q}
is injective on the families containing all the X_i. Consequently,

 sum_(eta containing all X_i) w_eta
 <= (product_i lambda_(X_i)) sum_(eta' avoiding all X_i) w_eta'
 <= (product_i lambda_(X_i)) Z.

Sum this inequality over ordered distinct q-tuples in L_S and
divide by the SAME Z. The left side is E[(N_S)_q]. Dropping the
distinctness restriction in the nonnegative right side bounds it
by Lambda_S^q. Finally,

 exp(tN) = sum_(q>=0) (N)_q (exp(t)-1)^q/q!

for integer N>=0. Positivity and the factorial estimate prove the
exponential bound. No lower estimate on Z and no division by a
possibly tiny individual nu_X(E_X) occurs. QED.

3. OUTER INTEGRATION

Let all the objects depend measurably on o, and let the original
positive representation have weight W(do) w_eta(o). Suppose its
total mass is finite and positive. If

 Lambda_S(o) <= epsilon |S|

for almost every o, then for every outer-measurable event A,

 E[exp(tN_S) 1_A]
 <= exp((exp(t)-1) epsilon |S|) P(A).

Proof: multiply the pointwise inequality Z_t(o)<=C Z_0(o) by
1_A(o) W(do), integrate, and divide by the original total mass.
This works for A equal to the entire outer space. If the hypotheses
are verified only on a sector A, it proves only the stated
sector-restricted estimate.

4. WHAT THIS RELAXES

The allowed old-history space need not be a Cartesian product.
One can integrate all private histories against fixed component
measures and put their shared admissibility restriction in H_eta.
Only the pointwise erasure implication is needed.

Equivalently, discrete histories can be promoted to decorated
component labels. The present formulation avoids treating each
history as if it separately received the same aggregate mass bound.
If the source gives a bound on the SUM over histories, retain that
sum inside nu_X. Counting all histories once more would be wrong.

Fixed-catalogue factorization alone does not imply assumptions (a)
or (b). Nor does positivity alone imply them. They must come from
the native definition of the component operation and the actual
old-history erasure map at fixed NEW outer data.

5. RELEVANCE OF THE NEW PRIMARY PDF

Bałaban, Large field renormalization II, CMP 122 (1989), 355--392,
pp378--379, equations (1.71)--(1.72), visibly provides:

- a component operator defined using its own restricted old
  history, gauge average, and old private integrals;
- the common new small-field cutoff and effective action outside
  the first-class component sum;
- retained second-class factors outside that same sum;
- interior V(X^~,U_k) inside the corresponding private operator;
- the explicit warning that the history sum in the operator also
  acts on the interaction exponential.

The last point is why xi_X includes the history and why the
interaction V_eta must retain its dependence on all those records.
Replacing each operator by its total mass BEFORE evaluating the
interaction would not be an identity.

This theorem concerns a count attached to the actual first-class
component sum. It does not identify that count with a continuum
observable, or establish rescaled moment bounds, Euclidean
invariance, Osterwalder--Schrader reconstruction, or a nonzero
continuum reflection norm.
