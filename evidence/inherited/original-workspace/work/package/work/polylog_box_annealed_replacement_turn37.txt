ANNEALED GROWING-BOX CONTROL AND NORMALIZED REPLACEMENT
19 October 2026. Root derivation, turn37. Companion to the conditional tail.

RESULT AND SCOPE

The bad-boundary mass in the bare Wilson growing-box theorem can itself
be bounded uniformly in total lattice volume. A polynomial geometric
energy threshold, the original bare Wilson chessboard inequality, and
the exact conditional normalization give a sparse positive replacement
theorem on boxes of side R <= C_R [log(1/g)]^b. Neither independence of
the boundaries nor reflection positivity of the replacement is assumed.

This is still a theorem about one original bare Wilson measure and a
separated family of boxes. It does not identify a nonlinear averaging
fiber, supply an iterated effective conditional law, or control fields
with divergent renormalized sup norms. Its physical boxes shrink in
the usual ultraviolet scaling.

1. BOUNDARY GAUGE WITH AN EXPLICIT POLYNOMIAL ENERGY COST

Let Omega_R={0,...,R}^4. On its entire graph choose the ordinary
coordinate tree based at 0: the root-to-x path first changes x4, then
x3, then x2, then x1. These paths have a common-prefix tree structure.
In its tree gauge, denote the complete link field by y. A non-tree
edge in direction mu closes its tree loop by commuting that single
mu step past the lower-index coordinate legs. The loop is filled by
at most three coordinate strips of width at most R and height one.
Each strip uses distinct plaquettes; their directions differ. In
particular, the total filling area is <=3R, and the weaker <=C R^2
estimate is enough below. Tree links themselves equal identity.

The same nonabelian product and Cauchy--Schwarz argument as in the
conditional geometry audit yields

              max_e ||y_e-I|| <= C R sqrt(S_Omega(U)).       (1.1)

Here S_Omega is the uncentered nonnegative sum over ALL plaquettes
wholly inside the cube, including its boundary plaquettes. No boundary
values have been fixed in (1.1), and every vertex may be gauged.

Now choose a breadth-first spanning tree of the BOUNDARY graph, rooted
at 0. Every boundary vertex has intrinsic boundary-graph distance
<=4R from 0. To see this, keep one zero coordinate fixed while reducing
the others; if the available boundary coordinate equals R instead,
keep it fixed while reducing the others to zero, and then reduce it
while one of the other zero coordinates keeps the path on the boundary.
Consequently every fundamental boundary-tree loop has length <=8R+1.

Let B(V) be the unique boundary tree representative, up to conjugation
at the root, of the given boundary-link data V. Define

                 d_B(V)=max_(boundary e)||B(V)_e-I||.

This is continuous and gauge invariant, and depends ONLY on boundary
links. Compute it after the full-cube tree gauge used in (1.1).
Each boundary chord holonomy is a product along a boundary-tree loop;
its distance to identity is at most (8R+1) max_e||y_e-I||.
It follows, for every extension of V to the interior, that

             d_B(V) <= C R^2 sqrt(S_Omega(U)).               (1.2)

No filling inside the boundary sphere and no boundary-only action
coercivity is needed for this comparison. The full-cube energy is used
only to prove an inequality for the boundary-measurable event.

Use the constant c_0 from the conditional theorem and set

 delta_R=c_0 R^-24,       B={d_B(V)>delta_R}.

Then the contrapositive of (1.2) gives the literal positive-event bound

                     B implies S_Omega >= c R^-52.          (1.3)

The constant contains c_0^2 and is fixed independently of R and g.
On B^c the boundary tree representative meets exactly the boundary
hypothesis of the conditional tail theorem, in that fixed frame.

2. COMPATIBLE BARE REFLECTION CELLS AND THE ACTUAL DENOMINATOR

Use the positive normalized Wilson law exp[-S_tot/g^2]/Z_g on a
compatible periodic four-dimensional lattice, with normalized Haar
measure per link. Choose a standard Wilson chessboard tiling by closed
cells of side L=4R (any fixed integer multiple with a buffer works).
Require the torus periods to be compatible with this tiling and the
reflection/chessboard theorem. Place Omega_R strictly inside one cell,
at integer coordinates, and reflect it to obtain the other core boxes.
These cores have pairwise disjoint vertex sets. Boundary trees and
axial forests are reflected with their core, not reset independently.

Let m be the number of cells, C_j their closed copies, and S_Cj their
within-cell plaquette sums. A plaquette can belong to at most a fixed
q_* (for example 16) of these closed-cell sums, so

                  sum_j S_Cj <= q_* S_tot.

Disseminate the positive boundary event B by the actual reflections.
Its support is entirely in the core boundary, hence in the cell.
On the event that every reflected copy is bad, (1.3) implies

                      S_tot >= c m R^-52.

Reserve half the action and integrate the rest to obtain

 Z_g(all bad) <= exp[-c m g^-2 R^-52] Z_(sqrt(2)g).

For completeness, the old elementary lower subset is uniform in R:
restrict every bare link to a ball of radius c g. Its Haar volume is
>=c' g^(dim G), and each plaquette action is <=C g^2. If N_0 is the
total number of bare sites, then for 0<g<g_0

                Z_g >= exp[-C N_0(1+log(1/g))],
                Z_(sqrt(2)g) <= 1.

Take the chessboard root AFTER this normalized ratio. Since
N_0/m=L^4=O(R^4), each prescribed intersection of distinct reflected
bad-boundary cells satisfies

 mu_g(intersection_(i in A) B_i) <= beta_(R,g)^|A|,           (2.1)

 beta_(R,g)=min{1,
   exp[C R^4(1+log(1/g))-c g^-2 R^-52]}.                    (2.2)

The multilinear chessboard inequality is applied with the event in
the selected cells and the constant one elsewhere. No independent
boundary law, effective-action sandwich, or new ultraviolet vacuum
cancellation is used. The entire coarse-cell entropy R^4 remains.
For R<=C_R ell^b, ell=log(1/g), the negative term in (2.2) has order
at least exp(2ell) ell^(-52b), which defeats this entropy. In particular
beta is smaller than exp[-c ell^p] for EVERY fixed p at small g.

3. ACTUAL CONDITIONING, WINDOW, AND COMBINED BAD EVENT

Let X contain every link other than the nonboundary interiors of the
selected core boxes, in particular all their boundary links. Since
the cores are vertex-disjoint and embedded cubes, every plaquette
reading an integrated interior edge lies wholly in that box. Thus
the ORIGINAL Wilson conditional measure factors exactly:

                 mu_g(domega,du)=mu_X(domega) product_i Q_i^omega(du_i).

On B_i^c use B(V_i) as the boundary frame. Extend that boundary gauge
to the box in any fixed covariant tree construction, then use the
anchored forest gauge of the conditional theorem. Define W_i to be
the window about the ACTUAL conditional minimum x_*(B(V_i)):

                    max_e |x_e-x_*,e|<=gT.

The complete resulting tree representative, minimum and invariant
norm transform only by constant root conjugation, so the lifted
event is gauge invariant. The transforms are finite products, and
the minimum is analytic on the admitted boundary chart, hence this
is a measurable conditional window. No identity centering is used.

The conditional theorem gives, uniformly for all good boundaries,

 Q_i^omega(W_i^c)<=alpha_(R,g,T),

 alpha=min{1,
  exp[C R^4 log(2+R)-c R^-8 T^2]
 +exp[C R^4 log((2+R)/g)-c R^-20/g^2]}.                    (3.1)

Let C_i be the actual original-box defect: B_i, or (B_i^c and W_i^c).
Conditional independence, (2.1), and expansion over the boundary-bad
subset give the normalized, unconditional joint estimate

              mu_g(C_i for every i in A) <= (alpha+beta)^|A|. (3.2)

Indeed for a chosen boundary-bad subset J the conditional factors
for A\J are <=alpha, leaving E[product_(i in J)1_Bi], bounded by
beta^|J|. The other good-boundary restrictions can be dropped.
This proves a literal bare-event estimate, not just one for auxiliary
failure labels. It does not assert iid domination of C_i.

4. NORMALIZED REPLACEMENT WITH NO EXTERIOR REWEIGHTING

The archived exact conditional replacement now applies with the
explicit growing-geometry constants above. For small g so alpha<1,
put P_i=Q_i(.|W_i) on B_i^c and P_i=Q_i on B_i. Set

                   nu(domega,du)=mu_X(domega) product_i P_i^omega(du_i).

Writing p_i=1_(B_i^c) Q_i(W_i^c), the density is

                 dnu/dmu=product_(i:B_i^c) 1_Wi/(1-p_i).

Each factor has conditional expectation one. Therefore nu preserves
the EXACT original exterior marginal, and the same operation on the
unnormalized measure preserves the exact raw partition mass Z_g.
The factors (1-p_i)^-1 depend on the boundaries and are retained.

The explicit mixture coupling Q_i=(1-p_i)P_i+p_i Q_i(.|W_i^c)
(with an arbitrary unused last kernel when p_i=0) produces discrepancy
marks D_i dominated by iid Bernoulli(alpha), conditionally and jointly
with the exterior. On bad boundaries the two samples are identical.
The uncontrolled marks B_i OR D_i obey the joint bound (alpha+beta)^|A|.
In nu the actual box defect C_i is just B_i almost surely, so its
joint inclusion bound improves to beta^|A|.

For bounded f depending on the exterior and at most |A| selected
interiors,

                  |mu(f)-nu(f)|<=2 ||f||_infty |A| alpha.

The exact joint-with-exterior total variation is
E_muX[1-product_(i in A)(1-p_i)], at most |A|alpha.
The forward relative entropy D(nu||mu) on this retained marginal is
E_muX sum_(i in A)[-log(1-p_i)],
at most |A|alpha/(1-alpha). All archived finite bounded-source
identities and local cumulant comparisons remain valid with these
same constants. None is a statement about a renormalized unbounded
field without its own norm estimate.

On a deterministic adjacency graph of maximum degree Delta, the
joint bound also gives connected-defect-cluster tails <=
[Delta^2 min(1,alpha+beta)]^k for size at least k at a specified root,
using the elementary spanning-tree traversal count. This is useful
when the bracket is less than one; otherwise it is only a trivial
upper bound. It is not an identification with native RG determining
sets or history components.

5. POLYLOGARITHMIC CONSEQUENCE AND PRECISE LIMIT

For R<=C_R ell^b and T=ell^a with a>6b+1/2, the conditional theorem
gives alpha<=C exp[-c ell^(2a-8b)]. Equation(2.2) gives a smaller beta.
Thus (3.2), the replacement error, and the sparse-cluster estimates
all hold uniformly in total volume with

                    epsilon<=C exp[-c ell^(2a-8b)].

This closes the GOOD-BOUNDARY MASS issue for this separated growing
bare-box operation. It does not close the corresponding issue for
an iterated effective action or a coarse constrained fiber. The bare
chessboard entropy is still the number of fine sites in the cell.
If R is of order a_uv^-1 at a fixed physical size while g follows
asymptotic freedom, this entropy estimate gives no useful conclusion.
Nor has the replacement law been shown to preserve the native block
average, all observables, or reflection positivity. It preserves exactly
the exterior marginal and the partition mass stated above. The full
Yang--Mills continuum existence theorem remains unproved.

In particular the comparison |A|alpha is not claimed to vanish when A
contains all boxes in a fixed physical region. Under g^2 of order
1/log(1/a_uv), ell is of order log log(1/a_uv), whereas that region
contains order a_uv^-4/R^4 boxes. The displayed alpha majorant does
not beat this count. Local comparisons require the actual product
|A|alpha to tend to zero. The joint sparse-array estimates remain
uniform in total volume, a different conclusion.
