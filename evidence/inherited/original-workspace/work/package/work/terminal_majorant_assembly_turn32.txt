2. A SHARP COUNTERTEST: h=1 DOES NOT BOUND UNGROUPED M

Let X_1,...,X_N be independent fair signs and S_N=sum X_i. In an ordinary
source variable u,

 e^(-N log cosh u) E exp(u S_N)=1.                        (2.1)

Retain the individual configurations as history labels. The absolute
first coefficient of the left hand integrand has total mass E|S_N|.
Holder interpolation gives

 E|S_N| >= (E S_N^2)^(3/2)/(E S_N^4)^(1/2)
          = N^(3/2)/(3N^2-2N)^(1/2) >=sqrt(N/3).         (2.2)

For two disjoint independent groups A,B, the exactly renormalized h(s,t)
is again 1, the true mixed coefficient and cumulant are zero, but

 M_12=E|S_A S_B|=E|S_A| E|S_B|
                                >=sqrt(N_A N_B)/3.      (2.3)

The same conclusion holds in the square-zero algebra. The two one-label
vacua vanish for fair signs; the mixed vacuum is zero by independence.
The problem is not the existence of an ordinary complex source disk.
Grouping each independent local expectation BEFORE absolute value gives
1 and removes the spurious majorant. Retaining just the two counts instead
of individual configurations does not help: their centered absolute
fluctuations still have the size in (2.2)--(2.3).

There is a version with arbitrarily small per-cell activities. Let K be
Binomial(N,p), p=z/(1+z). Normalize the local Bernoulli law and subtract

                        <=3sigma^4+sigma^2.

If sigma^2>=1, the same interpolation gives

                       E|K-Np|>=sigma/2.                (2.4)

Thus z can tend to zero, even as any fixed inverse logarithmic power,
