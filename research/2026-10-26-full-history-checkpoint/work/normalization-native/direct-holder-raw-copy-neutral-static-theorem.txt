4. A DISCRETE POLYNOMIAL JET GAUGE WITH UNIFORM C1 CONTROL

Use the convention U^g_mu(y)=g(y)U_mu(y)g(y+eta e_mu)^(-1).
The first gauge is

                g1(y)=exp(i sum_mu y_mu A_mu(0)).                 (14)

It gives U^g1_mu(0)=I EXACTLY for every root bond, not only to first
order. Its transformed log A1 has A1_mu(0)=0 and a uniform C1,alpha
bound. This follows from the analytic product/exponential formulas and
uniform divided-difference bounds; the smooth linear polynomial in(14)
has all required derivatives. No axial path integration differentiates
the curvature, and no derivative is lost.

Set b_(nu,mu)=partial_eta,nu A1_mu(0) and
s_(nu,mu)=(b_(nu,mu)+b_(mu,nu))/2. Define

 phi2(y)=(1/2)sum_mu s_(mu,mu)y_mu(y_mu-eta)
                  +sum_(mu<nu) s_(mu,nu)y_mu y_nu,
                         g2(y)=exp(i phi2(y)).                    (15)

Then g2(0)=g2(eta e_mu)=I. The discrete derivative is exactly

             partial_eta,mu phi2(y)=sum_nu s_(nu,mu)y_nu.         (16)

The shifted diagonal polynomial is essential to(16); replacing it by
y_mu^2/2 would leave a spurious mesh-order term.

Finite lattice Taylor sums give A1_mu(y)=sum_nu b_(nu,mu)y_nu plus
a remainder whose value and first differences are bounded by
C K |y|^(1+alpha) and C K(|y|+eta)^alpha, respectively. For separations
at least eta the mesh correction is absorbed by the same power.
Applying the exact analytic gauge formula with(15),(16), its linear
part removes the symmetric jet. The nonlinear BCH terms have at least
one phi2 or its difference and one A1 or phi2 difference; A1(0)=0.
They have cubic spatial size, with the corresponding quadratic first-

are uniform as eta decreases.
