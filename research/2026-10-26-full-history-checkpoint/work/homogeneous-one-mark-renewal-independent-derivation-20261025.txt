HOMOGENEOUS ONE-MARK RENEWAL: INDEPENDENT DERIVATION
25 October 2026. experiment_hints. Finite-prefix theorem, uniform estimates.

SCOPE AND INPUTS

This is a quantitative consequence of the two newly scoped homogeneous
modules, not a mixed-history source theorem:

  homogeneous-full-R-body-marked-correction-producer-20261025;
  homogeneous-marked-correction-fixed-core-entry-20261025.

Every operator below uses the same original Gaussian/cut/normalizer and
the same admitted source maps as those modules. Source roots, profiles,
carrier weights and calibration are part of the actual norms. The source
is already admitted initially. No real bound on an arbitrary observable
is substituted for that norm. There is no Reynolds average of the mark.

Fix a finite actual weak prefix 0,...,N with

 x_k>=Y,  |x_(k+1)-x_k|<=B,
 x_j>=x_n+lambda(n-j)-C0  (j<=n),                 (1)

where Y is larger than fixed multiples of C0+B+1. Set
a_j=c x_j^(-1/2)(log x_j)^q, q fixed, and b_j=c_b a_j.
All radius/mother/noise hierarchies required by the input modules are
assumed verified. In particular b_j<rho and actual marked feedback uses
this same marked fresh class, whereas the body lives on its larger class.

Write A_j=C_j+E_j for the exact marked homogeneous ordinary operator,
where C_j is the fixed-core comparator at the SAME actual g_j. Its
correction is a NEW source birth at j+1 with

  ||E_j F||_(birth,j+1) <= epsilon_j ||[F]||_(fresh,j),
  epsilon_j=C_E a_j^2 chi_j,
  chi_j<=C_body x_j^(-s)+e_j(P),   e_j(P)>=0.        (2)

The output norm in (2) is a full value norm, including the scalar output;
the input can be a quotient norm because E_j annihilates every genuine
field-independent constant. Square brackets and their limitation are
specified below. The native proof of (2), including its prepared body
norm, is an input rather than an inference from a numerical beta bound.

1. THE SCALAR QUOTIENT IS ONLY A DEVICE FOR THE FIELD-DEPENDENT ESTIMATE

Let B_j be the actual coherent homogeneous source germ space, retaining
all original external source labels. Divide by genuine field-independent
constants, with quotient norm inf_c ||F-c||. This is equality of germs on
every admitted independent pair and every source profile, not equality
only on the physical minimum graph. Both normalized operators preserve
constants, so they induce operators on this quotient; E_j kills them.

Keep an exact scalar ledger. For a coherent bounded flat evaluation
sigma_j with sigma_j(1)=1, write F_j=c_j+f_j, sigma_j f_j=0. Then

 c_(j+1)=c_j+sigma_(j+1) C_j f_j
                  +sigma_(j+1) E_j f_j,
 f_(j+1)=(I-1 sigma_(j+1))(C_j+E_j)f_j.             (3)

Nothing below deletes either scalar term. The renewal estimate does not
by itself bound their total uniformly over the horizon. A scalar may be
a nonconstant function of a later mixed history or exterior datum; such
a quantity is NOT a constant in that larger algebra and cannot be
discarded. The current theorem concerns one coherent homogeneous chain.

2. A CORE PROPAGATOR WITH ONE ENTRY PRICE

Let C_[n,i)=C_(n-1)...C_i, with the empty product the identity. A source
born fresh at i widens through b_(i,r)=min(rho,Lambda_s^r b_i), where
1<Lambda_s<L and Lambda_s exceeds the fresh adjacent-radius ratio.
Its first fixed-radius age is

 t_i=ceil(log(rho/b_i)/log Lambda_s).

Let K=max(1,K_col L^4), nu=log K/log Lambda_s, and

             H_i=max(1,(rho/b_i)^nu).                       (4)

Each available pre-entry step costs at most K. Thus K^t_i<=K H_i.
After entry use the given fixed-radius centered-family static/quantum
bound with exponent p>=0. Take a fixed c_*>C0+B+1 and write X_j=x_j+c_*.
There is a fixed C_P such that, on the quotient,

 ||C_[n,i) F||_(fresh,n)
       <=C_P H_i (X_i/X_n)^p ||F||_(birth,i),  n>=i.          (5)

The endpoint at n may be restricted from a wider source class to the
marked fresh class; restriction costs no value factor. For n<i+t_i,
(5) follows directly from K^(n-i)<=K H_i and bounded recovery in (1).
For n>=i+t_i, apply entry once and then the polynomial core bound; the
short entry segment has x_(i+t_i) comparable with x_i. No repeated
H_i price is charged while the same cohort ages.

If the initial source is already at the fixed radius, its analogue of
(5) has H_initial=1 (up to a fixed norm comparison). A fresh initial
seed instead pays H_i. Every newly born E_j source always starts with
its own H_(j+1). Do not charge H_initial to a source already at fixed
radius, and do not omit the prices of subsequent fresh corrections.

If the finite horizon ends before entry, the actual source remains in
its admitted running class. Using the upper bound H_i in (5) does not
perform fictitious steps beyond that horizon or assume future couplings.

All constants in (5) require the actual high-to-current static bound and
the restored current-to-birth quantum carrier estimate. Pointwise value
contraction or a repeatedly deteriorating carrier exponent is insufficient.

3. EXACT DUHAMEL FORMULA AND THE CONJUGATING WEIGHT

For an initially admitted source F_i define F_n=A_[n,i)F_i. Finite product
expansion gives the exact identity

 F_n=C_[n,i)F_i
        +sum_(j=i)^(n-1) C_[n,j+1) E_j F_j.                 (6)

This uses the actual prefix F_j in each correction. It does not replace
it by the unperturbed source or reinsert a correction twice. Each term
is labelled by its true correction date. All source roots remain those
of the original F_i; body labels have already been summed through the
prepared body norm in (2).

Put u_n=||[F_n]||_(fresh,n), and set

 W_(n,i)=(X_i/X_n)^p,     v_n=u_n/W_(n,i).

If h_i=1 for a fixed-radius initial source and h_i=H_i for a fresh one,
(2), (5), and (6) imply

 v_n<=B_i+sum_(j=i)^(n-1) k_j v_j,
 B_i=C_P h_i ||[F_i]||,
 k_j=C_P H_(j+1) epsilon_j (X_(j+1)/X_j)^p.                 (7)

The last ratio is bounded by a fixed constant, uniformly after the weak
floor, since |x_(j+1)-x_j|<=B. The power p cancels out of the long-age
Volterra weight. This exact cocycle cancellation is why p does NOT enter
the strict rare-power threshold for propagation RELATIVE to the allowed
core polynomial growth.

Discrete Gronwall, proved by induction or the expansion of a product,
gives

 v_n<=B_i product_(j=i)^(n-1)(1+k_j)
          <=B_i exp(Q_(i,n)),
 Q_(i,n)=sum_(j=i)^(n-1) k_j.                              (8)

No smallness assumption Q<1 is needed. A finite uniform Q gives a finite
uniform multiplier. The weak floor may subsequently make Q small when
a perturbatively close comparison is desired. The same calculation in
(6) bounds the actual-minus-core quotient by

 ||[A_[n,i)F_i-C_[n,i)F_i]||
   <=C_P h_i W_(n,i)||[F_i]|| [exp(Q_(i,n))-1].             (9)

Enlarging the fixed constants in (7)--(9) handles the choice of bounded
centered representatives. This does not imply a full scalar-ledger bound.

4. THE SHARP POWER REQUIRED BY THIS MAJORANT

Adjacent radius comparability and (4) imply, for weak x_j,

 H_(j+1) a_j^2 <=C x_j^(nu/2-1)(log x_j)^r,
                 r=q(2-nu).                               (10)

Consequently the rare part of (7) is bounded by

 k_j^rare<=C x_j^(-1-d)(log x_j)^r,
                    d=s-nu/2.                             (11)

For d>0 the summand is decreasing beyond a fixed floor. From (1),
x_j>=x_n/2+lambda(n-j), so comparison with an integral gives

 sum_(j=i)^(n-1) k_j^rare
       <=C x_n^(-d)(log x_n)^r
       <=C' Y^(-d)(log Y)^r.                               (12)

Here constants can be enlarged for the finite initial part of the
decreasing range. This proves a horizon-uniform bound, tending to zero
with the weak floor, under the simple sufficient condition

                  s>nu/2, together with s>1.               (13)

The s>1 condition is inherited from the body theorem, not from the
Volterra algebra. At the borderline s=nu/2, the same majorant is summable
if r<-1, with bound C(log Y)^(r+1). For r>=-1 its infinite-horizon
integral diverges. For s<nu/2 this majorant also diverges. These are
integrability statements for the available envelope, not counterexamples
to a stronger native cancellation theorem.

In the present body notation s=(kappa0-4)/2, the strict relative condition
is kappa0>nu+4 as well as kappa0>6. This is only a numerical requirement;
the actual parameter-order theorem must supply kappa0 after, or in a
compatible joint choice with, the constants defining nu.

5. WHY AN ABSOLUTE FORCING ESTIMATE HAS A DIFFERENT THRESHOLD

If independent births B_(j+1) satisfy
||B_(j+1)||<=C a_j^2 x_j^(-s) with an already factored original amplitude,
their unperturbed total at n is bounded directly by

 C X_n^(-p) sum_(j<n)
       x_j^(p+nu/2-s-1)(log x_j)^r.                         (14)

Uniform absolute summation by this estimate requires
s>p+nu/2 (or the analogous logarithmic borderline). When strict,
(14)<=C x_n^(nu/2-s)(log x_n)^r. In contrast, (13) controls repeated
corrections RELATIVE to the existing core growth W_(n,i); it does not
remove the initial factor h_i W_(n,i) or make an uncalibrated UV source
uniformly bounded. The distinction between (13) and (14) must remain.

6. FINITE-PERIOD ERRORS ARE AMPLIFIED WITH THEIR TRUE ENTRY PRICE

The error part of the renewal exponent is

 Q^err_(i,n)(P)
   <=C sum_(j=i)^(n-1) H_(j+1) a_j^2 e_j(P),                (15)

including the bounded adjacent X ratio if desired. It is not enough to
know merely e_j(P)->0 without retaining this weighted sum. For each
FIXED finite prefix, all its weights are finite and e_j(P)->0, so a
compatible period can be chosen with Q^err_(0,N)(P)<=epsilon_N for any
specified positive tolerance. No uniform rate in N is claimed or needed
for that diagonal choice.

If a single e_N(P) bounds all dates, a sufficient explicit requirement is

 e_N(P)<=epsilon_N /
             [C sum_(j=0)^(N-1) H_(j+1) a_j^2].             (16)

Alternatively the stronger datewise e_j(P)<=C x_j^(-s) absorbs the errors
in (12). Finite-P errors must also satisfy the source-image/slack inputs;
the body theorem already supplies those on its chosen strict domains.
Neither (15) nor (16) assumes a limit of the new source functions.

7. ROOT COUNTS, SCALARS, AND WHAT HAS NOT BEEN PROVED

Every L^4 adjacent census is already in the birth/compiler constants and
K in (4). The long-age core theorem includes the actual profile/root
norm. Equation (6) does not introduce a new independent microscopic
root count per correction; it retains the original marked root and sums
body roots using the prepared local norm. A proof which erased the
original calibration or used only a total unrooted supremum would not
supply (7).

The estimate proves homogeneous one-mark centered stability, with exact
scalar recursion (3) retained. It does not prove uniform full-scalar
control, two-source contact renewal, all higher cumulants, original
mixed-history source entry, reflection positivity of a posterior-product
algebra, or continuum nontriviality. A physical observable still needs
its actual initial norm and its complete original mixed-history identity.

The only smallness needed for the algebra is the already admitted native
body/compiler ball. Gronwall itself requires summability, not a contraction
norm less than one. Under the stated strict inequality, its correction
factor can be made close to one at a further weak floor, provided that
the parameter hierarchy is genuinely compatible with that choice.
