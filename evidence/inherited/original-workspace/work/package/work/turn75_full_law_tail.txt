RESULT

There is an original-law route which does not require action-density
source differentiation: distinguish two conditional laws of an ORIGINAL
positive-time block given two original boundary events. Section 2 proves
a quantitative bounded-observable reflection lower bound, and a converse
showing exactly what boundary information a bounded witness must retain.

Section 3 gives an actual new consumer of the TURN37 bare-box theorem.
Mirror-paired half-local coarse-preserving replacements remain reflection
positive, and their centered reflected norms differ by at most alpha
times the total core oscillation of the observable. This comparison is
for the same original observable and preserves the full denominator.
It is not an identification with the TURN70 selected law.

These facts do not yet produce the needed lower bound. Section 4 gives
a sharp positive smooth reflection-positive model in which BOTH coarse
and positive-time marginals are exactly fixed and nondegenerate, the
complete joint density is uniformly comparable to a product density,
and nevertheless every bounded positive-time reflected channel tends
to zero. This tests a different shortcut from the earlier selection
counterexample: no selection or changed coarse marginal is involved.

Section 5 identifies a sufficient local JOINT-density comparison, much
more specific than partition stability or a one-body density estimate.
Section 6 checks the actual retained native inputs. They do not supply
this comparison, the original conditional contrast, or its positive
physical-time lower bound. Full nontrivial YM4 existence remains open.

1. EXACT ORIGINAL REFLECTION GEOMETRY

Use a finite original Wilson lattice with even temporal period N>=4,
site reflection theta:t->-t, and normalized Haar measure. Let B be the
spatial link data on BOTH fixed slices t=0,N/2. Every Wilson plaquette
belongs to one half-cylinder or one fixed slice. Thus the full original
law has the exact disintegration

 mu(dB,dU_+,dU_-)=pi(dB) Q_B(dU_+) (theta_*Q_B)(dU_-).    (1.1)

All normalizers in Q_B and pi are the original ones. This is the finite
Wilson conditional-product identity already proved in TURN74 Section 7.
In particular, for real F with complete read in the positive half,

 C_mu(F):=Cov_mu(theta F,F)=Var_pi(m_F),
 m_F(B)=Q_B F.                                           (1.2)

For a positive physical-time witness below, require that F read a block
at distance at least delta>0 from BOTH fixed slices in physical time,
with delta fixed independently of the ultraviolet regulator. Merely
taking F=G(B) would
give Var_pi(G), but would not test transmission to positive physical
time. This distinction is essential in a cutoff limit.

Let X=X(U_+) be a compact-valued ORIGINAL gauge-invariant block record,
with complete read inside such a positive-time block. It can be a finite
collection of gauge-invariant observables, or the compact gauge-orbit
record of a finite block. It is not a newly drawn native reference field.
All functions F considered in Section 2 are real functions of this X.

The relevant operator is the centered original conditional channel

 K_0 F(B)=E_mu[F(X)|B]-E_mu F(X).                         (1.3)

Then C_mu(F)=||K_0 F||_(L2(pi))^2. Positivity of pi, even with precise
upper and lower density bounds, says nothing by itself about K_0.

2. A BOUNDED, SOURCE-FREE WITNESS AND A QUANTITATIVE CONVERSE

Take any measurable boundary event E, with p=pi(E) in (0,1), and set

 nu_+=Law_mu(X|E),  nu_-=Law_mu(X|E^c),
 d_E=||nu_+-nu_-||TV,   TV=sup_A |nu_+(A)-nu_-(A)|.       (2.1)

If gauge invariance is required, take E invariant under the boundary
gauge action. For X already gauge-invariant all functions below have
that property automatically.

There exists measurable F_E(X) in [-1,1] with

 C_mu(F_E) >= 4p(1-p)d_E^2.                              (2.2)

PROOF. Choose a Hahn decomposition of nu_+-nu_- and let F_E be its
sign. Then nu_+F_E-nu_-F_E=2d_E. Put G=1_E-p. Direct calculation gives
Cov_mu(F_E,G)=p(1-p)(nu_+F_E-nu_-F_E), while Var_pi G=p(1-p).
Since G is boundary-measurable, Cauchy--Schwarz applied to K_0 F_E and G
gives (2.2). No canonical action normalization, complex source disc,
or source-dependent partition factor is involved.

On a compact metric record space, the same supremum can be approached
by continuous F in [-1,1]. Indeed finite Borel measures are regular;
approximate the Hahn sets in the L1 norm of nu_++nu_- by closed/open
sets, then use a continuous separating function. If a block gauge
action is retained instead of passing to the orbit space, average this
function over the compact gauge group. Invariant nu_+,nu_- preserve
its expectations and supremum bound. This is a finite-regulator
approximation; it gives no uniform analytic derivative or carrier norm.

There is also a converse strong enough to locate the missing estimate.
Suppose |F|<=1 and C_mu(F)=v>0. Let m=E[F|B], a=EF and
E={m>=a}. Since |m-a|<=2,

 Cov(F,1_E)=E[(m-a)_+]=(1/2)E|m-a| >= v/4.              (2.3)

The same covariance is p(1-p)(nu_+F-nu_-F), whose absolute value is
at most 2p(1-p)d_E. Hence

 p(1-p)d_E >= v/8,
 p(1-p)>=v/8,             d_E>=v/2.                     (2.4)

The last two inequalities use d_E<=1 and p(1-p)<=1/4. In particular
a bounded witness with a uniform reflected norm requires a genuinely
distinguishable original positive-time conditional law; a nondegenerate
boundary marginal alone cannot replace it.

Equivalently, with

 D_X=sup_(E subset B) sqrt[p(1-p)] d_E,
 S_X=sup_(|F(X)|<=1) C_mu(F),

the preceding argument proves

                 4D_X^2 <= S_X <= min(1,4D_X).          (2.5)

For the upper bound, (2.3)--(2.4) imply
sqrt[p(1-p)]d_E >= v/(8sqrt[p(1-p)]) >= v/4.
This criterion concerns bounded probes of the stated original block.
Its converse permits a boundary event depending on all B. Requiring E
to lie in a particular small boundary subalgebra is a useful stronger
construction target, not a consequence of the converse.

Even a nonzero bound in (2.2) is not by itself a positive-energy or
interacting continuum theorem. The chosen block, time separation,
regulator dependence, common observable algebra, and spectral content
still require control. A persistent zero-energy coarse variable could
also give a nonzero reflected norm.

3. ACTUAL BARE-BOX TRANSFER OF THE REFLECTED NORM

Apply precisely the bare coarse-preserving construction of
coarse_preserving_box_replacement_turn37.txt, Sections 2--8, in boxes
strictly inside the positive half and their reflected copies. Require:

 (i) complete box interiors, collars, gate and coarse-statistic reads
     are in their own closed half, with shared reads only in B;
 (ii) no Wilson plaquette reads two active interiors;
 (iii) the negative-half kernel is the reflected positive kernel, and
       the two half operations are applied independently, without a
       shared unretained ancillary choice;
 (iv) every operation is a source-free normalized local kernel and
      keeps its exterior and declared coarse statistic pointwise.

The existing theorem's conditional discrepancy bound is used in its
actual scope. At fixed exterior omega, disintegrate Q_i^omega over
Y_i with kernel R_i. With p_i=R_i(W_i^c), and
G_i={good boundary,p_i<=theta}, the new fiber is R_i on G_i^c and
R_i(.|W_i) on G_i. The annealed original bound gives

 d_i(omega)=int 1_(G_i) p_i dnu_i^omega <= alpha          (3.1)

uniformly in omega. On a bad boundary the replacement is the identity.
No smallness at every individual coarse value is assumed.

There is a coupling preserving (omega,Y) in which U_i is the original
fiber draw, and the replacement V_i equals U_i unless G_i and U_i is
outside W_i. On that event draw V_i from R_i(.|W_i). The V_i marginal
is exactly the declared conditioned fiber. Its discrepancy probability
averaged over Y_i at fixed omega is d_i(omega), not a product of inverse
acceptance probabilities. Different core couplings can be performed
conditionally independently; independence is not needed for (3.2).
The product application of the two half kernels in (iii) is retained
when asserting the reflected factorization (3.3).

For a positive-half observable F define c_i(F) as a deterministic upper
bound on its oscillation when only core i is changed while its exterior
and prescribed coarse value are fixed, allowing all other admissible
fields in the supremum. Put O(F)=sum_i c_i(F), over positive-half cores.
This is a finite or summable sum. Telescoping the changes and (3.1) give

 E[|F(U)-F(V)| | omega] <= alpha O(F).                    (3.2)

If F reads no interior it has O(F)=0. Merely |F|<=1 gives only c_i<=2,
and O(F)<=2 times the number of read cores; it does not give O(F)<=2.

Because the operations are half-local and reflected, applying them to
(1.1) gives exactly

 nu(dB,dV_+,dV_-)=pi(dB) Q'_B(dV_+) (theta_*Q'_B)(dV_-).  (3.3)

Thus nu is reflection positive and has the SAME original boundary
marginal pi. A gate or derived minimum reading the opposite half would
invalidate this argument and is excluded by (i).

Since B is among the unchanged exterior data, (3.2) implies

 ||m_mu-m_nu||infinity <= alpha O(F),
 m_mu=E_mu[F|B],       m_nu=E_nu[F|B].                   (3.4)

Centering is an orthogonal projection in the SAME L2(pi). The reverse
triangle inequality and (1.2),(3.3) therefore prove the new consumer

 |sqrt(C_mu(F))-sqrt(C_nu(F))| <= alpha O(F).             (3.5)

In particular a replacement-law margin sqrt(C_nu(F))>=c0 transfers
to the full original law whenever alpha O(F)<c0. The proof is for the
literal observable F evaluated on original and coupled link fields;
it does not replace an original old-source mark by a fresh R reference.

One may combine (2.2) with (3.5) if a conditional block contrast under
nu has actually been proved. The Hahn-sign discriminator then needs
its OWN oscillation estimate: (2.2) alone does not supply it.

This does not identify nu with the all-selected TURN70 path. Its exact
full coarse/exterior marginal is retained; bad fibers are unchanged,
and its next outgoing density differs from the globally windowed law.
There is no current proof of the required positive-time contrast for
this nu. The known alpha, superlogarithmic in log(1/g), also does not
pay a generic polynomial physical read count. Thick/flowed bounded
probes would need the actual oscillation or another continuity bound;
none is imported from a linear heat approximation.

4. SHARP OBSTRUCTION: FIXED MARGINALS AND STRONG POSITIVE DENSITY BOUNDS

This is an inference counterexample, not a Yang--Mills counterexample.
Take two compact probability spaces with measures pi(db),nu(dx), and
real smooth functions k,l with mean zero and |k|,|l|<=1. For
0<=epsilon<=1/2 define

 Q_b^epsilon(dx)=[1+epsilon k(b)l(x)]nu(dx),
 mu_epsilon(db,dx_+,dx_-)
       =pi(db) Q_b^epsilon(dx_+) Q_b^epsilon(dx_-).       (4.1)

The denominator is exactly one. Both one-half marginals are exactly
nu, and the boundary marginal is exactly pi, for every epsilon.
Reflection exchanges the halves. For every complex half function A,

 E[(Theta A)A]=int |Q_b^epsilon A|^2 dpi>=0.              (4.2)

The complete density relative to pi tensor nu tensor nu lies between
(1-epsilon)^2 and (1+epsilon)^2; it is smooth, positive and tends
uniformly to one. Thus even volume-independent two-sided density
bounds, exact normalization and unchanged nondegenerate one-body laws
hold, more strongly than a partition stability bound.

For every bounded real F of x alone,

 E[F|b]=nu F+epsilon k(b)nu(F l),
 C_mu_epsilon(F)=epsilon^2 pi(k^2)[nu(F l)]^2.            (4.3)

Consequently all |F|<=1, even epsilon-dependent choices, satisfy
C_mu_epsilon(F)<=epsilon^2. A positive-time marginal variance floor
also fails to help: Var(F)=Var_nu(F) is unchanged, while its reflected
norm vanishes. The zero mode is absent from the centered channel when
epsilon=0 despite all these positive density statements.

A concrete gauge-invariant compact example uses normalized SU2 Haar
for pi and nu, with k(b)=Tr(b)/2 and l(x)=Tr(x)/2. These are smooth
class functions, their Haar means vanish and their second moments
are 1/4. For F=l,

                 Var(F)=1/4,       C_mu_epsilon(F)=epsilon^2/64.

The joint law is invariant under independent conjugations of each
holonomy variable. This is a compact holonomy model, not a claim that
it has the four-dimensional Wilson action or its continuum dynamics.
Arbitrary independent flags with rare-event bounds can be appended to
both halves without changing (4.3). Their complete normalization and
marked rare-event upper bounds still supply no lower channel estimate.

Unlike TURN71's selection counterexample, no rare selection reweights
the coarse law here. The channel itself is arbitrarily weak while all
the indicated unmarked marginal information stays identical. Hence
positive coarse-marginal bounds cannot establish a nonzero reflected
original mean without an additional ORIGINAL joint-channel estimate.

5. WHAT SORT OF UNMARKED DENSITY ESTIMATE WOULD ACTUALLY SUFFICE

A local joint comparison can avoid analytic action-source transport,
but it must identify the original boundary/positive-time joint law.
Here is one quantitative finite-regulator consumer.

Let lambda be a comparison law for the SAME pair (B,X), and let E be
a boundary event with p_lambda in [p0,1-p0], 0<p0<=1/2. Suppose its
conditional X laws have TV contrast at least d0>0. If

 ||Law_mu(B,X)-lambda||TV <= delta,
 delta <= p0 d0/16,                                     (5.1)

then some |F(X)|<=1 obeys

                         C_mu(F)>=p0^2 d0^2/4.          (5.2)

PROOF. Since d0<=1, delta<=p0/16. Both original event probabilities
are at least p0/2. For either sign of E, subtract conditional ratios:
the conditional-law TV error is at most 2delta/mu(E_sign), hence at
most 4delta/p0. The two errors lower the original contrast by at most
8delta/p0<=d0/2. Apply (2.2), with p(1-p)>=p0^2/4.

For example, an exterior-INTEGRATED joint log-density comparison
dmu_pair=e^L dlambda/int e^L dlambda with osc(L)<=eta implies
TV<=tanh(eta/4). This follows by the usual extremal two-valued
likelihood calculation: the likelihood ratio has maximum/minimum
ratio at most e^eta, and the maximal TV is
(e^(eta/2)-1)/(e^(eta/2)+1). It is the local JOINT density whose
oscillation is needed. The scalar partition factor alone cancels
and does not bound this oscillation or supply d0.

The comparison law need not be reflection positive for this consumer;
(1.2) is used for the actual original Wilson law. Its positive
contrast must still be known. Mere comparability to a product law
does not supply it, as Section 4 demonstrates. Uniform small errors
relative to an explicitly coupled reference could suffice; the
retained native results do not yet establish those hypotheses for
an original positive-time physical block.

7. PRECISE MATHEMATICAL CONSEQUENCE

For bounded original positive-time probes, one can now pose a concrete
source-free alternative to the action-density induction: establish an
original boundary-conditioned block contrast, or compare the original
local joint law to an explicitly coupled law as in (5.1). The existing
bare replacement can transfer such a margin by (3.5) when its total
core oscillation is controlled. These are explicit finite-regulator
consumers and not a declaration that the needed contrast is known.

The unmarked native estimates currently retained do not prove that
contrast. Coarse-marginal positivity, a nonzero marginal variance,
mass preservation, rare private histories, and even uniform product
density comparability do not imply it. Section 4 proves this precise
logical obstruction while keeping reflection positivity and both
marginals fixed.

No selected law is substituted for the original Wilson law, no
boundary-only fluctuation is called a positive-time physical witness,
and no ordinary same-support L2 condition is imposed on renormalized
unbounded composites. Those may instead use the separate OS/source
route already retained. Neither route is completed here; no continuum
YM4 existence or nontriviality claim is made.
