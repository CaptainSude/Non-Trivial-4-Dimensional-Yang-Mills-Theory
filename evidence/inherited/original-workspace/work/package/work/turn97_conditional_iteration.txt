21 October 2026. Agent heatbath97. Finite SU(2) Wilson theorem.

RESULT AND RELEVANCE

The proposed repetition of actual heatbath conditional expectations is
legitimate while retaining the original Wilson probability measure. It
does not preserve the one-step source-response estimate from TURN96.
There is an explicit homogeneous-Wilson two-link fiber, with a smooth
bounded-speed exterior path, for which the first conditional source has
the proved coupling-independent response bound, but the second has
exterior derivative

  -64 beta/(105 pi) + E_beta,       |E_beta| <= 2/pi.       (0.1)

This is a quantitative obstruction to an unrestricted real Lipschitz
source class, not a counterexample to a cutoff-uniform averaged source
estimate or to a restricted small-field block estimate. It is important
because neither a changed action nor a replacement conditional law is
involved: both updates are the actual single-link Wilson conditionals.
The obstruction survives inclusion of the entire constant-profile
Wilson action: its leading response coefficient is
beta[1/4-64/(105 pi)], which is strictly positive. Thus it cannot be
removed simply by summing neighboring constant-profile source terms.

The same example also gives a precise weaker fact: the large derivative
forms a bounded-variation transition layer. Along the specified real
exterior path, its integrated absolute derivative is at most 2, uniformly
in beta. Converting that fact into a full Wilson bound would require a
bound for the actual exterior density in this path coordinate. No such
density estimate is proved here.

1. WHAT REPEATED ORIGINAL-LAW CONDITIONAL EXPECTATIONS DO PRESERVE

Let mu_beta be the original finite periodic four-dimensional SU(2)
Wilson measure, with every lattice period at least four, and beta>0.
For a link e let P_e be conditional expectation over U_e, keeping every
other original link fixed. If a companion observable F does not read e,
then for every integrable G with the needed products integrable,

  E_mu G = E_mu P_e G,
  Cov_mu(F,G) = Cov_mu(F,P_e G).                          (1.1)

Consequently, for a sequence e_1,...,e_n, ALL absent from the read of F,

  Cov_mu(F,G)=Cov_mu(F,P_{e_n}...P_{e_1}G).              (1.2)

These are compositions of actual conditional-expectation operators. They
are different from the additive star-source transformation in TURN96.
The kernel for a new update remains the original Wilson heatbath kernel,
but its input observable is the full already-transformed function.
There is no permission to replace that function by a Wilson star energy.

A later update can reintroduce dependence on a link integrated earlier:
its kernel depends on that earlier link, which is still a coordinate of
the unchanged original state space. Thus repeated heatbath projection is
not a decimation map, a conditional expectation onto an increasing or
decreasing sigma-algebra, or automatically a coarse-lattice source.

Individual P_e are orthogonal projections on L2(mu), hence contractions,
but that fact alone gives no small cutoff-uniform source norm: the
untransformed integrated source still has its microscopic root census.
No new reverse-martingale estimate is claimed from (1.1)-(1.2).

2. AN ACTUAL TWO-LINK CONDITIONAL FIBER

Choose two opposite parallel links of one elementary plaquette. Write
their oriented quaternion variables as u,v in S^3 so that the common
plaquette trace is u dot v. All transverse links used in their plaquette
stars can be held equal to I. Their other five plaquettes contribute
staple sums A,B in R^4. Condition on every link except these two. Up to
exterior-only terms, the literal homogeneous-Wilson density is

  exp[beta (u dot v + A dot u + B dot v)] du dv.          (2.1)

Use the real quaternion I=(1,0,0,0), choose

  A=I,             B(t)=(-1+t) I,        |t|<1/2.        (2.2)

These sums are realized by actual unit-quaternion staples with a
bounded-speed dependence on t. To see this explicitly, choose a fixed
imaginary unit quaternion J. Three of the five staples can be

  I,  -I/2 + sqrt(3) J/2,  -I/2 - sqrt(3) J/2,

whose sum is zero. The other pair, for any -2<c<2, can be

  c I/2 + sqrt(1-c^2/4) J,
  c I/2 - sqrt(1-c^2/4) J.                              (2.3)

Their sum is c I. Take c=1 for the u-star and c=-1+t for
the v-star. On |t|<1/2 each derivative in (2.3) is bounded by an
absolute constant independent of beta. If the orientation of an
individual plaquette requires inversion, choose the inverse parallel
link; inversion preserves these bounded-speed statements.

All ten parallel exterior links prescribing these staples are distinct.
For example take the raw parallel links in direction 1 at sites 0 and
e_2. The five other parallel links for the first star are at -e_2,
plus/minus e_3, plus/minus e_4. Those for the second star are at
2e_2, e_2 plus/minus e_3, e_2 plus/minus e_4. Periods at least four
prevent identification among these ten links or with the raw pair.
Changing the second set therefore does not change the first star or
its five-staple sum A. Plaquettes containing only exterior links are
conditional constants in (2.1), as they must be.

3. FIRST UPDATE: EXACT SAME ONE-LINK RESULT AS TURN96

Consider the constant-profile six-plaquette source of the u link:

  S_u(u,v)=6-u dot (I+v).                               (3.1)

This is the literal sum of its six original Wilson plaquette energies.
Let m(s)=I_2(s)/I_1(s), continued by m(0)=0. The first actual heatbath
conditional expectation is

  H_beta(v):=(P_u S_u)(u,v)
    =6-r(v) m(beta r(v)),       r(v)=|I+v|.
                                                           (3.2)

It is independent of u and independent of the path parameter t.
As a function of the staple sum I+v its gradient has norm at most one,
by TURN96. The global flat-value subtraction 6[1-m(6 beta)] is a scalar
and has no effect on any derivative below.

4. SECOND UPDATE: AN EXTERIOR RESPONSE OF ORDER BETA

Now apply P_v to the CHANGED observable H_beta. Evaluate the resulting
function at u=I. By (2.2), the actual conditional staple sum for v is

  u+B(t)=t I.

Thus the full second projection, with its actual normalizer, equals

  T_beta(t) := (P_v P_u S_u)(I,v;B(t))
    = [integral_(S^3) H_beta(v) exp(beta t v_0) dv]
      /[integral_(S^3) exp(beta t v_0) dv],              (4.1)

where v_0=I dot v. It is independent of the displayed old v. Since the
mark H_beta has no t dependence, differentiation of the normalized
integral gives exactly

  T_beta'(0)=beta Cov_Haar(H_beta(v),v_0).               (4.2)

The constant six drops out, and E_Haar v_0=0. Moreover

  0 <= H_beta(v) - [6-r(v)]
     =r(v)[1-m(beta r(v))] <= 3/(2 beta),               (4.3)

including r=0, by the global Gamma domination inequality proved in
TURN96. The scalar coordinate of Haar S^3 has density

  (2/pi) sqrt(1-x^2) dx,   -1<=x<=1,

and E_Haar |v_0|=4/(3 pi). It follows that

  |T_beta'(0)+beta E_Haar[v_0 sqrt(2+2v_0)]|<=2/pi.
                                                           (4.4)

The remaining integral is elementary. Set y=(1+x)/2:

  E[v_0 sqrt(2+2v_0)]
    =(16/pi) integral_0^1 (2y-1)y(1-y)^(1/2) dy
    =(16/pi)[2 B(3,3/2)-B(2,3/2)]
    =(16/pi)[32/105-4/15]
    =64/(105 pi).                                      (4.5)

Equations (4.4)-(4.5) prove (0.1) without an asymptotic Bessel
expansion. In particular, for beta>=105/16,

  |T_beta'(0)| >= 32 beta/(105 pi).                     (4.6)

The exterior path has a uniformly bounded speed in the product of
original-link quaternion metrics. Hence no uniform original-exterior
Lipschitz constant for P_v P_u S_u is possible on ALL real exteriors.
This remains true after any t-independent flat-value or vacuum-scalar
subtraction.

The balanced second staple sum at t=0 is essential. The first one-link
response estimate included a special cancellation between the star
energy mark and its own heatbath score. After P_u, the mark H_beta is
no longer the v-star energy; that cancellation is absent. Keeping the
action fixed does not keep this special source class fixed.

4A. COMPLETING THE SOURCE DOES NOT CANCEL THE LARGE RESPONSE

The source consisting of the union of both complete original stars has
eleven plaquettes, since the common plaquette is counted once. It is

  G(u,v;t)=11-u dot v-A dot u-B(t) dot v.                (4.7)

Its source coefficients are constant numbers, all one. The displayed
t dependence comes only from the underlying exterior links, not from
any change of the source profile. The first projection is exactly

  P_u G=11-r(v)m(beta r(v))-B(t) dot v.

At u=I the second projection therefore is

  K_beta(t):=(P_vP_u G)(I,v;t)
     =5+T_beta(t)+(1-t)m(beta t),                        (4.8)

where m is extended as an odd smooth function through zero. Since
m(0)=0 and m'(0)=1/4, differentiating (4.8) gives

  K_beta'(0)=beta c_*+E_beta,
  c_*=1/4-64/(105 pi)>0,       |E_beta|<=2/pi.            (4.9)

Now complete G to the full original Wilson energy S_all. The remaining
plaquettes contain neither raw link, so their sum S_ext is unchanged
by both conditional projections. Only the two exterior links in the
moving pair (2.3) depend on t. At t=0 each has quaternion speed 1/sqrt(3).
Each original link lies in six plaquettes, and the derivative of one
plaquette energy is bounded by the sum of the speeds of its moving
links, because multiplication by unit quaternions is isometric. Thus

  |S_ext'(0)|<=12/sqrt(3)=4sqrt(3),

where this deliberately loose bound is enough. Consequently

  d_t (P_vP_u S_all)(I,v;t)|0
    =beta c_*+R_beta,
  |R_beta|<=2/pi+4sqrt(3).                              (4.10)

The same statement holds for any deterministic source profile that is
identically one on the eleven raw-link plaquettes and on all plaquettes
incident to the two moved exterior links. Terms farther away do not
depend on u,v or the moved links and have zero derivative. In particular
one can take a smooth compactly supported physical profile that equals
one in a fixed neighborhood of this microscopic patch, for all fine
enough meshes. A separated companion insertion can be placed outside
the patch, so the exact covariance-preservation premise in (1.2) is
also consistent with this example.

This strengthens the obstruction from an individual star term to the
whole locally constant source. It still does not estimate the weight
that the original Wilson measure assigns to these exterior configurations.

5. THE LARGE RESPONSE HAS A UNIFORM INTEGRATED VARIATION BOUND

The function x -> H_beta(x), with r=sqrt(2+2x), is nonincreasing:
the function s m(s) has positive derivative. For any tilted probability
in (4.1), covariance of a nonincreasing function of x with x is
nonpositive. Consequently

  T_beta'(t)=beta Cov_(beta t)(H_beta(v_0),v_0)<=0.       (5.1)

The range of H_beta is the interval

  [6-2m(2beta), 6].

For any subinterval [a,b] of the actual realizable path |t|<1/2,

  integral_a^b |T_beta'(t)| dt
    =T_beta(a)-T_beta(b)<=2m(2beta)<=2.                 (5.2)

For any fixed 0<delta<1/2, as beta goes to infinity the tilted law at
t=delta concentrates at v=I, whereas the tilted law at t=-delta
concentrates at v=-I. The uniform approximation (4.3) then gives

  T_beta(delta)->4,     T_beta(-delta)->6,
  integral_(-delta)^delta |T_beta'(t)|dt ->2.             (5.3)

Away from t=0 the same concentration gives convergence to these
constant limits. Therefore the derivative measures on (-delta,delta)
converge weakly to -2 times a unit point mass at t=0. This is an actual
response concentration, not a loose beta factor introduced by an
absolute score bound.

For a nonnegative density w in this ONE specified exterior coordinate,
(5.2) would give integral w(t)|T_beta'(t)|dt <=2||w||_infinity.
The full Wilson measure is not the arbitrary path measure w(t)dt, and
no beta-uniform bound on its corresponding conditional density has been
established. The inequality is recorded only to identify what an
averaged-response strategy would have to control.

6. CONSEQUENCES FOR THE MAIN PROGRAM

(a) Exact projection identities can preserve a separated covariance
while the companion insertion avoids all updated links. They do not
by themselves generate a controlled multiscale source class.

(b) The one-link 1-Lipschitz response and O(beta^-2) flat quadratic
coefficient cannot be promoted to an all-real-exterior iterative
estimate by repeatedly applying the same one-link formula. The changed
observable already produces (4.6) at the second original-law update.

(c) The example is away from a uniformly nondegenerate-staple/small-field
region. It does not rule out a perturbative flat-block expansion, a
large-field probability estimate paying for this response, or an
integrated conditional-density estimate. It specifies exactly why one
of those inputs is needed.

(d) The large derivative survives completion to the full original action
or a smooth physical source profile locally constant on the specified
patch, by (4.10). The single-star monotonicity and integrated variation
bound of Section 5, however, are stated only for T_beta and should not
be transferred unchanged to that completed source.

(e) This is not an estimate for a history-conditioned native kernel;
no history indicators or alternate reference distributions appear.
There is also no claim of an actual Wilson covariance lower bound,
continuum nontriviality, or failure of such a bound.
