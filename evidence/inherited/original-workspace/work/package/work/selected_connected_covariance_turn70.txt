1. THE RAW LOGARITHM AND ITS EXACT DERIVATIVE

Fix a finite compatible step, suppress j, and keep its actual background
chart, coarse datum w, covariance, product color-ball window, and real
baseline coupling frozen. In the ambient coordinates the normalized
selected conditional expectation is

  <F>_(g,E,w)=
       E_muT[F exp V_(g,E)(w,xi)]/
       E_muT[exp V_(g,E)(w,xi)].

The inherited part of V is E(U_g(w,xi))-E(V(w)), where V=U_0.
The other terms are the same full physical remainder/coarea factors
of the actual selected sector. Define

 F_raw(g,E;w)=log E_muT exp V_(g,E)(w,xi).             (1.1)

This is the RAW connected logarithm; its value at w=0 is NOT deleted.
Any external prefactors independent of E have zero inherited derivative.
For source families X,Y, use their literal interpolated insertions

 u(f)=X(I_R f,U_g)-X(I_R f,V),
 v(h)=Y(I_R h,U_g)-Y(I_R h,V).

Differentiation on the fixed nonzero analytic domain gives exactly

 D_E^2 F_raw(g,E)[X(f),Y(h)]
     =<u(f)v(h)>-<u(f)><v(h)>
     =Cov_(g,E,w)(X(I_R f,U_g),Y(I_R h,U_g)).         (1.2)

The values at V are constants in xi and cancel from covariance.
Equation (1.2) contains the field-independent mixed covariance as well.
Replacing F_raw by its field-normalized quotient would remove that
part and would not give the conditional variance in (1.2).

Input families differing by any field-independent source total have
the same derivative (1.2), because their inherited differences vanish.
Thus the raw covariance descends in EACH input to the normalized source
quotient. Almost-minimal representatives may be used for its bound
without changing its actual numerical value.

2. STRONG TWO-MARK FAMILY ON ONE FIXED SET OF MARGINS

For X write X(f,A)=sum_(H,x) f_x X_(H,x)(A), with the strong norm

 ||X||_src=sup_i sum_(H contains i) e^(a0|H|)
                                  sum_x ||X_(H,x)||_b.

Take a fixed a2 with 0<a2<a0 below the strict connected catalogue's
available output reserve. This exponent is chosen ONCE. The resulting
two-mark families are used for terminal covariance estimates; they are
not fed back as smaller-exponent inputs at later scales.

The actual inherited composition is on the same fixed field b-domain,
with a strict old-field inclusion margin. The scalar inherited Banach
ball has a fixed positive margin around the baseline E_j. Fix smaller
g and inherited-input discs once, uniformly over j and all compatible
volumes in the admitted trajectory. These are precisely the strict
joint domains used for the one-mark estimate.

Differentiate the actual connected catalogue twice in the inherited
input, keeping the declared source labels. The outcome has the form

 D_E^2 F_raw(g,E)[X(f),Y(h)](w)
       =sum_(K,x,y) f_x h_y C_(K;x,y)(g,E;w_K),       (2.1)

where K is connected and contains BOTH output source vertices x,y
and every chart, background and fluctuation read.

Here is the absolute-majorant justification, beyond scalar Cauchy.
The inherited exponent is linear in its input coefficients. Expand
its marked exponential and the connected logarithm on the original
occupied-support catalogue. One derivative marks one inherited
occurrence, and two derivatives mark two occurrences (also allowing
the same original carrier with its ordinary multiplicity). The
logarithm keeps them in one connected cluster. Assign the original
declared carrier and its source vertex to each occurrence before
composition; append every strict classical/chart read and joining tree.
The pre-existing fixed absolute cluster margin pays the finite
two-mark choices. Equivalently take two scalar coefficient variables
on a fixed inherited-input disc in that absolute majorant. Cauchy
costs only its fixed inverse radius squared, not g^-P.

For the strong label sums, perform these operations first for the
declared scalar coefficients X_(H,x),Y_(H',y), then sum their absolute
norms in the majorant. The two sums are exactly bounded by
||X||_src ||Y||_src. Any extra finite carrier-choice powers are paid
from a0-a2. This is stronger than a bound of the summed function for
fixed f,h, and does not infer the former from the latter.

Source interpolation has nonnegative row weights of sum one. A source
vertex is replaced only by vertices in its fixed output collar. Its
absolute marked coefficient sum therefore has no extra label factor.
The ordinary finite R-dependent root/composition constants remain in C.
No ultraviolet-depth or whole-volume factor is introduced.

Thus, on a fixed complex g-disc, the strict differentiated family is
bounded in

 ||C||_(2src)=sup_i sum_(K contains i)e^(a2|K|)
                                    sum_(x,y in K)||C_(K;x,y)||_b

by C||X||_src||Y||_src, uniformly in E in the fixed smaller ball.
This conclusion imports the actual absolute inherited-composition and
connected catalogue, not only abstract Banach holomorphy of a quotient.

3. WHY THE TWO-MARK FAMILY HAS AN ACTUAL g^2 FACTOR

At g=0, U_g=V and every inherited difference vanishes. Therefore the
RAW logarithm (1.1) is independent of E there, including its vacuum.
Its second inherited derivative is exactly zero.

The substitution (g,xi)->(-g,-xi) preserves the centered color-ball
measure and the complete exponent. It gives evenness in g of (1.2).
The same parity can be imposed on its supported family by averaging
each coefficient at g and -g. This preserves all source/read supports
and does not increase the majorant. At g=0 each differentiated
connected inherited activity is zero, so its constant term vanishes.

Banach-valued radial Schwarz on the fixed g-disc, or analytic division
by g^2 followed by fixed-disc Cauchy, therefore yields

 ||C(g,E;X,Y)||_(2src)
                     <=C g^2 ||X||_src||Y||_src.      (3.1)

The g-disc radius and the two inherited Cauchy margins are fixed before
the weak ceiling. The constant may depend on b,T,R,J and those margins,
but not on j, preceding classical depth, volume, or the number of UV
steps. No constant-source rank cancellation is needed for (3.1);
the latter gives stronger one-mark information but is not assumed for
this second derivative.

In particular insert the actual one-mark trajectories X_j(f),X_j(h).
Their normalized strong quotient norms are bounded by C_X from the
audited one-mark induction. The raw constants can be omitted inside
(1.2) because covariance ignores them, rather than because they were
discarded from the density.

4. SPATIAL SEPARATION AT THE OUTPUT SOURCE SCALE

Let ell=ell_(j+1) be the OUTPUT vertex spacing for (2.1), and let d>0
be a lower bound for the periodic physical distance between the ACTUAL
continuous multilinear interpolant supports F,H. The source vertex
profiles are samples of these same functions at every stage, so their
nonzero sampled supports have at least this separation. A gap only
between terminal nodal sets is insufficient without their interpolation
collars. All source
interpolation/face weights are literal; no source is rescaled by a
bare inverse coupling or by an extra lattice power.

A connected K containing x,y has

                  |K| >= c d/(J ell)-C.

For a nonzero term f_x h_y, this extracts exp(-c' d/ell) from its
fixed exponential weight; J is fixed. Let N_f(ell) be the number of
output J-cells containing a vertex where f is nonzero, and similarly
N_h. Root first at a cell containing x and use (3.1), or root at y.
It follows for every real admitted w that

 |Cov_j(w;f,h)|
  <=C g_j^2 C_X^2 min(N_f(ell),N_h(ell))
            exp(-c d/ell)||f||infinity||h||infinity.  (4.1)

There is no product N_f N_h in this step. The second source label is
already summed inside the strong two-mark rooted norm. This also
shows explicitly why mere scalar analyticity without a complete
two-root connected catalogue would not suffice.

For sources in fixed bounded physical boxes and ell<=ell_*,
N_f(ell),N_h(ell)<=C_support ell^-4. This proves (R1). Periodic
separation, rather than a chosen nonperiodic drawing of the supports,
is required; a wrapped short separation is not exponentially small.

5. SUMMING THE CONDITIONAL BIRTHS, INCLUDING A UV TAIL

At fixed macro ratio R>1 write the output scales as
ell_m=ell_* R^-m. The weak trajectory obeys g_j<=g_star. Hence

 sum_j |Cov_j(w_j;f,h)|
 <=C g_star^2 C_X^2 ||f||infinity||h||infinity
             sum_(m>=0) ell_m^-4 exp(-c d/ell_m).     (5.1)

The sum is finite independently of the initial UV cutoff. For example
put u_m=d/ell_m. The sum of u_m^4 e^(-c u_m) on a geometric sequence
is uniformly bounded for any starting u_0>0: below one sum the
geometric fourth powers, and above one use the exponential tail.
Thus (5.1) is at most C g_star^2 C_X^2 d^-4 times the displayed
support/source factors.

More precisely, for 0<epsilon<=min(d,ell_*),

 sum_(ell_m<=epsilon) ell_m^-4 exp(-c d/ell_m)
                   <=C epsilon^-4 exp(-c' d/epsilon), (5.2)

where 0<c'<c can be fixed once. If the finite grid contributes any
scales below epsilon, its largest such scale lies in
(epsilon/R,epsilon]. Factor its fourth power and use the
superexponential remaining geometric series; if there is no such
scale the assertion is trivial. This proves (R2).

The estimates hold pointwise on the real weak coarse datum domain.
Taking expectations under any positive selected joint law that has
these conditional kernels preserves them. The exact reverse-martingale
identity is proved separately in selected_covariance_martingale_turn70;
it is the necessary chronology/normalizer attachment.
