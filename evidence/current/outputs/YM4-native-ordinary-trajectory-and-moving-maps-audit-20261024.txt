1. Literal native map and flat power identity

I checked B109 printed252--254(0.3),(0.5)--(0.12), using the available
primary text and the previously inspected page formulas. The endpoint
contours are the shortest coordinate-order paths inside the centered
odd-L block. Their first mean derivative is the arithmetic contour
mean. Thus the endpoint contribution to the ordinary derivative is
exactly a coarse gradient, Q=S+d psi, with the displayed path-length
bound on psi. Native endpoint equivariance gives Q d=d r with r the
restriction to the coarse centers. It is not a scalar block average.

The noncommuting operator telescope has the correct order:
Q^n-S^n=sum_j Q^(n-1-j)(Q-S)S^j.
Using Q^k d=d r^k on its left factors yields the asserted bound
||Q^n||_infty<=L^n+2 c_psi(L^n-1)/(L-1).
In particular the constant C_*=1+d occurs once, not at every grade.
All operator spaces are the successive unit-coordinate lattices.
The same identity is valid on compatible periodic lattices.

The primitive analytic chart is legitimate on a fixed smaller complex
supremum ball: the original group mean, finite path products and both
logarithms have finitely many arguments at fixed L. Their common strict
margin and finite-dimensional Cauchy estimates give(2), uniformly over
the number of blocks. No large-composite analytic radius is assumed.

   =(s_j/L) sum_(l<j)s_l <= s_j^2/[L(L-1)].

The fixed smallness choice(8) closes the induction on every complex

Consequently every intermediate primitive map is defined on the same

3. Actual corrected maps and uniform Hilbert products

At the identity, the actual endpoint variation is Z_0=psi, so the
moving-reference correction gives Cbar_0=S. The scalar block mean
has counting Hilbert norm L^-2; the L translated straight means
give ||S||<=L^-1 in four dimensions. These exact flat scales are
the required starting point. Replacing them by fixed larger factors
at each step would spoil the argument, and the producer does not do so.

B_theta and Cbar_theta are the literal moving-reference maps with
their finite primitive reads. Fixed-L coefficient differentiation
and bounded row/column incidence prove the Schur estimates(19),
including all factors of L in the fixed constant c_L. The derivative
of F(theta_j) includes both F' theta_j'' and F''[theta_j',theta_j'];
the second-jet scale in(20) is therefore justified.

Product Leibniz uses one-insertion sums sum s_j and two-insertion
sums sum s_j^2+(sum s_j)^2. They are uniformly bounded. The product
norm costs exp(C a_* sum s_j), not C^p. No inverse or singular-value
lower bound for an averaging map is needed. The resulting factors
are precisely L^-p for Cbar and L^-2p for B, and multiplication by
mu^-2 in J gives the asserted bounded first and second derivatives.
For complex fields the transpose is the analytic bilinear transpose;
its Euclidean Hilbert norm equals the original matrix norm. Complex
orthogonality alone is not substituted for an operator bound.

The corrected product is not the derivative of a fictitious fixed-
identity corrected nonlinear map. It is the chronological product
at the actual ordinary intermediate references; the registered exact
moving-reference intertwining identities apply to precisely this
product. Thus no different constraint has entered the estimates.

4. Complete reads and exact scope

All parameter derivatives keep the complete recursive union of the
original path/endpoint/incident-bond reads. Centered odd-L blocks
compose into the centered final block. Endpoint paths inside its
coarse subblocks remain inside the final block; the translated bond
strings require its two neighboring final endpoint blocks and their
incident bonds. The safe literal recursive-union formulation already
in the producer suffices even if one does not simplify that geometry.
No estimate discards part of this union or sums its K^4 coordinates
after applying the operator bound.

The constants are independent of p and compatible volume. They are
also independent of old classical grade because the primitive ordinary
map itself contains no such grade. This proves(T),(M) on the bounded
physical complex ball, not admission into arbitrary original sharp
windows or mixed histories. It supplies no new constrained minimum
inverse and does not negate the separate growing raw mean-zero
gauge-vertex example.

The proof is directly meaningful on the infinite lattice and on its
compatible finite periodic ordinary constructions. A comparison with
a different finite winding/regulator definition would require its own
matching, but is not an extra prerequisite to applying this result
directly to native infinite-density identities at fixed grades.
Uniform source vertices, differentiated Schur/heat estimates, and
the signed logarithmic coefficient retain their own proof obligations.
