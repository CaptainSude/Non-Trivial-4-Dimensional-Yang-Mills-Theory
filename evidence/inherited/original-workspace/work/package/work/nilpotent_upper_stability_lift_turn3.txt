NILPOTENT BANACH-ALGEBRA LIFT OF UPPER STABILITY: VALID SCOPE AND EARLY MARKS
18 October 2026. Exact algebraic result; the joint producer premise is distinguished explicitly.

1. DIMENSION-FREE COEFFICIENT ALGEBRA

For n labels let A_n=C[eta_1,...,eta_n]/(eta_1^2,...,eta_n^2), with
commuting generators. For positive weights w_i define

 ||sum_(S subset [n]) a_S eta_S||_w=sum_S |a_S| product_(i in S)w_i.

This norm is submultiplicative. Indeed the coefficient of eta_S in
ab sums only pairs of disjoint label sets whose union is S. Taking
absolute values and summing gives a sub-sum of ||a||_w||b||_w.
The constant is one and independent of n. Therefore

 ||exp a||<=exp||a||,
 ||exp a-1||<=exp||a||-1,
 ||integral f dmu||<=integral ||f||d|mu|.                    (1.1)

For a scalar real action A_0 and nilpotent N, retain the scalar body:

             ||exp[-A_0+N]||_w<=exp[-A_0]exp||N||_w.         (1.2)

This retains positivity/coercivity of the original Wilson and Gaussian
parts. It does not replace them by absolute values of their actions.

For carrier activities the appropriate local norm is, schematically,

 ||a||_(kappa,w)=sup_x sum_(X containing x) exp[kappa d(X)]
                    sum_S w_S ||a_S(X)||_(original domain). (1.3)

The label sum is INSIDE the rooted spatial norm. A sum of separate
suprema over labels would introduce the number of copies and is the
wrong hypothesis for a volume-uniform copied-source theorem.

2. WHICH ORIGINAL PROOF OPERATIONS LIFT

Keep the Gaussian kernels, integration regions, gauges, field maps,
reference denominators and source-free Wilson action fixed. Treat the
extra source coefficients as A_n-valued activities. Finite sums,
products, integration, Taylor remainders, contour Cauchy estimates and
the localizer's integral formulas obey the same norm inequalities
as in the scalar case by (1.1). Commutativity permits the same Mayer
identities. Ward identities are coefficientwise linear identities.

Consequently an ABSOLUTELY convergent Mayer/polymer/connected expansion
lifts with the same numerical majorant whenever its full A_n-valued
input norm satisfies the original smallness condition. There is no
new factorial or factor depending on the dimension 2^n merely from
changing the coefficient algebra. The existing graph combinatorics
already counts products and remains the same.

This conclusion is a proof by the same majorants, not an appeal to
holomorphy in arbitrarily many ordinary complex sources. Nilpotence
is useful because the target coefficients have at most one occurrence
of each label. It does not remove any norm-smallness hypothesis.

If a normalizer is source dependent, write it as z_0(1+u), with z_0
the original scalar normalizer. A dimension-free inverse bound follows
from ||u||<1: ||(1+u)^-1||<=1/(1-||u||). Algebraic nilpotence alone
only makes the inverse a finite series; when ||u|| is large, its norm
can grow with n. One must either keep the normalizer genuinely frozen
or obtain its logarithm/inverse from the convergent connected expansion.

4. HUGE EARLY SINGLETONS CANNOT BE FED BLINDLY INTO MAYER

Even for a singleton a=eta_i M, the Mayer activity exp(a)-1=eta_i M
has norm w_i|M|. If that number is large, it violates the small-activity
criterion. The finite formal series still exists, but no uniform
cluster bound follows. Applying the generic exponential estimate to
many such early marks gives an expense exp[sum_i w_i|M_i|], which can
spend much more than the native stretched-log large-field reserve.

There is nevertheless a useful exact alternative before a common
integration, provided one has a JOINT INTEGRAND representation. Suppose
the whole opened kernel has the form

 K_H(eta)=K_H(0) product_i [1+eta_i M_(H,i)],                 (4.1)

and on its actual complex contour

 |M_(H,i)|<=P(gamma_b) n_(H,i),
 n_(H,i) nonnegative integers,
 sum_i n_(H,i)<=C N_H.                                     (4.2)

For a common weight w,

 ||product_i(1+eta_i M_(H,i))||_w
 <=product_i[1+w P(gamma_b)n_(H,i)]
 <=exp[C N_H log(1+w P(gamma_b))].                          (4.3)

The last step uses 1+a m<=(1+a)^m for integer m>=0. This charges a
LOGARITHM of the potentially huge amplitude per original row cell,
rather than that amplitude itself. If the unmarked absolute kernel
has an UNUSED row-count reserve at least the exponent in (4.3), its
integrated A_n norm keeps the remaining small factor. The component
can then enter the ordinary small-activity Mayer expansion.

Formula (4.3) has no dependence on the number of labels other than
their actual packed row count. It is a legitimate way to postpone
smallness until after a large-field kernel, if (4.1)--(4.2) hold.

The reserve inequality is indispensable. A one-mark proof may have
reserved only enough to absorb one polynomial, not a per-label packed
expense. In particular a first-date scalar factor alone cannot pay
an arbitrary number of labels; the original spatial/lifetime ledger
must supply the per-row debit used here.

4A. AN EXPONENTIAL-ARM VERSION WITH NO INTEGER PACKING ASSUMPTION

There is a useful weaker joint premise. Suppose label centers form a
uniformly separated set in the relevant row-scale metric and

 |M_(H,i)|<=P(gamma_b) sum_(x in rows(H)) exp[-kappa dist(x,label_i)].

For a=w P(gamma_b), positivity gives

 log(1+a sum_x k_(x,i))<=sum_x log(1+a k_(x,i)).

Indeed product_x(1+a k_(x,i))>=1+a sum_x k_(x,i). Summing labels and
using their bounded spatial density gives, in dimension d,

 sum_i log(1+a exp[-kappa dist(x,label_i)])
                          <=C[1+log(1+a)]^(d+1).             (4.4)

To prove (4.4), split at R=2 kappa^-1 log(2+a). Inside the ball there
are O((1+R)^d) centers and each summand is <=log(1+a). Outside use
log(1+u)<=u and sum the exponential tail. Thus (4.3) is replaced by

 ||product_i(1+eta_i M_(H,i))||_w
           <=exp[C N_H(1+log(1+w P(gamma_b)))^(d+1)].         (4.5)

In four dimensions this costs a fifth power of a logarithm. For a
POLYNOMIAL weight w=G^K and gamma_b>=G, the cost is a fixed multiple
of (log gamma_b)^5 per row. A sufficiently strong reserved native
per-row logarithmic debit can absorb it. This would already produce
an arbitrary fixed inverse power G^-K in the copied bound, which may
suffice for the fusion limit. Taking w inverse to a stretched-log
error is much more expensive and is not necessary for that purpose.

This refinement still requires actual joint integrand arms, not only
post-integration singleton bounds. Also the separation must hold in
each row metric used; it is automatic only when the copy spacing is
at least the row scale. If original localization arms have been summed
away, (4.4) cannot be invoked retrospectively.

5. WHY ONE-MARK OUTPUTS DO NOT ESTABLISH (4.1)

Before any shared integration the original linear nilpotent source
does have the product form product_i(1+eta_i F_i). After integrating
shared fluctuations, however,

 K[product_i(1+eta_i F_i)]

contains mixed connected coefficients. It is generally different from
product_i(1+eta_i K F_i). This is not a technical small error.

For example take one centered Gaussian X of variance M^2 and labels
1,2. Then KX=0, while

 K[(1+eta_1 X)(1+eta_2 X)]=1+M^2 eta_1 eta_2.                (5.1)

The singleton outputs vanish exactly and the mixed coefficient can
be arbitrarily large. Thus an estimate proved separately for every
one-mark producer does not imply a bound in (1.3).

The same warning applies to a renormalized one-mark normal form
produced AFTER prefix integrations. Multiplying its singleton outputs
omits the mixed terms generated in those integrations. To use (4.3)
one needs a simultaneously coherent integrand-level representation
before the relevant shared integration, or a bound for its mixed
connected coefficients. Reopening the original bare integrand restores
the product identity, but the original insertions can then have the
UV/contact sizes that their one-mark renormalization had removed.

An actual proof can exploit separation of copied cells: before their
first common cluster, labels may propagate separately, while mixed
coefficients carry connecting propagator/tree factors. But this is a
joint marked-cluster statement. Its source arms and their first joining
scales must remain in the local norm; neither the algebra itself nor
the individual one-mark result supplies it.

6. THE COPIED-PRESSURE PAYOFF, ONCE THE JOINT PREMISE IS PROVED

Suppose the lifted producer gives the original inductive bounds in
(1.3), uniformly for n separated copies, for weights w_i=w. Restore
the SAME scalar UV normalization in both the marked partition element
Z(eta) and Z(0). Upper stability and the scalar lower stability then give

                ||Z(eta)/Z(0)||_w<=exp[C V_stop].           (6.1)

If the reflected array has n cells of fixed stopped volume v, then
V_stop=n v. Extracting the full coefficient yields

 |[eta_1...eta_n] Z(eta)/Z(0)| <=(exp[C v]/w)^n.             (6.2)

Choosing w proportional to the inverse small defect amplitude makes
(6.2) the copied bound needed before taking the chessboard root.
This route uses no pointwise normalized probability of a surviving
history. It is mathematically valid if the FULL coefficient algebra
input bounds are obtained.

All source-dependent vacuum coefficients must remain in Z(eta).
They contain mixed cumulants and cannot be discarded because the
scalar vacuum factor canceled. Likewise the source-free references
must be identical in numerator and denominator throughout.

7. AUDIT CONCLUSION

The dimension-free Banach-algebra lift of the absolute majorant and
upper-stability proof is valid. It requires the original smallness
assumptions in the full local label norm, or an explicit delayed
smallness argument such as (4.1)--(4.3) before Mayer expansion.

The precise producer obligation is joint: control all copied-label
coefficients generated by shared prefix integrations, keeping their
spatial arms, and prove the packed original-row debit for any huge
early marks. A collection of singleton bounds is insufficient, as
(5.1) shows. No completed copied YM pressure theorem is claimed here.
