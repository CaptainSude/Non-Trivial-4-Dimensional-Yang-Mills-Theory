1. THE PRECISE NATIVE HESSIAN AND ITS COVARIANCE

Let U(a)=exp(i a) on the current lattice, and define the symmetric
bilinear left-relative Hessian by

 L(a)[b,c] = D_b D_c S_m(exp(i b) U(a)) at b=0,

where the two derivatives mean the symmetric second derivative in the
same left tangent chart. Under a FIXED site gauge u, the tangent at
bond e based at x transforms as b_e -> Ad_(u(x)) b_e. This identity is
linear in b and exact before differentiation. Gauge invariance of the
actual minimum VALUE S_m therefore gives exact block-adjoint covariance
of L, at stationary and nonstationary backgrounds alike.

An ordinary Hessian in the raw coordinate a is not being declared
covariant. If c(a,b)=log(exp(i b)exp(i a))/i, its relation to L is

 L(a)=c_b(a,0)^T S_coord''(a)c_b(a,0)
                         + S_coord'(a) contracted with c_bb(a,0).

The second, gradient-dependent term is retained. Taylor coefficients
of L through degree2 require action derivatives through degree4 and
only finite, bond-local BCH-coordinate coefficients. The new native
rooted action kernels therefore give the complete rooted row AND
column bounds required in the archive, including these current/chart
contributions. No Hessian-comparison inequality is differentiated and
no current term of the actual S_m is removed.

For the flat q=L(0), translation covariance and global color invariance
give a color-scalar kernel in the actual current-lattice bond fibers.
Gauge Ward gives q(k)d(k)=0. Taking k to zero in every direction implies
q(0)=0. The original Wilson minimum is nonnegative to quadratic order;
positivity for both signs of real k then gives q'(0)=0 componentwise.
The new uniform exponential quadratic rows supply the corresponding
uniform absolute moments and analytic strip. Thus both zero moments
required by the archive hold without importing its stronger displayed

These continuous-momentum statements are on the compatible infinite

2. ORDERED LAURENT FACTORIZATION

The archived operator B_rho telescopes a Laurent function by setting
successive variables to one. Therefore, when q(1)=0,

 q(z)=sum_rho (z_rho-1) B_rho q(z).

Its first moment vanishing gives (B_rho q)(1)=0, so telescope each
B_rho q once more. Since z_rho-1=(z_rho^-1-1)(-z_rho), this proves

 q(z)=sum_(rho,tau)(z_rho^-1-1) H_(rho,tau)(z)(z_tau-1),
 H_(rho,tau)=-z_rho B_tau B_rho q.

The ordering in this formula is important and correct. It does not
symmetrize away the skew bond-component/odd-displacement entries of a
forward-difference Maxwell kernel. A monomial of length l produces
at most l^2 divided monomials, with only the displayed one-step shift.
Spending exponential reserve controls this cost uniformly. The exact
numerical bound stated in the archive follows from
sup_(l>=0) l^2 exp(-sigma l/2)=16/(e^2 sigma^2), with its extra shift
factor exp(sigma/2); any harmless finite-fiber norm conversion is fixed.

Full transpose of H includes displacement reversal and all gradient
and bond indices. Replacing H by (H+H^T)/2 preserves q because q is
transpose symmetric. Hence q=D0^T H0 D0 with H0 symmetric, color scalar,
and uniformly exponentially summable in both matrix indices. This
factorization is valid without entrywise symmetry at fixed displacement.

3. LOCAL PATH GAUGE AND THE REPAIRED FLAT COMPARISON

Choose translated coordinate paths with reversal for the negative
displacements. For the matrix row at x, transport from x defines a
formal gauge g_x(z). Each transformed edge is a loop of length
O(1+d(x,z)) and admits O((1+d(x,z))^2) plaquettes in a coordinate filling.
For a= t mu A with

 ||A||_infty + mu^-1 ||nearest differences A||_infty <= a_*,

the first loop-angle coefficient obeys
|b1(z)| <= C mu^2 a_* (1+d(x,z))^2: discrete Stokes gains the physical
first difference. The second coefficient is a sum of pairs of original
link coefficients, including its BCH logarithm term; it obeys
|b2(z)| <= C mu^2 a_*^2 (1+d(x,z))^2. No extra derivative of A is needed.

These are formal jets, not a claim that g_x is a bounded global gauge.
One can truncate its polynomial gauge jets, apply exact finite gauge
covariance, and pass coefficientwise using the rooted exponential
kernels. The complete source/row/column roots make all polynomial
tails absolutely summable. This also avoids inserting an affine field
into a finite periodic flux sector.

In this gauge L(t mu A) compares with its dressed flat kernel
q(y-x) W_xy(t mu A). At first order the error is L'[b1]. At second
order it is L'[b2], (1/2)L''[b1,b1], and L'[b1] times the first path
transport coefficient. Their Schur bounds respectively have the
required mu^2 a_* and mu^2 a_*^2 powers; the last two second-order

until summing the gauge-path/background roots, as the archive does.

Dress H0 by endpoint transports and define Qcov(a)=D_a^T H^P(a)D_a.
