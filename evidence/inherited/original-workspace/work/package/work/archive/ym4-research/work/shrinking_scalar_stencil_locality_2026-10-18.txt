LOCALITY OF THE HEAT-LIMIT FIELDS FROM SHRINKING SCALAR STENCILS
18 October 2026. Working domain proof; no tensor covariance or Gram rank.

1. INPUTS THAT ARE ACTUALLY USED

Work on the single reconstructed local scalar Hilbert space. The scalar
field and its adjoint act on the smooth-energy domain D_infty and have
polynomial energy bounds, including every fixed left energy weight.
Its time-zero spatially smeared value a(g) is only a FORM. The existing
scalar point/heat construction, or the finite-band argument of
heat_form_to_operator_distribution, gives, for a fixed integer q,

       ||(1+H)^-q a(g)(1+H)^-q|| <= C p_s(g).          (1)

It is not assumed that a(g) sends the vacuum, or D_fin, to a vector.
Scalar locality is used as an identity of forms after smearing, then
at time zero by the smooth matrix elements on D_infty.

For the new frame the already established data are:

 (a) exact actual scalar-fusion approximants, at most five factors
     per term, all complete lower-subset and scalar counterterms kept;
 (b) the heat-form Cauchy error
       d(G)=G^B[exp(-c(log G)^sigma)+G^-K], sigma>1;
 (c) shrinking complete stencil and prefix period
       delta_G+p_G <= exp(-cG)P(G),
     while the smallest positive internal packet time gap, all profile
     norms and normalization factors have inverse/absolute cost at
     most exp(CG);
 (d) the constructed fields Phi_v(f), with the polynomial all-weight
     bounds and reflected companions of the operator-distribution note.

The native exponent K is chosen once beyond the finitely many powers
below. The required powers depend on the fixed scalar/frame/domain
bounds, not on G, the bare cutoff, or the number of reflection copies.
The construction does not rely on the native power absorbing an
exp(polylog G) preparation loss. Here it absorbs polynomial G only.

2. A DOWNSTREAM PREFIX AVERAGE, NOT A NEW PRODUCER CLAIM

At each fixed G average the ACTUAL heat form under translations of
its prefix torus, transporting back the Hilbert-space translation:

 q_G^av(f)=p_G^-4 integral_[0,p_G]^4 T_-h q_G(f_h)dh.   (2)

For the time coordinate T is the Euclidean conjugation, interpreted
with positive heat slack or on finite spectral bands. This is a bounded
normalized average. It is performed after the copied bridge; no
arbitrary translated clone is inserted into the producer proof.

The actual scalar expression is a finite sum/integral of

          c_(G,S)(x;u) product_(a in S) phi(x+u_a),

with each deterministic center coefficient periodic with period p_G.
The underlying scalar factors translate continuously. Therefore (2)
averages c_(G,S) over its period and leaves a center-independent
coefficient (possibly still depending on the internal packet variables
u). The resulting expression is continuously translation covariant.
It is still a finite scalar-stencil expression, not an abstract RG
reference or a new Appell convention. Centering and reflection commute
with this normalized average.

Because q_infty already has continuous translation covariance,

       q_G^av-q_infty=average_h T_-h(q_G-q_infty)(f_h).

On a sandwich with heat slack a>4p_G its norm has the SAME d(G) bound,
with a fixed harmless change of a and of the containing compact. The
test seminorm of f_h is bounded uniformly for |h|<=p_G. There is no
factor p_G^-1 left: (2) is a probability average. Thus this averaging
does not change the limiting field or require a convergence rate for
lower limiting fusions.

By the original scalar ordered-kernel identification, the time-zero
form A_G^av is the corresponding center-independent sum of expressions

 exp(s_1 H)a(g_1)exp[-(s_1-s_2)H]a(g_2)...
                          a(g_k)exp(-s_k H),          (3)

integrated over the actual packet variables, with k<=5,
s_1>...>s_k, |s_a|<=delta_G. The packet supports preserve fixed positive
internal gaps, whose inverse costs are <=exp(CG). This formula is a
heat-regularized form identity. The a(g_a) are not being treated as
ordinary operators. Terms of smaller degree and identity terms obey
the same convention. Integrating their fixed-order seminorm bounds and
all coefficient absolute values costs at most exp(CG).

The temporal finite-band ODE gives the same A_G^av, by uniqueness.
Thus (3) is not an independent continuation chosen after the limit.

3. POINT-HEAT CONVERGENCE AT A SHRINKING HEAT SCALE

Let A be the time-zero form of the limiting field. For a fixed spatial
test g (and uniformly in a fixed spatial seminorm), the small-slab
inverse argument gives

 ||P_E(A_G^av(g)-A(g))P_E|| <= C(1+E)^p d(G)p_s(g),    (4)

provided G>=C[1+log(1+E)] and p_G+delta_G<<1/(1+E).
Indeed use the temporal test chi_tau, tau=(1+E)^-1, and undo only
4tau of heat on P_E. The factor is exp(8tau E)<=exp(8), not exp(aE).
The reciprocal Laplace multiplier has the same uniform Fourier-L1
norm as in the operator-distribution note. This proves (4) without
an exponential loss on the error d(G).

There is also a crude global finite-G sandwich bound, directly from
(3), (1), all the positive internal gaps, and the scalar coefficient
ledger:

       ||exp(-rho_G H)A_G^av(g)exp(-rho_G H)||
                                      <= exp(CG)p_s(g), (5)

where rho_G=C delta_G can be enlarged by the harmless prefix diameter.
If different stencil widths occur, choose one rho_G bounding them all;
it still satisfies rho_G<=exp(-cG)P(G). Internal-gap and inverse-radius
costs are included in exp(CG), not hidden in the constant.

Choose a_G=c_0 G^-1/2, where c_0>0 will later be fixed using the
spacelike separation. Let E_G=L_0 G/a_G. The constant L_0 is fixed
large enough compared with the C in (5). For large G, rho_G<a_G/4.
Unit energy-band Schur sums give

 ||exp(-a_G H)[A_G^av-P_EG A_G^av P_EG]exp(-a_G H)||
                    <= C a_G^-1 exp(CG-c a_G E_G).    (6)

To see this, (5) bounds its m,n block by
exp(CG)exp[-(a_G-rho_G)(m+n)]. Every retained block has m or n>E_G;
the row and column sums are bounded by the right side of (6), after
reducing c. No rank/count of vectors inside an energy band is used.
The corresponding tail of A follows from its polynomial band bound
and is bounded by a fixed power of a_G^-1 times exp(-c a_G E_G).

The low-energy piece is bounded by (4), with heat contractions. Since
E_G=O(G^3/2), its conditions hold for all sufficiently large G. Hence
for a fixed finite D_1,

 ||exp(-a_G H)(A_G^av(g)-A(g))exp(-a_G H)||
              <= C[G^D1 d(G)+exp(-c'G)]p_s(g).        (7)

The same proof works when a_G is changed by any fixed factor. The
constant may depend on c_0, which is fixed before G tends to infinity.
Real-time center smearing against f preserves (7), with an integral
spatial seminorm of f. We denote this heat-regularized finite-G real-
time insertion by Phi_G^av(f); it need not be an undamped tempered
operator at fixed G. The limit Phi(f) is the operator already proved.

4. A GAUSSIAN TIME AVERAGE WITH CONTROLLED COMPLEX CONJUGATION

Let B and its adjoint partner act continuously on D_infty and obey
the polynomial all-weight energy bounds. Both an original scalar
spacetime field and a new Phi(h) have this property. Write
alpha_t(B)=exp(itH)Bexp(-itH), and

 k_eps(t)=(2 pi eps^2)^-1/2 exp[-t^2/(2eps^2)],
                B_eps=integral k_eps(t)alpha_t(B)dt.  (8)

The integral is continuous as a map between the relevant energy
Sobolev spaces. The all-weight norms of alpha_t(B) are independent
of t, since the weights commute with exp(itH). Therefore

 alpha_z(B_eps)=integral k_eps(t-z)alpha_t(B)dt        (9)

defines an entire map in those same norms, with bound at most
exp[(Im z)^2/(2eps^2)] times the norm of B. On finite spectral bands
(9) is precisely exp(izH)B_eps exp(-izH); uniqueness and the weighted
bounds give its heat-regularized meaning without applying exp(sH)
to an arbitrary smooth-energy vector.

Also B_eps->B on D_infty in every fixed output energy norm, and the
same holds for its adjoint partner. This follows from strong smooth-
energy continuity of alpha_t(B) on a fixed input vector and the Gaussian
approximate identity. No operator-norm continuity of the unsmeared
unbounded B is claimed.

5. A LOCAL SCALAR FORM SEES ONLY THE GAUSSIAN TAIL

Suppose the spatial support of g and the support of B have a fixed
locality margin d>0, in the following precise sense:

              [a(g),alpha_t(B)]=0 for |t|<d          (10)

as forms on D_infty. For B=phi(h), this follows from scalar locality
when g at time zero and supp h are strictly spacelike separated.
To justify the time-zero evaluation, first smear the scalar in a
small real-time interval where locality holds. Matrix elements on
D_infty, including B(t) and its adjoint partner, are smooth in that
time by the polynomial band bounds. Remove the temporal mollifier.

Combining (9),(10), the form

                C_(s,eps)=[a(g),alpha_(is)(B_eps)]

is represented by the integral over |t|>=d only. Products in this
commutator are form products on D_infty. For a fixed sufficiently
large energy weight q', (1) and the bounds for B and its adjoint give

 ||(1+H)^-q' C_(s,eps)(1+H)^-q'||
 <= C p_s(g) N(B) exp[s^2/(2eps^2)]
                                      exp[-d^2/(4eps^2)]. (11)

Increasing q' finitely handles either order of the product. This uses
two-sided scalar FORM bounds and one-sided/all-weight B bounds; it
does not assert that the scalar time-zero form is an operator.
The elementary Gaussian tail estimate proves (11). Constants can
depend on the fixed B test and d, but not on G, eps, or s.

6. COMMUTATOR OF AN ENTIRE FINITE SCALAR STENCIL

For one term (3), expand its commutator with B_eps by the product
rule. In the a-th summand the scalar form a(g_a) is replaced by

             [a(g_a),alpha_(i s_a)(B_eps)],

with the surrounding exp(s_a H), exp(-s_a H) left in place. Thus all
positive internal heat gaps s_b-s_(b+1) remain positive and unchanged.
The complex conjugation of B_eps is exactly (9).

This algebra can be justified by putting finite spectral cutoffs in
the scalar forms, using bounded identities, and passing in their
weighted form norms with a slightly larger fixed energy exponent.
The positive internal gaps and positive external heat make the passage
operator-norm convergent. The Gaussian entire weighted bounds allow
B_eps through each heat factor. No negative internal heat gap or
ordinary time-zero operator product is introduced.

More precisely, if R=(1+H)^-1 and C=(1+H)^-q a(g)(1+H)^-q is bounded,
then the cutoff error in weight q+1 is
R[C-P_E C P_E]R. Its norm is <=2||C||/(1+E), since
||(1-P_E)R||<=(1+E)^-1. Thus one additional power on BOTH sides
gives operator-norm form convergence. Increase the fixed weight again
as needed to compose with B_eps using its all-weight bounds. The same
estimate applies to the commutator forms; fixed positive heat gaps
absorb every resulting fixed polynomial weight. No finite-dimensional
energy subspace, compact resolvent, or compactness of exp(-aH) is used.

If an actual packet coefficient is jointly smooth in several spatial
stencil variables rather than already a tensor product, this step is
still legitimate. Rescale its k<=5 fixed separated packet boxes to
unit boxes, insert product cutoffs, and expand in a product Fourier
series. Integrating by parts L>s+4 times in EACH three-dimensional
slot makes the sum of coefficients times the products of the needed
spatial p_s norms finite. Only kL derivatives are used, a fixed number.
Rescaling and those finitely many coefficient derivatives cost at most
exp(C'G), by the actual fixed-order packet/source bounds. Keep the
ordered internal time variables in their disjoint intervals and
integrate them in absolute value afterward. This yields an absolutely
convergent nuclear sum of the expressions (3), with the same exp(CG)
majorant after enlarging C. It is not an assumption that an arbitrary
joint smooth kernel has a finite-rank tensor expansion.

Apply (11) at the replaced position. The increase of its fixed energy
exponent costs a fixed additional inverse power of the neighboring
positive gaps, still at most exp(CG). All other fields are bounded
using (1), again preserving their actual gaps. For an external heat
a>2rho_G, summing terms and actual packet variables gives

 ||exp(-aH)[Phi_G^av(f),B_eps]exp(-aH)||
 <= C exp(CG) a^-D2 N(f)N(B)
       exp[delta_G^2/(2eps^2)] exp[-d^2/(C_2 eps^2)].   (12)

Here the spacelike margin is chosen uniformly for every center in
supp f and every scalar stencil support, for large G. Since the
stencils shrink, strict spacelike separation of the two compact
center supports supplies such a d. Constants C,D2,C2 are fixed;
the order k<=5 is fixed. The bound also covers all lower-subset terms.
Identity terms commute exactly. Centering introduces no new term.

7. SIMULTANEOUS CHOICE OF GAUSSIAN WIDTH AND HEAT

Choose once c_0>0 sufficiently small that
d^2/(C_2 c_0^2)>C+2, where C is the fixed exponential coefficient in
(12). Put

                    eps_G=a_G=c_0 G^-1/2.

Then delta_G/eps_G tends to zero, and (12) tends to zero exponentially
in G, up to fixed powers. This choice is crucial: taking eps fixed
would leave exp(CG), while taking eps exponentially small would make
complex heat conjugations uncontrollable.

Compare the commutator in (12) to the one with Phi(f). Let
D_G=Phi_G^av(f)-Phi(f). By (7), at a_G/2 its heat norm is bounded by
G^D1 d(G)+exp(-cG). For example,

 exp(-aH)D_G B_eps exp(-aH)
 =exp(-aH/2)[exp(-aH/2)D_G exp(-aH/2)]
                            [exp(aH/2)B_eps exp(-aH)].

The last bracket has norm <=C a^-p_B exp[C a^2/eps^2]N(B), by (9)
and the one-sided energy bound of B. The reversed product uses the
adjoint companion and has the same bound. Since a/eps=1, the
exponential is a constant. All remaining losses are fixed powers of
G. Choosing K beyond B+D1+p_B/2 proves

       ||exp(-a_GH)[Phi(f),B_epsG]exp(-a_GH)|| ->0.    (13)

On D_infty, exp(-a_G H) tends to 1 in every energy norm, B_epsG tends
to B in the required energy norms, and Phi(f) is continuous between
the corresponding energy spaces. Thus the matrix elements of the
left side converge to [Phi(f),B]. Equation(13) proves

                         [Phi(f),B]=0 on D_infty.    (14)

All variable-scale constants have been displayed. In particular the
error d(G) was never multiplied by exp(a_G E_G), exp(CG), or by a
preparatory exp(polylog G) loss. The only extra error factor is a
fixed power of G. The separate finite-G scalar anchor exp(CG) was
paid by the Gaussian locality tail in (12).

8. TWO APPLICATIONS GIVE MUTUAL LOCALITY

First apply (14) with B equal to the ORIGINAL scalar field phi(h),
and then its adjoint, for compact tests f,h with strictly spacelike
separated supports. The premise (10) is original scalar locality.
This proves relative locality of every new field and reflected
companion with the scalar field.

Next fix B=Phi_w(h), or its reflected companion. The operator-
distribution theorem gives all required polynomial energy bounds
on the same D_infty. The first application gives relative locality
of B with each scalar spacetime field. By the time-zero smoothing
argument in Section5 this supplies (10) for every scalar constituent
of the small stencil. Applying (14) again gives

              [Phi_v(f),Phi_w(h)]=0 on D_infty         (15)

for strictly spacelike separated supports, including the corresponding
adjoint fields. No Haag duality, strong generation of a scalar von
Neumann net, or new Lorentz covariance was used in this second step.
The argument is about common-domain field locality.

The result uses the actual finite scalar-stencil provenance as well
as the heat Cauchy estimate. A generic nonlocal field with the same
energy bound would not satisfy (12). Conversely mere Euclidean
permutation symmetry would not have proved (15): the Gaussian
commutator argument is the missing controlled crossing step.

9. SCOPE

Subject to the precisely listed copied/source/scalar inputs, the
closable translation fields constructed in the preceding note are
mutually local and relatively local to the original scalar sector.
They and their companion family act on one invariant dense domain,
have polynomial energy bounds, and retain the original positive-energy
translation representation and vacuum.

No conclusion about a nondegenerate 21-component Gram matrix, the
continuous O(4)/Lorentz tensor law, full gauge-field identification,
or the mass gap is made. The local fields may still have linear
relations or zero directions. Finite reflections and field adjoints
must still be matched to their tensor labels if that stronger statement
is desired. This note closes a locality/domain interface, not those
independent identification questions.
