RESULT

The new Dirichlet-box theorem applies without a change of law to a
BARE Wilson conditional interior, with its complete boundary/exterior
fixed and no block-average observation conditioned upon. It does not
directly apply to the native regular T integral: that integral has a
hard nonlinear average constraint already at its first step. Its later
Gaussian representation solves this constraint; it is not a softening
or deletion of it. A concrete Gaussian calculation below proves why
an ambient small-tail estimate cannot be passed to those fibers.

There is a different, genuinely native positive result: the admitted
R-reference integral in II(1.2) has a fixed-current-size axial slice,
an actual quadratic lower bound, and an actual same-reference
denominator estimate. It has normalized Gaussian tails in its current
fluctuation coordinates. This was already proved in the turn15/21
reference notes; it does not identify that reference with the new bare
Dirichlet box. The two results have complementary, distinct scopes.

2. EXACT PLACE WHERE THE NEW BARE RESULT APPLIES

Let D be the open cubical box with every boundary link fixed to e.
Include in S_D every Wilson plaquette reading a free edge. Such a
plaquette lies in the box. The positive bare action splits as

             S(U)=S_ext(e)+S_D(u;e).

The exact conditional law is therefore

 mu_D(du|e)=Z_D(e)^-1 exp[-S_D(u;e)/g^2] dHaar(u).       (2.1)

Gauge transformations are identity at ALL boundary vertices. The
one-anchor-per-component forest in polylog_box_geometry_audit_turn37
is an exact Haar change of variables for (2.1). For boundary data in
the SAME coherent frame satisfying delta<=c R^-24, the theorem in
polylog_box_conditional_tail_turn37 applies with its actual minimum
x_*(e), actual Z_D(e), and R<=C log(1/g)^b. Constants are independent
of the surrounding volume. Integrating (2.1) over admitted exteriors
is legal; unadmitted exterior mass must remain separate.

This is a definite stage of the original positive bare law. It is not
a theorem for a conditional law obtained by additionally revealing
block averages, history flags, nonlocal minimizing fields, or auxiliary
variables. Such revealing generally changes the conditional law even
when the unconditioned marginal has not changed.

3. HARD-FIBER OBSTRUCTION AND EXACT GAUSSIAN NORMALIZATION

Let x in R^N have density exp[-x^t H x/(2g^2)], H>0, and let Q have
m independent rows. Use the ordinary Lebesgue delta convention.
Put Sigma=Q H^-1 Q^t. Direct Gaussian integration gives

 Z_Q(b)=(2 pi g^2)^((N-m)/2) (det H)^(-1/2)
             (det Sigma)^(-1/2)
             exp[-b^t Sigma^-1 b/(2g^2)].                (3.1)

The constrained mean and covariance are

 x_b=H^-1 Q^t Sigma^-1 b,
 Cov(x|Qx=b)=g^2[H^-1-H^-1 Q^t Sigma^-1 Q H^-1].        (3.2)

Thus conditioning is singular relative to the ambient distribution.
For example take H=I and Qx=x_1. For b=2gT, the fiber has probability
one of lying outside {|x|_infty<=gT}, although the ambient failure
probability is at most 2N exp(-T^2/2). With T a power of log(1/g), b
still tends to zero and is inside any fixed weak-data neighborhood.
This is a counterexample to the INFERENCE from a bare centered tail
to a tail at every small block datum, not a counterexample to a new
constrained theorem centered at its own minimum.

The nonlinear version is equally exact: if a retained datum v is
outside the image M_e(W) of the proposed bare good window, then its
entire fiber M_e^-1(v) avoids W. Even near identity, a shrinking gT
window need not cover all admitted coarse data. The native critical
configuration in B109(2.3) is chosen to handle this dependence; it
cannot be replaced by the unconstrained Dirichlet minimum.

There IS a useful positive Gaussian statement. If H>=lambda I, then
the bracketed covariance in (3.2) is <=lambda^-1 I. Hence

 Prob(max_l |x_l-x_b,l|>gT | Qx=b)
                  <=2N exp(-lambda T^2/2).              (3.3)

No determinant ratio is discarded in deriving this normalized bound:
(3.1) is its exact denominator. For fixed-dimensional color blocks,
only fixed constants change. With N<=C R^4 and lambda>=c R^-8 this is
a polynomial-box Gaussian tail, uniformly in the value b. This
establishes a real matching principle at the LINEAR Gaussian level.
It does not control the nonlinear constrained compact complement.

All rows here are independent after fixing the gauge and the boundary.
Redundant coarse gauge rows must first be removed or treated on their
actual quotient. An ambient determinant with redundant rows is not
the native normalization.

6. SOFT CONSTRAINTS AND EXACT USE OF AUXILIARY VARIABLES

A factor exp[-tau |F_e(x)-v|^2] is a soft physical constraint and gives
a different conditional law. Sending tau to infinity requires the
coarea and normalization information in (4.1); a bound whose constants
grow with tau is not a hard-fiber result. Conversely, adjoining a
normalized Gaussian auxiliary variable can preserve the x marginal
exactly AFTER integrating that variable. Conditioning on its value
tilts x and restores a new conditional normalizer. These two operations
must not be confused with the native gauge-fixing quadratic G(V).

An elementary comparison valid on one unchanged support is
 Prob_(e^E mu)(A)<=exp(osc E) mu(A).
It can transfer the bare theorem when a finite real E is actually
known there. It cannot cover a hard average delta, a changed gauge
quotient, or cutoff-induced zeros by merely naming them interactions.

7. POSITIVE FIRST-STEP BRIDGE: ANNEALED FIBERS, NOT A UNIFORM FIBER TAIL

The uniform-fiber obstruction in Section3 does NOT obstruct the
following exact construction. This is the additional route proposed
by root and independently audited by admission_proof.

Fix an admitted exterior omega and let Q^omega be the actual bare
Dirichlet probability on one core. Let W be its centered good window
and Q^omega(W^c)<=alpha. For ANY measurable retained datum Y disintegrate

 Q^omega(du)=nu^omega(dy) Q_y^omega(du),
             p(y)=Q_y^omega(W^c).

Standard Borel spaces suffice; no differentiability, coarea lower
bound, regular-value assumption or density in y is needed here.
Then

 integral p(y) nu^omega(dy)<=alpha,
 nu^omega(p>theta)<=alpha/theta,                          (7.1)

for 0<theta<1. On p<=theta replace Q_y^omega by its normalization on
W; on p>theta leave it unchanged. Call the resulting joint law Qhat.
The COMPLETE (omega,Y) marginal is unchanged exactly. On good fibers
the new denominator is at least 1-theta, and

 TV(Q_y^omega,Q_y^omega(.|W))=p(y).

Thus a maximal coupling has mean discrepancy at most alpha. If a
fiber is called uncontrolled when p>theta or the good-fiber coupling
disagrees, then its mean uncontrolled indicator is at most alpha/theta:

        1_(p>theta)+p 1_(p<=theta) <= p/theta.             (7.2)

This sharp estimate does not add alpha and alpha/theta separately.
An uncontrolled-boundary contribution remains separate. A joint sparse
boundary parameter beta, if independently established in the required
conditioning, combines with the conditional alpha/theta estimate;
the bare box theorem does not itself establish that beta.

For separated bare cores, condition on their complete common exterior.
The bare interiors are independent. If each coordinate of Y reads
only one core plus that exterior, the disintegration and maximal
couplings can be performed independently. Consequently every selected
set of uncontrolled good-boundary cores has the corresponding product
bound (alpha/theta)^number, conditional on that exterior. Merely
disjoint output supports are not enough: the complete datum reads
must have this separation.
