 (a) analytic continuation of the small-field q-core in a relative common-coupling disc;

3A. THE BOUNDARY TAG, ITS LIFETIME BUDGET, AND ITS ENDPOINT

This subsection supplies an essential qualification to the preceding finite-step
consequence: the coefficient in(3.4) is NOT an arbitrary-power bulk R bound.
It may include an O(zeta) Wilson marginal on the replaced region. It must remain
in the component/boundary ledger until the actual large-field component is
eliminated. Neither a small per-cell coefficient nor a small current volume
licenses moving it directly into the homogeneous R family.

Exact exponent ledger. Write one frozen replacement with old variable v and new
reference variable y, and suppress unchanged exterior variables. Let S_o(v)
be the Wilson part in the incoming density and S_n(y) the Wilson part extracted
from the output. The sourced integral is

  exp[-(gamma+zeta)S_n(y)] / D_gamma
    * int w_gamma(y) exp[gamma S_n(y)]
             exp[-gamma S_o(v)+E_o(v)]
             exp{zeta[S_n(y)-S_o(v)]} dv.             (3A.1)

All original Haar, axial-gauge, and characteristic factors are included in
w_gamma and dv. This is just multiplication by the extracted exponential;
no new normalizer or logarithm has been introduced. In the source's multiscale
version the Wilson actions have its partition-of-unity weights and the shifts
are zeta_n in each scale layer. The identical algebra holds term by term.
The weights have fixed bounded overlap, so |zeta_n|<=2r suffices. The gamma
pieces cancel the frozen inverse D_gamma exactly as in the original proof.
There is NO uncancelled inverse D_(gamma+zeta) in(3A.1). Formula(3.2) is an
optional comparison with a recomputed reference and is not used for estimates.

Attach the extra difference to the actual replacement component Z even if its
evaluation happens to be independent of some coordinates of Z. Its localizations
are defined with a carrier containing Z and every connecting localization tree;
the carrier is not replaced by the smallest apparent support of the resulting
function. A history with no such component has no such extra term. In particular
one must not manufacture a homogeneous source Wilson correction by forgetting Z.

The two parts of this difference are treated differently. On real integration
variables the negative old-action contribution stays with the positive Wilson
weight and uses(3.5); it is never bounded by a regular-field supremum. The new
reference action and the regular-background pieces use(3.3). Localization of

multiply each localized Wilson summand by its bounded zeta coefficient rather

On complex external fields, first make the source's comparison to a real

Here is why arbitrarily many ages and source volumes do not introduce a new
factor proportional to the UV depth. Each replacement creates its defect once.
At a later step that same term is transported or resummed, not created again.
Let Z_i be the actual surviving large-field domains of a history, and put

  c_i(Z_i)=M^d R_i^(d+1) d'_i(Z_i),                 (3A.2)

with the source convention for d'_i and its harmless single-cube correction.
The added regular-background component cost is at most C_r|Z_i| and hence

do not obstruct that choice. The original vacuum and reference-normalization
cost already has the larger form C c_i(Z_i).

The source's lifetime reserve is exactly designed for the accumulated sum of
these debits: CMP122-II(1.79)--(1.80) bounds the remaining reserve of a component
by the sum of the costs of its successive enlarged descendants until it can be
eliminated. In a step without a new large field, its reserve loses the next
debit, as in(1.83). On merging components the available reserves add, and newly
created large fields supply new reserve, as in(1.85). Replacing the fixed debit
constant C by C+C'_r leaves this induction unchanged. Choose the source
large-field constants first with that larger debit and a fixed coercivity

This is stronger than separately bounding each age and then summing its bound.
Repeated localization sums are those already paid by the source's spatial
decay; attaching an existing deterministic tag creates no independent sum.

Endpoint rule. Expand the original UNNORMALIZED component operators and the
boundary interactions, keeping the tags. A connected polymer intersecting a
surviving component remains a boundary polymer. If it is disjoint from every
surviving component but contains a tagged defect, that defect's component must
have been eliminated by one of the actual T operators in this polymer.
Consequently its T-operator count is at least one. This is exactly the two-case
distinction in CMP122-II(1.95)--(1.97): the count may start at zero for boundary
polymers, but starts at one for bulk polymers. The reserve bound(1.89), after
the fixed losses just described, extracts exp[-c p_0(g_k)] before the remaining
sum. The connected-log expansion then gives the bulk birth bound(1.100) with
weakened fixed constants. For p_0(g)=A(log g^-2)^nu, nu>1, this is smaller than
any prescribed gamma^-P by taking Gamma sufficiently large. Surviving boundary
polymers keep the boundary bound; they are never assigned this bulk bound.

Cutoffs and compensators. Frozen characteristic functions remain identical
factors on both sides of(3A.1). They are not differentiated or analytically
continued in their threshold. The bad-field restrictions used to obtain the
source reserve therefore remain available; only a fixed fraction of positive
Wilson/Gaussian coercivity has been spent. Inverse local normalizers are the
unchanged D_gamma and their already constructed background-analytic extensions.
Their large classical exponents cancel within the original gamma part before
the source difference is estimated. Any source-dependent vacuum contribution
extracted later stays in the vacuum ledger. It cannot cancel a boundary tag or
be used to discard the reference factor.

Conclusion of the audit. The common bounded source shift fits the original
resummed component-boundary induction and produces arbitrary-power BULK births
only by the endpoint rule above. It is not necessary to prove large-field
conditional zero-freeness. The required imported analytic localization and
physical field maps remain exactly those of the source. If a chosen replacement
or localization map does not preserve those interfaces, this argument does not
apply; keeping a formal tag by itself cannot repair an invalid map estimate.

This closes the |z_m|<=2r bootstrap used in Section3 with a strict margin. The

6A. EVERY PRIMITIVE PHASE STAYS IN THE SAME FIXED SOURCE DISC

Let a=0,...,q denote the phase within macro block m. Freeze the reference
phase radii b_{m,a}, windows, and gauge widths chosen in the real finite-phase
construction. Their ratios to b_m have fixed q-dependent upper and lower

 |partial_gamma beta_a|<=C_q epsilon_m/gamma_m,
 ||D_E beta_a||<=C_q epsilon_m/b_m^2,
 ||D_E (C_a)_E||<=C_q,
 ||partial_gamma (C_a)_E||<=C_q b_{m,a}^2/gamma_m.    (6A.1)

The E directions in the beta bound have zero Maxwell coefficient. Their
classical transport has zero extracted coefficient at EVERY phase, so its
possibly large fixed phase norm does not remove the epsilon gain. The
complete Gaussian beta at each prefix is common-coupling independent by
Section5A. Parity and common-disc Cauchy therefore apply to every a, even
when that prefix has no positive Gaussian drift. Only q is required to
have the positive drift and contraction used in(6.2).

On an admitted primitive prefix, the exact ledger with endpoint R subtraction
and actual birth/annular insertions gives an absolute error C_q rho_m for

7. TANGENTS AND THE NORMALIZATION DERIVATIVE

Apply Cauchy on |w|<r to the PROVED bounds and then restrict to |w|<=r/2. At w=0,
  |Gamma'_m|<=C,
  ||E'_m||/b_m^2<=C/gamma_m+C rho_m/r,
  ||R'_m||/b_m^2<=C rho_m/r.                         (7.1)
Here R'_m means a source derivative of the entire static history, NOT just a
derivative of the newest R birth. The factor1/r is harmless; (6.5) is summable
for P>3. Differentiating the exact core-plus-R ledger gives
  |Gamma'_(m+1)-Gamma'_m|
       <=C epsilon_m/gamma_m+C_r rho_m.              (7.2)
Thus Gamma'_m remains close to its initial value1 when Gamma is sufficiently
large. More generally all fixed source derivatives have factorial Cauchy
bounds on a smaller fixed disc. This does not supply nontriviality of arbitrary
physical observables; it is a running-coupling response statement.

The b_m in(7.1) is a SOURCE-INDEPENDENT reference radius, so its derivative is
zero. If instead one normalizes by the running holomorphic radius
 b(Gamma)=c Gamma^(-1/2)(log Gamma)^s, its exact logarithmic derivative is
  d/dw log b(Gamma)^2=(-1+2s/log Gamma) Gamma'/Gamma.  (7.3)
Hence for E/b(Gamma)^2 the extra derivative is
  -(-1+2s/log Gamma)(Gamma'/Gamma) E/b(Gamma)^2,
which is O(K|Gamma'|/gamma), the same allowed size as(7.1). If the background
argument itself is rescaled, E(b(Gamma)v), its additional chain-rule term is
also O(K|Gamma'|/gamma) on a fixed smaller background ball by Cauchy. No
normalization derivative has been discarded, and there is no claim that it
has the smaller epsilon/gamma size.
