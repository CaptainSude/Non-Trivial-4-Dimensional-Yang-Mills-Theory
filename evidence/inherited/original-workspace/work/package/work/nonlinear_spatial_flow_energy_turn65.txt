Those facts do not imply a cutoff-uniform flow-tangent bound. The
EXACT plaquette Hessian has a negative curvature term of order
sqrt(h_p), not h_p. The resulting physical-time Jacobi inequality
has potential a^-2 sqrt(h_p). An explicit homogeneous SU(2) solution

at least as 1+c s/(a^2 sqrt(beta)), although every initial plaquette

This is an obstruction to a proposed method, not a claim of tangent

2. EXACT PLAQUETTE HESSIAN

Vary all links through product-metric geodesics with initial tangent J.
After transporting each signed link tangent to the starting vertex
of one oriented plaquette, its word is exactly

 P(t)=exp(t A_1) exp(t A_2) exp(t A_3) exp(t A_4) P,
 A_i=i x_i.sigma/2.                                    (2.1)

The transports in (2.1) use the fixed base links. An inverse edge
contributes its negative tangent transported through that base edge.
This is an identity for a plaquette containing each variable edge
at most once, not a Taylor replacement of the group word.

Write P=p0 I+i p.sigma, and d_U J(p)=sum_i x_i. Since

 sum_i A_i^2+2sum_(i<j)A_i A_j
             =(sum_i A_i)^2+sum_(i<j)[A_i,A_j],
 [A_i,A_j]=-i(x_i cross x_j).sigma/2,

direct differentiation yields

 Hess h_p[J,J]
    =(p0/4)|d_U J(p)|^2
             -(1/2)p.sum_(i<j)(x_i cross x_j).           (2.2)

This is the Riemannian Hessian quadratic form because the input
curves are product geodesics. No coordinate connection term has
been discarded.

Use p0=1-h_p, |p|=sqrt(h_p(2-h_p)),
 |sum_i x_i|^2<=4sum_i|x_i|^2, and
 sum_(i<j)|x_i||x_j|<=(3/2)sum_i|x_i|^2.
Then

 Hess h_p[J,J] >= (1/4)|d_UJ(p)|^2
          -[h_p+(3/4)sqrt(2h_p)]sum_(e in p)|J_e|^2.  (2.3)

As0<=h_p<=2, summing proves, with one fixed numerical C,

 Hess S[J,J] >= (1/4)||d_UJ||_2^2
                  -C sum_e rho_e(U)|J_e|^2,
 rho_e(U)=sum_(p containing e)sqrt(h_p(U)).             (2.4)

Every norm here is the unscaled finite graph norm. The transports
are isometries, so |x_i|=|J_e|; all expressions are gauge covariant.
The positive part is a covariant CURL. It is not the full covariant
gradient, and no gauge fixing or Hodge gap is silently inserted.

The sqrt(h) loss in this local estimate is real. For one plaquette
choose transported tangents x_1=e1,x_2=e2,x_3=-e1,x_4=-e2.
Their sum is zero and their ordered cross sum is2e3.
At P=cos(theta)I+i sin(theta)sigma3, (2.2) gives

                  Hess h_p[J,J]=-sin(theta),           (2.5)

whereas h_p=1-cos(theta)=O(theta^2). Thus an otherwise identical
per-plaquette estimate with a negative term only C h_p sum|J|^2
would be false for small positive theta. Other plaquettes can
contribute in a whole-lattice variation; (2.5) alone does not assert
a negative global eigenvalue. Section5 supplies a genuine global
nonlinear example.

3. JACOBI ENERGY AND WHAT AVERAGING DOES NOT PERMIT

Let J_s=D Phi_s(U)J_0 be a tangent variation of the exact flow.
Covariant differentiation of a gradient flow gives

            nabla_s J_s=-(4/a^2)(Hess S)_(U_s)J_s.

Consequently (2.4) gives the actual finite-lattice inequality

 (1/2)d/ds ||J_s||_2^2
    <=-a^-2 ||d_(U_s)J_s||_2^2
          +(C/a^2)sum_e rho_e(U_s)|J_(s,e)|^2.           (3.1)

Pure gauge variations are transported by equivariance. The curl-only
coercivity does not justify replacing (3.1) by a scalar heat equation.
For gauge-invariant final observables their vertical directions
drop out, but a quantitative horizontal/augmented evolution remains
to be controlled.

Under the original Wilson law the exact averaged consequence is

 (1/2)d/ds E||J_s||_2^2
 <=-a^-2 E||d_(U_s)J_s||_2^2
       +(C/a^2)sum_e E[rho_e(U_s)|J_(s,e)|^2].          (3.2)

All quantities are smooth and bounded at each finite regulator,
so differentiation under that finite measure is legitimate.
The last expectation is a MARKED correlation. It cannot be replaced
by E rho_e times E|J_e|^2: J_s depends on the same initial field.

Even if such a factorization were available with
E rho=O(beta^-1/2), the resulting crude rate would be
C/(a^2 sqrt(beta)); at fixed s it is not cutoff uniform along
beta comparable to log(1/a). This observation diagnoses the crude
bound, not the true Wilson-averaged tangent growth.

For the magnetic scalar B=Phi(X_s), its second derivative further
contains the term D Phi(X_s).D^2 X_s, as recorded in TURN64.
Neither (3.1) nor total-action monotonicity cancels that term.
The homogeneous scalar equation in Section5 retains the full
nonlinear dependence useful for studying it directly.

4. ACTUAL ORIGINAL-LAW ENERGY CONSUMERS

Let n be the number of spatial sites. There are3n spatial plaquettes.
The exact action dissipation identity is

 dS(U_s)/ds=-(4/a^2)||grad S(U_s)||_2^2.                (4.1)

TURN64 gives E h_p(U)<=3/(2beta) for each original plaquette. Hence

 E S(U_s)<=E S(U)<=9n/(2beta),
 E integral_0^s ||grad S(U_r)||_2^2 dr
                                  <=9n a^2/(8beta).   (4.2)

These statements use the actual Wilson initial law and its actual
nonlinear spatial flow; there is no Gaussian replacement.
The entire full-slice energy moment also persists:

 E exp[lambda beta S(U_s)]
          <=(1-2lambda)^(-9n/4),     0<=lambda<1/2.     (4.3)

This follows from pointwise S(U_s)<=S(U), followed by TURN64.
It is a full-slice estimate; local subregion energy is not asserted
to be monotone under a flow crossing that subregion's boundary.

Spatial translation invariance is preserved by the flow. Therefore,
on a rectangular spatial torus,

 E sum_(i<j spatial)h(P_ij(U_s,x))<=9/(2beta),
 E h(P_ij(U_s,x))<=9/(2beta).                           (4.4)

On a cubic isotropic spatial torus, permutation symmetry improves
the last constant to3/(2beta). In either case
E rho_e(U_s)<=C/sqrt(beta), uniformly in s, by Cauchy--Schwarz
and bounded plaquette incidence. This proves the unmarked estimate
mentioned in Section3 for ALL flow times; it still does not control

The companion physical_variance_floor_turn65.txt independently
derives the same mean estimate and its elementary radial-probe

There is a useful initial local high-probability estimate. Let B
contain m plaquettes. The all-plaquette Gamma bound in TURN64 gives,
with probability at least1-exp(-x),

 beta sum_B h_p <= (3m/2)+sqrt(6mx)+2x.                 (4.5)

Define the physical plaquette-strength array F_a(p)=a^-2 sqrt(h_p)
and its discrete critical3/2 norm on B by

 K_B=[a^3 sum_B F_a(p)^(3/2)]^(2/3)
                      =(sum_B h_p^(3/4))^(2/3).

Holder gives the exact inequality
 K_B<=m^(1/6)(sum_B h_p)^(1/2). Thus (4.5) yields

 K_B<=C beta^-1/2 [m^(2/3)+m^(1/6)sqrt(x)]             (4.6)

on the same event. For a box of lattice radius R and x proportional
to its volume, this is C R^2/sqrt(beta), with failure probability
exp(-cR^3).

Accordingly these energy estimates certify small critical curvature
on boxes R much less than beta^(1/4). Their physical radius
a beta^(1/4) and diffusive time a^2 sqrt(beta) both tend to zero
on the logarithmic weak trajectory. Even a favorable small-critical-
norm parabolic absorption argument based on (4.6) would begin at
that shrinking scale, not immediately at fixed physical s.

This is a statement about what the bound certifies. It is not a
lower bound on the actual random critical norm in larger boxes.
Using a covariant Sobolev/Gaffney estimate to consume K_B would
require its actual gauge and boundary hypotheses in addition to
(4.6); no such theorem is smuggled into the calculation.

5. AN EXACT HOMOGENEOUS NONLINEAR FLOW

Take all spatial links in direction1 equal to
 U1=cos(alpha1/2)I+i sin(alpha1/2)n1.sigma,
all links in direction2 equal to the analogous U2, and U3=I.
Translation equivariance preserves this finite-dimensional family.
Set

 w_i=sin^2(alpha_i/2),       z=n1.n2.

The only nontrivial spatial plaquette orientation is12. The exact
SU(2) commutator trace gives

                 h12=2 w1 w2(1-z^2).                 (5.1)

The full slice action is n h12. The full link metric on this
homogeneous submanifold is

 n sum_(i=1,2) [dalpha_i^2+4w_i |dn_i|^2].             (5.2)

The factor n cancels between the gradient of the action and this
metric. The flow of the axes is

 dn1/ds=(4/a^2)w2 z(n2-z n1),
 dn2/ds=(4/a^2)w1 z(n1-z n2).                          (5.3)

Equal initial amplitudes remain equal. Write w=w1=w2. Equations
(1.1),(5.1),(5.2) then give the EXACT closed system

 w'=-(8/a^2)w^2(1-w)(1-z^2),
 z'= (8/a^2)w z(1-z^2).                               (5.4)

It has the conserved quantity

                       I=z w/(1-w).                   (5.5)

Indeed the logarithmic derivatives of z and w/(1-w) cancel wherever
nonzero, and continuity covers zero. Equivalently

 w'=-(8/a^2)(1-w)[w^2-I^2(1-w)^2],
 h12=2[w^2-I^2(1-w)^2].                               (5.6)

These formulas retain the nonlinear angular/amplitude coupling.
They can be used to compute observable variations without replacing
the field by its flat linearization.

At the orthogonal-axis background n1=e1,n2=e2, z=0. The axes are
stationary and

                 w'=-(8/a^2)w^2(1-w).                 (5.7)

For0<w0<=1/2, w decreases and

 1/w0+4s/a^2 <=1/w(s)<=1/w0+8s/a^2.                  (5.8)

This follows by differentiating1/w. It already displays a genuine
nonlinear decay of this homogeneous background.

6. HORIZONTAL TANGENT GROWTH, WITH THE FULL METRIC

Perturb the axes initially by

 n1(epsilon)=(cos epsilon,sin epsilon,0),
 n2(epsilon)=(sin epsilon,cos epsilon,0),

at fixed equal amplitudes. At epsilon=0, dz0/depsilon=2.
The amplitude variation is zero to FIRST order, since w' depends
on z^2. The relative-axis linearization obeys

 (delta z)'=(8/a^2)w(s) delta z.

Combining with (5.7), its exact amplification is

 J_z(s)=[w0/(1-w0)]/[w(s)/(1-w(s))].                   (6.1)

Each axis derivative is multiplied by J_z. In the full link metric,
the initial tangent norm squared is8n w0 and its evolved norm
squared is8n w(s)J_z(s)^2. Hence

 R(s)^2=||D Phi_s J0||^2/||J0||^2
        =[w0/w(s)] [(1-w(s))/(1-w0)]^2
        >=1+4w0 s/a^2.                                (6.2)

The factor n cancels exactly. This is not an artifact of measuring
one homogeneous collective coordinate with the wrong volume metric.

Nor is it a gauge direction. In LEFT-trivialized matrix coordinates,
with c=sqrt(1-w), u=sqrt(w), the two link variations at one site are

 J1=i u c sigma2-i w sigma3,
 J2=i u c sigma1+i w sigma3,          J3=0.              (6.3)

The adjoint of the periodic lattice gauge derivative, applied to a
homogeneous field, is
 sum_i [J_i-U_i^-1 J_i U_i].
The i=1 term in (6.3) is-2iw sigma3, the i=2 term is+2iw sigma3,
and the sum is exactly zero. Thus this mode is orthogonal to ALL
local infinitesimal gauge variations. The same computation holds
along the base flow, with w=w(s) and the factor J_z(s).
Equation(6.2) is therefore also growth in the horizontal quotient
metric along this mode.

Choose w0=(2beta)^-1/2, beta>=2. Then
 h12(U0)=2w0^2=1/beta, while all other spatial plaquette energies
vanish. Equation(6.2) gives

           R(s)^2>=1+(2sqrt(2))s/(a^2 sqrt(beta)).      (6.4)

On a logarithmic weak sequence this diverges for fixed s.
This is an exact deterministic finite-lattice result at arbitrarily
small plaquette energies.

The observable qualification is essential. For the radial magnetic
point scalar, |p12|^2=h12(2-h12), and (5.1) is even in z.
Its first variation along this mode vanishes at z=0. Consequently
(6.4) is NOT a lower bound on that scalar's gradient norm.
The second variation also involves the amplitude response in (5.4);
dropping that response would be incorrect. The exact equation(5.6)

7. WHAT THE ENERGY THEOREM ALONE CANNOT IMPLY

Extend the homogeneous configuration constantly through Euclidean
time and set temporal links equal to I. All temporal plaquette
energies vanish. At the initial field chosen in Section6,
beta h_p belongs to{0,1}. Thus for every plaquette set and every
0<=s_p<1/2,

 exp(sum_p s_p beta h_p)
       <=exp(sum_p s_p)
       <=product_p(1-2s_p)^(-3/4).                     (7.1)

The last inequality uses
-(3/4)log(1-2s)>=3s/2>=s.
Gauge-average this fixed configuration with the normalized product
Haar law. The resulting probability is gauge invariant and
translation invariant. All energy values, and the flow-map operator
norm on the corresponding transformed horizontal direction, are
unchanged by gauge equivariance and isometry.

This law therefore satisfies the full TURN64 energy bound while its
mean squared quotient-tangent operator norm is at least(6.4).
It is NOT the Wilson Gibbs law and is not offered as a YM model.
It proves the logical point that the energy moment inequality,
gauge symmetry and translation invariance alone do not imply the
desired uniform raw tangent control. Additional information about
the actual Wilson distribution of coherent backgrounds is needed.

The continuum stability literature does not supply that missing
input for free. Gross's finite-action condition includes
integral_0^T r^-1/2 ||F(r)||_2^2 dr<infinity.
For the canonical equal-time four-dimensional Gaussian reference,

On any fixed spatial volume, its expected action integral from

logarithmic weak coupling. This is a free scaling check of the

The source's continuum augmented variational theorem depends on
