SPATIAL GAIN, RUNNING FRAMES, AND JOINT OS GROWTH
19 October 2026. Root research note. Full YM4 remains UNPROVED.

Purpose. Quantify the genuinely joint error left AFTER local single-field
renormalization. Treating every n-leg running matrix as arbitrary can give
an artificial n-fold spectral-spread loss: the companion turn19 audit
handles the tensor-product connection exactly. This note addresses a
different object, a forcing term with n external legs. Its norm can still
carry one inverse-frame factor per leg. A positive spatial power absorbs
that loss with a FIXED factorial exponent in n. Superpolynomial smallness
in the running coupling alone need not do so.

These are proved analytic implications. The mixed-history YM forcing
estimate to which they would apply has not been proved. In particular,
do not substitute a conditional fluctuation cluster for a correlation in
the unconditioned coarse state.

1. AN ELEMENTARY SUM WITH THE NEEDED ARITY DEPENDENCE

Fix c>0 and q>=0. There are C>0 and an integer K>=q such that, for every
integer n>=1,

 sum_(k>=0) (1+k)^(q n) exp(-c k) <= C^n (n!)^K.             (1.1)

Choose any integer K>=q (K=0 is allowed when q=0). For K>=1, compare
each summand to the integral over [k,k+1]:

 (1+k)^(Kn) exp(-c k)
 <= exp(c) integral_k^(k+1) (1+x)^(Kn) exp(-c x) dx.

The total is at most exp(2c) c^(-Kn-1) (Kn)!. The multinomial
inequality (Kn)! <= K^(Kn) (n!)^K proves (1.1), absorbing constants
independent of n into C^n. The case K=0 is a geometric sum. No constant
in (1.1) depends on n. The exact fractional Gamma estimate could improve
K, but that improvement is unnecessary for OS growth.

A shifted version follows from 1+j0+k <= (1+j0)(1+k):

 sum_(k>=0) (1+j0+k)^(q n) exp(-c k)
 <= C^n (n!)^K (1+j0)^(q n).                              (1.2)

Fixed logarithmic factors per leg cause no difficulty. For any eta>0
and v>=0, (1+log(1+j))^v <= C_(eta,v)(1+j)^eta.
Raising this SINGLE-leg estimate to n costs only C_(eta,v)^n.

2. A SEPARATED-SUPPORT CONVERGENCE THEOREM

Let r_j=L^(-j), L>1, j>=0. Consider one fixed finite field frame and its
complete, normalized n-point approximants G_(n,j). All components,
subtractions, coupling normalizations and physical denominators are part
of these approximants; their definition does not depend on n except for
taking an n-point function. For separated test boxes let 0<d<=1 be their
minimum separation and let

 j_d=ceil(log_L(c0/d)), with c0>=1.

Write Q_n(f) for the product of the required fixed-order local test norms.
Assume, for every n>=1 and j>=j_d, that the SAME hierarchy satisfies

 |G_(n,j+1)(f)-G_(n,j)(f)|
 <= A^n (n!)^B (1+j)^(a n+b)
       (1+log(1+j))^(v n+w)
       (r_j/d)^delta d^(-s n-t) Q_n(f),                    (2.1)

where delta>0; all constants A,B,a,b,v,w,s,t are fixed nonnegative
numbers independent of n,j,d and the UV/volume regulators. Assume also
the analogous initial bound at j_d without the factor (r_j/d)^delta:

 |G_(n,j_d)(f)|
 <= A^n (n!)^B (1+j_d)^(a n+b)
       (1+log(1+j_d))^(v n+w) d^(-s n-t) Q_n(f).            (2.2)

Then G_(n,j)(f) converges as j->infinity and, for every epsilon>0,

 |G_n(f)| <= A_epsilon^n (n!)^(B+K)
                d^(-(s+epsilon)n-t) Q_n(f),               (2.3)

for a finite integer K independent of n. Thus the running frame changes
only the fixed factorial exponent and an arbitrarily small fixed linear
collision exponent. It does not demand an error epsilon_j^n.

Proof. Since n>=1, replace a n+b by (a+b)n and v n+w by (v+w)n. Choose
eta>0 and absorb the logarithm by the single-leg inequality in Section1.
Set q=a+b+eta. For j=j_d+k,

 (r_j/d)^delta <= c0^(-delta) exp[-delta(log L) k].

Equations(1.1)--(1.2) sum the differences by a bound

 C^n (n!)^(B+K) (1+j_d)^(q n) d^(-s n-t) Q_n(f).

For every epsilon>0, (1+j_d)^q <= C_epsilon d^(-epsilon),
because j_d grows only like log(1/d). This proves (2.3), also absorbing
the initial term (2.2). The same argument proves absolute Cauchy
convergence at each fixed n and separated configuration. For d>=1 use
j_d=ceil(log_L c0) and the usual (1+d^-1) convention; no new short-distance
loss occurs. The n=0 normalization is handled separately by G_0=1.

This theorem is local in test configurations. Passing to S^0 requires the
previously proved separated-box/Whitney assembly with linear derivative
order and polynomial large-distance weights. If Q_n itself has TOTAL
required derivative order O(n), the combined order remains O(n). An
O(n) order in EACH of n slots would not meet this condition. Equation(2.3)
alone does not supply
missing derivative, infrared or partition-of-unity estimates. Reflection
positivity, Euclidean covariance, compatibility of mixed words, clustering,
and nontriviality are also separate requirements.

3. WHY ONE-LEG MIXING AND JOINT FORCING ARE DIFFERENT

If physical fields obey a genuine local running connection A(g), the
connection on n separated insertions has the form sum_a A(g)_a. With
S(g) acting in the one-leg frame, S(g)^(tensor n) transforms it exactly
leg by leg. After conjugation by the corresponding free powers, an
error sum_a E(g)_a has norm at most n ||E(g)||, not a bound obtained by
applying the full n-leg spectral spread to an arbitrary error matrix.
The turn19 full-hierarchy audit proves this explicitly.

In contrast, a genuinely joint vector forcing e_(n,j) can satisfy only

 ||(P_j^-1)^(tensor n) e_(n,j)||
 <= ||P_j^-1||^n ||e_(n,j)||.

For ||P_j^-1||<=C j^alpha(1+log j)^nu this gives precisely the
polynomial and logarithmic losses appearing in (2.1). Section2 shows that
a FIXED positive power of the physical scale ratio is enough to sum
them for every n, with acceptable fixed-factorial growth. Increasing the
number of insertions does not require an ever larger spatial power.

At the one-step regular conditional level, such a spatial factor has a
natural source. After extracting the exact conditional single-leg outputs,
every connected fluctuation cluster in the residual partition contains
at least two marked supports. If its localized tree length is at least
c d/r_j and the ORIGINAL connected expansion retains a fixed exponential
length reserve, then

 exp[-c' d/r_j] <= C_delta (r_j/d)^delta                    (3.1)

for any FIXED delta>0. The constant depends on delta, not n. Factoring
one spatial reserve from a connected tree does not spend a reserve on
every external leg. Marked polynomial moments can carry ordinary fixed
factorial powers and remain compatible with Section2.

Equation(3.1) is an exact numeric implication. The statements preceding
it are hypotheses about the actual localized conditional expansion. In
particular: a common fluctuating COARSE background can correlate distant
supports with no exp[-c d/r_j] gain; original mixed large-field components
can connect supports across dates; sharp conditional backgrounds can fail
the local packet Taylor bound. The turn17 sharp-block example rules out
silently treating those issues as solved by (3.1).

3A. A WIDENING LOCALIZATION MESH AND RAW PACKET NORMALIZATIONS

The physical localization range may be r_j R_j, rather than r_j, with
R_j<=C_R(1+j)^b for fixed b>=0. This includes fixed polylogarithmic
functions of 1/g_j when g_j^2 is comparable to 1/j. Do not erase this
factor when reading a native tree metric. It is nevertheless harmless
for the present summation if the ACTUAL joined coefficient has the
exponential bound exp[-c d/(r_j R_j)].

Fix D>=0, delta>0, and put p_n=D n+delta. For y>0,
e^(-c y)<= (p_n/(c e))^(p_n) y^(-p_n). Consequently

 r_j^(-Dn) e^[-c d/(r_j R_j)]
 <= C^n(n!)^K0 d^(-Dn) (r_j/d)^delta R_j^(Dn+delta),    (3.2)

for a fixed integer K0>=D+delta (enlarged if needed). The factorial
bound follows from p_n<=K0 n and maximization/Stirling; constants
depend on c,D,delta, never n,j,d. Since

 R_j^(Dn+delta)<=C1^n (1+j)^[b(D+delta)n],

the right side has exactly the shape of (2.1), with an additional fixed
factorial exponent and linear collision/date powers. Hence Section2
sums even RAW r_j^(-Dn) joint normalizations while retaining OS growth.
The dimension/order D is fixed for the finite field frame; it is not
allowed to grow with n. No n-dependent redefinition of packets occurs:
p_n is only an exponent chosen in this numerical upper bound.

This conclusion requires a connected path in the ORIGINAL absolute
metric of physical scale r_j R_j. It supplies no such metric estimate
for a normalized history operator or an uncharged component interior.

4. SUPERPOLYNOMIAL COUPLING SMALLNESS IS NOT THE SAME INPUT

If g_j^2 is comparable to 1/j, a fixed remainder g_j^p is comparable to
j^(-p/2). The crude joint forcing estimate with j^(alpha n), alpha>0,
is nonsummable for all sufficiently large n at any fixed p. This does
not affect the tensorized homogeneous connection in Section3.

Even an envelope smaller than EVERY fixed inverse power of j need not
give fixed-factorial constants after summation. Fix a,c>0 and p>1, and set

 W_n=sum_(j>=2) j^(a n) exp[-c(log j)^p].                    (4.1)

Then log W_n is of order n^(p/(p-1)). In particular there are no fixed
A,B with W_n<=A^n(n!)^B for every n.

Proof of the obstruction. Put t_n=(a n/(c p))^(1/(p-1)) and choose an
integer j_n nearest exp(t_n), increasing n if necessary. Its logarithm
is t_n+o(1). The logarithm of this one summand is

 a n log j_n-c(log j_n)^p
   =(p-1)c t_n^p+o(n^(p/(p-1))).

This lower bound grows faster than n log n. For completeness, an upper
bound of the same order follows by comparison with the integral whose
logarithmic variable t has exponent (a n+1)t-c t^p. Young's inequality
bounds it by C n^(p/(p-1))-(c/2)t^p, leaving an integrable factor.
The sum/integral comparison only changes fixed constants and the same
exponent. Stirling's estimate then proves the claim.

This is a limitation of that envelope, not a counterexample to the YM
trajectory or to reconstruction. It says that an argument using native
exp[-c(log g^-2)^p] tails and an unrelated per-leg running norm still
has an arity problem. One must exploit extra locality/correlation
structure, or obtain different order-dependent estimates. Flatness of
test functions at external diagonals does not remove a large constant
already present at a fixed separated configuration.

An elementary probability realization confirms that the distinction is
real. Normalize the weights exp[-c(log j)^p] on integers j>=2, and set
X=j^a. The constant Euclidean random field phi(x)=X has moments W_n/W_0,
is Euclidean invariant and reflection positive, and defines a tempered
n-point distribution for each fixed n. To test S^0, choose n disjoint
translated copies of one unit-integral compact Gevrey bump, with centers
spaced two units apart on a line, and take their product F_n. This product
vanishes near every collision diagonal. For every fixed linear seminorm
order sn, p_(sn)(F_n)<=C_s^n(n!)^(B_s): the support radius is polynomial
in n, and fixed Gevrey derivative bounds at TOTAL order O(n) have this
growth. Yet G_n(F_n)=W_n/W_0. Thus no fixed-factorial E0' bound holds
even on S^0. Identical overlapping bump products would not be a valid
S^0 test for this argument. The field is nonclustering and is
NOT a YM model. This example blocks an implication to our sufficient
OS growth condition; it does NOT show that such growth is necessary
for every possible reconstruction (the constant field can itself be
represented as a multiplication operator).

5. A FINITE-ORDER GEVREY FALLBACK, WITH COMPATIBILITY EXPLICIT

Here is a second possible route for a genuinely joint remainder when
Section2's positive spatial power is unavailable. It needs more input.
Suppose an EXACT same-hierarchy difference has a decomposition

 D_(n,j)=Q_(n,j)^(N)+R_(n,j)^(N)

for every integer N>=0. Q contains all explicitly retained terms, with
the same physical normalization and field definition. Suppose its
calibrated sum has separately been bounded at the selected order, and
the calibrated remainder satisfies

 |R_(n,j)^(N)(F)|
 <= C^(n+N) (n!)^B ((n+N)!)^s
      (1+j)^(alpha n+tau-sigma N)
      (1+log(1+j))^(nu n) p_(c n+d N)(F),                 (5.1)

where sigma>0 and the other constants are fixed and nonnegative. Choose a fixed eta>0 to
absorb the logarithm into C^n (1+j)^(eta n). For any fixed zeta>0, set

 N(n)=ceil[((alpha+eta)n+tau+1+zeta)/sigma].

Then N(n)<=K0 n for some fixed integer K0 (enlarge K0 for n=1), and
the remaining date exponent is at most -1-zeta. Consequently

 sum_j |R_(n,j)^(N(n))(F)|
 <= C1^n (n!)^(B+s(1+K0)) p_((c+d K0)n)(F).               (5.2)

Proof: sum the inverse date power, then use
((1+K0)n)! <= (1+K0)^((1+K0)n)(n!)^(1+K0).

Only finite Taylor orders are used; no convergence or Borel summability
of the full perturbation series is needed. But (5.1) is an ACTUAL
uniform remainder estimate with a fixed factorial exponent in N and n.
Fixed-order theorems with unspecified C_N do not imply it. Nor does a
bound on formal coefficients prove a bound on the remainder of the
physical Wilson correlation. The moving-domain counterexamples from
turn18 remain relevant.

Most importantly, N(n) may change the PROOF of a correlation, not its
DEFINITION. Defining a different field counterterm scheme for every n
would destroy the common hierarchy, positivity and consistency. Retained
Q terms cannot be discarded; their cancellation or summability must be
established in the same physical hierarchy. If an order-N theorem is
only valid beyond a date J_N, the missing early-date contribution also
needs its own factorial-compatible bound. Equation(5.2) does not silently
cover it.

6. CONSEQUENCE FOR THE ACTIVE PRODUCER TASK

The economical target is now local single-leg calibration plus a
complete connected residual with a positive power of r_j/d, retaining
one distinguished action remainder and all internal subtractions before
absolute values. One-leg tensorization and Section2 avoid imposing
high perturbative order just because an n-point function has many legs.
The Gaussian complete angular construction already has an r^2 error;
the one-step regular connected fluctuation expansion suggests another
source of spatial gain for multi-leg corrections. Neither observation
establishes the required interacting full-history input.

This note changes which estimate should be sought. It does not prove
the scalar continuum hierarchy, the full SU(2) observable family, their
Yang--Mills short-distance matching, or nontriviality. Those remain
part of the unchanged existence goal.
