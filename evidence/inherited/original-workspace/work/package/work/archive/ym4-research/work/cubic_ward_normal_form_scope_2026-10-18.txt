1. REMOVING THE COMPLETE QUADRATIC KERNEL GIVES A LEGWISE CUBIC WARD IDENTITY

Let W be a whole gauge-invariant rooted analytic function of ordinary
physical link logarithms. Write W=W(0)+W2+W3+..., where Wk is homogeneous
of degree k. Constant-color invariance gives W1=0. Let C(Q) denote the
existing nonlinear invariant completion of the FULL quadratic kernel
Q=W2, not merely its 21-dimensional local marginal projection.

For Z=W-W(0)-C(Q), Z0=Z1=Z2=0. An infinitesimal physical gauge action has

                  delta_lambda A=d lambda+B1(A,lambda)+O(A^2 lambda).

Differentiate DZ(A)[delta_lambda A]=0 and retain degree two in A. The
term involving B1 multiplies Z2 and is zero. Thus the symmetric trilinear
polarization Gamma=Z3 obeys

              Gamma(d lambda,A,B)=0                         (1)

for every admissible lattice gauge generator lambda and all A,B.
Polarization gives (1) in each slot separately. There is no use of field
equations or of a gauge condition here. Subtracting only a local Maxwell
coefficient would leave Z2 nonzero and would NOT imply (1).

For nonwrapping carriers, a gauge generator on the carrier and its fixed
collar can be cut off and made periodic outside the carrier. In particular
local constant potentials can be removed this way. A harmonic mode on a
finite torus cannot be removed by a global periodic linear gauge; (1)
does not declare a winding cubic kernel to be a local curvature monomial.

2. A FINITE AFFINE-CURVATURE PROBE AND THE TWENTY LOCAL REFERENCES

This paragraph specifies a projection without putting a globally affine
unbounded field into an analytic ball. Label the six oriented coordinate
two-planes by A=1,...,6. Choose an ordinary rooted cutoff radius R>=4,
smaller than one quarter of the torus side when the volume is finite.
Let a_R(F) be the LINEAR link field obtained from the midpoint affine
potential -1/2 F_(mu nu)(x_nu-z_nu), cut off outside a rooted radius 2R
and equal to that potential inside radius R. Fix the sign so its linear
plaquette flux on the reference plaquettes is F. Define

                   Pi3^R Gamma(F)=Gamma(a_R(F),a_R(F),a_R(F)).       (2)

Equation(2) evaluates a cubic polynomial, not the original function at
an arbitrarily large affine field. If the cubic link kernel has an
exponentially rooted summable coefficient bound, the three factors
|a_R(F)_e|<=C(1+dist(z,e))|F| cost only fixed carrier moments. The resulting
projection norm is uniform in R and volume. Two radii agree up to
C exp(-c min(R,R')) times the stronger input coefficient norm. On an
infinite lattice their limit is therefore the local affine-jet symbol.
On a finite torus the cutoff/tail qualification remains explicit.

Constant SU(2) color invariance says that every invariant cubic color
tensor is a multiple of epsilon_abc. As the six F_A commute as ordinary
coordinate variables, its coefficient in the plane labels must be
totally antisymmetric. Thus (2) has the unique expansion

             Pi3^R Gamma(F)=sum_(A<B<C) c_ABC det(F_A,F_B,F_C).       (3)

There are binomial(6,3)=20 coefficients. This is SU(2) invariant-tensor
algebra, not a claim of O(4) covariance of the constructed fields.

For each plane A choose a small oriented plaquette based at z. Its
holonomy V_A transforms by conjugation at z. Set
L_A=pi_g((V_A-V_A^-1)/(2i)), with the normalization already used for
quadratic completion. Choose the invariant color determinant so its
value on the orthonormal color basis is one, and put

                       T_ABC=det(L_A,L_B,L_C).                      (4)

Each T is exactly gauge invariant, has zero jets of orders zero, one,
and two, is analytic on a fixed geometric ball, and has cubic jet
det((dA)_A,(dA)_B,(dA)_C). Consequently Pi3^R(T3) is the corresponding
unit coordinate, for every allowed R. Its rooted norm is <=C b^3 and
its constant geometric-radius bound is fixed. These are local reference
