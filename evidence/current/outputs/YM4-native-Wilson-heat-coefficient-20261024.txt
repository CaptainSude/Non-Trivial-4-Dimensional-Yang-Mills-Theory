1. THE EXACT PLAQUETTE AND LEFT-RELATIVE CONTINUUM VERTICES

Introduce a physical mesh epsilon. At one plaquette use jet variables
a=A_mu, b=A_nu, c=nabla_(epsilon,mu)A_nu,
e=nabla_(epsilon,nu)A_mu. The EXACT plaquette is

 P_epsilon=exp(i epsilon a)exp(i epsilon b+i epsilon^2 c)
                 exp(-i epsilon a-i epsilon^2 e)exp(-i epsilon b).

P_0=I and its epsilon-linear coefficient is zero. Thus
F_epsilon=(P_epsilon-I)/epsilon^2 is jointly holomorphic at zero.
The identity P+P^-1-2=(P-I)^2 P^-1 gives the exact physical density

 L_epsilon=-(1/2)tr(F_epsilon^2 P_epsilon^-1),
 S_epsilon=epsilon^4 sum_(x,mu<nu)L_epsilon(J_epsilon A).

Direct expansion yields F_0=i(c-e)-[a,b] and
L_0=(1/2)tr(c-e+i[a,b])^2. On each fixed bounded complex jet
polydisc the difference L_epsilon-L_0 and every required fixed
field derivative are O(epsilon), by holomorphy and Cauchy.

The physical plane-wave difference symbol is
(exp(i epsilon k)-1)/epsilon=ik+O(epsilon), jointly analytically
on bounded momenta. Differentiating the exact density in amplitudes
therefore identifies the continuum action

 S_c(A)=(1/2) integral sum_(mu<nu) tr(F_munu(A)^2) dx,
 F_munu=partial_mu A_nu-partial_nu A_mu+i[A_mu,A_nu],

and the ordinary n-point vertices satisfy, n=2,3,4,

 Gamma_n^W(epsilon p_1,...,epsilon p_n)
       =epsilon^(4-n) Gamma_n^c(p_1,...,p_n)
                         +O(epsilon^(5-n)).               (3)

The estimates include the fixed external momentum derivatives below.
The bare quadratic fourth-order remainder is a MIDPOINT-coordinate
statement; source-site cross entries may have cubic phases. The FULL
flat vector Feynman symbol is exactly delta(k)I in either convention,
delta(k)=4 sum_mu sin^2(k_mu/2), and hence has fourth-order error.

For the left-relative chart,
Xi_(epsilon,A)(B)=log(exp(i epsilon B)exp(i epsilon A))/(i epsilon)
=A+B+O(epsilon). The Hessian is BOTH
S_coord''[Xi_B B,Xi_B C] and S_coord'[Xi_BB(B,C)]. The second
term is retained. The first relative background correction has
momentum order at least two from Gamma_2; the second has order at
least one from Gamma_3, or at least two from Gamma_2/current terms.
They stay within the errors in(3). The actual gauge generator obeys

 epsilon^-1 D_(epsilon A) -> partial+i ad_A.

Integration by parts in S_c gives the limiting scalar and vector
operators

 L_c=-D_c^2,
 (B_c)_munu=-D_c^2 delta_munu-2i ad_(F_munu).               (4)

The opposite consistent gauge-generator sign convention gives the
same heat traces. The flat coefficient of -partial^2 is ONE.

2. ENDPOINT-SAFE LATTICE/CONTINUUM COMPARISON

Use source-site Fourier conventions throughout. The flat lattice and
continuum symbols are delta(k)I and |k|^2 I. For rho=|k| in a fixed
small ball, their differences through r<=2 derivatives are
O(rho^(4-r)). Let V(k,p) be the first background vertex, mapping
quantum momentum k to k+p, and W(k;p,-p) the mixed second vertex.
Equation(3) and the retained relative chart terms give at p=0

 |partial_p^r(V_W-V_c)|<=C rho^(2-r),
 |partial_p^r(W_W-W_c)|<=C rho^(1-r), r<=2.                 (5)

The shifted return vertex V(k+p,-p) satisfies the same bound when
BOTH arguments are differentiated. The rho^-1 second derivative
bound in the second line is deliberately loose; the actual lattice
derivative is analytic and bounded. Baselines obey the sufficient
bounds |partial^r V|<=C rho^(1-r), |partial^r W|<=C rho^-r.

The exact total heat Hessian for compact sources is

 H_ab(s,p)=-s integral tr(exp(-sB(k))W_ab(k;p,-p)) dk/(2pi)^4
  +s^2 integral_0^1 integral tr(exp(-s(1-v)B(k+p)) V_a(k,p)
                  exp(-svB(k)) V_b(k+p,-p)) dk/(2pi)^4 dv. (6)

There is no extra factor2. This follows by differentiating the
first total trace variation; only total-trace cyclicity is used.
Lattice integrals are on the Brillouin zone, continuum integrals
on R^4. Positive-time Gaussian smoothing justifies the continuum
compact-source differential insertions. Equivalently(6) defines
their absolutely convergent momentum kernel directly.

For E(t,k)=exp(-tB(k)), direct derivatives or Duhamel give

 |partial_k^r E|<=C rho^-r(1+t rho^2)^r exp(-ct rho^2),
 |partial_k^r(E_W-E_c)|
       <=C t rho^(4-r)(1+t rho^2)^r exp(-ct rho^2).          (7)

These have no inverse heat-time singularity and hold at t=0.
After two external derivatives of(6), a tadpole vertex difference
is bounded by C rho^-1 exp(-cs rho^2). Its four-dimensional integral
times s is O(s^-1/2). The heat-difference tadpole term has
C s rho^4 exp(-cs rho^2) before its outer s and is O(s^-2).

In the bubble, telescoping the four factors replaces either a vertex
or a heat factor. A vertex replacement has degree2+1 before the
two derivatives, hence bound C rho P(s rho^2)exp(-cs rho^2).
Its integral times s^2 is O(s^-1/2). A heat replacement yields
C s rho^4 P(s rho^2)exp(-cs rho^2) after differentiation, whose
integral times s^2 is O(s^-1). The two heat times add to s, so these
bounds are uniform over v in[0,1], including its endpoints.
Outside the small ball the lattice contribution is exponentially
small in s; the continuum contribution and tail are likewise
controlled by polynomial vertices times a Gaussian. Therefore

            |partial_p^2(H_W-H_c)(s,0)|<=C s^-1/2.         (8)

The lattice diagonal heat density is individually gauge invariant,
and its first color jet is zero. The exact rooted density/moment
identity at each fixed s gives

              h_W(s)=(1/4)partial_(p_nu)^2 H_aa(s,0),
                                    a=alpha H.            (9)

Continuum kernels may contain contact distributions. No absolute
continuum spatial-moment identity is asserted; the next explicit
transverse diagram verifies the same quarter factor there directly.

3. CONTINUUM DENSITY AND A DIRECT TRANSVERSE CHECK

Let q range over all eigenvalues of ad_H in the complexified adjoint
space, with both signs and their multiplicities. For F_12=fH, the
scalar heat density of one charge is

             k_q(s;f)=(4pi s)^-2 (qfs)/sinh(qfs).          (10)

Indeed Fourier transformation in one magnetic direction gives the
oscillator levels |qf|(2n+1); the degeneracy per area is |qf|/(2pi).
Their geometric sum and the two free heat directions give(10).
It is regular at qf=0. The spin matrix in(4) has eigenvalues
0,0,2qf,-2qf. Thus its vector density is

                     k_q(s;f)[2+2cosh(2qfs)].              (11)

Using x/sinh x=1-x^2/6+O(x^4) and
2+2cosh(2x)=4+4x^2+O(x^4), one obtains

 [f^2]k_q=-q^2/(96pi^2),
 [f^2](vector density)=10q^2/(48pi^2).                    (12)

Here is an independent check of the coefficient relevant to(9),
without a continuum spatial-moment assumption. Take an external
alpha-potential with momentum p=p_nu e_nu, alpha!=nu. The scalar
first vertex is 2q k_alpha and the second is 2q^2, the latter
independent of p. The bubble in(6) is exactly

 2q^2 s (4pi s)^-2 integral_0^1 exp(-sv(1-v)p^2) dv.

Its second p derivative is -q^2/(24pi^2), since
integral_0^1 v(1-v)dv=1/6. Multiplication by1/4 gives the scalar
value in(12). The vector orbital term is four copies of this
scalar term. Its two spin vertices have Lorentz trace product
8q^2 p^2. Their bubble contributes q^2/(4pi^2) after the same
quarter derivative, yielding the vector value in(12). Mixed
spin/orbital terms vanish by the zero Lorentz trace of one spin
matrix. Thus the diagram derivative and magnetic density agree.

Complexification preserves the trace of the original real adjoint
operator; summing both charge signs is already that trace and does
not warrant another factor2. Summing(12) therefore gives continuum
h_s=-alpha_g/2 and h_v=10alpha_g. Equations(8)--(9) prove(2).

For SU(N), write H=diag(h_i), sum h_i=0. In normalized trace
tr(H^2)=1 means sum h_i^2=N. The adjoint charges are h_i-h_j,
including zero diagonal directions, and
sum_(i,j)(h_i-h_j)^2=2N sum_i h_i^2=2N^2.
In particular C_H=8 for SU(2), positive for the specified nonzero H.
