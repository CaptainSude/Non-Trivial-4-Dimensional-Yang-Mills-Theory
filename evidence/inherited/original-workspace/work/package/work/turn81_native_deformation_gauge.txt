2. AN ACTUAL ORIGINAL-LAW Lp BUDGET

  E(beta h_p)^r <= 4^r r! 2^(3/4),
  ||F||_r <= C_r W_f,
  C_r=4(r!2^(3/4))^(1/r)<=4r 2^(3/(4r)).                    (2.1)
The first inequality is x^r<=r!4^r exp(x/4); the second is Minkowski.
No conditional exterior-energy inequality is used. Expectations on the
augmented law equal these original expectations for original functions.

Let G_j be a decreasing sequence of complete retained sigma-algebras,
G_0 containing the original field, formed from the tail of the positive

Discarding needed choice/record labels is not part of this assertion.

and mixed logarithmic jet Z_j=V_j-X_jY_j. Then

  ||V_j||_r<=C_(2r)^2 W_f W_k,

These follow from conditional Jensen, Holder and (2.1). The normalized

3. DEPTH-INDEPENDENT MIXED INNOVATION BUDGET

Put d_j^F=X_j-X_(j+1), d_j^H=Y_j-Y_(j+1). Reverse-martingale
orthogonality gives
  sum_(j<n) E|d_j^F|^2=Var(F)-Var(X_n)<=Var(F),               (3.1)
and similarly for H. The raw/log recursion is exactly
  Z_(j+1)=E(Z_j|G_(j+1))
                   +E(d_j^F d_j^H|G_(j+1)), Z_0=0.         (3.2)
Indeed expand V_j=Z_j+X_jY_j and take the next conditional expectation;
both single d terms vanish. Hence
  Cov(F,H)=sum_(j<n) E(d_j^F d_j^H)+Cov(X_n,Y_n).            (3.3)
By Cauchy--Schwarz in the joint (date,probability) space,
  sum_(j<n) E|d_j^F d_j^H|
     <=sqrt[Var(F)Var(H)]
     <=32*2^(3/4) W_f W_k.                                 (3.4)
The terminal term is retained, not included in the sum and then forgotten.
Eq(3.1) follows either by telescoping conditional Pythagoras or by
orthogonality of distinct innovations. Thus the real full source jets do
not intrinsically grow by a product of inverse analytic radii with depth.
Those products in analytic estimates measure the strength of the target
norm/domain, not growth of these actual real random variables.

This advances the precise real consumer while leaving the spatial issue
exposed. For separated source supports there is no distance in (3.4).
The full covariance can be carried by Cov(X_n,Y_n); an identity for the
innovation sum cannot set it to zero. Neither the upper bound nor source
entireness yields a positive lower bound for a reflection norm.

Physical normalization is substantial. For a fixed physical support and
f_p a sampled O(1) profile, W_f is O(a^-4), so (3.4) is O(a^-8) for two
such sources. Multiplying f_p by a^4 makes this particular estimate
bounded, but changes the observable by a^4; it is not a renormalization
theorem for the canonical integrated action. Subtracting deterministic
vacua leaves (3.1)--(3.4)'s covariances unchanged and does not create a
spatial improvement. TURN70's selected martingale identity and TURN74's
four-jet recursion already supply the algebra; the present additional
consumer is the genuine full-Wilson Lp/squared-sum budget with its exact
normalization and limits.

4. SOURCE-RETUNED SADDLES: THE ENVELOPE THEOREM DOES NOT KILL TWO MARKS

Fix an actual finite Wilson conditional with its exterior links and coarse
constraint held fixed. Work on a smooth constrained physical gauge slice
near a stable minimum U*, removing gauge-null directions. Let
  S0(U)=sum_(p touching free links) h_p(U),
  S_f(U)=sum_p f_p h_p(U), S_k similarly.
All action plaquettes touching these variables, including crossing
plaquettes, are included. Suppose U* is a nondegenerate constrained
minimum of S0, with physical Hessian A>=kappa Id>0. This hypothesis
is local to this admitted saddle; no uniform all-history gap is inferred.
Use local intrinsic coordinates on the fixed constraint manifold.

Let U(s,t) be the nearby constrained stationary point of
S0+s S_f+t S_k and m(s,t)=beta[S0+s S_f+t S_k](U(s,t)).
Differentiating stationarity gives
  U_s=-A^-1 grad S_f, U_t=-A^-1 grad S_k at zero,
  m_s=beta S_f(U*),
  m_st=-beta <grad S_f,A^-1 grad S_k>.                      (4.1)
The first equality for m_s is the envelope theorem. The final term is
the mandatory response of the constrained minimum, not an error removable
by gauge invariance. In particular m_ss<=0. The gradients are physical
tangent gradients; pure gauge directions vanish for every f, but the
physical gradient generally does not.

For any constants c_f,c_k, constrained stationarity gives EXACTLY
  grad S_f=sum_p(f_p-c_f)grad h_p,                          (4.2)
and similarly for k. Thus complete constant profiles have zero saddle
motion, while varying profiles have a controlled oscillation response.
This cancellation fails for an arbitrarily masked partial action unless
the missing crossing terms are also retained.

Here is a concrete Wilson bound with explicit normalization. Give each
SU(2) link the orthonormal left generators i sigma_a/2. On a link in one
nondegenerate plaquette,
  |grad_link h_p|^2=h_p(2-h_p)/4.
A link belongs to at most z=2(d-1) action plaquettes. Cauchy on each link,
summation over the four distinct edges and orthogonal tangent projection
therefore give
  |grad S_f|^2<=2z sum_p(f_p-c_f)^2 h_p.                   (4.3)
In d=4, 2z=12. Combining (4.1)--(4.3),
  |m_st|<=12 beta/kappa
       [sum_p(f_p-c_f)^2 h_p(U*)]^(1/2)
       [sum_p(k_p-c_k)^2 h_p(U*)]^(1/2).                  (4.4)
If the COMPLETE constrained-action plaquette set lies in a region of
physical diameter ell and both profiles are Lipschitz there, choose
constants at one point. This gives the product ell^2 Lip(f)Lip(k),
times 12 beta S0(U*)/kappa. A small source support alone is not that
diameter hypothesis after global constant subtraction. This is an
actual finite conditional classical
estimate at its stated saddle, including adaptive fixed exterior data.
It is not a cutoff-uniform bound on kappa^-1 or the minimum's action,
and it does not control determinant/fluctuation/source-scalar terms.

It refines the proposed deformation test without repeating an off-pair
rank assertion: every object in(4.1) is on the physical constrained slice.
The native soft-gauge precision is not inserted into S_f. A sourced
gauge-fixed parametrization may compute the same answer but must retain
its Jacobian and gauge/constraint normalization. For full variable
couplings or a different reference action, their actual Hessian replaces
A; it cannot be identified with a homogeneous Gaussian by notation.

4A. SEPARATED PROFILES: THE PRECISE CONSTRAINED-INVERSE INPUT

Let P be the orthogonal projection from ambient free-link tangent vectors
onto the physical constrained tangent at U*, and extend the inverse by
  G=P A^-1 P.
Its link-to-link entries are 3-by-3 matrices in orthonormal link frames.
For the ambient force g_f(e)=sum_(p containing e) f_p grad_e h_p,
  m_st=-beta <g_f,G g_k>.                                  (4.5)
The ambient force retains its literal source support. Applying P first
and then declaring its result local would in general be incorrect.

Suppose this ACTUAL constrained inverse, on the complete determining
row geometry, has
  ||G(e,e')||op<=C_G exp[-mu d(e,e')],
  B_(mu/2)=sup_e sum_e' exp[-mu d(e,e')/2]<infinity,          (4.6)
with the intended volume-uniform constants. Assume d is symmetric.
For D=dist(supp g_f,supp g_k), entrywise splitting of the exponential
and the row/column Schur bound yield
  |m_st|<=beta C_G B_(mu/2) exp[-mu D/2] ||g_f||2 ||g_k||2
    <=12 beta C_G B_(mu/2) exp[-mu D/2]
          [sum_p f_p^2 h_p]^(1/2)[sum_p k_p^2 h_p]^(1/2).  (4.7)
Indeed every pair in the restricted kernel has d>=D, leaving the
summable exp[-mu d/2] after taking out exp[-mu D/2]. The same Cauchy
calculation as(4.3), without the tangent projection, gives the second
line. No translation invariance or equality of boundary conditions is
required. Empty force support gives zero directly.

The support in(4.7) is the complete force support. For a pulled-back
original source it includes its reconstructed field/frame/constraint

One may use the better of(4.4) and(4.7). One may not subtract a global

4B. BLOCKWISE CONSTANTS LEAVE AN EXACT CONSTRAINT-ROW RESPONSE

There is a useful physical-saddle identity making the boundary issue
explicit. Write Q for the derivative of the actual defining constraints,
including any slice constraints needed so that the tangent is ker Q.
At a regular constrained point, stationarity gives
  g_1=grad_ambient S0=Q*lambda,
and G Q*=0. Redundant rows may be removed, or any multiplier solving
this equality may be used. All rows and their complete footprints remain.

Choose arbitrary scalar link values c_e and constraint-row values d_a;
let C,D be their diagonal multiplication operators. Define
  r_f(e)=sum_(p containing e)(f_p-c_e) grad_e h_p,
  b_f=r_f+(C Q*-Q*D)lambda.                                (4.8)
Then the exact equalities
  g_f=C g_1+r_f=Q*D lambda+b_f,
  G g_f=G b_f,
  m_st=-beta <b_f,G b_k>                                  (4.9)
follow, with separate choices C,D for each profile. The row contribution
at (e,a) in(4.8) is (c_e-d_a)Q*_(e,a)lambda_a. It is not legitimate to
drop it because a profile is constant on a proper interior subregion.

For a complete constant source, choose all c_e=d_a equal to that same
constant. Both r_f and the commutator vanish, recovering(4.2). For a
piecewise constant source, r_f is supported where an action plaquette
and its edge weight differ, and the second term is supported on every
actual constraint-row footprint where c_e differs from its row value.
If a constraint row is extended, that entire footprint is present; no
automatic thin-boundary assertion follows. The multiplier is the actual
physical constraint current and is retained. The estimate leading to
(4.7) also applies to b_f,b_k if their complete supports and norms are
controlled, but does not supply these controls by itself.

This is a finite classical admission/response calculation, not a native
off-pair representative substitution. TURN69's regular_source_transport
and TURN74 regular_source_gauge Section3 already retain the varying-
profile first noise jet and its physical row-sum cancellation; TURN70
invariant_source_relocalization Section9 also retains weighted constraint
commutators. The additional statement here is the exact moving-minimum
susceptibility(4.1), its quantitative Wilson bound, and the explicit
physical inverse/complete-row input needed to make it spatially local.
It is not a new proof of the older constant-source Ward cancellation.

5. MOVING CUTS AND MOVING REFERENCES: WHAT CANCELS EXACTLY

Let chi_h(s,t;U) be a real source-dependent partition of unity of the
ORIGINAL integrand. For plus sources,
  partial_st[chi_h(s,t;U) exp(sF+tH)] at zero
     =chi_h FH+(partial_s chi_h)H+(partial_t chi_h)F
                                      +partial_st chi_h.    (5.1)
Summing over the COMPLETE h family kills the last three contributions,
since sum_h chi_h=1 pointwise and F,H do not depend on the artificial
label. It does not kill them branchwise or in an incomplete regular/raw
subfamily. Hard characteristic cuts require distributional boundary
terms or an equivalent exact domain calculation. Rounded hierarchy
parameters need not even depend differentiably on the source.

A source-dependent coordinate change likewise has a Jacobian/divergence
response; a branch with boundary has its corresponding flux. Exact
integration on the complete original compact group cancels artificial
coordinate dependence, but a localized estimate must exhibit the same
cancellation with its supports. The envelope theorem for the minimum
does not cancel these terms or the mixed term(4.1).

If a reference q_(s,t)(dy) is normalized for every source,
  integral q_(s,t)=1,
then its first and mixed derivatives integrate to zero when no y-dependent
future factor is present. A later y-dependent history predicate or source
read prevents pulling that zero through the integral. Its score and
mixed score must then stay in the actual branch formula. Freezing q and
the cuts avoids these artificial derivatives entirely, but leaves the
original old conditional source; it does not turn it into a current
reference-field observable.
