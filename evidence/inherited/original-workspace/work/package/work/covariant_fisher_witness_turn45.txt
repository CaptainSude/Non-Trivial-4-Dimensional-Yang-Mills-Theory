COVARIANT FISHER DIRECTIONS IN THE ORIGINAL WILSON LAW -- TURN45
19 October 2026. Exact finite-lattice results; no continuum existence claim.

0. RESULT AND ITS LIMIT

The deterministic Lie-direction obstruction in rotation_route_turn44,
Section 9 can be bypassed by a field-dependent gauge-covariant vector
field. The exact Fisher identity then has a nonzero numerator. In
particular, for the SU(2) Wilson law on any finite nondegenerate
four-dimensional hypercubic lattice, every elementary plaquette with
at least one integrated edge obeys

       Var_mu(h_p) >= c/(1+beta)^2,                         (0.1)

where h_p=1-(1/2)Re Tr U_p, dmu=Z^-1 exp(-beta sum_p h_p)dHaar,
and c>0 is independent of volume, boundary data and beta>=0. This is
an estimate in the ORIGINAL compact Wilson law, including large fields.
The statement applies to free, fixed, and ordinary periodic boundaries
provided the varied edge is integrated and occurs once in each incident
elementary plaquette. Fully fixed boundary plaquettes are excluded.

However, a strictly local Fisher score whose complete read support lies
on one side of a reflection is exactly orthogonal to every observable
on the other side. In particular its own reflected norm is ZERO. Thus
nonzero ordinary variance obtained this way does not itself certify a
nonzero physical state after Osterwalder--Schrader reconstruction.

Sections 1--3 prove (0.1) by a covariant direction. Sections 4--5 isolate
the exact reflected-null obstruction and the normalization/contact cost.
No RG source theorem, gauge fixing, small-field cutoff or convexity
assumption is used in these finite-volume results.

1. EXACT COVARIANT FISHER IDENTITY

Let M=G^E with product bi-invariant Riemannian metric and normalized Haar
volume, where G is compact and connected. Put dmu=Z^-1 exp(-beta S)dvol,
with smooth real S. For any smooth real vector field V on M define

       sigma_V = beta V S - div V.                         (1.1)

Integration of div(F V exp(-beta S)) on the compact manifold gives

       E_mu[V F] = E_mu[F sigma_V],     E_mu sigma_V=0.     (1.2)

Consequently, when E sigma_V^2>0,

       Var_mu(F) >= |E_mu V F|^2 / E_mu sigma_V^2.          (1.3)

For V=grad F, the numerator is (E|grad F|^2)^2. If both S and F are
gauge invariant, then grad F is gauge covariant because gauge actions
are isometries of the product bi-invariant metric. The score is gauge
invariant. Local gauge symmetry no longer forces E V F to vanish: it is
nonnegative and strictly positive unless F is constant on every varied
connected component almost surely.

One can use only a subset of the links: take V=grad_I F in the product
of their G factors, holding all other variables fixed. Its divergence,
Laplacian and score use exactly those factors. The full-law identity
(1.2) remains valid by conditional integration by parts.

For the full gradient, let L=Delta-beta grad S dot grad. Then
sigma_grad F=-L F. The integrated Bochner identity is

 E[(L F)^2] = E[|Hess F|^2
                   +(Ric+beta Hess S)(grad F,grad F)].    (1.4)

To verify the sign, use
 (1/2)L|grad F|^2-grad F dot grad(LF)
 =|Hess F|^2+(Ric+beta Hess S)(grad F,grad F),
integrate L|grad F|^2 to zero, and integrate the remaining gradient
pairing by parts. Formula (1.4) does NOT assert Hess S>=0. Negative
curvature of the action is retained. It is an exact identity, not a
volume-uniform lower-curvature assumption.

2. THE CONDITIONAL SU(2) LAW AND A UNIFORM GRADIENT RESPONSE

Identify SU(2) with the unit round sphere S^3 of quaternions q. Normalized
Haar is normalized round volume. Fix an unoriented edge variable U_e=q
and condition on all other links. Every incident Wilson plaquette is

                    h_r=1-a_r dot q,

where a_r is a unit vector in R^4 determined by the other links and by
orientation. The inverse of q is an orthogonal linear operation, so the
formula also holds when the plaquette traverses e in the reverse direction.
There are at most m=2(d-1)=6 such plaquettes in dimension four. Thus

 dmu(q | other links) = Z_b^-1 exp(beta b dot q)domega(q),
       b=sum_{r containing e}a_r,       |b|<=6.             (2.1)

For the chosen plaquette p write a=a_p and x=a dot q, so h_p=1-x.
If b is nonzero write n=b/|b|, kappa=beta|b|, t=n dot q and
alpha=a dot n. For b=0 choose n arbitrarily and put kappa=0.
The marginal density of t on [-1,1] is proportional to

                    exp(kappa t)(1-t^2)^(1/2).             (2.2)

Claim: with r=1+kappa,

              E_b(1-t^2) >= c/r.                         (2.3)

For 0<=kappa<=2 the conditional density relative to Haar is bounded
below by exp(-4), so (2.3) follows from the positive Haar expectation.
For kappa>=2 put u=1-t. The normalizer is at most

 exp(kappa) sqrt(2) int_0^infinity exp(-kappa u)u^(1/2)du
             = C exp(kappa)kappa^(-3/2).

On u in [1/(2kappa),1/kappa], the unnormalized mass is at least
c exp(kappa)kappa^(-3/2): exp(-kappa u)>=exp(-1), and
sqrt(2u-u^2)>=sqrt(u). Therefore this band has probability at least c.
On it 1-t^2=2u-u^2>=u>=1/(2kappa), proving (2.3).

By transverse rotational symmetry, if s is any unit transverse
coordinate then E_b s=E_b(ts)=0 and
E_b s^2=(1/3)E_b(1-t^2). Write x=alpha t+sqrt(1-alpha^2)s.
Since grad h_p=-a+xq on S^3,

 N_b := E_b|grad_e h_p|^2 = E_b(1-x^2)
      = alpha^2 E_b(1-t^2)
           +(1-alpha^2)[1-(1/3)E_b(1-t^2)]
      >= c[alpha^2/r+(1-alpha^2)] >= c/r.                 (2.4)

This is the nonzero covariant Fisher response. It holds for every
conditioned exterior, even a large-field exterior or a vanishing staple b.

3. A SHARP-POWER FINITE-WILSON VARIANCE LOWER BOUND

Use V=grad_e h_p, varying only the chosen edge. On the unit round S^3,

 Delta h_p=3x,        Hess h_p=x g_S3,       Ric=2g_S3,
 S(q | other links)=constant-b dot q,
 Hess_e S=(b dot q)g_S3.

The exact score is therefore

 sigma = beta[a dot b-(a dot q)(b dot q)]-3(a dot q).     (3.1)

It is the same globally gauge-invariant scalar obtained from (1.1).
Conditional Bochner gives

 D_b := E_b sigma^2
       = E_b[3x^2+(2+kappa t)(1-x^2)]
       <= 3+(2+kappa)N_b
       <= C(1+kappa)N_b.                                (3.2)

The final inequality uses (2.4), so 3<=C(1+kappa)N_b.
In (3.2) the possibly negative kappa t term was only bounded above;
no assertion of positive Hessian of the Wilson action was needed.
Conditional Fisher gives

 Var_b(h_p) >= N_b^2/D_b
             >= c N_b/(1+kappa)
             >= c/(1+kappa)^2
             >= c/(1+6beta)^2.                           (3.3)

The law of total variance proves (0.1), with a changed absolute constant.
Alternatively, the SAME unconditional vector field proves it directly:
N=E N_b>=c/(1+6beta), D=E D_b<=C(1+6beta)N, and (1.3) gives
Var_mu(h_p)>=c/(1+6beta)^2.

The inverse-square beta power is sharp for this uniform conditional
statement. If b is a fixed positive multiple of a and kappa tends to
infinity, u=1-a dot q has density proportional to
exp(-kappa u)u^(1/2)(2-u)^(1/2), 0<=u<=2. After z=kappa u,
dominated convergence of moments gives z -> Gamma(shape=3/2,rate=1).
Hence Var_b(h_p)=(3/2+o(1))/kappa^2. This sharpness example describes
a conditional fiber and does not claim it is a typical full-law exterior.

4. EXACT CONTACT ORTHOGONALITY AND REFLECTED NULLNESS

The variance bound above is not a separated-correlation lower bound.
There is a stronger structural reason than simply a weak estimate.

For ANY vector field V differentiating only a set of link variables I,
and any smooth H independent of those variables, (1.2) gives

                   E_mu[sigma_V H]=E_mu[V H]=0.           (4.1)

This remains true even when the coefficients of V depend on links
outside I; only differentiating H matters. It is an equation-of-motion
or contact identity in the original finite Wilson law.

Now choose a reflection theta and suppose V and the complete read
support of sigma_V lie strictly in the positive half. This is possible
for V=grad_e h_p if p and every action plaquette meeting e lie far
enough from the reflection plane. Then Theta sigma_V depends only on
negative-half links, hence on none of I. Equation (4.1) yields

                E_mu[(Theta sigma_V)sigma_V]=0.           (4.2)

More generally E_mu[(Theta F)sigma_V]=0 for every positive-half F.
When the law is reflection positive, (4.2) says sigma_V is exactly an
OS-null vector. In particular (1.2) may have E[V F]>0 and
E[F sigma_V]>0 while its reflected analogue has zero right hand side.

This is not an impossibility theorem for every Fisher-based continuum
argument. A direction whose support reaches the separating surface,
a global constrained direction, or a comparison that links an ordinary
variance to a positive separated spectral component may avoid this
particular nullness. But each requires an additional construction and
estimate. A local score lower bound alone cannot be substituted for
the needed positive reflected witness.

This obstruction is separate from, and consistent with, TURN44's
Gaussian cross-scale cancellation example. That example shows why a
positive one-step separated coefficient need not survive summing all
scales. The present identity shows why a positive unreflected local
Fisher response can live entirely in a reflected-null direction.

5. THE ULTRAVIOLET CONTACT PRICE AND SOURCE NORMALIZATION

Centering h_p changes neither (0.1) nor its Dirichlet response. If a
prescribed physical observable is O_{p,a}=z_a(h_p-E h_p), then

             Var(O_{p,a}) >= c z_a^2/(1+beta_a)^2.         (5.1)

The estimate does not determine z_a. Choosing a cutoff-dependent
normalization by demanding variance one is circular if the purpose is
to identify the prescribed Yang--Mills observable and its source.
Moreover, single-plaquette variance does not control covariance between
two regions at a fixed positive physical separation.

One can expose the contact-volume cost without assumptions on signs
of arbitrary covariances. Choose plaquettes p_i with designated edges
e_i so that (a) no Wilson action plaquette contains two e_i, and
(b) p_i contains e_i but no other designated edge. A fixed-orientation
plaquette at bases in 4Z^4, with its same oriented first edge designated,
is one such choice on a large non-wrapping box. Conditional on all
other links, the e_i laws are independent and each h_{p_i} depends
on only its designated variable. Thus for deterministic real c_i,

 Var_mu(sum_i c_i h_{p_i})
   >= E Var(sum_i c_i h_{p_i} | other links)
   = sum_i c_i^2 E Var(h_{p_i} | other links)
   >= c(1+beta)^(-2)sum_i c_i^2.                         (5.2)

This is a sparse observable family, not an assertion for every full
plaquette sum: in a full sum several plaquettes can involve one
designated link and their linear coefficients can combine or cancel.

On a fixed physical four-volume with mesh a, this sparse family has
order a^-4 plaquettes. For the smeared discretization

           X_a(f)=a^4 z_a sum_i f(x_i)(h_{p_i}-E h_{p_i}),

(5.2) gives, for a smooth nonzero f in a fixed interior region,

             Var X_a(f) >= c z_a^2 a^4/(1+beta_a)^2.      (5.3)

For the weak-field engineering choice z_a=beta_a a^-4 and beta_a>=1,
this is at least c a^-4. Accordingly this sparse local approximation
cannot have uniform L2 bounds under that choice. Diverging variances
alone do NOT prove failure of tightness without further moment/control
assumptions, and (5.3) is not a contradiction to a renormalized
off-diagonal Schwinger-function or OS construction. It records the
contact fluctuations that such a construction must treat correctly.

At the alternative noise normalization z_a=beta_a a^-2, the lower
bound is merely order one. Such a choice can retain ultraviolet noise;
it does not identify a Yang--Mills continuum field. In general a
positive ordinary variance can be carried by contact terms. A
nontrivial physical witness needs positive correlation across a fixed
positive separation, or another precise argument excluding a pure
contact/OS-null contribution.

No exact finite Wilson identity here fixes the running source
normalization. The needed all-scale source representation, local
remainder bounds, and nonzero separated physical kernel remain open.

6. A BOUNDARY FISHER ROUTE AND THE PROPAGATION ESTIMATE IT REQUIRES

There is an exact way to turn Fisher into a reflected-witness problem,
but its numerator is then a genuine propagation correlation.

Take a reflection-symmetric finite box with a fixed mirror layer B of
links, positive variables X, and their reflected negative copy Y. Assume
the literal action splitting

         S(B,X,Y)=S_0(B)+S_+(B,X)+S_+(B,theta Y).

For example a site reflection of a free-boundary hypercubic box has
this splitting when B contains the links lying in the mirror layer.
Write Z_+(B)=int exp(-beta S_+(B,X))dX and let nu_B be the normalized
positive-half conditional law. The actual boundary law is

         dr(B)=Z^-1 exp(-beta S_0(B))Z_+(B)^2 dHaar(B).

For a real positive-half observable F define m_F(B)=E_nuB F. Conditional
independence of the two halves gives the exact reflected covariance

 E_mu[(Theta(F-E_mu F))(F-E_mu F)] = Var_r(m_F).         (6.1)

For any boundary vector field W, its score under r is

 sigma_W^r = beta W S_0-2W log Z_+-div_B W
           = beta W S_0+2beta E_nuB(W S_+)-div_B W.      (6.2)

Hence (1.3), now on the boundary manifold, gives

 reflected covariance >= |E_r W m_F|^2/E_r(sigma_W^r)^2. (6.3)

For F with no explicit dependence on the boundary links,

        W m_F(B) = -beta Cov_nuB(F,W S_+).               (6.4)

If F does depend on B, add E_nuB(W F) to the right side. Differentiating
the EXACT normalized conditional law proves (6.4), including its
normalizer term. All vector-field coefficients are held at their
boundary values while differentiating in each tangent direction.

Thus a boundary Fisher proof would require both a lower bound on the
averaged force-observable covariance in (6.4), reaching from the mirror
to the fixed physical support of F, and an upper bound on (6.2). The
single-link estimate of Sections 2--3 provides neither. Choosing
W=grad_B m_F makes the response positive but presupposes quantitative
control of the very boundary response to be proved. Defining W by an
inverse covariance or normalizing it by that unknown response does
not establish the required bounds.

Equation (6.3) is a concrete legitimate alternative to the OS-null local
score, and it specifies exactly where the all-scale propagation input
returns. No lower bound for (6.4) at a fixed nonzero physical distance
has been established here.

7. AUDIT CHECKLIST AND STATUS

* V=grad_e h_p is field dependent and gauge covariant; the proof does
  not reuse a deterministic Lie direction forbidden by local symmetry.
* The Haar divergence is retained, producing the essential -3x term.
* The single-link conditional law is exact, not a weak-coupling Gaussian.
* The beta^-2 lower bound is uniform over ALL exterior fields and volumes.
* Round-S^3 metric conventions are used consistently; rescaling the
  bi-invariant metric rescales numerator and denominator compatibly.
* No sign is assigned to separated plaquette covariances.
* The complete read support of the score, including all action staples,
  must lie in the positive half before asserting the OS-null conclusion.
* The sparse-array estimate is not promoted to an arbitrary full-source
  sum or to a continuum tightness theorem.

These are elementary derived identities. They require no unverified
import from the newest QED3 paper. They establish a real original-law
local variance result while specifying why it does not finish the
nontrivial four-dimensional Yang--Mills existence proof.
