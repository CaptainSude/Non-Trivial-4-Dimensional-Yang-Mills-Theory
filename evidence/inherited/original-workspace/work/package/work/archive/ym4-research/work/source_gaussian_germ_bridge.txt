MAIN FINDING

There is a substantially narrower bridge than full finite-g intertwining.
Choose the same primitive ratio, mean, contour family and Wilson convention.
The constrained CLASSICAL actions then agree as finite-regulator germs by
the source's uniqueness of the regular critical orbit. The normalized
Gaussian leading amplitudes agree by the common finite constrained Laplace
integral. A different admissible real window does not change that amplitude.

This matches the complete Gaussian correction, including its pivot/coarea
factor. It does NOT identify the positive-g recursion or its non-Gaussian
remainder. The source's own remainder theorem, with its precise parameter
order and fixed-domain extraction, is the remaining scalar interface.

1. MATCH THE ACTUAL PRIMITIVE MAP, NOT JUST ITS DIFFERENTIAL

Fix an odd L satisfying the source's size restrictions (CMP109 uses L>11).
Set our primitive ell=L and, for this comparison only, s=1 and R=L.
Use identical centered blocks, positively oriented independent bonds,
coordinate-order shortest paths with their multiplicities, reference
center strings, logarithm branch, and one identical admissible group mean.

Our Notebook27 equations cmg-contour and cmg-ordinary are literally the
choice CMP109 (0.11),(0.12), pp253--254:

 P_L(V;y,x)=M({V(Gamma):Gamma in G_L(y,x)}),
 U_x=P_L(V;y,x) V([x,x+Le_mu]) P_L(V;y+Le_mu,x+Le_mu)^(-1),
 F_L(V)(c)=exp[L^(-d) sum_(x in B_L(y)) log(U_x R_c^(-1))] R_c.

Choose the local mean by the same implicit equation (0.10), or use the
same other admissible mean on both sides. CMP109 permits that choice.
With these choices there is NO nonlinear averaging mismatch: F_L is
identical, hence its j-fold iterate is identical. This would not be true
merely from matching its linearized average, and would not identify a
direct L^q average with F_L^q. Our long-ratio map is the latter.

The source action (0.2), p252, uses normalized trace tr(1)=1. Match one
unoriented plaquette per square and the coupling outside the action. In
d=4 its mesh prefactor is 1. Counting-coordinate changes are fixed linear
changes whose determinant factors must be retained, then cancel in the
identity-normalized amplitude. The quadratic form, not a matrix written
with an unmatched inner product, is the object being compared.

2. THE CLASSICAL ACTION IDENTITY HAS A SHORT PROOF

Fix a finite compatible periodic regulator and old refinement M=L^k.
Let U_O(V) be our Notebook15 ordinary constrained minimizer and define
S_M^O(V)=A(U_O(V)). Let S_M^B(V)=A(U_B(V)) be the source homogeneous
minimum action for exactly the same iterated average and Wilson action.

Use as an explicit published input the source variational theorem:
the regular constrained problem has one critical orbit, which is its
minimum orbit. CMP109 (1.1), p260, restates this and refers to Theorem1
of Balaban, CMP102 (1985), 277--309, DOI10.1007/BF01229381.

Claim: S_M^O=S_M^B as real analytic germs at V=1 on this finite regulator.

Proof. Our U_O(V) is analytic, U_O(1)=1, and satisfies the same ordinary
constraint exactly. At any fixed finite regulator, all of the source's
regularity tests (including scaled current tests) are continuous functions
of finitely many link variables in their logarithm charts. They vanish at
the identity. Thus after shrinking V's real neighborhood, U_O(V) belongs
to the source's strict regularity domain. No uniform-in-M radius is claimed.

Our minimum lies in the INTERIOR of its physical potential comparison
ball. Consequently its derivative vanishes in every tangent direction
to the same finite-dimensional constraint fiber: a sufficiently small
variation in any fixed finite tangent direction remains in that ball.
It is therefore a regular constrained critical point in the source sense.
The source uniqueness theorem identifies its critical orbit with U_B(V).
Gauge invariance of A gives equality of action values. Both action germs
are analytic, so equality on the real neighborhood identifies their
holomorphic germs and all derivatives at the identity. QED.

Equivalent proof: shrink until each minimum is an admissible competitor
for the other's minimum problem, then compare the two action values in
both directions. The critical-orbit proof above needs fewer representative
choices and makes the role of the source regularity domain explicit.

This does not prove uniform source regularity for our minimizer on its
entire common ball. Such a result is unnecessary for this germ identity.
It would be necessary for many finite-g source-domain transfers.

3. OUTER SADDLES AND THE COMPLETE CONSTRAINED GAUSSIAN

For small coarse W, consider the common one-step fiber F_L(V)=W with
phase S_M(V), where S_M is the common germ from Section2. The source's
stationary phase is CMP109 (2.2); its saddle is (2.3), p265. Our Notebook27
cmi-current-minimum and cmi-rooted-background give the same constrained
critical orbit, with contour-zero representative.

The source uses the averaged-contour exponential gauge fixing from
(0.14)--(0.16). With the same contour mean and strength it is exactly our
G_L(V)=sum_(y,x!=y) [1-Re tr P_L(V;y,x)]. The source's additional contour
restriction is one near its zero saddle. Thus the phases, constraints,
and contour-zero local saddles agree after a finite germ shrink.

For clarity the intrinsic finite Gaussian data are these. At V_*(W), use
left-relative coordinates x. All Lie algebra coordinates below are
orthonormal for the same normalized-trace metric, and all transposes refer
to that metric. The gauge strength is included in T. In nonorthonormal
coordinates T^T T must instead be T^* G_tau T, with its metric determinant
also retained in the density. Put

  f(x)=log(F_L(exp(ix)V_*)W^(-1))/i,  C=f'(0),
  s(x)=S_M(exp(ix)V_*),  s'(0)=C^T lambda,
  H=s''(0)-sum_c lambda_c f_c''(0).

Let C_p be an invertible selected pivot block and K_0 the exact linear
retained insertion with CK_0=0. Let T be the derivative of the contour
logarithms. The reduced precision and leading amplitude are

  A_T=K_0^T(H+T^T T)K_0,
  a_M,L(W)=|det C_p(W)|^(-1) det A_T(W)^(-1/2).

The normalized logarithmic correction is

  D_M,L(W)=log[a_M,L(W)/a_M,L(1)].                 (G1)

The constraint-current term in H is essential. Source CMP109 (1.5) and
(2.7)--(2.11) explicitly retain the corresponding nonlinear-constraint
current contribution. Ignoring it would create a genuine operator mismatch.

Source CMP109 (2.10)--(2.12), pp267--268, eliminates the same constrained
variables before defining the Gaussian covariance. Its COMPLETE Gaussian
normalization therefore includes the coarea/pivot density. Equation(G1)
must be compared with the total g^0 normalization of that derivation, not

4. EXACT FINITE-GERM LEADING-AMPLITUDE IDENTITY

Let I_B(W,g) be the source zero-inherited-interaction one-step integral
localized to its stationary branch, and I_O(W,g) our corresponding
one-step local integral. Remove their common classical phase. For either
scheme a finite-dimensional Laplace expansion yields

  lim_(g->0) exp[(S_ML(W)-S_ML(1))/g^2]
                 I_sigma(W,g)/I_sigma(1,g)
       =a_M,L(W)/a_M,L(1), sigma=B,O.             (G2)

Here every source cutoff must be one in a fixed neighborhood of the saddle,
or its rescaled domain must exhaust the Gaussian fiber. CMP109's choice
(2.9) has this property: using fresh symbols to avoid the source's reuse,
the original relative variable x is straightened as x=psi(B), then B=g xi,
so x=g xi+O(g^2|xi|^2). The fixed original retained cutoff |x_b|<epsilon_1
therefore exhausts the scaled Gaussian domain. Straightening changes only
pivot bonds, so retained coordinates in fact coincide.
Our product cutoff has T(g)->infinity and gT(g)^3->0. The extra source
gauge restriction also contains a neighborhood of its gauge-zero saddle.

One may prove(G2) on a smaller fixed tube with positive phase gap away from
the nondegenerate saddle and then use the retained-coordinate Gaussian
estimate. This is a fixed-regulator Laplace statement. It neither exchanges
the g->0 limit with infinite volume nor supplies a volume-uniform Laplace
error. The identity of the resulting analytic finite amplitude germs is
exact and does not require such a uniform error.

At the saddle, left-relative Haar densities have j(0)=1, the normalized
straightening Jacobian is 1, and the remaining density is the pivot factor.
Target Haar in a relative coordinate at W contributes its fixed density at
the identity, not j(log W). Gauge normalizations, Haar constants, powers of
g, and (2pi) factors are independent of W and cancel in the ratios.

If different gauge strengths or admissible contour gauges are retained,
our Notebook32 dgb-gauge-lemma gives the same quotient amplitude after its
orbit Jacobian is included. More globally the exact compact based-gauge
insertion has normalization independent of W. Thus gauge choice is not
an extra one-loop coefficient when all factors are tracked.

Equations(G1)--(G2) prove the finite-germ identity

  D_M,L^B = D_M,L^O.                             (G3)

This is a mathematical bridge from common primitive data and the published
classical uniqueness input. It does not rely on an informal universality
claim, nor assert I_B=I_O at positive coupling. Equality of analytic germs
implies equality of every finite zero-background derivative. It is not
necessary to differentiate a nonsmooth cutoff or a global error term.

5. WHAT THIS DOES AND DOES NOT TRANSFER TO BETA

The source beta is defined by its thermodynamic Hessian moment, CMP109
(1.20)--(1.22). Our finite short-lift beta is a different finite convention.
Therefore(G3) first identifies finite Hessian kernels. Pass these kernels
to a common infinite structural system, using the actual exponential
moment bounds. Only THEN identify their infinite Maxwell functionals after
fixing the same normalized trace and polarization convention.

Our Notebook32 cocycle applies to the iterated F_L map, hence it applies
to the source leading amplitudes identified in(G3). Notebook37's actual
coefficient theorem is available for K=L^q at fixed primitive ell=L:

  beta(D_M,L^q)=22 alpha_g q log L + O(1),

with its stated uniform structural hypotheses. This yields a positive
Gaussian q-block for sufficiently large fixed q once the source infinite
kernel is identified with the same finite-germ limit. No use of our
finite-g amplitude induction with R=L is required. Our quantum induction
may have needed s>1; that is immaterial to this Gaussian-only comparison.

The remaining infinite-kernel check is concrete: source beta must use the
limit of the same finite homogeneous Gaussian kernels, with matching
volume/old-depth conventions. Pointwise finite-germ equality plus uniform
Hessian moment tails gives equality of the limiting moments; equality of
names or a finite nonuniform continuity claim does not.

The actual limit argument can be short. Fix M and q first, and take one
common cofinal periodic family. Equation(G3) makes every finite Hessian
kernel identical. The source thermodynamic limit, when taken as defined
in (1.20)--(1.22), is therefore a structural limit of the identical kernels.
Our exponential second-moment tails justify the Maxwell moment passage.
The Notebook37 bound holds in every such structural system with the same
constant independent of M, so the resulting source estimate is uniform
in M, even though the equality-of-germs radii were not. No exchange of a
nonuniform Laplace limit with an infinite-volume limit is used: first
identify the exact finite Gaussian germs, then pass their kernels.

For normalization, write the infinite quadratic kernel as
Pi_(mu nu)(p)=b[delta_(mu nu) sum_rho d_rho(-p)d_rho(p)
                  -d_mu(-p)d_nu(p)]+higher spatial order.
Then the source -partial_mu partial_nu Pi_(mu nu)(0) equals b for mu!=nu.
On a constant-curvature Cartan probe with tr(H^2)=1, the corresponding
quadratic density is (b/2)t^2, so our 2[t^2] convention also equals b.
This comparison uses the quadratic form with the normalized trace; no
unidentified color or orientation multiplier is absorbed into b.

6. THE SMALL REMAINING OPERATOR INTERFACE

The comparison now reduces to the source's actual decomposition

  beta_j^B = beta_j^(Gaussian) + beta_j^(nonGaussian).

The second term is NOT the full inherited E evaluated at the outer saddle;
that transported term is removed in defining the new increment. It is the
new normalized fluctuation logarithm after inherited subtraction, including
cutoff-only activity. The paper's own fixed-cutoff source construction
must be distinguished from the later complete-density construction with
logarithmic retained cutoffs. CMP116 (2.41) is the fixed-cutoff norm input,
not our product-ball norm. The later cutoff is independent of background
after pivot elimination, so a direct threshold variant of CMP116 is a
concrete option; see source_full_density_cutoff_crosswalk.txt. Its actual
full-density inherited state also reads old R terms. A theorem about the
autonomous zero-R small-field recurrence does not yet identify that state.

To close positive block drift it remains to establish explicitly

  |beta_j^(nonGaussian)| <= C_ext A_rem epsilon_1,

uniformly in old depth and every source state/history needed by shooting,
where C_ext and A_rem are fixed before epsilon_1 is made small. This needs
the source's localized analytic remainder on a fixed (U,J) neighborhood and
the first response into every slot used by that remainder. Our previously
proved Cauchy/moment extraction lemma supplies the abstract implication
once those source constants are matched. Its radius must not shrink with
epsilon_1 so quickly that C_ext epsilon_1 ceases to be small.

The direct Gaussian germ identity removes the need to prove a full nonlinear
conjugacy of the positive-g recursions. It also removes gauge and primitive
cutoff equality as obstacles at Gaussian order. What remains is an exact
source-domain extraction/uniformity check and the compatibility of the
source homogeneous coefficients with its completed full-density scheme.
