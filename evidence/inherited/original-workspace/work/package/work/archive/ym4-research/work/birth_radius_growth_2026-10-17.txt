ACTUAL HOMOGENEOUS BIRTH-RADIUS GROWTH AND A NONLINEAR CURRENT BOUND
17 October 2026. Proof module; source-locality conversion is separated below.

RESULT

The ordinary homogeneous minimizer has a uniformly bounded nonlinear
physical Wilson current on its fixed coarse potential ball. With the
explicit uniform-fraction source-domain inclusion hypothesis below, a
source (U,J)-functional with shrinking radii specializes to a true
current-link product ball of comparable radius, not just a formal germ.
The exact retained chart, expressed before rooting the stationary
background, consumes at most C_L g R_cut of that radius and contracts
the external stationary angle by C0/L. This proves the proposed radius
recurrence when C0/L<1. The primitive inequality C0/L<1 is not contained
in the existing estimates: C0 is uniform in an endpoint ratio, but can
depend on the primitive ratio. Section 5 proves a fixed finite-block
remedy for the composed homogeneous chart maps, modulo a controlled
holomorphic gauge. Matching those maps to a specific restricted source
kernel requires the exact covariance/iteration ledger stated below.
No separate quantum integrals are replaced by a large-ratio integral.

1. PRECISE IMPORTS FROM THE EXISTING HOMOGENEOUS THEOREMS

All graphs below are finite compatible periodic regulators. The primitive
odd ratio ell, group, physical trace metric and Holder exponent are fixed.
M=ell^k is arbitrary; constants are independent of M and periodic volume.
Fine physical spacing is eta=M^{-1} when the prescribed coarse spacing
is one. Complex transposes use the physical bilinear pairings.

For the corrected constraint F(A)=QA+R(A), Notebook 15 provides:

  F(A_*(B))=B,  ||A_*(B)||_{N_alpha}<=C||B||_infinity;
  ||D F(A)^T||_{infinity->infinity}<=1+C||A||_infinity;
  ||m(A,B)||_infinity<=C(||B||+||A||_{N_alpha}^2),            (1.1)

on one fixed complex B ball. Here m is the exact multiplier defined in
work/nonlinear_fixed_gauge_minimizer_fragment.tex, nm-multiplier, and
the transpose estimate is nm-adjoint-composite. The formula is

 m=(I+H^T D R(A)^T)^{-1}
                 {q[B-R(A)]+H^T N(A)},                   (1.2)

where E(A)=KA+N(A) is the ordinary-potential Wilson Euler derivative.
No pointwise bound on N(A) alone is used or assumed.

The tangent decomposition in work/nonlinear_gauge_slice_research.tex,
ng-tangent-decomposition, proves stationarity on the full corrected
constraint, not only on its gauge slice. The ordinary representative is
U_ord(B)=U_*(B)^{h_*(B)}, where h_* and its inverse are holomorphic
and bounded in supremum norm on a uniform ball (ot-ordinary-representative).

For an endpoint ratio R=ell^s, the actual ordinary prefix P_{M,R}
of the MR-refined minimum satisfies, with a=log P_{M,R}(exp(iw))/i,

  ||a||_infinity<=C0 ||w||_infinity/R,
  ||Delta a||_infinity<=C0 ||w||_infinity/R^2.              (1.3)

These are pgh-scale-bounds in work/pregauge_holder_prefix_fragment.tex.
Its full complete-field holomorphy and coherent strict support expansion
are pgh-angle-norm and pgh-family-bound. C0 is uniform in M,R,volume
for the fixed primitive ell. It is not asserted uniform in ell.

2. NEW NONLINEAR CURRENT ESTIMATE

PROPOSITION 1.
Let J_phys(U) be the physical left Wilson Euler current: variations
U(t,e)=exp(i eta t v(e))U(e) satisfy

  d/dt S_eta(U(t))|_0=<J_phys(U),v>_eta.                    (2.1)

This is the curvature-divergence current in the source specialization,
with its fixed convention factors retained. On a fixed complex coarse
ball, the ordinary minimum obeys

  ||J_phys(U_ord(B))||_infinity<=C||B||_infinity,            (2.2)

uniformly in refinement and volume. This current is a holomorphic
map into fine supremum norm. Its first variation at zero is KH_ord,
as used in Notebook 41, but (2.2) is nonlinear.

Explicitly, use S_eta(U)=eta^(d-4) sum_p[1-tau(P_p+P_p^-1)/2],
one unoriented plaquette once, and <v,w>_eta=eta^d sum_e tau(v_e w_e).
For each incidence of a positive bond e in a plaquette, orient that
plaquette boundary and cyclically base it so its first edge is e, and
call its holonomy P_{p,e}. Then the analytically continued current is

  J_phys(U)(e)=eta^-3 sum_{p incident e} pi_g Im P_{p,e},
  Im P=(P-P^-1)/(2i).                                    (2.2a)

Repeated periodic incidences are counted with multiplicity. Here pi_g
is the orthogonal Lie-algebra projection in the normalized trace metric,
complexified for analytic fields. The variational identity (2.1) fixes
the convention even on graphs with repeated incidence. In d=4 the
coupling-free action has no remaining eta prefactor.

Proof. On a real finite regulator, full constrained stationarity and
surjectivity of D F(A_*) imply E(A_*)=D F(A_*)^T lambda for a
unique coarse multiplier lambda. Surjectivity follows from the explicit
right inverse H_A=H(I+D R(A_*)H)^{-1}. Apply H^T to this equality.
Since QH=I, H^T K A_*=q[B-R(A_*)], and E=KA_*+N(A_*), the
result is exactly (1.2). Thus lambda=m(A_*,B), and

  E(A_*(B))=D F(A_*(B))^T m(A_*(B),B).                   (2.3)

Both sides are holomorphic in B on the common ball. Equality on the real
ball therefore proves (2.3) for complex B. Equations (1.1) and the
minimizer bound give ||E(A_*(B))||_infinity<=C||B||.

Let T_X be the left logarithmic differential of the exponential, defined
by d/dt exp(i(X+tY))|_0=i T_X(Y) exp(iX). The change from potential
variation A+t v to (2.1) gives

  E(A)=T_{eta A}^T J_phys(exp(i eta A)).                  (2.4)

T_X and its inverse are uniformly bounded on a fixed small complex
matrix ball, independently of eta<=1. Applying the inverse transpose
proves the current bound for U_*. Under the ordinary gauge transfer,
the current is conjugated at its basepoint. The bounded holomorphic
gauge h_* and inverse change its supremum norm by only a fixed factor.
This proves (2.2), including the uniform holomorphic norm. QED.

This argument is not a false estimate of a rough outside divergence.
It obtains its cancellation from the exact Euler equation before taking
the supremum. No uniform second derivatives of the fine potential were
needed. At each finite graph all tangent directions are legitimate
directions inside an open norm ball after shortening the parameter;
the full-constraint stationarity is enough for the finite-dimensional
multiplier argument.

HYPOTHESIS UF (uniform-fraction source-domain inclusion).
For the particular family of radii alpha0,alpha1 under consideration,
source conditions (i)--(iii), with each radius decreased by a fixed
factor c_src>0, imply the full reconstructed subdomain condition (iv).
The same c_src works as those radii shrink and for every relevant
homogeneous local restriction. This is an additional uniformity input:
the fixed-radii smaller-constant statement in CMP109 and Notebook 41
does not alone prove it for an arbitrary shrinking-radius family.

COROLLARY 2 (conditional source specialization on a comparable ball).
Suppose a source local functional R_X(U,J) is holomorphic and bounded
by A_X on its stated source domain with radii alpha0,alpha1, and UF
holds. Then

  f_X(B)=R_X(U_ord(B),J_phys(U_ord(B)))                    (2.5)

is holomorphic and bounded by A_X on the FULL coarse-link product ball

  ||B||_infinity<r,  r=c min(alpha0,alpha1,b_*),            (2.6)

where c>0 is uniform in M, X and compatible volume. Required collars
are restrictions of the same global minimizing field.

Proof. The existing ordinary potential bound controls its physical C1
norm by C||B||. Elementary plaquette multiplication gives
||U(dp)-I||<=C eta^2(||B||+||B||^2). Proposition 1 controls the
independent current condition after specialization. For complex fields
take real reference configuration I and the small complex potential
as the complex perturbation. A fixed choice of c puts these bounds
strictly inside the smaller source conditions. The stated source
inclusion supplies the reconstructed subdomain conditions. Composition
is holomorphic and the source bound applies. QED.

The source domain invocation is CMP109 pp262--263, (1.10)--(1.16),
with its smaller-constant paragraph; the radii for the later class are
CMP119 (2.28). This corollary applies to a local R_X restricted from a
global homogeneous field even when X is an arbitrary source polymer.
It does not prove a mixed-boundary minimizing-field theorem, or strict
locality of f_X as a function of B. The global minimizer generally reads
B outside X. The latter locality must be obtained by a quantitative
localization/support argument before invoking a prepared-potential norm.

3. AN EXACT NOISY HOMOGENEOUS FIBER CHART WITH RADIUS GAIN

For this section let L=ell be one primitive step. Put
V_pre(w)=P_{M,L}(exp(iw)), a_pre=log V_pre/i. Root this current
background by its actual block contour mean:

  h(w)(x)=P_{V_pre(w)}(y,x), x in the block centered at y,
  h(w)(y)=I,  V_rt=V_pre^{h(w)}.                          (3.1)

Then P_{V_rt}(y,x)=I and M_L(V_rt)=exp(iw). The block size L is
fixed; the contour functions and h,h^{-1} are bounded holomorphic
maps on a sufficiently small w product ball, uniform in M and volume.
The source retained chart of Notebook 19 is

  U_rt(w,z)=exp(i Phi_{V_rt(w)}(g z)) V_rt(w),             (3.2)

with all retained coordinates z unchanged by pivot straightening.
For ||z||_infinity<=R_cut, it obeys the exact coarse constraint and
||Phi_{V_rt}(gz)||_infinity<=C_L g R_cut. Undo the SAME background
gauge h(w), independently of z, and set

  B(w,z)=log(U_rt(w,z)^{h(w)^{-1}})/i.                    (3.3)

PROPOSITION 3 (actual chart radius consumption).
There exist b_cap>0, rho_L>0 and fixed C0,C_L such that B is
holomorphic on ||w||<b_cap, ||g z||<rho_L, satisfies B(w,0)=a_pre(w),
and

  ||B(w,z)||_infinity <= C0||w||_infinity/L
                                      +C_L g R_cut.      (3.4)

It has the prescribed ordinary coarse average exp(iw). Thus a scalar
current functional f holomorphic on ||B||<r can be read in this actual
chart throughout a next-scale ball ||w||<r' whenever

  r'<=b_cap,  (C0/L) r'+C_L g R_cut<r.                   (3.5)

The integrand inherits the same supremum bound as f.

Proof. Exact equivariance and h(y)=I at coarse endpoints give the
constraint. At zero fluctuation the gauge is undone exactly. More
explicitly, the group field in (3.3) equals

  exp(i Ad_{h(w)(e_-)^{-1}} Phi_{V_rt(w)}(gz)(e))
                                             exp(i a_pre(w)(e)).

On a smaller common chart the difference between its logarithm and
a_pre has norm at most C times the first exponent. The bounded gauge
adjoint and the finite-range retained-chart estimate prove (3.4).
The first term in (3.4) uses the actual nonlinear estimate (1.3),
not a linearization. Formula (3.5) is then direct composition. QED.

The rooted stationary background itself need not have the C0/L angle
bound: rooting can concentrate angles on crossing links. The undoing
in (3.3) is essential. Scalar gauge invariance permits this exact change
for the inherited functional; the gauge fixing weight, covariance and
measure factors remain those of the actual rooted integration. No
claim is made that a gauge-dependent interaction can be moved this way.

A finite-cutoff integral of such an integrand is holomorphic in w when
its remaining factors are holomorphic and have a finite common majorant.
Its logarithm requires an additional zero-free or convergent-cluster
argument. Proposition 3 proves the domain inclusion, not that logarithm
bound or a localized activity norm.

4. ITERATING THE RADII DURING A SHORT STARTUP

Suppose a fixed step or verified fixed-block chart has a bound

  ||B(w,z)||<=theta ||w||+e_j,  0<theta<1.                 (4.1)

Choose 1<lambda<theta^{-1} and mu=1-theta lambda>0.
If e_j<=mu r_j/2, then the choice

  r_{j+1}=min(lambda r_j,b_cap)                            (4.2)

lies strictly inside the old radius. Starting from r_i, a fixed ball
is reached in at most 1+log(b_cap/r_i)/log lambda steps. There is a
uniform fractional margin at every startup step, so Cauchy estimates
in the normalized field variable B/r_j incur fixed constants rather
than a new inverse power of g_i at each step.

To verify the noise budget on a bootstrap prefix, suppose
gamma_j=g_j^{-2}, |gamma_{j+1}-gamma_j|<=D and the startup length is
at most c0+c1 log(1/g_i). For a sufficiently weak ceiling,

  gamma_i/2 <= gamma_j <= 3gamma_i/2

through this startup, since D(c0+c1 log(1/g_i))<=gamma_i/2.
Hence g_j and g_i, and their large logarithms, are comparable there.
If r_i=c g_i(log g_i^{-2})^q and e_j<=C g_j(log g_j^{-2})^p with q>p,
then e_j/r_i tends uniformly to zero as g_i tends to zero. The whole
startup noise budget follows, because r_j>=r_i. If q=p, a sufficiently
large available initial radius constant can instead suffice; it must
fit the actual source radii in (2.6). If q<p, this argument does not
establish even its first step.

The source statement that q0,q1>1 does not by itself verify q=min(q0,q1)>p.
That source parameter compatibility must be checked or explicitly chosen
within its allowed parameter order. Nor may the bounded-increment
bootstrap hypothesis above be replaced by mere weakness g_j<=g_*.

5. A FIXED FINITE-BLOCK NOISY CHART GAIN

The bound C0/R in (1.3) is uniform in endpoint R=ell^s. Choose once
and for all q with ell^q>2C0. Write F_M(w,t) for Proposition 3's
nonrooted angle map with t=g z. On smaller fixed balls F_M is
holomorphic, F_M(w,0)=a_{M,ell}(w), and

  ||F_M(w,t)-F_M(w,0)||<=C_ell||t||,
  ||D_w F_M(w,t)||_{infinity->infinity}<=C_prim.            (5.1)

The second bound follows by Banach Cauchy on the fixed outer product
ball, using the first bound and (1.3); neither bound counts coordinates.
Their constants are independent of M and volume.

PROPOSITION 4 (actual finite-block field-domain gain).
For every fixed finite q there are b_q,rho_q,C_q>0, independent of
M and compatible volume, and a holomorphic background-only gauge
gamma_{M,q}(w), with gamma and inverse uniformly bounded, such that
the exact sequential chart substitution

  B_{M,q}(w;t)=F_M(F_{M ell}(...F_{M ell^(q-1)}
                             (w,t_{q-1})...,t_1),t_0)

has a gauge-equivalent representative

  tilde B_{M,q}(w;t)=log[(exp(i B_{M,q}(w;t)))^
                                         gamma_{M,q}(w)]/i

holomorphic on ||w||<b_q, max_a||t_a||<rho_q, and obeying

  ||tilde B_{M,q}(w;t)||
              <=(C0/ell^q)||w||+C_q max_a||t_a||.         (5.2)

All q chart variables remain present. In particular t_a=g_{i+a}z_a is
allowed with ||z_a||<=R_cut(g_{i+a}). The proposition is a theorem
about the specified chart composition. Its use for a particular nested
source kernel has the additional exact matching condition below; it is
not an identification with a single-step quantum integral at ratio ell^q.

Proof. Since q is fixed, shrink the final w and noise balls so all
intermediate F maps remain inside the fixed balls of (5.1). Telescope
the compositions, changing one t_a at a time. The mean-value integral
on the complex convex input ball and (5.1) give

  ||B_{M,q}(w;t)-B_{M,q}(w;0)||
                 <=C_ell sum_{a=0}^{q-1} C_prim^a||t_a||.
                                                               (5.3)

The zero-noise composition is an actual iterated ordinary classical
prefix. The two-minimum comparison of Notebook 31, csg-comparisons,
identifies it modulo gauge with P_{M,ell^q}(exp(iw)). We need an
analytic choice of that gauge, not merely its pointwise existence.
The proof there constructs it by the ordinary-to-corrected transfer
ot-ordinary-to-corrected, the flow ng-flow into the fixed slice, and
the inverse transfer from the common strict slice minimizer. All
algebraic transfer maps are uniformly holomorphic. The flow has a
holomorphic vector field on the complex X_alpha ball and retains the
exact identity R0 D^T A(t)=exp(-t)R0 D^T A(0). The absolute estimate
||A'(t)||_{X_alpha}<=C exp(-t)||R0 D^T A(0)||_{Y_alpha}
therefore holds on a smaller complex ball as well. Continuation keeps
the solution there for all t>=0; the uniform integrable speed gives
a holomorphic limit. Its generating scalar gauge has the corresponding
integrable Z_alpha bound, so its gauge ODE and inverse converge uniformly
and holomorphically, with gauge minus identity O(||A(0)||).

Apply these explicit maps to the finitely many comparisons on the same
fine graph. The fixed ratio rescalings in csg-rescaling only change
constants depending on q and ell. This constructs gamma_{M,q}(w),
holomorphic and bounded on a common b_q ball, with gamma(0)=I and
gamma-I=O_q(||w||), uniformly in M and volume. On the real ball the
minimum equality identifies the transformed fields. The identity
theorem extends that identity to the common complex ball:

  (exp(i B_{M,q}(w;0)))^gamma=P_{M,ell^q}(exp(iw)).

Apply the SAME gamma(w), independently of all t_a, to the noisy field.
The group action and logarithm have a uniformly bounded derivative in
the old angle variable on a smaller fixed ball: gamma, its inverse and
all edge products stay uniformly bounded there. Thus (5.3) remains
bounded by C_q max_a||t_a|| after gauge transformation. Its zero-noise
value has norm at most C0||w||/ell^q by (1.3). This proves (5.2).
QED.

Consequently the startup recurrence of Section 4 holds at q-step
endpoints with theta=C0/ell^q<1/2 and
e_i=C_q max_{0<=a<q} g_{i+a}R_cut(g_{i+a}), provided its explicitly
stated noise/radius hypotheses hold. This removes the ambiguity in
the primitive constant C0/ell; it does not resolve the source radius
hierarchy or UF.

Gauge invariance is used for the inherited scalar functional only.
The source (U,J) domain is interpreted as its gauge-covariant orbit
domain; equivalently continue the scalar through the explicit small
gauge charts. A bounded analytic representative on a B-ball, without
this gauge invariance/domain compatibility, does not suffice. No
gauge-dependent covariance, cutoff, insertion or Jacobian is discarded.

EXACT KERNEL COMPOSITION LEDGER (conditional source matching).
Suppose the actual one-step operator, with the specified retained
sector, has for every gauge-invariant scalar input f the identity

  (K_j f)(w)=int a_j(w,z) f(F_{M_j}(w,g_j z)) dz,          (5.4)

and K_j f is gauge invariant. The weight a_j includes ALL original
rooted gauge factors, measures, Jacobians, cutoffs and normalizations.
The one-step identity unroots only the argument of f and is exact by
scalar gauge invariance. Assume all intermediate arguments lie in its
domain. Then iterating (5.4) gives the composed F fields of Proposition 4
with the weights evaluated at the successive actual coarse arguments:
for two steps the weight is a_{j+1}(w,z_1)
a_j(F_{M_{j+1}}(w,g_{j+1}z_1),z_0). This follows by applying (5.4)
first to the invariant scalar K_j f and then inserting its identity.
No gauge has been commuted through an earlier weight. Finite-cutoff
Fubini, when its common majorant holds, gives the corresponding nested
integral identity. The final comparison gauge changes only f's value.

The needed preservation of gauge invariance must include the chosen
retained restriction. Full conditional gauge covariance alone does not
prove covariance of an arbitrary extra cutoff. This matching condition
has NOT been derived here for every retained or mixed source sector.
For the original regular source kernel it is stated explicitly in
CMP109 p269, (2.16) and the adjacent paragraph: all factors and the
measure transform covariantly, B'(b) is conjugated locally at b_-,
and the characteristic function is invariant under that orthogonal
real transformation. An invariant scalar insertion preserves the same
identity. A replacement retained-link cutoff prod_b 1{|B'(b)|<gR}
also preserves this argument if |.| is the invariant trace norm and
the retained bond set is unchanged. Extra restrictions still need their
own covariance check. These are identities for the original regular
homogeneous kernel and its specified radial-cutoff variant, not a proof
of the full marked RG iteration or all mixed-sector geometry.
The geometric bound (5.2) itself does not need this condition.

6. THE ACTUAL REMAINING PREPARED-NORM CONVERSION

Finally, (2.6), (3.5), and (4.2) are FIELD DOMAIN facts. A prepared
fluctuation-potential norm also requires localization, anchored sums,
collar losses, current specialization, and any subtraction of the saddle
value. The elementary pointwise bound for one term is

  |f_X(B(w,z))-f_X(B(w,0))|<=2 A_X,                       (6.1)

or, when (3.5) leaves a fractional old-radius margin,

  |f_X(B(w,z))-f_X(B(w,0))|
                           <=C A_X g R_cut/r_j.           (6.2)

Equation (6.2) is Banach Cauchy along the fluctuation difference inside
the old current product ball. Its constant has no fine-dimensional
count. Deriving the SOURCE PREPARED-POTENTIAL norm from it needs a
strict or localized support decomposition of the specialization and
the exact source geometric summations. The source (U,J) localization
does not automatically become strict locality in its current B field.

After the startup, a fixed-radius invariant E class could use the
published age-decaying fluctuation-potential estimates and a one-step
cluster Lipschitz bound. That last route must treat regenerated marginal
coefficients and their running-coupling feedback. The new propositions
here prove the nonlinear homogeneous current bound and the actual
one-step and fixed-block field-radius inequalities; they do not yet prove a
scale-uniform marked cluster recursion or four-dimensional existence.
