MERGED GROUPS: A PREFIX-FOREST CHARGE WITH ONE TREE RESERVE
18 October 2026. Exact abstract estimate and its source interface.

This treats groups AFTER a first multicolour connection. It does not
replace the separate singleton/cohort estimate. No positive polynomial
kernel bound is inferred from a homogeneous 21 by21 matrix estimate.

1. ACTUAL RETAINED GRAPH AND THE ROUGH NORM HYPOTHESIS

Work at a finite regulator, primitive dates 0,...,N and physical cell
length r_i=ell^(i-N), ell>1. The weak terminal band is fixed. Thus
bounded primitive coupling increments give

 gamma_i <= C_Gamma (1+N-i),
 M R_i <= C_Gamma (1+N-i)^d_R.                       (1)

A polylogarithm of gamma_i satisfies this deliberately weak polynomial
bound. Constants can depend on the fixed terminal weak band and the
fixed source geometry, but not on N or the fusion inverse coupling.

Let m complete copy supports K_a be pairwise separated by at least
tau, 0<tau<=1, including their original source/read stencils. Consider
ONE term of the actual connected expansion, after complete-copy
regrouping in eta_a^2=0. Retain the original attachment/connecting
graph, with every edge or connected carrier e stamped by its actual
creation date b(e). Its positive dimensionless tree cost t_e obeys

 physical length(e) <= C r_(b(e)) M R_(b(e)) t_e.     (2)

A carrier can be replaced by one of its paid connected spanning
trees; fixed collar factors belong to MR in (2). Paid interior cells
may likewise be retained as atoms with their own geometric debit.
No connection is recreated at a later date with its old cost forgotten.
Let T_i=sum_(b(e)<=i)t_e. The prefix graph contains all connections
actually established by date i, including the original attachments
to its labelled supports.

The per-edge condition(2) is SUFFICIENT, not necessary. Repeated S
enlargements of one paid atom can invalidate that literal birth-scale
length bound. The proof uses only this weaker source alternative:
for each EARLY i<=i_cut of(5), the current propagated covers and
retained attachments admit a network on each actual prefix label
group with

 total physical network length <= C s_i T_i,
             s_i <= C ell^(-(N-i))(1+N-i)^d_R.        (2a)

For example suppose the actual propagated cover and attachment graph
has at most C T_i paid pieces, each of diameter at most C s_i, with
its genuine overlaps/connecting edges retained. Choose the early
cut so that C s_i<tau/4. Each piece then meets at most ONE copy.
A spanning tree of the connected piece graph, together with one
attachment point per met copy, has length <=C s_i T_i. This proves
(2a) in exactly the range needed below. Repeated enlargement is
estimated at i rather than charged as a fictitious new birth edge.

There is deliberately NO such assertion at late dates. One old paid
piece grown to radius s_i>>tau could contain (s_i/tau)^4 copies, whose
spanning network need not have length O(s_i). The late estimate uses
only b_i<=m. It is still essential in the early argument that these
are ACTUAL paid pieces/covers and paths, not arbitrary late reference
supports. The geometric reserve for T is separate from any fraction
already spent on raw occupation. Source verification of this early
cover/count assertion is the appropriate weaker geometry target.

Groups may merge but not split as LABEL sets. A mixed scalar output
retires its labels; for the estimate keep its already paid graph and
those labels as a noninteracting 'ghost component' through date N.
No label may occur again in another factor of this term, by eta_a^2=0.
Let b_i be the number of labels which by date i belong to components
containing at least two labels, counting these ghosts. Singletons
before that event are deliberately absent from b_i.

Assume the available rough regular/operator propagation estimate is
of the following form: after first merger, a group with h labels
loses at most exp(C h) per primitive date. A fixed number of primitive
preparation maps per date is included in C. New same-date mergers are
included in this whole-map estimate, or have total cost exp(C h) for
the labels of that connected output. This is a bound on the ACTUAL
local/group map, not on its translation-invariant projection.

Polynomial source-extraction losses may be charged ONCE per original
copy when it first joins, as (1+gamma_(i_a))^D. They may not be silently
recharged to every old descendant at every later merge. A polynomial
factor per new merger edge is harmless as well; a forest has at most
m-1 such edges. The all-order connected-map/entropy bound needed to
justify these hypotheses remains an explicit source input.

2. PREFIX PACKING BOUND

Under(2), put s_i=C sup_(v<=i) r_v M R_v. Every edge of the prefix
graph has physical length at most s_i times its cost. Alternatively
use the early network and s_i in(2a). In the latter case the following
bound is asserted ONLY for i<=i_cut. If a component of this
graph touches h>=2 pairwise tau-separated supports, its physical
connected network has length at least c tau(h-1). Indeed choose one
point in each support. Doubling and pruning the network yields a
spanning tree on these points of length at most twice the network;
each edge of the point tree has length at least tau. Summing over
components and using h<=2(h-1) gives

                 b_i <= C s_i T_i / tau.             (3)

Components with no labelled point only increase T_i, which is safe.
It is immaterial whether a future label meets an already long old
carrier without adding a long new edge: the UNION prefix graph still
contains the entire network needed in (3). No first-merger tree is
spent separately again for each later descendant.

By (1), with a=N-i,

 s_i <= C ell^(-a)(1+a)^d_R,
 sum_(N-i>=A) s_i <= C ell^(-A)(1+A)^d_R.             (4)

The supremum over all earlier dates causes no UV-depth factor. To
check this, write v=i-k and bound (1+a+k)^d_R by
(1+a)^d_R(1+k)^d_R; the remaining ell^-k sum is finite.

3. ONE RESERVE PAYS ALL EARLY ROUGH PROPAGATION

Let kappa>0 be the tree exponent reserved from the original source
majorant. Choose

 A_tau=ceil[K(1+log(1/tau))],   i_cut=N-A_tau,          (5)

with K fixed sufficiently large after C,kappa,d_R and the terminal
band. If i_cut<0 there is no early range. Equations(3)--(4) imply

 sum_(i<=i_cut) b_i
   <= (C/tau) sum_(i<=i_cut) s_i T_i
   <= C ell^(-A_tau)(1+A_tau)^d_R T_N/tau.            (6)

Choose K so that the coefficient times every fixed rough-loss
constant is <=kappa/2. The elementary function
tau^-1 ell^-K(1+log(1/tau))(1+K(1+log(1/tau)))^d_R
has arbitrarily small supremum on (0,1] as K tends to infinity.
Thus a SINGLE fraction of exp(-kappa T_N) pays the complete early
rough amplification exp(C sum b_i).

For the remaining dates use b_i<=m:

 sum_(i>i_cut) b_i <= m(A_tau+1).                    (7)

Hence the rough amplification over the full future satisfies

 exp(C sum_i b_i) exp(-kappa T_N)
      <= C_0^m tau^(-C_1 m) exp(-kappa T_N/2).        (8)

This is a global prefix charge. It does not assert that each late
joining edge is individually long enough to pay the group history.
Nor does it multiply exp(C h_v(N-i_v)) over nested merger vertices,
which would count the same labelled trajectory many times.

4. INITIAL POLYNOMIAL LOSSES AND SCALAR ROOT DENSITIES

If i_a is the first merger date of label a, then
log(1+gamma_(i_a))<=C(1+N-i_a) by (1). Also

 sum_a(N-i_a) <= sum_i b_i <= sum_a(N-i_a+1),          (9)

up to the harmless choice of inclusive endpoints. Therefore every
ONCE-per-copy polynomial source loss at its first merger is paid by
the same early charge and the same late polynomial tau loss.

A genuinely mixed scalar output born at i has the fine-root density
factor r_i^-4=exp(4 log(ell)(N-i)). Its h>=2 labels are retired at that
scalar birth, so ghost continuation contributes h(N-i) to sum b_i.
Thus its density factor costs at most exp(2 log(ell) h(N-i)). Distinct
scalar factors in one nilpotent term have disjoint labels. All such
scalar root factors are consequently absorbed into the constant C
in (8). Singleton scalar densities are NOT covered: they have no
joining tree and must be removed by exact universal extraction and
full-state centering, as in stopped_scalar_chronological_normal_form.

If a scalar pressure coefficient has a fixed extra polynomial root
or preparation moment, retain it in (2)/(4) or in the once-per-copy
polynomial cost. If it contains an exponential factor not bounded
by exp(C h age), that factor needs its own estimate.

5. LAMINAR BOOKKEEPING AND WHAT IS NOT BEING ASSUMED

At each date the groups are disjoint label subsets. Transport over
an interval gives exp[C sum_groups |group| times interval_length].
Summing intervals is precisely exp(C sum_i b_i), because every label
has one trajectory after first merger. A nonlinear merge multiplies
the child norms and produces one union label set. An exp(C h) cost
for that whole same-date operation is included once for that union.
Several algorithmic binary merges at one date must first be collected
into the original connected output/majorant; charging exp(C h) for
each arbitrary binary rebracketing would be a false new loss.

This bound also explains two limitations.

(a) A homogeneous 21-dimensional transfer estimate is not a proof of
the actual rough local/group propagation hypothesis in Section1.
However an existing bounded source/operator map on original local
families can supply that hypothesis even if its bound is merely C
per primitive step; no positive polynomial spatial kernel is needed.

(b) The argument is TERMWISE in the original connected expansion.
It leaves exp(-kappa T/2), all existing anchoring and lifetime factors,
and the original coefficient/algebra combinatorics. The original
connected majorant must still sum those terms with the available
reserve. Summing arbitrary newly invented labelled forests would
introduce an uncontrolled combinatorial factor; (8) does not authorize
that replacement. Similarly, an extra polynomial gamma_j factor
charged anew to every old label at every late step gives potentially
exp[C m log(1/tau) log log(1/tau)], not the fixed polynomial in (8).
Such a map would require a sharper ledger or a different final bound.

6. RELATION TO THE MIDPOINT SMALL FACTOR

Let epsilon_mid be the per-copy factor already produced by the
singleton/first-contact argument, with gamma_mid possibly much larger
than the coupling at a later first merger. This lemma multiplies and
transports the EXISTING factors epsilon_mid; it never manufactures
epsilon_mid from the weaker coupling at a late join.

For a connected group created before the midpoint, an early stopped
tree/occupation estimate must establish its original per-copy budget
before (8) is used. For a group first created after the midpoint, its
children must already carry their singleton or subgroup budgets; the
whole connected-algebra multiplication preserves their product.
In particular, a history-dependent SINGLETON deficit born at l and
first joined at v>l must already be covered by that singleton budget
through v. A ghost starting at v cannot pay an old-root factor accrued
before v. Universal singleton scalars factored globally never join;
unhealed branch constants are different and remain part of this input.
Subsequent use of (8) spends only a separate tree fraction on rough
propagation and scalar density. This is the correct pre/post census
dichotomy. If one of these initial budgets is absent, the forest
estimate cannot replace it.

Actual source interfaces to check are therefore concrete: the retained
prefix graph has all original labelled attachments and satisfies (2)
or the weaker current-cover bound(2a);
the group map has bounded per-date growth in the same local/original
norm; complete-copy labels are not reset at mergers; and the original
connected majorant survives a fixed tree-exponent reduction. Under
these inputs the first-merger spatial issue closes by (8), without a
homogeneous-to-local l1 inference or a repeated-tree expenditure.

6A. SMALL SOURCE-BALL NONLINEARITY: ONE RADIUS LOSS PER NEW EDGE

The rough hypothesis need not use a fixed physical a_j^2 ball.
Suppose the actual marked map Phi_j, with its source-free base
subtracted, is analytic on the original local activity ball

 ||X||<R_j,    R_j=c g_j^kappa,
             sup ||Phi_j(X)||<=C R_j.                (9a)

All norms include the whole new scalar coordinate and use one working
carrier/operator norm. Cauchy on scalar multiples of X bounds its
degree-p homogeneous Taylor polynomial by C R_j^(1-p)||X||^p.
Polarization gives an ordered multilinear majorant
C_1^p R_j^(1-p) product_l||X_l||; p^p/p!<=e^p is absorbed in C_1^p.
Thus unary propagation costs only C, and a p>=2 merger loses
R_j^(-(p-1)), one inverse radius per NEW merger edge.

This last assertion is about the Taylor/ordered-tree norm majorant.
A naked coefficient of p distinct square-free variables can also
contain the p! permutations of its inputs. Their original algebra/
cluster multiplicities are retained, as Section5 requires. No claim
that an arbitrary unordered labelled coefficient has a C^p bound is
being made just from analyticity. The radius power, however, is exactly
p-1 irrespective of that existing combinatorics.

Here is the rigorous once-only assignment of those radius powers.
For each component of the merger forest perform the following
induction. A leaf retains itself as one unassigned representative.
At a vertex with p children, each child has one such representative.
Assign p-1 of these distinct leaves to the p-1 new merger edges and
keep the last as the parent representative. No selected leaf can be
selected again. There are exactly m-number_of_roots selected leaves.

If leaf a is selected at a date-j merger, its first merger date i_a
satisfies i_a<=j. From(1),

 log_+(R_j^-1) <= C + (kappa/2)log(1+gamma_j)
              <= C(1+N-j) <= C(1+N-i_a).             (9b)

Summing(9b) over the distinct selected leaves bounds ALL nonlinear
radius losses by exp[C m+C sum_i b_i]. Therefore the SAME early
prefix reserve and late polynomial padding bound(8) pay them.
There is no inverse g_j^kappa charged to every old label at every
date or at every later ancestor. The factors C_1^p also cost only
C_2^m, since sum_(nonlinear v)(p_v-1)<=m-1 and
sum_(nonlinear v)p_v<=2(m-1). Unary dates are already in sum b_i.

Original field projection does not require an extra a_j^-2 factor
when its full norm is used: ||Pi F||<=C a_j^-2||F|| and
||Qhat_j c||<=C a_j^2|c| give ||Qhat_j Pi F||<=C||F||.
Both sides must be measured in that SAME original norm. This algebra
does not prove contraction or reentry of a physical-only representative.

Thus (9a), if furnished by the actual fine-carrier R/connected-map
estimate with its complete scalar and chronological coordinates,
suffices for the nonlinear radius part of the rough merged-group
argument. It is strictly weaker than a fixed a^2 admission ball or
a positive polynomial propagation kernel. The source domain and
the unchanged original connected combinatorics still must be verified.

7. THE NARROW CLONE COROLLARY ACTUALLY NEEDED

For the slab estimate it suffices to take translated/reflected/
conjugated clones of ONE complete compensated E_G(f), with the same
fusion inverse coupling G and fixed finite 21-dimensional coefficient
combinations. Suppose its pre-join singleton history norm supplies
epsilon(G) G^B for each clone, and suppose the actual local mixed maps
and connected majorants satisfy Section1. Apply (8) after each first
join, leaving the once-per-copy factor epsilon(G) G^B untouched.
The remaining connected clone-array bound then has per-copy cost

                    C tau^-D epsilon(G) G^B,        (10)

besides the already stated unmarked volume pressure and universal
mixed-vacuum ledger. All constants and B,D are fixed independently
of clone number, G and UV depth. This is a conditional PRODUCER
corollary to the forest lemma, not a claim that its singleton/local-map
premises have been established by a 21-component homogeneous graph.

There is no need here for a theorem about arbitrary mixed copy ages
or source types. Once (10) gives each individual padded operator,
ordinary operator composition can combine distinct already bounded
operators. Conversely an original G^B extraction loss must not be
replaced by a smaller late gamma_i^B just because first joining is
late. It belongs to the initial singleton factor in (10).
