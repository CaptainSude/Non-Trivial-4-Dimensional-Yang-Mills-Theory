2. EXACT TERM-BY-TERM EXTERIOR MARGINAL AND COUPLING

Fix a positive source history h before its R replacement. Include all
retained old variables, auxiliary choices, and component labels in h or x.
Let x denote all unintegrated variables and y the actual current bond
variables integrated by this replacement. Write its positive incoming
measure as nu_h(dx,dy), with marginal m_h(dx). Let w_h(x,y')dy' denote the
source reference measure, INCLUDING its Haar/axial-gauge factors and cutoff.
The normalization analyzed in (1.10) is

  D_h(x)=int w_h(x,y')dy' >0.

The normalized replacement term is exactly

  (Rnu)_h(dx,dy')=m_h(dx) K_h(x,dy'),
  K_h(x,dy')=w_h(x,y')dy'/D_h(x).                         (2.1)

The factors from the unperformed old integrations pass through this identity.
There is no assertion D_h(x) is constant in x. Equation (2.1) is precisely
why the normalizer must be retained: integrating y' gives m_h, pointwise in
x. Thus every bounded observable depending only on x is preserved exactly.
For physical gauge-invariant observables, any subsequent undoing of axial
gauge also leaves the observable invariant. Arbitrary gauge-dependent
observables need an explicit account of that gauge averaging.

Disintegrate nu_h=m_h(dx) P_h(x,dy), and define the positive joint measure

  pi_h(dx,dy,dy')=m_h(dx) P_h(x,dy) K_h(x,dy').             (2.2)

Its marginals are nu_h and (Rnu)_h. This proves, for every bounded F,

 |nu_h(F)-(Rnu)_h(F)|
   <= int m_h(dx) int P_h(x,dy)K_h(x,dy') |F(x,y)-F(x,y')|.
                                                               (2.3)

In particular define osc_{A_h}F(x) as the oscillation under changes of the
actual replaced current variables A_h, with all retained variables fixed.
For normalized total measures, summing (2.3) over the positive histories
gives

 |mu(F)-(Rmu)(F)| <= E_mu_history[osc_{A_h}F]
                 <=2||F||_infinity P_mu_history(A_h intersects supp F).
                                                               (2.4)

The first bound is usually more useful. If a replacement is performed by a
sequence of component kernels, apply (2.3) at each component with its actual
conditional law. No independence of the original components is assumed.
The probability is the normalized POSITIVE history probability before R.
It is not a connected-log polymer coefficient.

3. EXACT MULTISCALE TELESCOPE USING ONLY ACTUAL PREFIX LAWS

Let T_i be the actual positive mass-preserving block pushforward and let
nu_0=mu_0, mu_i=T_i nu_{i-1}, nu_i=R_i mu_i. Thus nu_i is the actual modified
source prefix, while the original Wilson blocked measure at level n is

  mu_n^W=T_n ... T_1 mu_0.

For a bounded final observable F put
  F_i=T_{i+1}^* ... T_n^* F.
These are pure-blocking pullbacks, not adjoints of future R operations.
Linear telescoping gives the exact identity

  mu_n^W(F)-nu_n(F)=sum_{i=1}^n [mu_i(F_i)-nu_i(F_i)].       (3.1)

This identity is valid even though R may be defined through the incoming
state; only the linearity of the T_i is used. Combining with (2.3),

 |mu_n^W(F)-nu_n(F)|
    <=sum_i E_{mu_i,history}[osc_{A_i}F_i]
    <=2||F||_infinity sum_i P_{mu_i,history}(A_i intersects S_i), (3.2)

where S_i is an actual strict support of the future pure-block pullback.
For deterministic block averages it is their backward dependency support.
For stochastic local T_i it is the corresponding kernel-read support.
This needs no unknown law of the completely unmodified history at scale i.

4. WHY THERE IS NO AUTOMATIC FULL-COARSE-FIBER IDENTITY

The conditional variables x in (2.1) are the UNINTEGRATED source variables,
not a future coarse average of y. The local reference law is positive on
an open set of independent nongauge coordinates y. A nonconstant future
coarse observable of those coordinates is therefore not fixed by this law.
A replacement preserving a specified complete coarse field would instead
need a reference supported on that coarse fiber and its fiberwise normalizer.
The source normalizer above is an unconstrained local reference integral.

One cannot identify this with Notebook40's deliberately constructed
full-coarse-fiber replacement. That notebook's preservation identity would
be useful for observables, but it belongs to a different replacement unless
one rebuilds the source large-field bounds for that constrained operation.
The old V_h integrations mentioned before (1.1) do not repair the mismatch,
because h=k-N<k and those variables are not future coarse constraints.

5. WHAT THE PRINTED SUPPRESSION DOES AND DOES NOT BUY

CMP122-II (1.98)--(1.100) bounds connected-log R' activities. These activities
may have signs; their sum is not the probability in (2.4). To convert them
into a component-hit probability one must retain a distinguished positive
history component through the numerator/denominator expansion. The positive
T'_k(X) bounds (1.73),(1.89) are relevant inputs, but a coefficient estimate
alone must not be relabelled as a normalized probability.

Even grant a local component-hit bound per current cell
  p_i <= C (log gamma_i)^K exp[-c(log gamma_i)^p], p>1.
At fixed physical final support, its strict backward support has a number
of fine cells growing exponentially in n-i (for a block-filled support,
of order L^{4(n-i)}). A raw loop support already grows at least with its
length, of order L^{n-i}. Along an asymptotically free weak history
gamma_i comparable to Gamma+c(n-i), the corresponding estimate in (3.2)
contains

  L^{alpha(n-i)} exp[-c(log(Gamma+c(n-i)))^p], alpha>0.      (5.1)

This upper-bound expression eventually grows, because linear age dominates
every fixed power of its logarithm. It therefore does NOT prove any
cutoff-uniform small comparison. This is a failure of that estimate, not
a proof that the actual hit probability is large. The same problem applies
to arbitrary fixed polynomial birth suppression gamma_i^{-P}.

Thus the useful exact target is the FIRST line of (3.2): the effect of a
replacement on a properly normalized physical observable. Pure rarity and
the source scalar partition-function normalizations do not settle it.

6. A PRECISE OS WITNESS TRANSFER TEST

For a bounded positive-time observable F, ||F||_infinity<=1, and reflection
theta, set
  Q_mu(F)=mu[(theta conjugate F)F]-|mu(F)|^2
for a reflection-invariant measure. Define
  Delta(G)=sum_i E_{mu_i,history}[osc_{A_i}(T_{i+1:n}^*G)].
Then (3.1)--(3.2) imply

  |Q_{mu_n^W}(F)-Q_{nu_n}(F)|
       <=Delta((theta conjugate F)F)+2Delta(F).             (6.1)

Indeed |muF|,|nuF|<=1, so their squared-modulus difference is at most
2|muF-nuF|. A lower bound Q_{nu_n}(F)>=c>0 therefore transfers if the
right side of (6.1) is uniformly below c. A positive ordinary variance is
insufficient: a product measure can have positive variance and zero
centered reflection norm for observables separated from the plane.

No such nontrivial reflection-norm lower bound or vanishing/controlled
Delta for a physical continuum observable is proved by the source bounds
audited here. Equations (2.3), (3.1), and (6.1) give exact denominators and
actual prefix laws for the missing observable calculation, rather than a
generic assumed resampling model or an identification with full fibers.

7. THE SOURCED PARTITION ROUTE AVOIDS THIS COMPARISON ENTIRELY

This is a route correction, not a withdrawal of the preceding identities.
The estimates in Sections3--6 are needed only if one chooses to compare an
UNSOURCED final observable with the same function under the modified law.
They are unnecessary for correlations extracted from an exactly transported
sourced partition function.

Freeze a finite regulator and the real source-free choices: cuts, partitions,
history bookkeeping, reference couplings, configuration maps, and normalized
R reference kernels. Include retained histories in the state space. Every
block operation and every normalized R operation is then a linear,
mass-preserving map on finite complex measures. For the local R replacement,
if eta^w(dx,dy) is a complex incoming measure, its output is

  (R eta^w)(dx,dy')=[int eta^w(dx,dy)] K(x,dy'),
  int K(x,dy')=1.                                      (7.1)

Consequently int R eta^w=int eta^w exactly, with the real denominator D(x)
from Section2 retained. No source-dependent inverse or logarithm is required
for this identity. A source-dependent reference kernel is also allowed if
it is normalized and its denominator does not vanish; freezing it avoids
that additional algebraic requirement.

For any finite collection of bounded finite-regulator observables O_i put
rho_0^z=exp(sum_i z_i O_i)rho_0. These densities are entire with values in
finite measures. Composing the frozen maps gives

  int rho_n^z = int rho_0^z = Z_n(z),                    (7.2)

for all complex z. Integrating its derivatives at zero therefore gives the
ORIGINAL Wilson moments. Differentiating the logarithm near zero gives the
original connected correlations. One does not identify those derivatives
with inserting O_i of the new current variables. In the frozen positive
joint process, the derivative density divided by rho_n^0 is the conditional
expectation of the original insertion given the final state; higher
derivatives are conditional moments of products. This is the exact location
of the transported observable.

Equation(7.2) is not an imported physical-observable theorem. It is a direct
consequence of the actual source normalization (1.10) and the linear
integrate-and-reinsert identity. Its specialization is explicitly stated in
the repository Raw II, (5.1:eq-correlation-theorem-c), around lines2930--2936:
the integral of rho_k^w equals the integral of rho_0^w. The repository uses
more elaborate sourced preparation to estimate this exact integral.

8. SOURCE DOMAIN: OVERLAP IS ALLOWED; THE UNIFORM ESTIMATE IS THE INPUT

At a fixed physical stopping scale, let nu_i=||f_i||_sharp and
sum_i |z_i|nu_i<r. For arbitrary compact smooth or Lipschitz profiles,

  ||sum z_i f_i||_infinity <= sum |z_i|nu_i,
  Lip(sum z_i f_i) <= sum |z_i| Lip(f_i).               (8.1)

The weight classes W_j of Raw II2722--2787 use only these two inequalities:
|w|<r_j and Lip_j w<l_j. Their nesting proof puts (8.1) in all earlier W_j.
The profiles may overlap and may have either sign. Separation enters only
clause(iv) of the correlation theorem, after vacuum subtraction, not the
source domain or the proposed sourced induction in clause(iii).

The following explicit one-cutoff estimate is enough for subsequential
off-diagonal correlations; two-cutoff closeness and uniqueness are stronger
than necessary. On a common ball ||w||_sharp<r, suppose

  Z_N(w)=exp(E_N(w)) Ztilde_N(w),  E_N(0)=0,
  |Ztilde_N(w)|<=A_h,  Ztilde_N(0)>=1/B_h>0,            (8.2)

uniformly in the fine cutoff N, at the chosen fixed stopping lattice h.
The bare source-independent vacuum normalization must be the same on both
sides; arbitrary source-dependent scalar normalizations are not permitted.
Assume additionally that E_N is a sum of source-local analytic terms with
a uniform exponentially weighted tree norm. These are quantitative source
inputs, not consequences of (7.2).

Put H_N=Ztilde_N/Ztilde_N(0) and Q=A_h B_h. Then H_N(0)=1 and |H_N|<=Q.
Apply the Schwarz lemma to (H_N(lambda w)-1)/(Q+1) on the complex line through
w. It gives

  |H_N(w)-1| <= (Q+1)||w||_sharp/r.                    (8.3)

Thus H_N is zero-free on ||w||_sharp<r/[2(Q+1)], and there |Log H_N|<=log2.
For n profiles, choose each source radius r/[2n(Q+1)nu_i]. Cauchy's formula
then bounds every mixed n-th derivative of Log H_N by

  log2 [2n(Q+1)/r]^n product_i nu_i.                  (8.4)

Zero profiles can simply be omitted. These bounds apply to overlapping
profiles too; it is the derivative of E_N that may diverge there.

For pairwise separated profiles, the mixed derivative of a source-local
term vanishes unless its source carrier meets every support. In particular,
its physical diameter must be at least the separation d. An exponential
tree bound at lattice spacing h_j gives a factor exp(-c d/h_j), up to a
fixed collar allowance. At fixed torus, even the crude number of anchors
O(Vol h_j^-4) is summable:

  sum_j h_j^-4 exp(-c d/h_j) <= C d^-4                 (8.5)

for a geometric sequence h_j<=1, with a harmless change of C when d>1.
To verify(8.5), split at h_j approximately d; the coarser geometric sum
is O(d^-4), and the finer sum is d^-4 times sum_m L^(4m) exp(-c' L^m).
Fixed powers of |log h_j| are handled in the same way. After Cauchy in the
source variables this proves cutoff-uniform separated derivatives of E_N
under the stated local source norm. This is the elementary local mechanism
behind the deposited correlation theorem; the work is to establish(8.2)
and that source norm for the actual sourced full recursion.

9. CONTACT SUBTRACTIONS AND REFLECTION POSITIVITY: AN EXACT USEFUL LEMMA

Overlapping sources require a local contact subtraction or another specified
extension; separated estimates alone do not bound their original cumulants.
There is, however, no algebraic obstruction from reflection positivity to
a LOCAL subtraction.

Let Z_N(w)/Z_N(0) be the original reflection-positive exponential generating
functional, and let C_N(w) be a scalar counterterm with C_N(0)=0. Assume
reflection conjugation covariance and exact additivity on disjoint supports:

  C_N(theta conjugate w)=conjugate C_N(w),
  C_N(u+v)=C_N(u)+C_N(v) when supp u and supp v are disjoint. (9.1)

For sources w_i supported a positive distance inside the positive half-space,
the reflection matrix of the subtracted functional
Hhat_N(w)=exp[-C_N(w)]Z_N(w)/Z_N(0) is

 Hhat_N(theta conjugate w_i+w_j)
 =exp[-conjugate C_N(w_i)]
   [Z_N(theta conjugate w_i+w_j)/Z_N(0)] exp[-C_N(w_j)]. (9.2)

This is a diagonal congruence of a positive semidefinite matrix, hence remains
positive semidefinite. Any convergent common-source limit retains this
reflection positivity. Local integrals of finite source jets satisfy the
disjoint-support additivity in(9.1). Ordinary probability-positive-definiteness
does not follow from this argument and is not claimed.

The full quasilocal vacuum functional E_N in(8.2) generally does NOT satisfy
(9.1): finite-scale terms can connect the two half-spaces. One must separate
its genuinely local divergent contact part before using(9.2), retaining its
finite cross-support part in the physical correlations. Proving a uniform
remainder after this subtraction, common-source convergence, continuous
Euclidean covariance, and a nonzero OS witness remains separate analytical
work. Equation(9.2) identifies a valid positivity mechanism, not a completed
contact-renormalization theorem for the current Yang--Mills construction.
