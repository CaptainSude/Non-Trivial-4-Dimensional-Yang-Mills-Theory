1. THE EXACT FINITE-CUTOFF STATEMENT

Let mu_a be the original positive finite-lattice Wilson probability law,
and let G_0 superset G_1 superset ... superset G_J be sigma-fields of
specified deterministic coarse functions of its links. These functions
must actually compose; if they do not, replace G_j by a genuinely nested
chosen family rather than invoking the tower property incorrectly.

For an integrable ORIGINAL source F_a define

             F_(a,j)=E_mu_a[F_a | G_j].                    (1.1)

Then E[F_(a,j)|G_k]=F_(a,k) for j<=k, all means agree, and

             ||F_(a,j)||_p <= ||F_a||_p,  1<=p<=infinity. (1.2)

For p>1 the finite reversed Doob maximal inequality also costs only
p/(p-1), but its right side is still ||F_a||_p. In L2,

 ||F_(a,j)||_2^2-||F_(a,j+1)||_2^2
                 =||F_(a,j)-F_(a,j+1)||_2^2.              (1.3)

None of these is a lower bound on the retained centered norm. Exact
coefficient masses are preserved if one transports the COMPLETE source
array E[product F_a | G_j]; it is generally false that this equals the
product of its singleton conditional expectations.

These statements need no analytic density ratio or complex zero-free
region. They also supply no holomorphic original-pair norm, rooted
carrier bound, local support, or field-coordinate calibration. A global
conditional expectation usually reads the entire coarse configuration.

2. AN ACTUAL FREE CURVATURE-SQUARE NORM DIVERGENCE

Take a periodic four-dimensional cubic lattice of spacing a=1/L,
with N=L^4 vertices, and one real color. Remove gauge and harmonic
one-form directions. Let d_1 be the lattice curl from one-cochains to
oriented plaquette cochains, using one orientation for each of the six
plaquette types. The Gaussian action is

                         (1/2)||d_1 A||^2.

The curvature Y=d_1 A has covariance

       C=d_1(d_1^*d_1)^+d_1^*,

the orthogonal projection onto im d_1. This is an honest finite Gaussian
law on that subspace; no inverse on gauge or harmonic modes is used.
The first cohomology of the periodic cubical complex has dimension four,
so rank d_1=4N-[(N-1)+4]=3(N-1). Translation and coordinate symmetry give

                C_pp=(N-1)/(2N) for every plaquette p.    (2.1)

Equivalently, with the canonical nearest-neighbor cochain differential,
the Fourier symbol is exterior multiplication by
d(k)=(exp(ik_mu)-1)_mu. At zero momentum it has rank zero, and at each
nonzero momentum the map Lambda^1->Lambda^2 has rank three. This gives
the same rank without a gauge-fixing convention or a cohomology count.

For any nonnegative nonzero smooth physical test f, supported if desired
strictly inside a positive-time half, put f_p=f(center(p)) and

       Q_a(f)=sum_p f_p [Y_p^2-C_pp].                     (2.2)

With F_phys=a^(-2)Y this is exactly
a^4 sum_p f_p :F_phys,p^2:. The coupling-normalized Wilson energy has
this quadratic germ, up to a fixed convention-dependent factor. Colors
just give independent copies and a fixed multiplicative factor.

Gaussian contraction gives the exact formula

 Var Q_a(f)=2 Tr(M_f C M_f C)
           =2 sum_(p,q) f_p f_q |C_pq|^2.                (2.3)

Because f>=0, retain the diagonal terms for the lower bound. Since C is
an orthogonal projection, Hilbert--Schmidt contraction gives the upper
bound. For L>=2,

 (1/8) sum_p f_p^2 <= Var Q_a(f) <= 2 sum_p f_p^2.

Consequently

               c_f a^(-4) <= Var Q_a(f) <= C_f a^(-4).  (2.4)

For the full-torus constant test f=1 this simplifies further:
Q_a(1) is chi-square with 3(N-1) degrees of freedom minus its mean,
and Var Q_a(1)=6(N-1). For a supported nonnegative f the diagonal
argument above is the separate proof; it does not use a constant test
or mistakenly replace a supported source by the full torus energy.

This includes the exact mean subtraction. Further deterministic vacuum
subtractions cannot reduce the variance.

For completeness this is an Lp, not merely L2, obstruction. Diagonalize
the real symmetric quadratic form on im C, writing Q=sum lambda_r(Z_r^2-1).
Then

 E Q^4=12(sum lambda_r^2)^2+48 sum lambda_r^4
                          <=15 (E Q^2)^2.

Interpolation gives ||Q||_1>=||Q||_2/sqrt(15), and hence every p>=1 has
||Q||_p>=c_f a^(-2). Paley--Zygmund also gives

 P{|Q| >= sqrt(Var Q/2)} >=1/60.

This also gives the direct L1 lower bound ||Q||_1>=sqrt(Var Q/2)/60,
without interpolation. The preceding interpolation bound is sharper.

Thus these centered ordinary random variables are not even tight without
an additional normalization or change of observable. This is a rigorous
free comparison-model obstruction to the proposed abstract implication;
it is NOT a proved interacting-YM variance asymptotic.

For separated f and h the diagonal mechanism is absent. The lattice curl
Green kernel has its ordinary off-diagonal scaling C_pq=O(a^4 d^-4) at
fixed positive physical separation d. Formula(2.3) with f,h therefore
has a finite separated limit, as in the already audited free reflected
witness and free packet notes. No such off-diagonal fact was needed to
prove(2.4). Ordinary E[Q_a(f)^2] tests coincident fields across its two
factors; the separated or reflected OS pairing does not. Replacing the
latter by the former is the specific overly strong norm demand.

A multiplicative factor of order a^2 would repair the ordinary variance
here, but it also kills the nonzero separated dimension-four correlation.
It is not the required physical field normalization. Source-dependent
contact counterterms may define off-diagonal/extended Schwinger objects,
but subtracting a quadratic term from log Z is no longer a positive
moment-generating function to which ordinary Jensen can be applied.

3. CONDITIONING CAN REMOVE THE DIVERGENCE, BUT JENSEN DOES NOT PROVE IT

Let X be any centered finite Gaussian vector with covariance C and let
B be a jointly Gaussian coarse vector. On its nonsingular range set

 m(B)=E[X|B]=H B,  C_m=Cov(m),  D=C-C_m.

For a symmetric matrix M write Q_M=X^t M X-Tr(MC). Then exactly

 E[Q_M|B]=m^t M m-Tr(M C_m),
 Var E[Q_M|B]=2 Tr(M C_m M C_m).                          (3.1)

Uniform control can therefore hold even while Tr(MCMC) diverges, if
the ACTUAL reconstructed covariance C_m smooths the necessary slots.
For example, with N independent standard Gaussians and
B=N^(-1/2)sum X_i,

 E[sum_i(X_i^2-1)|B]=B^2-1.

The input variance is 2N and the output variance is 2. This establishes
the possible gain directly; contraction alone only gives the bound 2N.
After dividing the input by sqrt(2N), it also gives a unit-variance
source whose conditional singleton tends to zero in L2.

For two quadratic marks the missing joined coefficient is explicit:

 E[Q_M Q_N|B]
   =E[Q_M|B] E[Q_N|B]
      +2 Tr(M D N D)+4 m^t M D N m.                     (3.2)

For symmetric M,N,D the last scalar is equal to its transpose, so no
additional symmetrization factor is missing. These contacts preserve
the original product even if both singleton projections become small.
They need not have a favorable sign for separated/reflected choices.
The exact full-array construction of turn8 already retains them.

For the native interacting proposal, (3.1) identifies what a genuinely
new bound would have to replace. It is an estimate of the actual
conditional source after integration, not a generic Banach contraction.
An estimate of the regular homogeneous conditional kernel alone omits
the full conditional large-field/history terms in that same source.

4. THE CONDITIONAL SOURCE IS AN EFFECTIVE-DENSITY RESPONSE

At finite cutoff let nu_t=q_*[e^(tF)mu] be the UNNORMALIZED pushed-forward
measure, with nu_0=q_*mu. For bounded F (automatic for each fixed compact
lattice source, with its cutoff-dependent bound),

       d nu_t/d nu_0 (v)=E_mu[e^(tF)|q=v].               (4.1)

Thus, wherever the real conditional expression is defined,

       partial_t log(d nu_t/d nu_0)|_0=E[F|q=v].         (4.2)

For normalized nu_t/nu_t(1), the right side is E[F|q=v]-E F.
Higher derivatives are the conditional connected source coefficients.
These identities can equally be formulated weakly without a density
with respect to Haar; gauge-slice singularities do not invalidate them.

Consequently defining a source by conditional expectation avoids choosing
a particular low/high decomposition, but it does not by itself estimate
the response. A native regular-step proof may establish such an estimate
by expanding the right side. Its mature source profiles, scalar vacuum
part, and joint contacts have not disappeared; they are now implicit in
the single function (4.2). A local estimate of that function in a weaker
real norm would be a legitimate alternative producer. It is not supplied
by (1.2), by a source-free partition stability bound, or by a bound for
the separately normalized reference kernel.

In particular, a uniform real exponential-moment bound for the centered
bare Q_a(f) would contradict(2.4). The scalar complete-source machinery
used for separated/nilpotent derivatives should not be silently promoted
to such an ordinary exponential-moment statement.
