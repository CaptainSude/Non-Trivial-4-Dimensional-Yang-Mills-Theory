Let D=U_j^c(X,alpha0,alpha1,gamma0) be the literal native pair domain.
By its definition at printed262, D is a union of full complex-gauge
orbits with action

 (F,K)^g=(F^g,Ad_(g_-)K).

Let z denote all external parameters, source localizers, and the
ORIGINAL real noise values as parameters, and suppose a selected pair
P_L(z)=(F_L(z),K_L(z)) is holomorphic in the complex parameters on one
specified open domain. Assume P_L(z) belongs to D at every required
point. Let g(z) be any well-defined complex gauge on the relevant
vertices, possibly parameter-dependent and not spatially C1 regular.
Then P_ax(z)=P_L(z)^(g(z)) belongs to D by the orbit definition.

For a scalar E holomorphic on D and invariant under (1.10),

                       E(P_ax(z))=E(P_L(z)).             (1)

If g and P_L are holomorphic, all finite parameter derivatives of
these composites agree wherever defined. This follows by differentiating
the identical holomorphic functions; no independent estimate on spatial
or parameter derivatives of g is needed to bound the invariant composite
using the right side. This does not mean derivatives of g vanish in a
componentwise chain rule; rather their contributions cancel in the full
invariant scalar.

For B109's chosen continuation, the pair to retain is literally

 F_L=exp(i xi A)U,
 K_L=(L^(j-1)eta)^3 J + Delta_U^xi A - P_1 A + F_1(U,A). (2)

After source localization, K_L is the selected analytic companion.
It must not be replaced by the physical Euler current of F_L. The
need for the independent companion is expressly explained on p272:
localization controls the covariant Laplacian but not D*D.

The sufficient source-response bound used on p272(3.14) controls all
four entries

            |A|, |P_1A|, |nabla_U^xi A|, |Delta_U^xi A|,

and p273 includes the complete old/current domain intersection(3.16).
Thus pair-orbit invariance does not remove these admission hypotheses.
It merely permits checking them in the Landau representative when the
full selected pair there is already admitted.

Original-noise rule. Formula(1) is evaluated after each original real
noise x has been inserted into the actual native maps. It permits
conjugating evaluated outputs. It does NOT assert

 Phi(U^g,J^g,x)=Phi(U,J,x)^g.

Any true equivariance identity for Phi must carry the actual noise
representation, e.g. Phi(U^g,J^g,rho(g)x)=Phi(U,J,x)^g, and must be
proved for its specific embedding/private-pivot/localizer conventions.
Even then, using rho(g)x as a deterministic complex argument for estimates
requires domain inclusion; it is not a change of integration variables,
and no real noise cut or Jacobian may be declared unchanged.
