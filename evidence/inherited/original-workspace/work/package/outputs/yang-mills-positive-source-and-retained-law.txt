1. THE LITERAL POSITIVE INCREMENT

Let mu_a be the homogeneous original SU(2) Wilson law on a periodic
four-dimensional lattice. Write
 G_n=(1/n)sum_(0<=t<n)phi_t-(1/n)sum_(n<=t<2n)phi_t,
 0<=phi_t<=1, 2n<=N,
where each phi_t is the same spatial-flow and smear recipe on its slice.
The reflection t -> 2n-1-t modulo N preserves mu_a and negates G_n.
Consequently E_mu G_n=0, |G_n|<=1 and, for s>0,
 M_a(s)=Z_a(s)/Z_a(0)=E_mu cosh(sG_n),
 (s^2/2) E_mu G_n^2 <= M_a(s)-1
                         <=(cosh s-1) E_mu G_n^2.       (1.1)
The action, source recipe and denominator do not change with s.

Put K_s(x)=cosh(sx)-1 and C_s=cosh s-1. For any complete positive
probability lift Pi of this SAME original law, and any measurable event E,
 tau=1_E Pi,
one has
 Var_mu G_n >= tau(K_s(G_n))/C_s.                      (1.2)
Here the original G_n is a passive function of the original coordinates
of Pi. The event need not be reflection invariant and its conditional law
need not center G_n. If Pi=J/Z_a(0) for an unnormalized positive joint J,
the numerator in (1.2) is
       J(1_E K_s(G_n))/Z_a(0),                         (1.3)
not J(1_E K_s(G_n))/J(E).

For lower bounds the omitted complement contributes nonnegatively.
The baseline subtraction must occur inside the event integral. The
different expression tau(exp(sG_n))-1 would pay for missing event mass
and is not what (1.2) uses. For the same reason (1.2) does not require
E to contain almost every configuration. Directly, and more sharply
when its second moment is available,
       Var_mu G_n >= tau(G_n^2).                      (1.4)
The real-source form is useful when the retained calculation produces a
partition ratio or a marked terminal density rather than a second jet.

If E has actual probability p_a and its normalized source increment is
at least q_a, (1.2) gives p_a q_a/C_s. Since 0<=K_s<=C_s on [-1,1], a
vanishing p_a cannot yield a fixed positive floor by this selected-event
bound, even if its normalized response is well behaved.

2. A QUANTITATIVE TERMINAL TRANSFER CONSUMER

This is an elementary sufficient criterion, not an asserted Wilson RG
estimate. Let A be a retained variable of Pi, p=Pi(E)>0, and let rho be
the law of A conditional on E. Let gamma be a specified probability
reference on the same A space, and assume
 d_TV(rho,gamma):=sup_(|f|<=1)|rho(f)-gamma(f)| <= Delta. (2.1)
The convention has maximum distance 2. Let g(A) be a real proxy in [-1,1]
and define the actual old-source error
 eta^2=E_Pi[(G_n-g(A))^2 | E].                         (2.2)
Neither (2.1) nor (2.2) follows just from an identity of partition masses.

Since probabilities have the same mass, for 0<=f<=B,
 |rho(f)-gamma(f)|<=B Delta/2:
apply (2.1) to 2f/B-1. Also |K_s'(x)|<=s sinh(s) on [-1,1]. Hence
 tau(K_s(G_n))
 >=p[gamma(K_s(g))-C_s Delta/2-s sinh(s) eta],          (2.3)
and therefore
 Var_mu G_n
 >=p[gamma(K_s(g))/C_s-Delta/2
                         -(s sinh(s)/C_s)eta]_+.      (2.4)
The positive part is valid because the left side is nonnegative.
This needs one integrated marked comparison at the chosen real s; it
does not require all source derivatives or all retained Borel tests if
those stronger inputs are unavailable.

There is a sharper second-moment version. If gamma(g^2)>=v,
 rho(g^2)>=max(v-Delta/2,0),
and the reverse triangle inequality in L2(Pi(.|E)) gives
 Var_mu G_n
 >=p[ sqrt(max(v-Delta/2,0))-eta ]_+^2.                (2.5)
No centering of g under the selected law is presumed. It is the original
G_n that is globally centered by reflection. Thus p>=p0>0, v-Delta/2>=v0>0
and eta<=sqrt(v0)/2 would give Var_mu G_n>=p0 v0/4.

A weaker first-mark criterion is enough. Set
 m_E(A)=E_Pi[G_n|A,E],
 eta_m=||m_E-g||_L2(rho).
Conditional Jensen gives
 tau(K_s(G_n))>=p rho(K_s(m_E)),
 tau(G_n^2)>=p rho(m_E^2).
Repeating the same Lipschitz and reverse-triangle arguments proves
(2.3)--(2.5) with eta_m in place of eta. Orthogonal conditional
decomposition gives eta_m<=eta, but eta_m can be small even when the
discarded conditional fluctuations have large variance. Thus this
consumer only needs a quantitative approximation of the FIRST marked
posterior, not small conditional variance or an entire old-source
generating family. This is a sufficient reduction, not an estimate of
that posterior under the Wilson law.

All three quantities p, Delta and eta refer to the actual original law
and its actual complete lift. In particular, at a native basic R
replacement a new reference draw is not automatically the forward coarse
coordinate of the old field. Its good reference variance can coexist
with zero conditional old-source pullback. Equation (2.2), or a directly
estimated first posterior or source coefficient, prevents that
substitution. The smaller error eta_m must also be computed in that
actual complete law, rather than a convenient replacement fiber.

The exact terminal disintegration makes the missing object particularly

so tau(K_s(G_n))=int b_s(A) dnu_E(A), b_s>=0. A theorem about nu_E alone
does not estimate b_s. One may instead estimate this one positive marked

TPM1's inspected theorem estimates the actual retained linear photon

3. AN EXACT GLOBAL MICROSCOPIC ANGLE-CAP BOUND

Theorem. Consider the original homogeneous SU(2) Wilson probability on
a periodic four-dimensional rectangular lattice, every period at least
4, V sites, and beta>0. For W in SU(2), let theta(W) in [0,pi] be its
principal angle, so (1/2)Re Tr W=cos theta(W). For 0<r<=pi/6 define
 A_r={theta(U_p)<=r for every plaquette p}.
Then
 mu_beta(A_r)
 <=[1+exp(-18 beta r)]^(-V/7)
 <=exp[-(V/14)exp(-18 beta r)].                        (3.1)
Integer rounding can strengthen the first exponent; the displayed real
exponent is sufficient. The same estimate holds if all plaquettes are
required only in one fixed orientation, such as (1,2).

Proof, independent conditional coordinates. Take all links in direction
1. Two such links occur in a common elementary plaquette only when their
base sites differ by plus or minus one unit in direction 2,3 or 4. Their
conflict graph has degree at most 6. Greedy selection yields a set I of
m>=V/7 links, no two of which occur in any common plaquette. Conditional
on every other link, the original action is a sum of single-link terms
for e in I plus a constant. The selected links are therefore conditionally
independent. All Haar measures remain normalized.

For each e=(x,1) in I choose the oriented plaquette p_e=(x;1,2), in which
e occurs first and positively. No other selected link belongs to p_e.
Its holonomy is U_e B_e with B_e fixed under this conditioning. The cap
C_e={theta(U_e B_e)<=r} is a Haar translate of the identity cap.

Fix any R in SU(2) of principal angle 3r and replace U_e by R U_e.
The bi-invariant spherical distance from the identity is theta. Its
reverse triangle inequality gives, on C_e,
 theta(R U_e B_e)>=theta(R)-theta(U_e B_e)>=2r>r.
Thus the Haar-preserving map sends C_e into a disjoint subset of its
complement. Choosing 3r<=pi/2 ensures the indicated principal angle for
R and avoids any ambiguity; the distance inequality itself is global.

The link belongs to exactly 6 plaquettes. In each, whether it occurs
positively or negatively, unitarity and cyclicity imply
 |h(U_p')-h(U_p)|
 <=||R-I||_op=2 sin(3r/2)<=3r,
where h(W)=1-Re Tr W/2. Thus the change of the FULL action is at most
18r in absolute value. For its normalized conditional density f_e,
       f_e(R U_e)>=exp(-18 beta r) f_e(U_e).           (3.2)
No bound on staples, boundary gauge or partition ratio is needed.

Integrating (3.2) on C_e and using the disjoint image proves
       1>=P(C_e | exterior)[1+exp(-18 beta r)].        (3.3)
Conditional independence now bounds the probability that all selected
plaquettes lie in their caps by the product of these bounds. A_r implies
that selected event. Integrate over the actual conditioned exterior to
get the first inequality of (3.1). Finally log(1+x)>=x/2 for 0<=x<=1
gives its second inequality. No external event probability or effective
RG conditional measure was used. This completes the finite proof.

4. WHAT THIS TEST DOES TO A DIRECT QED-STYLE ADAPTATION

Take a sequence with fixed positive physical four-volume, V_a comparable
to a^-4, beta_a -> infinity and beta_a<=C log(1/a). This is a stated
regime, not a proof of the Yang--Mills running coupling. For any fixed
q>=0 and C0>0 let
       r_a=C0(log(2+beta_a))^q/sqrt(beta_a).
Eventually r_a<=pi/6, and
 beta_a r_a=O(sqrt(log(1/a))
                    (log log(1/a))^q)=o(log(1/a)).
It follows rigorously from (3.1) that
       mu_(beta_a)(A_(r_a))<=exp[-a^(-4+o(1))] -> 0.   (4.1)
Equivalently, beta h_p bounded by a fixed power of log beta at EVERY
fine plaquette defines an asymptotically negligible global event in
this regime. To justify the equivalence of the cutoff scales, use
1-cos theta comparable to theta^2 near zero; changing fixed constants
does not change (4.1).

More generally the finite theorem forces vanishing whenever
       log V_a-18 beta_a r_a -> +infinity.
It gives no such conclusion when that condition fails. It also does not
describe the probability of a fixed finite set of coarse observables.

In TPM1 Proposition 3.2, equation (24), the good event instead restricts
all orthonormal Gaussian fluctuation coordinates of a FIXED linear tower
and the terminal curvature. At UV depth d its charge obeys
 e_j=e_* L^(-d/2), v_j=-log e_j=v_*+(d/2)log L,
and the box radius p_(0,j)=v_j^9 yields tails exp(-v_j^18/2), against
coordinate multiplicity of order L^(3d). This explicit summability
mechanism is superrenormalizable. Merely replacing v_j by order log d
does not give the same estimate against L^(4d).

Our cap event is NOT TPM1's linear-coordinate event and is NOT asserted
to equal a Bałaban native multiscale cutoff. Formula (4.1) is a separate
actual-Wilson result ruling out the naive version which discards every
bad microscopic plaquette with this usual logarithmic cutoff. It must
not be cited as a theorem that all native RG good sectors have zero mass.

For the positive-increment route, an event contained in A_(r_a) gives
 tau(K_s(G_n))<=C_s mu(A_(r_a)) -> 0,
so it cannot supply the desired fixed lower contribution through (1.2).
One must allow the relevant microscopic exceptional configurations in
the complete positive history sum, or choose a different actual sector
whose mass and marked response can be controlled. A positive sector at
a fixed coarse scale is not excluded, but its full normalized law and
its old-source coefficient remain to be proved.
