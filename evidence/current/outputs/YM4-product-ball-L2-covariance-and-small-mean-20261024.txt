PRODUCT-BALL L2 COVARIANCE AND A RADIUS-INDEPENDENT SMALL MEAN
24 October 2026. audit_two_root. Finite proof; native registration separate.
Archive and outputs unchanged.

RESULT

The proposed mean improvement is valid. On a fixed product of symmetric
convex balls, a uniform weighted Hessian gap gives the usual exponential
covariance estimate with L2 gradients, not only bounded gradients.
The Neumann boundary creates no loss because the spatial weight is
scalar on each entire coordinate block. Uniform centered fourth moments
then replace the crude noise-radius square in the primitive gradient.
For H_t=xQx/2+tV and the local quadratic-growth gradient premise below,

                   sup_i |E_t x_i| <= tan(C g t),

and hence at most2Cgt for Cg<=pi/4. Constants are independent of every
ball radius and the number of blocks. The proof concerns the original
fixed convex-product law; it does not apply after an arbitrary hard
mask, to a moving support, or to nonproduct constraint coordinates.

1. PRECISE FINITE ASSUMPTIONS

Let I be a finite set in a metric lattice of bounded density, with

              C_a=sup_i sum_j exp[-a d(i,j)]<infinity    (1)

at the fixed positive rates used below, uniformly in I. Each x_i is
in R^m, with m fixed (e.g. the finite Lie-algebra color dimension).
Let K=product_i B_(S_i)(0), with positive finite S_i. Unequal radii
are allowed. Put

 dnu_t=Z_t^-1 1_K exp[-H_t(x)]dx,
 H_t=one half x^T Q x+tV(x),       0<=t<=1.              (2)

Q is real symmetric, and H_t is C2 on a neighborhood of K (smooth
approximation is sufficient). Suppose, uniformly in x,t,

 H_t'' >= c0 I,
 Re <v, W H_t'' W^-1 v> >= cnu |v|^2                  (3)

for all real vectors v and the block-scalar weights
W_i=exp[+/-nu d(i,j)] centered at any j. More generally the argument
allows every fixed-in-x W with nu-Lipschitz logarithm. Both c0,cnu
are positive and independent of I,S_i,t.

The second inequality follows, for example, from the unweighted gap
and a small two-sided Schur commutator bound for W H_t'' W^-1-H_t''.
It is a COMPLETE Hessian condition, not just convexity without spatial
control. If it holds at t=0 and t=1 with the same constants, convexity
of the quadratic form gives it for their linear interpolation.

Finally assume the full primitive has the pointwise gradient bound

 |grad_j V(x)| <= C_V g sum_k exp[-mu d(j,k)](1+|x_k|^2) (4)

on ALL of K. C_V,mu are fixed. A bounded finite-read polynomial
gradient is a special case. All native stationary/current/gauge/Haar/
pivot terms must be included before verifying (3),(4). This proof
does not derive (4) from an undifferentiated interaction cap.

2. THE NEUMANN ONE-FORM IDENTITY ON THE PRODUCT

Fix one t and suppress it. Let

       L=-Delta+grad H dot grad

be the positive scalar generator with Neumann boundary condition.
For centered G, solve Lu=G-EG and set v=grad u. For smooth data its
weak differentiated equation is

 a(v,w)=<grad G,w>_(L2(nu)),                             (5)

on the vector form domain consisting of H1 vector fields tangent to
EACH smooth boundary face. Its form is

 a(v,w)=integral_K [sum_l <partial_l v,partial_l w>
                              +<H''v,w>]dnu
       +sum_faces integral_face II(v_tan,w_tan)dnu_face. (6)

On the face |x_i|=S_i the outer normal has entries only in block i;
the second fundamental form is (1/S_i) times the tangential inner
product in that block and is zero on other coordinate blocks. It is
nonnegative. The density in the face integral is the same normalized
e^-H surface density. There is no extra derivative of the normalizing
constant and no boundary force of unspecified sign.

To verify the sign in(6), differentiate the Neumann condition
grad u dot n=0 along a tangent vector w. It gives
(partial_n grad u) dot w=-II(grad u,w). Integrating L grad u by parts
and using grad Lu=L grad u+H''grad u gives exactly(5),(6).

Corners of a product need not be rounded into a nonproduct domain.
A precise approximation is available from the tensor products of
the smooth Neumann eigenfunctions on the INDIVIDUAL balls. Their
finite sums are smooth up to every face and form an operator core
for the product Neumann Laplacian. Convexity gives its H2 estimate;
the bounded smooth drift is a relatively bounded first-order
perturbation, so the Neumann Poisson solution above is in the same
H2 domain. Approximate u in that graph/H2 norm by these finite sums.
Their gradients converge in H1, including the face traces. To pass
the right side of(5), first integrate it as

 -integral (Lu)(div w-grad H dot w)dnu;

the boundary term is zero since w is tangent on every face. Thus
only L2 convergence of Lu is needed. Then use G in H1 and density
of smooth tangent tests. This proves(5) on the form domain. Face
intersections have codimension at least2 and create no edge integral.
Qualitative approximation constants may depend on the finite domain;
the quantitative estimates below use only(3), not those constants.

Equivalently, (6) is the absolute one-form/Neumann realization of
the differentiated weighted Laplacian. No claim about weighted
Neumann operators on general intersecting multiblock mask surfaces
is made: their tangent spaces need not be preserved by W.

3. LEGITIMATE L2 WEIGHTED AND SPATIAL COVARIANCE

For a spatial block-scalar W, both Wv and W^2v remain tangent on
every product face. W is constant in x and commutes with every face
II operator. Test(5) with W^2v. The first term in(6) becomes
sum_l ||W partial_l v||_2^2 and the boundary term is nonnegative.
The Hessian term is bounded below by cnu||Wv||_2^2 by(3). Hence

          ||W grad u||_2 <= cnu^-1 ||W grad G||_2.       (7)

The scalar Neumann integration by parts gives
Cov(F,G)=<grad F,grad u>. Therefore for F,G in H1(nu),

 |Cov(F,G)| <= cnu^-1 ||W^-1 grad F||_2
                                      ||W grad G||_2.  (8)

These are genuine L2 norms under the SAME exact normalized law.
Neither gradient needs a pointwise uniform bound.

There is also the familiar blockwise version. Let B be the positive
one-form operator of(6). The same weighted energy argument applies
to Bv=h for any L2 vector forcing h, not necessarily a gradient.
For h supported at block j, take W_k=exp[nu d(k,j)]. It yields

 ||P_i B^-1 P_j||_(L2->L2)<=cnu^-1 exp[-nu d(i,j)].      (9)

Since Cov(F,G)=<grad F,B^-1 grad G>, decompose the forcing into its
actual coordinate blocks and obtain

 |Cov(F,G)| <= cnu^-1 sum_(i,j) exp[-nu d(i,j)]
                         ||grad_i F||_2 ||grad_j G||_2. (10)

All sums are finite before taking uniform bounds. This does not
assume that each separated component of grad G is itself a gradient.
The one-form inverse is defined on arbitrary vector forcing by its
coercive form. For F=e dot x_i, |e|=1, either(8) with
W_j=exp[-nu d(i,j)] or(10) gives the required local L2 response.

4. CENTERED FOURTH MOMENTS INDEPENDENT OF THE CUT RADIUS

Linear tilts of nu have Hamiltonian H-s e dot x_i, on the SAME K,
and preserve H''. Taking W=1 in the preceding proof gives
Var(e dot x_i)<=1/c0 in every such tilted law. The second derivative
of its logarithmic moment generating function is this variance.
Integrating twice gives

 E exp[s e dot(x_i-E x_i)] <= exp[s^2/(2c0)].            (11)

Thus every color component has a centered Gaussian tail. A finite
color-net or component estimate gives

 E|x_i-m_i|^4 <= C_m c0^-2,
 ||1+|x_i|^2||_2 <= C_(m,c0)(1+|m_i|^2), m_i=E x_i.   (12)

For example the component tail gives fourth moment at most16/c0^2;
sum the fixed m components. No product radius, number of blocks or
ambient mean norm occurs. The moments are centered at the ACTUAL
coordinate mean, which need not initially have been estimated.

5. THE MEAN DIFFERENTIAL INEQUALITY

Let M(t)=max_i |m_i(t)|. From(4), Minkowski and(12),

 ||grad_j V||_2
 <=C_V g sum_k exp[-mu d(j,k)] ||1+|x_k|^2||_2
 <=C_1 g(1+M(t)^2),                                    (13)

where C_1 includes the uniformly finite row sum C_mu in(1).
The domain is fixed, so differentiation of its normalized ratio is
exactly

                 m_i'(t)=-Cov_t(x_i,V).                 (14)

Test against an arbitrary unit color vector before taking the norm.
Equation(8) or(10), together with the fixed spatial sum in(1), gives

          |m_i'(t)| <= C_2 g(1+M(t)^2).                 (15)

At t=0 the correlated truncated Gaussian is globally even under
x->-x because both its quadratic and the symmetric product K are
even. Thus m_i(0)=0 exactly, despite correlations between blocks.
Each m_i is C1 on this finite product; M is Lipschitz and its upper
Dini derivative obeys

              D+ M <= C_2 g(1+M^2), M(0)=0.

Comparison with the Riccati solution, or integrating arctan M at
almost every point, yields

       M(t)<=tan(C_2 g t),  0<=t<pi/(2 C_2 g).          (16)

For C_2 g<=pi/4 and 0<=t<=1 this is at most2 C_2 g t.
The bootstrap is noncircular: fourth moments were centered at the
unknown m_i, and M was closed only in the scalar differential
inequality. No initial bound g S^2 was inserted.

6. NATIVE USE AND LIMITATIONS

If the literal cancelled first-bare primitive satisfies(4), and the
complete action has the uniform weighted gap on its ENTIRE product
|x_i|<=S with S<=epsilon_cap/g, then(16) permits that large fixed
physical window. The old crude mean estimate Cg(1+S^2) can be replaced
by Cg. This alone does not prove(4), the whole containing product's
admission, a source-compatible inverse chart or a moving-domain tail.

An arbitrary held exterior force changes m(0). The same calculation
then starts from its actual M(0), not zero. For the native guarded
boundary z one can instead start at z=0 and use the independently
proved spatial boundary-score response along the admitted path:

 |E_z x_i| <= Cg+C sum_b exp[-nu d(i,b)]|z_b|.

This is valid only if that complete mixed Hessian/score premise is
verified; a distant boundary is not silently deleted. It explains
how the radius-independent central mean can coexist with larger
means near a retained boundary.

All estimates use the complete source-zero normalized product law.
Hard earlier-mask conditioning must still be transferred by its
actual mass or a separate theorem. Block-scalar weights, fixed
product support, complete Hessian accretivity and the primitive
gradient growth are essential hypotheses, not presentation choices.
