3. NORMALIZED COMPONENT RESPONSE WITHOUT AN INVERSE MASS

This lemma applies to the actual positive component integration
before the last boundary regrouping, at fixed real retained field
and fixed histories/maps/cuts. The integrand to which it is applied
may also be complex-valued.

Let mu be a finite positive measure, m=mu(1)>0, and P=mu/m.
Let Delta be real-valued and |Delta|<=a everywhere. Define

 mu_t(F)=mu(e^(t Delta) F), m_t=mu_t(1), P_t=mu_t/m_t,
                                                        0<=t<=1.

Then

 |d/dt log m_t| <= a,
 d/dt P_t F = P_t(F Delta)-P_tF P_tDelta,
 |d/dt P_t F| <= 2a ||F||_infinity,                       (3.1)

for an F independent of t. If F=F_t, add P_t(dF_t/dt).
The proof is differentiation of finite integrals and positivity.
It uses no lower bound on m. It gives, in particular,

 e^(-a)m <= m_1 <= e^a m,
 |P_1F-PF| <= min(2,2a)||F||_infinity.                    (3.2)

The less sharp but useful density comparison is

 e^(-2a) <= dP_1/dP <= e^(2a).                            (3.3)

For a complex Delta and a complex source z, the same integrals
are analytic. If |z|a<=log(3/2), then

 |m_z/m-1| <= e^(|z|a)-1 <= 1/2,
 |P_z F| <= 3 ||F||_infinity.                            (3.4)

In particular the logarithm continued from m has no zero on that
disc. Derivatives on a strictly smaller disc cost C a. This is a
local component statement; its radius depends on a, as it must.
It does not assert a volume-independent source disc for the full
partition function.

Application. With a=E_k v_k(Y), formula (3.1) controls the normalized
operation in (1.103). A component of extremely small mass does not
amplify a same-domain action perturbation by 1/m_Y. The extra cost
is its prepared insertion bound, hence its volume, not inverse mass.

Important limitation. An estimate

                 ||mu^+-mu||_TV <= epsilon W

where W is merely an upper bound for m and m^+ does NOT imply
||P^+-P||_TV=O(epsilon). For example take mu=epsilon W delta_0
and mu^+=epsilon W delta_1. The raw difference is small compared
with W; the normalized measures are mutually singular. Thus (3.1)
cannot be used for unmatched hard-cut/history domains without the
pointwise action representation just proved necessary.

4. THE RAW BOUNDARY OBJECT: ONE MASS PER COMPONENT

Fix disjoint retained components Y_1,...,Y_m. Denote their positive
operations by mu_i and their masses by m_i. Their private integration
variables are disjoint, with the exterior fields kept fixed. Let
B_A be finitely many bounded boundary functions and put B=sum_A B_A.
The exact object occurring before normalizing retained operations is

 D = (tensor_i mu_i)(e^B)
   = (product_i m_i) (tensor_i P_i)(e^B).                 (4.1)

Equation (4.1) retains each m_i ONCE. It is an identity for this
native representative; it does not assert pointwise equality of
that representative to the original Wilson density.

For same-domain perturbations mu_i^t=e^(t Delta_i)mu_i,
B=B_t, differentiation directly in the RAW expression yields

 D'_t = (tensor_i mu_i^t)
             [ e^(B_t) (sum_i Delta_i + B'_t) ].          (4.2)

Thus, if |Delta_i|<=a_i and |B'_t|<=b'_X on the joint support X,

 |D'_t| <= (sum_i a_i+b'_X)
              exp[ sup Re B_t + sum_i a_i ] product_i m_i.
                                                               (4.3)

The elementary proof is dominated differentiation. The same result
follows by differentiating the right side of (4.1): covariance
subtractions from P_i exactly combine with derivatives of m_i.
Dropping the masses before differentiating obscures this cancellation.

For complex external fields, apply (4.2) against the positive
reference measures used in the native component majorant; keep
the source's complex-to-real domination constants. The lemma does
not assert positivity of a complex-continued component kernel.

4A. A RAW CONNECTED GROUPING THAT DOES NOT REUSE MASSES

There is an exact finite combinatorial version of (4.1). Expand

 e^B = product_A (1+h_A),        h_A=e^(B_A)-1.

     = (product_(i in I)m_i) h_C^normalized.              (4.4)
