ACTUAL SPATIAL-FLOW DERIVATIVES AND GAUGE-ORBIT TEMPLATE ENTROPY
TURN66 -- 20 October 2026. gauge47.

RESULT AND PRECISE LIMIT

The TURN65 finite-template consumer asks for deterministic linear
source arrays with variance proxy O(a/beta), logarithmic family size
O(beta), and a separately controlled actual remainder. A natural
attempt is to approximate the actual magnetic probe's random derivative
by a uniform relative net in the source norm.

That particular uniform-net attempt fails on an explicit smooth
background for the ACTUAL nonlinear compact SU2 spatial flow.
Its gauge orbit alone has a fixed-relative-radius packing with

                         log M >= c a^-3.

The derivative has the desired squared source norm comparable to
a/beta. The obstruction is not a loose cover of arbitrary matrices:
every packed array is the true derivative of the same gauge-invariant
flowed scalar, at a gauge transform of one specified background.

There is also a one-edge counterexample to inheriting TURN65 after
a field-dependent gauge choice. Making the present link the identity
turns the scalar aligned increment into its positive plaquette energy.
It has strictly positive mean, contradicting the centered source bound
that such a direct substitution would require.

These results do NOT disprove the full finite-template consumer with
an actual L2 remainder. We do not prove a uniform Wilson mass lower
bound for the derivative-spread backgrounds. A different observable-
level approximation, a probability-dependent exception set, or a new
properly centered gauge-fixed source theorem remains possible.

1. THE ACTUAL PROBE, METRIC AND SOURCE ARRAYS

Fix physical L,s,g_*,alpha>0 and cubic spatial tori of spacing a=L/N,
with N tending to infinity. Use positively oriented spatial edges
and the Pauli/2 metric. The actual compact spatial flow is

                 dV_s/ds=-4a^-2 grad S_sp(V_s).

At a fixed spatial plaquette p0 of orientation12 let

 F_a(U)=exp[-alpha |2p(P12(V_s;0))/(g_*a^2)|^2],       (1.1)

where P=p0 I+i p.sigma. This is the original finite-regulator,
bounded, gauge-invariant scalar, not a Gaussian replacement.

Identify each link with its unit quaternion q_e in R4. Define b_e(U)
to be the ROUND-EUCLIDEAN tangential gradient of F_a on that factor:

 DF_a(U)[delta q]=sum_e b_e(U).delta q_e,
 b_e(U).q_e=0.                                      (1.2)

This is the canonical tangential coefficient array, not an arbitrary
extension in the normal direction. Its source norm at homogeneous
beta is

                 ||b||_beta^2=beta^-1 sum_e |b_e|^2. (1.3)

The quaternion gauge action is orthogonal on each edge. Equivariance
of the flow and invariance of F_a therefore imply the EXACT identity

               b_e(U^g)=O_e(g)b_e(U).                 (1.4)

For a center gauge g_x=sign_x I, sign_x in{+1,-1},
O_e(g)=sign_x sign_y I4 on e=(x,y).

2. A WEIGHTED STAR-CUBE ENTROPY LEMMA

Let V be any independent set of spatial vertices, so no edge joins
two members. Their incident edge stars are disjoint. For a fixed
coefficient array b put

 q_v=beta^-1 sum_(e incident to v)|b_e|^2, v in V.

Changing the center sign independently at the vertices of V flips
the complete coefficient array on each corresponding star and
leaves other edges unchanged. Thus for two such gauges indexed
by signs epsilon,eta,

 ||b^epsilon-b^eta||_beta^2
                 =4 sum_(v:epsilon_v!=eta_v) q_v.     (2.1)

Suppose |V|=d and q_v>=q_min>0. A greedy binary packing supplies
at least exp(c d) sign vectors with mutual Hamming distance at
least d/4. Indeed a Hamming ball of radius d/4 has at most
exp[H(1/4)d+o(d)] elements, with H(1/4)<log2; divide the
2^d-element cube by this bound.

The corresponding derivative arrays have mutual distance at
least sqrt(d q_min). Hence every cover by balls of radius less
than sqrt(d q_min)/2 needs at least exp(c d) centers, even when
the centers are arbitrary arrays not themselves on the orbit.

If d q_min>=c0||b||_beta^2, this is a fixed-relative-radius
entropy lower bound. It uses complete incident stars, so no
edge affected by the gauge action is discarded.

3. AN EXPLICIT SMOOTH BACKGROUND FOR THE NONLINEAR FLOW

Let k=2pi/L and choose a sufficiently small FIXED epsilon>0.
Set

 U2(x)=exp[i theta2(x)sigma3/2],
 theta2(x)=a epsilon sin(k x1),    U1=U3=I.            (3.1)

All links are Cartan valued. The spatial flow preserves this
subgroup and the dependence on x1 alone. The only curvature angle
is phi=d1 theta2 on12 plaquettes. With d the dimensionless lattice
exterior derivative and d* its counting-measure adjoint, the
abelian flow and its curvature equation are exactly

 theta'=-2a^-2 d* sin(d theta/2),
 phi'=-2a^-2 Delta_1 sin(phi/2).                     (3.2)

Here Delta_1=2-shift_1-shift_1^-1 is the positive scalar lattice
Laplacian in the x1 direction. The other curvature components
stay zero.

Initially |phi|<=k epsilon a^2. For small a this is inside the
interval on which sin(phi/2) is increasing. At a maximum of phi
the right side of(3.2) is nonpositive, and at a minimum it is
nonnegative. Thus, for all 0<=t<=s,

                    ||phi_t||infty<=k epsilon a^2.    (3.3)

The real angles may be evolved on their real covering space;
exponentiating gives the exact compact flow. This is not a
claim about a gauge-fixed quantum field.

Since sin(phi/2)-phi/2=O(epsilon^3 a^6), Duhamel for the scalar
heat equation, using ||Delta_1||infty<=4, gives

 ||phi_t-exp(-t Delta_1/a^2)phi_0||infty
                         <=C_s epsilon^3 a^4.        (3.4)

At the plaquette based at x1=0, the single Fourier mode therefore
satisfies

           phi_s(0)/a^2 -> epsilon k exp(-s k^2).     (3.5)

In particular this physical magnetic component is nonzero, with
size comparable to epsilon. It remains small but fixed as a->0.

4. EXACT CARTAN LINEARIZATION AND ITS POINT-CURL ROW

Allow an arbitrary Cartan variation of every initial spatial link,
not just a variation preserving the one-dimensional ansatz. Its
linearized angle equation is

 delta theta'=-L_t delta theta,
 L_t=a^-2 d* C_t d,
 C_t(p)=cos(phi_t(p)/2).                              (4.1)

C_t=1 except possibly on12 plaquettes, and(3.3) implies

 ||C_t-I||infty<=C epsilon^2 a^4,
 E_t=L_t-L0, L0=a^-2 d*d,
 ||E_t||_(l1->l1)<=C epsilon^2 a^2.                  (4.2)

Let T(s,0) be this exact linearized propagator and let

                   k_a=T(s,0)* d* delta_(p0).         (4.3)

Then delta phi_s(p0)=sum_e k_a(e) delta theta_e(0).
The initial covector d*delta_(p0) has four entries of modulus1.

Both L_t and L0 preserve divergence-free1-forms, since
d_0* d_1*=0. On this subspace the flat L0 is the componentwise
scalar lattice Laplacian, divided by a^2, by the discrete Hodge
identity. The covector in(4.3) is in that subspace. E_t also maps
into it.

To estimate(4.3), solve the adjoint equation in reversed time:
w'(r)=-L_(s-r)w(r), w(0)=d*delta_(p0).
Then w(s)=k_a. Duhamel relative to scalar heat and(4.2) give

 ||w(r)||1<=4 exp(C epsilon^2 a^2 r).                 (4.4)

Let H_r^a be the scalar heat kernel on the physical L-torus.
It is nonnegative with l1 mass1. Its finite Fourier series gives,
for 0<r<=s,

 ||H_r^a||infty
    <=min{1,C_L a^3(1+r^-3/2)},
 int_0^s ||H_r^a||infty dr<=C_(s,L) a^2.             (4.5)

For example the first bound follows by comparing
a^3 L^-3 sum_k exp(-r omega_a(k)^2) with the Gaussian sum,
using omega_a(k)>=2|k|/pi in the Brillouin zone. Splitting
the integral at r=a^2 proves the second estimate.

Let k_a^0=exp(-s L0)d*delta_(p0). Equations(4.2)--(4.5)
give a pointwise, uniform-in-volume-sequence bound

                 ||k_a-k_a^0||infty
                         <=C_(s,L) epsilon^2 a^4.    (4.6)

This estimate does not assume the full nonlinear tangent is
uniformly parabolic on arbitrary gauge backgrounds. It concerns
the explicit Cartan background, its exact Cartan variation,
and a uniformly small coefficient perturbation of scalar heat
on the divergence-free subspace.

5. A POSITIVE-VOLUME REGION OF ACTUAL DERIVATIVE COEFFICIENTS

For fixed s,L the scalar lattice heat kernel satisfies

 a^-4[H_s^a(x+a e_i)-H_s^a(x)]
                      ->partial_i H_s,L(x)           (5.1)

uniformly on lattice points, where H_s,L is the continuum heat
kernel on the physical torus. This elementary comparison concerns
the FLAT propagator only. To prove it, use its finite Fourier
series, divide the difference multiplier by a, and dominate
each summand by C|k|exp(-c s|k|^2). The latter is summable
on the fixed dual lattice. The Fourier tails give uniform
convergence in x.

The periodic heat kernel is nonconstant at every finite s>0.
Consequently there is a fixed open region O of positive volume
where |partial_1 H_s,L|>=c_(s,L)>0. The edge2 component of
k_a^0 is, up to a harmless shift/sign, the difference in(5.1).
Therefore

 |k_a^0(e)|<=C_(s,L)a^4 on every edge,
 |k_a^0(e2,x)|>=c_(s,L)a^4 for x in O                (5.2)

for sufficiently small a (shrinking O slightly if needed).
Choose the fixed epsilon small enough that the right side of
(4.6) is less than half the latter lower bound. Then(5.2)
holds for the ACTUAL k_a with changed constants.

It remains to relate this Cartan row to the complete gradient,
rather than merely one component of it. The entire background
and its flow are fixed by every constant Cartan gauge rotation.
F_a is gauge invariant. Its derivative in either transverse
color at each edge must therefore vanish: those two components
rotate nontrivially under this stabilizer. Only the Cartan
tangent component can remain. This is an exact symmetry
statement at the chosen background.

On the Cartan output plaquette,

 F_a=exp[-4alpha sin^2(phi_s/2)/(g_*^2 a^4)],
 partial_phi F_a
         =-2alpha sin(phi_s) F_a/(g_*^2 a^4).          (5.3)

By(3.5), its modulus is between c epsilon/a^2 and
C epsilon/a^2. For q_e(theta) the initial quaternion,
|q'_e(theta)|=1/2. The tangential gradient in(1.2) is exactly

        b_e=4(partial_phi F_a) k_a(e) q'_e(theta).     (5.4)

Thus, with constants depending on fixed physical parameters
and the chosen small epsilon,

 |b_e|<=C epsilon a^2 on every edge,
 |b_(e2,x)|>=c epsilon a^2 on a positive-volume O,
 c epsilon^2 a/beta<=||b||_beta^2
                         <=C epsilon^2 a/beta.       (5.5)

No Gaussian-law comparison has been used. This is a coefficient
calculation for the exact nonlinear compact observable.

6. APPLYING THE ENTROPY LEMMA TO THIS ACTUAL DERIVATIVE

Among the sites x in O choose an independent vertex set V.
The degree-six spatial graph admits such a set with

                    d=|V|>=c_(O,L) a^-3.

Each selected vertex has an incident edge2 coefficient satisfying
the lower bound in(5.5); all its at most six incident coefficients
obey the upper bound. Hence

       c epsilon^2 a^4/beta<=q_v
                         <=C epsilon^2 a^4/beta.     (6.1)

Equations(5.5),(6.1) give d q_min>=c0||b||_beta^2.
Section2 now supplies exp(c a^-3) true coefficient arrays,
all on one gauge orbit of(3.1), with pairwise distance at least
c'||b||_beta.

For some fixed delta>0, independent of a and beta, every
deterministic weighted-l2 net satisfying

 sup_g inf_(r<=M)
       ||b(U^g)-b^(r)||_beta <=delta ||b(U)||_beta

therefore has

                         log M>=c a^-3.               (6.2)

In particular log M=O(beta) is impossible for this uniform-net
strategy whenever beta=o(a^-3), including logarithmic coupling.
At the same time the actual coefficient variance proxy is of
the desired order a/beta by(5.5).

The obstruction is entirely within gauge copies of one physical
background. Gauge transformations leave every plaquette energy,
the output F_a and the full Wilson density unchanged. Requiring
a weak REPRESENTATIVE does not eliminate its other gauge copies
from the original integral.

One can also quantify the center-gauge average. If a ball of
radius delta||b||_beta contains one cube vertex, all other cube
vertices in it differ from that vertex in at most
C delta^2 d signs, by(2.1),(5.5),(6.1). For delta sufficiently
small, its fraction of the 2^d sign cube is at most exp(-c d).
The union of M such balls covers at most M exp(-c d) of the
uniform center orbit. This requires no Wilson-probability
independence: it is the finite, exact gauge average at a fixed
physical orbit.

7. WHY QUOTIENTING THE FRAMES DOES NOT INHERIT TURN65

Under a common gauge change on the present slice, the actual
aligned quaternion increment transforms by the same O_e(g)
as b_e. The pairing b.Delta is invariant. It is tempting to
choose a field-dependent gauge g(U) making b simpler and then
apply TURN65 to the transformed increments with fixed templates.

This requires a NEW source theorem. The existing Fourier/unitary
proof assumes deterministic coefficients before the field is
drawn. A field-dependent change of representative rotates the
increments in a way correlated with their endpoints.

There is a direct one-edge contradiction to a naive inheritance.
For e=(x,y), choose g_x=U_e^-1 and g_y=I (all other choices may
be fixed). The current transformed link is exactly I. Transform
the actual aligned next link by the same gauge:

 U_e^g=I,     (V^h)_e^g=U_e^-1(V^h)_e.

The scalar component of its increment is

 [Delta_e^g]_0
     =1-(1/2)Tr[U_e^-1(V^h)_e]
     =h_temporal,e >=0.                              (7.1)

It has strictly positive expectation in the finite original
Wilson law: the latter has a strictly positive density on the
compact link space, while h_temporal is positive on a nonempty
open set.

If the centered deterministic source bound survived this
adaptive frame unchanged, it would give

                 E exp(t h_temporal,e)<=exp(t^2/(2beta))

for all real t. The right derivative at t=0 would force
E h_temporal,e<=0, contradicting (7.1). This obstruction
already occurs on one edge, without any covering entropy.

It does not prohibit a legitimate gauge-fixed source theorem
with its drift, density and constraints retained. It shows
exactly why one cannot use the old theorem after merely declaring
the coefficients fixed in quotient coordinates.

8. WHAT HAS AND HAS NOT BEEN EXCLUDED

The result excludes a uniform small-relative-error deterministic
net of the actual full derivative array with log M=O(beta).
It also excludes direct inheritance of the centered source
bound under arbitrary field-dependent gauge fixing.

It does NOT exclude the more general TURN65 pathwise consumer

 |F1-F0|<=max_r |Z_r|+|R|,       E|R|^2=O(a).

A scalar difference could admit an approximation that does not
approximate its full derivative in the weighted-l2 norm, or an
actual-probability exception set could be allowed. The explicit
smooth backgrounds above have positive measure neighborhoods at
each fixed regulator, but no cutoff-uniform ORIGINAL Wilson mass
lower bound for those neighborhoods is proved here. Their gauge
copies do not resolve that missing physical probability estimate.

The useful next alternatives are therefore more specific:
retain the physical invariant pairing and prove a centered
conditional source estimate in a controlled local gauge, or
construct an observable-level finite approximation with a
proved original-law remainder that avoids a uniform derivative
net. Either requires new estimates. No nonlinear magnetic
increment, physical variance floor or complete YM4 existence
theorem is claimed by this note.
