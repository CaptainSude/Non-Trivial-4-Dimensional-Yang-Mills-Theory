       ||B_(4tau)(v,f)|| <= C_K tau^(-D) |v| p_S(f),   (4)

Here is the explicit deduction. The original copied bound uses one

2. FINITE-ENERGY TEMPORAL DISTRIBUTIONS HAVE AN EXACT TIME-ZERO VALUE

Fix E and g. Compress the form to P_E H. Equations(4) and the original
fixed-compact heat bound make

           T_(E,g)(f)=P_E q_v(f tensor g)P_E

a distribution in one real variable with values in B(P_E H). Its
translation law is (1), with bounded H_E=H|_(P_E H). Write
L_E B=H_E B-B H_E, a bounded linear operator on that Banach space.
The distributional translation law implies the differential equation

                         dT/dt=L_E T.

Multiplying by the smooth bounded-on-compacts operator exp(-tL_E)
gives a distribution with zero derivative. Such a Banach-valued
distribution is a constant times Lebesgue measure: every compact test
of integral zero is the derivative of a compact smooth test, so the

operator A_E(v,g) on P_E H such that

 P_E q_v(f tensor g)P_E
        = integral f(t) exp(tH_E) A_E(v,g) exp(-tH_E)dt. (6)

This argument is an elementary distribution ODE with a bounded
generator. It does not evaluate an arbitrary distribution at a point.
The exact covariance is what supplies that evaluation after compression.

For E'<E, compression of (6) to P_E' obeys the same identity and the

            P_E' A_E(v,g)P_E'=A_E'(v,g).              (7)

It remains to prove a POLYNOMIAL bound in E. Exponential inversion of
a fixed temporal averaging kernel would be insufficient.

3. UNIFORM INVERSION OF A SMALL POSITIVE TEMPORAL AVERAGE

Choose once and for all chi real, even, nonnegative, smooth, supported
in (-1,1), and integral chi=1. Define

             Lambda(s)=integral chi(t)exp(ts)dt.

Lambda is smooth and positive on the real line; evenness and positivity
give Lambda(s)>=1 there. Equation(6) implies

 Q_E:=P_E q_v(chi_tau tensor g)P_E
                         =Lambda(tau L_E) A_E(v,g).  (8)

Take tau=(1+E)^-1. All spectral differences of H_E have |tau omega|<=1.
Choose a smooth compactly supported function m on R equal to
1/Lambda(s) on [-1,1]. Its Fourier transform has a fixed finite L1 norm.
The multiplier

 M_(E,tau)(B)=integral m_hat(u)
       exp(iu tau H_E)B exp(-iu tau H_E)du             (9)

therefore has operator norm <=||m_hat||_1, independent of E and tau.
Fourier normalization is chosen so m(s)=integral m_hat(u)e^(ius)du.
The spectral theorem on the left and right commuting actions of H_E
shows M_(E,tau)Lambda(tau L_E)=Id. One direct verification uses finite
spectral step approximations to H_E, which converge in OPERATOR NORM
because H_E is bounded. The Fourier integral in (9) passes in norm
by its L1 majorant; the compact-time integral defining Lambda also
passes in norm. Applying (9) to (8) recovers A_E.

Undo the heat in (4) only on P_E. Since tau E<=1,

 ||Q_E|| <= exp(8tau E)||B_(4tau)(v,chi_tau tensor g)||

This is the desired polynomial finite-band estimate. The bound on the

4. SPATIAL SCHWARTZ CONTINUITY AND THE CONSISTENT TIME-ZERO FORM

Equation(7) defines a form A_v(g) on D_fin. Spatial covariance of q
and uniqueness in (6) give

      A_E(v,g_y)=U(y)A_E(v,g)U(y)^*.

As U(y) commutes with P_E, the constant in (10) is uniform for translated
unit spatial compacts. Choose a smooth unit-cube partition of unity.
The sum of the translated local seminorms is bounded by

 p_(S,4)(g)=sum_(|alpha|<=S) sup_x
                       (1+|x|)^4 |partial^alpha g(x)|.

The convergent sum of these local forms extends A_E to all spatial
Schwartz g, with

          ||A_E(v,g)||<=C |v|(1+E)^p p_(S,4)(g).      (11)

The exponent 4 is simply greater than the spatial dimension 3.
This is where a uniform local estimate becomes a global tempered
estimate. It does not require a new infinite-support source theorem.

The time-zero form need NOT be an operator on D_fin. Its heat
regularization is nevertheless bounded: using unit energy bands in
(11) and block Schur gives

 ||exp(-tau H)A_v(g)exp(-tau H)||
       <= C |v|min(1,tau)^(-(p+1))p_(S,4)(g).          (12)

For example, a row sum is bounded by
sum_n exp[-tau(m+n)](1+m+n)^p <= C tau^(-(p+1)),
uniformly in m. The column bound is identical.

Equations(6),(12) identify the original Euclidean heat forms without

5. REAL-TIME SMEARING AND OFF-DIAGONAL ENERGY DECAY

Put P_n=1_[n,n+1)(H), n>=0. Define the consistent blocks
A_mn(v,g)=P_m A_E(v,g)P_n for E>max(m,n)+1. Equation(7) makes this
independent of E. From (11),

       ||A_mn(v,g)||<=C |v|(1+m+n)^p p_(S,4)(g).       (13)

For a spacetime Schwartz test f define blockwise

 Phi_mn(v,f)=integral_R exp(itH_m)
                  A_mn(v,f(t,cdot)) exp(-itH_n)dt.    (14)

This norm-converges because of (13) and Schwartz decay. Write
H_m=m+K_m, H_n=n+K_n, with ||K_m||,||K_n||<=1. The integrand is
exp[it(m-n)] times
exp(itK_m)A_mn(v,f(t,cdot))exp(-itK_n).
Integrate by parts k times when m!=n. Derivatives hitting the two
bounded residual generators cost at most constants depending on k;
the others hit f. Combining this with the direct m=n bound yields

 ||Phi_mn(v,f)|| <= C_k |v|(1+m+n)^p
                 (1+|m-n|)^(-k) N_k(f),              (15)

where the explicit finite Schwartz-continuous seminorm

 N_k(f)=sum_(j<=k,|alpha|<=S) integral_R dt sup_x
               (1+|x|)^4 |partial_t^j partial_x^alpha f(t,x)|

is bounded by an ordinary Schwartz seminorm using time weight 2.
No compact Fourier support assumption is needed. Such support would
give exact energy transfer, but the decay in (15) is enough.

Use 1+m+n<=C(1+n)(1+|m-n|). Multiplication of (15) on the right by
(1+H)^-p cancels (1+n)^p. Choosing k>p+1 makes both block row and
column sums uniformly finite. The elementary block Schur test gives
the bounded operator in (2). For (3), insert (1+m)^r on the left,
(1+n)^(-(p+r)) on the right and choose k>p+r+1. The remaining matrix
is again bounded by C(1+|m-n|)^(p+r-k).

These consistent matrices define Phi_v(f) on Dom(1+H)^p and in
particular on D_infty. Formula(3) shows Phi_v(f)D_infty subset D_infty.
The field map is continuous from Schwartz tests into continuous
operators on this common smooth-energy domain. In particular it is
an operator-valued tempered distribution in the usual domain sense.

Real-time and spatial covariance are exact, inherited from (14) and
