RESULT

The first rejected switch does have a quantitative, source-independent
endpoint estimate that was not the arbitrary-direction old-record row
refuted in TURN85. Hold the GLOBAL background of Large-I(1.53) fixed,
and include its complete native constraint flattening. The switch changes
the physical free angles by O(H_Z^(j)); the complete current field V_j,
including its dependent pivot bonds, consequently changes by

                  C_L epsilon_j exp(-R_j).                 (R.1)

Here L is the fixed primitive ratio. There is no factor g_j^-1 in this
group-valued estimate, no differentiation of C with respect to the
background, and no earlier-refinement factor. Section3 proves this at
the actual small real fluctuation chart used before(1.55). The estimate
does NOT assert that a counterfactual unswitched coordinate satisfies
the same rejected characteristic function.

This identifies the input that an older source coupling would have to
read: the COMPLETE fluctuating V_j, not the background M^j(U_k^(n+1)).
The first R integral is local and can retain old A slots, exterior fields,
and gauge-fixing factors. The primary formulas do not identify it with
the complete homogeneous, gauge-invariant kernel required to iterate
TURN85's corrected charts at a fixed local exterior. Section5 gives the
exact covariance/anchoring distinction and an explicit cut diagnostic.

The proposed stronger two-background chart identity has a useful exact
test. Interior determining rows really are common, because the LOCAL
background in(1.26) has index n+1, just like the GLOBAL background in
(1.53). Full chart equality additionally requires the entire physical
constraint and boundary data to agree. Matching some interior rows is
not matching a full fiber. Section4 states the exact identity on an
actually common fiber and locates the noncommon boundary reads. It is
not asserted for the full first-R component without that matching.

2. WHICH FIELD IS THE OLD SOURCE'S ARGUMENT?

Set n=j-h. With the same fixed outer determining data o, define

 U^+=U_k^(n+1)(o),       B=M^j(U^+),
 U_Z^+=U_(k,Z)^(n+1)(o), W=M^j(U_Z^+).                    (2.1)

The second line is the literal(1.26): its exponent is j+1-h=n+1,
not n. Large-I(1.53),(1.58) give

      V'_j=V_j B^-1,             B=exp(i H_Z^(j)) W.       (2.2)

All these are linkwise products in the common source convention, with
the comparison gauge already included in the definition of H as in
(1.40)--(1.41). One must not omit that comparison gauge and then treat
H as the difference of two unrelated coordinate representatives.

After removing the coarse delta-functions, use x=g_j b for the
PHYSICAL free-angle coordinates, and write the native chart as

   a_B(x)=C_B x-h_B Dtilde_B(C_B x),
   Psi_B(x)=exp(i a_B(x)) B.                             (2.3)

This is(1.60) with its complete C/h/D composition. C copies each free
nonpivot; h changes only the designated pivots. Thus on a nonpivot e,
Psi_B(x)_e=exp(i x_e)B_e. Every pivot still obeys the original nonlinear
coarse constraint. The symbol b denotes the new B_j free coordinate,
not the old A_j integrated on the separate set in(1.60).

If a complete homogeneous original-field prefix really ends at tier j,
its conditional source is (K_(j-1)...K_0 F)(V_j), evaluated here at
Psi_B(x). It is not evaluated at B. In the general local R history the
old source can also depend on A_j, earlier live proper-tier slots, and
the exterior/geometry. No source-resolved factorization that removes
those arguments follows from(2.3).

Likewise the background/current pair used by an inherited effective
coefficient is the actual U_k^n(Psi_B(x)) and its prescribed current.
It is not legitimate to replace the source by a function of U^+ alone.
When original fine fields are reconstructed below tier j, every earlier
map and inverse gauge belongs to that source read. The present result
controls the CURRENT endpoint, not that entire inverse record.

3. AN ACTUAL FINITE ENDPOINT ESTIMATE AT THE REJECTED SWITCH

Freeze o, hence B,H,C_B,h_B,Dtilde_B and the primitive free/pivot set.
The real switch from B119(1.22), invoked in Large-I immediately after
(1.58), has on each changed free bond the group form

 exp(i x_old)=exp(i x) exp(i sigma s(x)H_e),             (3.1)

where sigma is the fixed orientation sign, 0<=s<=1, and the switch is
smooth on its real domain. On some bonds the prescribed switch is1.
Nothing below needs an OCR choice of sigma or a plateau consequence
from the labels. Write x_old=S_H(x) using the principal weak logarithm.

Before this change, (1.27),(1.54) put the physical relative group field
in an O(delta'_j) neighborhood of1; after it, (1.59) supplies the
corresponding weak group domain. Since
||H||<=C epsilon_j exp(-R_j)<<delta'_j, both physical free-coordinate
arguments, and the short real interpolation between them, lie in one
strict primitive flattening neighborhood for a sufficiently weak floor.
This is a neighborhood of the analytic coordinate map, not necessarily
the support of every hard characteristic in the branch.

The logarithm is uniformly Lipschitz on that weak group neighborhood.
Unitary Duhamel applied to(3.1) therefore gives

                ||S_H(x)-x||_infinity <= C ||H||_infinity. (3.2)

The norm is the current-link Lie-algebra operator sup norm. Although
the RESCALED free variable b changes by O(H/g_j), the argument x=g_j b
of the physical reconstruction changes only by O(H).

The native unit-step flattening bounds give, on the same strict domain,

 ||C_B||<=C_L,  ||h_B||<=C_L,
 ||D Dtilde_B(y)||<=C_L ||y||.                           (3.3)

These are the uniform primitive chart bounds/implicit-solution estimates
in B109267--268 used already to construct(1.60). The finite constant can
depend on the fixed primitive ratio and the fixed gauge/averaging rules;
it does not depend on the earlier fine refinement or the number of
current cells. In particular C has the identity restriction on free
coordinates. Equation(3.3) is a NOISE derivative at fixed background,
not an estimate on background differentiation of C,h or Dtilde.

More explicitly, the flattening equation has the form
Dtilde(y)=R_B(y-h_B Dtilde(y)), where R_B starts at degree2. On a fixed
primitive weak chart its finite-average products/logarithms give
||D R_B(z)||<=K_L||z||, while the selected pivot coefficient has a
uniform inverse. Choose the strict radius so K_L r||h_B||<1/2.
Implicit differentiation bounds D Dtilde by 2K_L times the argument
radius. Linear elimination then bounds C_B using that same pivot
inverse. These bounds are taken in the actual native weak background
family used for(1.60); they are not extended to arbitrary compact-link
backgrounds. Real gauge changes allowed by that construction act
orthogonally on these link-algebra norms. This explains both the noise
derivative in(3.3) and its independence of the number of current cells.

From(2.3) and the fundamental theorem of calculus,

 ||D_x a_B|| <= C_L(1+C_L ||C_B x||),
 ||Psi_B(S_H x)-Psi_B(x)||_infinity <= C_L ||H||_infinity
                               <= C_L epsilon_j exp(-R_j). (3.4)

The second inequality uses unitary Duhamel again. All dependent pivot
links are included. Both outputs solve the SAME actual coarse
constraint, because the same fiber chart is used at both arguments.
The outer background and all retained old A slots stay fixed. No
background minimum response, moving gauge derivative, or all-age
original-field estimate is used in this proof.

For a four-link plaquette wholly read from this current field,
unitarity gives ||P_old-P_samechart||<=4C_L epsilon_j exp(-R_j).
This is a current-tier statement. The analogous original fine plaquette
requires the lower source reconstruction. Also a test on the second
endpoint is a counterfactual test on the SAME baseline measure; it need
not be an actual second realization of the same branch or the same
old conditional law. Full-density coordinate invariance is instead the
exact statement in turn86_native_switch_score_tail.txt.

This gives the actual small-input factor that was only an implication
in TURN85, Section5. The separate contraction of a homogeneous old
prefix, if admitted with all its weights and corrected states, can use
this factor. It cannot be concluded merely from the size of H or from
the first R component's real-field cut.

4. TESTING THE PROPOSED FULL-TRANSLATION FIBER REPAIR

Here the comparison sign can be fixed by the desired equality rather
than guessed from the switch typography. Suppose two COMPLETE physical
charts Psi_B and Psi_W have the same coarse constraints, same fixed
physical exterior and gauge conditions, and same free/pivot set. Define
the full free-coordinate translation S_1 by

 exp(i(S_1 x)_e)=exp(i x_e) W_e B_e^-1
                                      on every free e.   (4.1)

Then Psi_B(S_1 x) and Psi_W(x) have identical physical nonpivot links.
They solve the identical nonlinear constraint with identical remaining
data. The native unique weak pivot solution therefore implies

                    Psi_B(S_1 x)=Psi_W(x).                (4.2)

This proof includes the C/D dependence without differentiating it in
the background. It is an equality of full charts on a common fiber,
not the false identity obtained by dropping the pivot correction. If
S_s is the switched version of this translation, the same fixed-chart
argument as Section3 bounds Psi_B(S_s x)-Psi_B(S_1 x) by C_L||H||.

There IS primary evidence for an exact interior part of the fiber test.
Large-I(1.15)--(1.20) uses the same retained V determining data on the
part of Z outside Omega_k. Both n+1 minima in(2.1) thus have the SAME
specified determining row at tier j+1 wherever that row, including its
complete averaging footprint, belongs to the common interior data.
Since averaging is composed by its defining iteration,

 M(B)(c)=M^(j+1)(U^+)(c)=V_(j+1)(c)
       =M^(j+1)(U_Z^+)(c)=M(W)(c)                       (4.3)

on those rows. For example, a full primitive block/row strictly inside
the common raw determining region, with its entire read collar there,
passes this constraint-value test. No approximate minimum estimate is
needed for(4.3).

But(4.3) alone is not (4.2). For an actual finite subchart one must retain
ALL constraint rows touched by its free links, all pivot dependencies,
and its fixed physical boundary reads. A row that crosses the local
determining-set boundary is not justified by calling its endpoints
interior. If closure of the chosen row family reaches a noncommon row,
the resulting chart is not certified to be the same fiber. This note
does not assume strict finite dependence of every implicit nonlinear
pivot solution merely from finite range of the original averages.

The native localizer was introduced precisely to replace exterior
determining data. In(1.56) the remaining determining-data discrepancy
is supported in a boundary layer of Z; it is small, not stated to be
zero. A full component chart includes the corresponding boundary and
retained-field reads. They cannot be changed to local completion data
while the old exterior is simultaneously declared fixed.

There is an exact diagnostic if a proposed extension crosses such a
row. With the same physical free links q, write the two pivot solutions
as p(q;c_B,e_B) and p(q;c_W,e_W), where c is the full coarse datum and
e the fixed boundary datum. The missing term is

        p(q;c_B,e_B)-p(q;c_W,e_W).                        (4.4)

If c_B differs from c_W on even one retained constraint, equality of
the two complete fields would contradict that constraint. If the coarse
data agree but the fixed boundary reads differ, the full-field equality
already fails on those reads. A bound for(4.4) requires their explicit
response/decay estimate. Interior equality of selected rows does not
remove it. Equation(3.4) avoids this issue by never switching backgrounds.

Thus (4.2) is valid on an ACTUALLY matched interior fiber and provides
a precise check for a future local construction. The displayed native
formulas establish (4.3), but not the complete matched boundary chart
for the whole first R component. No unrestricted fiber equality is
claimed here.

5. WHAT GAUGE COVARIANCE SURVIVES AT FIXED LOCAL DATA?

Large-I178--179 explicitly notes that some preparatory functions are
not invariant under arbitrary transformations of V_j. On printed182,
before the additional Faddeev--Popov insertion, the integral(1.25) has
a specified gauge subgroup: gauges on the stated newly freed fine-site
set that are1 at its sites in T^(j+1). The precise set is displayed in
largeI182_high.png. This is a condition on both the region and anchors,
not authorization to apply every homogeneous comparison gauge with
the exterior held fixed.

The relative-field cut(1.27) is conjugation invariant if BOTH V_j and
its local background W are transformed together, with their complete
averages/paths. The radial free cut is invariant under its appropriate
adjoint noise transformation. A radial switch can likewise be chosen
conjugation covariant within the native allowed prescription. However
the explicitly gauge-fixing factors(1.5),(1.6) read V_j(y,x); transforming
only one of their endpoints does not conjugate this matrix.

A concrete elementary check uses V_j(y,x)=1, v(y)=1 and
v(x)=exp(i t sigma3). The changed gauge-fixing Wilson factor is
1-cos(t), while it was0. Its hard neighbor cut can also change. This
does not violate the source's anchored covariance: this is exactly a
transformation outside the fixed-data subgroup. It demonstrates why
an arbitrary local comparison gauge cannot be passed through those
weights at unchanged data.

For a local gauge-covariant kernel with interior datum w and exterior e,
the correct identity transforms the pair (w,e), and every remaining
test with them. Gauge invariance as a function of w ALONE is available
only for gauges preserving e and the other fixed restrictions. TURN85's
operator iteration uses invariance of the COMPLETE lower functional at
the intermediate field. At a fixed local exterior it cannot use the
joint covariance identity while silently suppressing the changed e.

The archived homogeneous comparison gauge has a supported/exponentially
decaying construction; it is not asserted to equal1 on the exact
first-R boundary and anchor set. Truncating it to1 gives a different
map and introduces an annular comparison of the complete source/kernel.
The primary minimum's decay is not already that original-source bound.
Conversely, applying a final gauge ONLY to a fully reconstructed closed
trace is harmless. That single scalar fact does not allow the same
gauge to be moved successively through local conditional weights.

This fixed-exterior issue is NOT an obstruction to gauge invariance of
the full Wilson law. A real gauge on a set of lattice vertices can be
extended by1 outside it; the entire old field, every exterior and
ancillary read, and the density representation can be transported
together. Closed original traces are then unchanged. A discontinuity
at the artificial boundary is not a physical action cost. What does
not follow is a quantitative contracting LOCAL homogeneous chart on
the resulting complete conditional state, with its cut/domain
admission uniform in age. Transforming all records may remove the
fixed-data convention, but it leaves precisely that analytic/geometric
identification to prove. Neither the local covariance statement nor
the unmarked domain inclusion supplies this contraction by itself.
