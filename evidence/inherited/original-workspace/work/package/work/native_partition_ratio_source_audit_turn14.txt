2. CONDITIONAL stronger lemma: normalized consequence of a Haar sandwich

This section is elementary mathematics, not an extra theorem attributed to
Balaban. After the correction in Section7, its pointwise upper hypothesis is
NOT source-attached. On a fixed finite current lattice let H be product Haar
measure on its links, or its corresponding normalized quotient measure. Let
rho>=0 be the complete density, and suppose the printed bound reads

  ell(V):=chi_k(V) exp[-B_k(V)-E_- N_k]
       <= rho(V) <= u:=exp[E_+ N_k].                         (2.1)

Assume I_k:=integral chi_k exp(-B_k) dH>0. Set Z=integral rho dH and
mu(dV)=rho(V)dH/Z. Integration and division give the EXACT implications

  exp(-E_- N_k) I_k <= Z <= exp(E_+ N_k),
  exp[-B_k(V)-(E_-+E_+)N_k] chi_k(V)
       <= dmu/dH(V) <= exp[(E_-+E_+)N_k]/I_k.               (2.2)

In particular, for any measurable cylinder C of current variables,

  mu(C) >= exp[-(E_-+E_+)N_k]
           integral_C chi_k(V) exp[-B_k(V)] dH(V).           (2.3)

Thus, under (2.1), no normalized rare-history estimate is needed for a cylinder
floor at FIXED total stopped volume. To make the floor uniform in original
UV refinement one must separately check, for the same density:
  (a) N_k is bounded after stopping-scale rescaling;
  (b) the terminal couplings lie in a fixed permitted range and E_++E_- is
      correspondingly bounded;
  (c) a fixed positive-Haar-measure set O is in {chi_k=1};
  (d) sup_O B_k is bounded uniformly in the preceding refinement.
If B_k=gamma_k A(U_k(V)), (d) is a bound for the actual minimizing background
action, not automatically a bound for an unrelated coarse plaquette action.
At fixed stopped coupling these are plausible native minimum inputs, but
they should not be silently read out of the damaged formula.

For bounded continuous functions f_1,...,f_r, (2.2) gives

  integral |sum_a c_a f_a|^2 dmu
    >= exp[-B_O-(E_-+E_+)N_k]
        integral_O |sum_a c_a f_a|^2 dH.                    (2.4)

If the restrictions f_a|O are linearly independent in L2(H|O), the finite
Haar Gram matrix has a positive smallest eigenvalue. Equation(2.4) is then a
uniform ordinary L2 Gram floor. For centered functions, use {1,f_1,...,f_r}
and minimize the right side over the constant: independence modulo constants
is the precise hypothesis. This establishes nondegenerate classical coarse
randomness, not an Osterwalder--Schrader Gram floor.

Under the EXTRA pointwise upper hypothesis, (2.2) gives weak-* compactness in L-infinity(H)
for a fixed finite current graph. This is compactness of these coarse laws;
it is neither projective consistency across stopping scales nor a continuum
Yang--Mills construction.

3. Why a volume bound is not the needed local ratio or energy tilt

Common vacuum extraction is an exact scalar cancellation: multiplying rho
by exp(-E_UV) does not change mu or a ratio of two integrals of THE SAME
density. It does not cancel the error factors exp(+-E N) in an inequality.
Nor may separately renormalized source theories be divided while dropping
the source-dependent difference E_UV(t)-E_UV(0).

A concrete counterexample isolates the limitation of volume-only bounds.
Let a product probability space contain a coordinate x with H(x>=0)=1/2.
For N>0 set rho_N=e^(CN) on x>=0 and e^(-CN) on x<0. Then

  e^(-CN)<=rho_N<=e^(CN),
  mu_N(x<0)=1/(1+e^(2CN)).                                  (3.1)

The event depends on one coordinate, yet its normalized mass vanishes
exponentially in TOTAL volume. The toy lower action is zero and chi=1, so
even a global positive density floor of the form(2.1) cannot improve this
logical implication. It does not contradict fixed-N conclusion(2.3).

The upper side of (2.1) gives no surviving negative Wilson energy. For an
energy tilt exp(lambda gamma S(V)), using it directly incurs
u integral exp(lambda gamma S) dH. Compactness bounds S, but only gives the
large gamma cost, not a small-coupling pressure estimate of order log gamma
per stopped cell. The lower side cannot provide an upper numerator bound.
One needs, for example, an admitted same-vacuum tilted-density theorem or
a local connected-pressure estimate

  log integral exp(F_K) rho dH - log integral rho dH
       <= C |K|                                            (3.2)

with the desired coupling dependence and the actual source F_K. No such
general source statement was located in the cited theorem hypotheses.
For a uniformly bounded F_K, the trivial bound by ||F_K||_infinity suffices;
the difficulty concerns cutoff-dependent energies/renormalized sources.

4. Positivity, history normalization, and observable identity

The exact averaging step alone defines a positive deterministic pushforward
of the preceding measure. The published full density after R is a different
object from that unmodified pushforward. A formula for a current observable
must use the observable's exact R transport before (2.3) or (2.4) can be
identified with an observable of the original Wilson measure.

Complex analytic or localized coefficients need not be positive. Bounds for
their absolute sums are not bounds for probabilities of the history labels.
Even for a positive unnormalized kernel K, the identity

  Kf=(K1) [Kf/K1]                                          (4.1)

shows why a small K1 does not give a small normalized operator. A local
denominator comparison or an integrated rooted mass estimate is genuinely
additional to an absolute kernel bound. The fixed-Y final expansion in the
source does not supply a sum of all surviving history geometries in a small
polymer norm at fixed exterior datum.

Also a positive density sandwich alone cannot prove reflection positivity
or quantum nontriviality. Product Haar measure itself has a positive density
floor. For independent reflected halves, the OS norm of a centered observable
supported strictly in one half is zero. An ordinary Gram floor such as(2.4)
must not be substituted for a separated OS Gram floor.

5. Exact innovation ledger: independence is unnecessary, locality is not

This is a possible alternative CONSUMER, proved abstractly here. Suppose an
actual positive construction has already been identified with a finite
Markov chain X_0,...,X_J, with kernels K_j(X_j,dX_(j+1)). Given a terminal
square-integrable f_J, define f_j=K_j f_(j+1). With the natural filtration,
M_j=f_j(X_j) is the martingale E[f_J(X_J)|X_0,...,X_j]. Orthogonality gives

  E|f_J(X_J)|^2-E|f_0(X_0)|^2
    = sum_(j=0)^(J-1) E Var(f_(j+1)(X_(j+1))|X_j).          (5.1)

All cross-covariances have analogous polarized identities. No conditional
independence among spatial components is required for (5.1).

If a kernel, for a given input x, changes only links in a set D_j(x), and
the full possible output support is known, then for a real observable

  Var_K(f|x) <= (osc_(D_j(x)) f)^2/4.                       (5.2)

For a complex observable the safe bound by its squared oscillation also
holds, or use (1/2) E|f(Y)-f(Y')|^2. This bound has no unknown denominator.
But it must be summed for the ACTUAL backward observables f_(j+1), the
actual supports, and the actual input law.

If a random choice C selects the refill domain, conditioning on C incurs

  Var(f(Y)|x)=E_C Var(f(Y)|x,C)
                +Var_C(E[f(Y)|x,C]).                       (5.3)

Dropping the second term is invalid. Auxiliary choices can be exposed as
additional Markov steps, provided their own innovation is retained. Similarly,
factorization of bare integration operators over disjoint domains does not
imply conditional independence after multiplying by interactions joining
those domains. Either keep the whole joint kernel or expose an exact
sequential disintegration; the latter can have nonlocal conditional kernels.

Primary-supported geometry conclusion: there are component-supported,
chronologically ordered retained operations, and a bounded local primitive
appears in II's denominator analysis. The evidence inspected does NOT
identify the complete R as a product of independent fixed-size refills, nor
bound the oscillations in (5.2). Its large-field sets are selected through
multi-scale field tests and history geometry, rather than fixed in advance.
The precise original positive Markov transition and selection sigma-field
would have to be recovered from Part I or proved directly; positivity of a
single real comparison integral is insufficient for that identification.

7B. A precise positive replacement lemma, conditional on the literal rule

There is no mathematical obstacle to a probability kernel having singular
output. Let a branch have variables (e,x), where e is the COMPLETE retained
exterior, and positive finite measure

  tau(de,dx)=m(de) q_e(dx),   q_e a probability measure.

Let s_e(dy) be a measurable probability reference law on the same component
configuration space, possibly supported on a gauge slice. The rule

  tau'(de,dy)=m(de) s_e(dy)                                  (7.3)

is exactly the pushforward of tau by

  K((e,x),d(e',y))=delta_e(de') s_e(dy).                     (7.4)

Indeed integration against any bounded measurable F gives the same result
on both sides. K is positive, K1=1, preserves the exterior, and discards x.
This proves mass preservation and supplies the joint old/new law. It does
NOT preserve a general component observable, nor a later nonlinear average.
The reference may depend on a fixed current state law; then K=K_mu is still
a legitimate kernel for that one prescribed evolution, not a single linear
map on the space of all input laws.

If the input is a finite/countable POSITIVE branch sum M=sum_h tau_h and each
branch has (7.4), write w_h=d tau_h/dM. Then

  K_M(v,dv')=sum_h w_h(v) K_h(v,dv')                         (7.5)

is a probability kernel M-a.e., and M K_M=sum_h tau_h K_h.
Equivalently keep h as an augmented state variable. This construction fails
for a signed Mayer/log decomposition: its coefficients are not posterior
probabilities. It also requires the actual branch rule and exterior to be
specified before forgetting h. Arbitrarily coupling M to a same-mass output
by independent sampling proves existence of some kernel, but supplies none
of the source's locality or innovation estimates.

7C. Gauge equivalence and the native attachment that remains open

The primary reverse-gauge identity supplies equality against the admitted
gauge-invariant integrated tests. It is not equality of arbitrary Borel
measures on the unreduced link configuration space. There are two legitimate
ways to formulate a kernel consumer:
  * keep the literal slice/reference measures as measures on link space and
    prove every replacement including gauge restoration as an actual kernel;
  * pass to the gauge-orbit quotient and prove the kernels descend there,
    with compatible exterior/boundary gauge groups and subsequent block maps.
Randomizing a fixed slice by Haar gauge transformations preserves integrals
of gauge-invariant functions, but changes its full link-space representative.
One must retain that randomization as an operation, not equate representatives.

What is primary-verified in this pass: exact T averaging; positive real
comparison integrals; retained component factorization; the first/second
class distinction and integration-only gauge equivalence; and integrated
stability. What remains conditional: the literal original Part-I normalized
reference rule on every branch; its measurability and complete exterior;
positive pre-localization branch decomposition; preservation of the desired
link-space law or precise quotient law through all gauge-equivalence steps;
and any spatial innovation budget. The Part-I Euclid link again returned an
interstitial and the DOI did not yield a readable PDF, so no new Part-I
