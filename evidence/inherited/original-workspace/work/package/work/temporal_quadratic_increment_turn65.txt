AN EXACT QUADRATIC TEMPORAL-INCREMENT BOUND -- TURN65
20 October 2026. tail47.

RESULT AND LIMIT

There is a new quadratic estimate for the ORIGINAL finite-volume SU2
Wilson law. It retains its complete partition denominator and arbitrary
time-independent, real gauge-invariant spatial action. Write a spatial
link as U_e=u_e^0 I+i sum_(c=1)^3 u_e^c sigma_c, so u_e is in S^3.
Use the actual temporal links on one slab to align the next spatial
slice V with the present slice U; denote the aligned quaternion by
v_e^h. For deterministic real vectors b_e in R^4,

 E exp[sum_e b_e.(u_e-v_e^h)]
      <= exp[(1/2)sum_e |b_e|^2/beta_e].                 (0.1)

The expectation is normalized by the full original Wilson partition
function. In particular, for deterministic matrices A_e:R^4->R^r,

 E |sum_e A_e(u_e-v_e^h)|^2
                    <= sum_e ||A_e||_HS^2/beta_e.      (0.2)

Thus a sum of squared coefficients, with no number-of-links factor,
is available for fixed ambient linear coordinates. Neither the
time spacing nor the spatial action has been approximated.

The actual nonlinear magnetic observable is not yet attached to this
estimate. Its Jacobian is a function of the field; replacing A_e by
that Jacobian is invalid. Section4 proves a bounded, gauge-invariant
observable class to which (0.2) does apply exactly. Section5 explains
the remaining magnetic issue, including why raw gauge-variant
coordinate covariance is not a physical magnetic covariance.

Section6 also proves the electric-only Haar Dirichlet comparison
with constant 2/beta. Section7 gives an exact analytic positive-transfer
counterexample to transporting that inequality as an equilibrium-
weighted local-gradient bound through arbitrary spatial multiplication.
The counterexample is not asserted to have the homogeneous Wilson
spatial potential. It rules out a purely positive-kernel/ground-state
argument that ignores that potential.

1. INPUTS, NORMALIZATION AND ACTUAL TEMPORAL ALIGNMENT

I read current_proof_frontier.txt (TURN64) and
transfer_matrix_witness_turn64.txt. The transfer factorization is the
same actual pure-gauge factorization used there:

 M. Luscher, Construction of a selfadjoint, strictly positive transfer
 matrix for Euclidean lattice gauge theories, CMP54(1977),283--292,
 DOI https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01614090.
 SectionII.A (9)--(10), SectionII.B (17),(20)--(23), Proposition1,
 printed p288, and SectionII.C (24)--(26).

The primary factorization and trace identification, not any claim
that -log T is a differential operator, are the imported facts.
All new inequalities below have direct proofs.

Let E be the finite spatial edge set; all Haar measures, including
the site gauge average, are normalized. Fix beta_e>0, a real bounded
gauge-invariant spatial potential V_sp, and

 M(U)=exp[-V_sp(U)/2].

The ordinary homogeneous, or orientation-dependent, Wilson spatial
potential is included. It must be the same at every time slice for
the positive remainder T^(N-1) used below. Take periodic temporal
length N>=4, and ordinary nondegenerate periodic spatial geometry.

For W=w0 I+i w.sigma, set h(W)=1-w0. With normalized SU2 Haar,

 c(beta)=int exp[-beta h(W)]dW
        =2 exp[-beta] I_1(beta)/beta,
 q_beta(W)=exp[-beta h(W)]/c(beta).

For quaternions u,v in S^3,

 Tr(UV^-1)/2=u.v,
 h(UV^-1)=|u-v|^2/2,
 q_beta(UV^-1)=exp[-beta|u-v|^2/2]/c(beta).            (1.1)

The normalized electric operator Q is the tensor product of these
convolutions. P is the orthogonal projection onto the gauge-invariant
subspace. Q and M commute with P. On the full slice space, use

 T=M Q P M=P T P.

On the physical space it is M Q M. It is positive and trace class
for finite volume. The exact Wilson partition function is

 Z_W=[product_e c(beta_e)]^N Tr_phys(T^N).             (1.2)

On a selected temporal slab, the integrated temporal links implement
the site gauge element h. With one consistent orientation their
electric factors are q_beta_e(U_e (V^h)_e^-1). Reversing the convention
for h only changes variables in normalized Haar. The increment in
(0.1) is precisely u_e-v_e^h, not an increment between two independently
chosen gauge representatives.

2. A GAUSSIAN FOURIER KERNEL INEQUALITY ON (S^3)^E

This section uses only (1.1); it does not use character monotonicity
or a small-angle approximation.

For deterministic b=(b_e), put

 B(b)=sum_e |b_e|^2/beta_e,
 z_b(u,v)=sum_e b_e.(u_e-v_e),
 Q_b^+(u,v)=Q(u,v) cosh z_b(u,v),
 Q_b^0(u,v)=Q(u,v) cos z_b(u,v).

These are real self-adjoint continuous-kernel operators. Q_b^+ is
not assumed to be a positive operator, despite its positive kernel.
As quadratic-form inequalities on the full slice L2 space,

 Q_b^+ <= exp[B(b)/2] Q,                              (2.1)
 Q_b^0 >= exp[-B(b)/2] Q.                             (2.2)

Proof. Regard a slice as the compact submanifold (S^3)^E of
R^(4|E|). Apart from the fixed positive normalization in (1.1),
the kernel is

 k(x-y)=exp[-(1/2)sum_e beta_e |x_e-y_e|^2].

Its ambient Fourier transform is a strictly positive Gaussian.
Multiplication by cosh[b.(x-y)] changes this transform to

 exp[B(b)/2] khat(xi)
        cos[sum_e b_e.xi_e/beta_e].

The cosine is at most1. Multiplication by cos[b.(x-y)] instead changes
it to

 exp[-B(b)/2] khat(xi)
        cosh[sum_e b_e.xi_e/beta_e],

which is at least exp[-B(b)/2] khat(xi). For any complex L2 function
f on the compact slice, f times its Haar measure is a finite complex
measure on the ambient Euclidean space. Fourier inversion evaluates
each quadratic form as the integral of the displayed Fourier
multiplier against the nonnegative squared modulus of the Fourier
transform of that measure. This proves both inequalities.
Gaussian integrability justifies inversion and the finite measure
justifies all exchanges. No ambient Lebesgue density is required.

This argument permits signed/complex test functions. It is therefore
an operator inequality, not just an estimate for a product of positive
endpoint weights.

3. THE FULL WILSON DENOMINATOR AND THE NONCOMMUTING GAUGE PROJECTION

Q_b need not commute with P. This commutation is not used.

For any scalar insertion s(u,v) in the aligned one-slab electric
kernel, let Q_s have kernel Q(u,v)s(u,v). Its exact original-law
numerator, after removing the same constants as in (1.2), is

 Tr[M Q_s P M T^(N-1)].

Set

 R=M T^(N-1) M.

Then R>=0, it is trace class, and R=P R P because M commutes with P
and T=P T P. Cyclicity therefore gives

 Tr[M Q_s P M T^(N-1)]
       =Tr[Q_s R]
       =Tr[(P Q_s P)R].                              (3.1)

Thus the extra projection can be inserted inside the trace. There
is no assertion that a noninvariant source commutes with P.
For s=1 the denominator is Tr[Q R]=Tr[T^N]>0.

The kernel of R is real symmetric. The antisymmetric part of
exp[z_b(u,v)] is sinh z_b, so its trace against R is zero. Equivalently
the aligned increment law is symmetric under exchanging its endpoints.
Using (2.1)--(2.2) against the positive operator R in (3.1) gives

 E exp[Z_b]=E cosh[Z_b]<=exp[B(b)/2],                  (3.2)
 E sin[Z_b]=0,
 E cos[Z_b]>=exp[-B(b)/2],                            (3.3)

where Z_b=sum b_e.(u_e-v_e^h). These are the fully normalized original
Wilson expectations, proving (0.1).

Since Z_b is bounded at finite volume, replace b by t b and compare
the even Taylor expansions at t=0. They have the same constant and
zero linear term. This yields

 E Z_b=0,      E Z_b^2<=B(b).                         (3.4)

This second-order comparison is justified by the even zero-source
normalization; it does not differentiate a generic moment-generating
upper bound to infer all higher mixed moments. Applying (3.4) to
the rows of A proves (0.2). Chernoff also gives

 Pr(|Z_b|>=r)<=2 exp[-r^2/(2B(b))]                    (3.5)

when B(b)>0. If B(b)=0 then Z_b=0.

The new estimates hold for one temporal slab with arbitrary
deterministic simultaneous sources there. A product of different
non-self-adjoint exponentially tilted kernels on multiple slabs
has not been controlled here. The independent TURN64 energy theorem
does control its own different, real plaquette-energy sources on
arbitrary slabs.

4. AN EXACT BOUNDED GAUGE-INVARIANT OBSERVABLE CONSEQUENCE

Let A_e:R^4->R^r be deterministic, and let Phi:R^r->R be bounded
and globally Lipschitz with constant L_Phi. More generally it suffices
to have this Lipschitz property on all attained arguments together
with0, the latter needed for(4.4). Define

 Z_A(U)=sum_e A_e u_e,
 F_A(U)=int Phi[Z_A(U^g)] dg,                         (4.1)

where dg is normalized Haar on the spatial site gauge group.
F_A is a bounded continuous gauge-invariant slice observable.

Then for the original Wilson law,

 (1/2)E[(F_A(V)-F_A(U))^2]
            <=(L_Phi^2/2)sum_e ||A_e||_HS^2/beta_e.  (4.2)

Proof. Gauge invariance permits replacing V by V^h, with the same
actual temporal alignment as above. For every pair U,V^h, use the
same integration variable g in the two gauge averages and Jensen:

 |F_A(U)-F_A(V^h)|^2
  <= L_Phi^2 int |sum_e A_e(u_e^g-(v_e^h)^g)|^2 dg.

Left and right multiplication of a quaternion by unit quaternions
give an orthogonal real4x4 matrix O_e(g). For each fixed g, the
coefficients A_e O_e(g) are deterministic and have the same
Hilbert--Schmidt norms as A_e. Equation(0.2), followed by integration
over g, proves (4.2).

For a complex-valued Phi, the same statement holds with modulus
squared, using its Euclidean Lipschitz constant. The centered
spectral witness in TURN64 uses the stated real case.

This is an exact class of physical, bounded original observables.
It does not claim that gauge averaging leaves a proposed weak-field
magnetic signal intact. In particular it supplies no nonzero
cutoff-uniform variance. The nonlinear spatially flowed magnetic
observable has not been represented by (4.1) with coefficients and
Lipschitz constant having the required physical scaling.

There is an exact limitation of certifying this ridge class solely
by making the right side of(4.2) small. Suppose E consists of distinct
positively oriented edges in a graph with no loops or parallel edges,
as for the present nondegenerate periodic lattice. For fixed U,
independent site Haar integration gives

 int u_e^g dg=0,
 int (A_e u_e^g).(A_f u_f^g)dg=0  if e!=f,
 int |A_e u_e^g|^2 dg=||A_e||_HS^2/4.                 (4.3)

For different edges there is an endpoint belonging to just one of
them; integrating that site's gauge element proves the cross-term
identity. For a single edge its transformed quaternion is uniform
on S^3, with covariance I_4/4. Consequently

 sup_U |F_A(U)-Phi(0)|
            <=(L_Phi/2) sqrt[sum_e ||A_e||_HS^2],
 Var_mu(F_A)<= (L_Phi^2/4)sum_e ||A_e||_HS^2          (4.4)

for EVERY probability mu on the original links.

For homogeneous beta, making the right side of(4.2) at most C a
by imposing L_Phi^2 sum||A_e||_HS^2<=2 C a beta also
forces Var_mu(F_A)<=C a beta/2. Along logarithmic beta, or more
generally a beta->0, that variance vanishes. With differing beta_e,
replace beta in this conclusion by max_e beta_e.

Thus shrinking deterministic ridge coefficients to obtain the
desired temporal bound by(4.2) does not also give a nontrivial
fixed-scale witness. This limits that sufficient certification,
not every ridge observable or a smaller actual increment obtainable
by other cancellations.

5. WHAT THIS DOES AND DOES NOT DO FOR A MAGNETIC PROBE

In the canonical linear spatial-heat curl benchmark, Pauli/2 angle
coefficients c_e obey

 sum_e c_e^2 <= C a/(g_*^2 s^(5/2))                   (5.1)

at fixed physical spatial flow time s>0. Near identity, the imaginary
quaternion coordinate is theta/2+O(|theta|^3). For a deterministic
linear quaternion surrogate with the corresponding factor2 in its
coefficients, (0.2) has the desired quadratic scaling O(a/beta).
The factor2 is mandatory if translating the Pauli/2 angle convention.

This is a scaling diagnostic, not an estimate for the original
nonlinear magnetic field. There are three distinct issues.

(i) On an unfixed ordinary gauge graph, covariance between many
different raw quaternion link coordinates already vanishes by
independent site gauge symmetries. The bound for their deterministic
linear combinations is not a covariance estimate for colors
transported to a common physical frame. It remains a useful exact
kernel bound, including after a fixed linear embedding, but physical
gauge-covariant derivatives involve field-dependent frames.

(ii) A path expansion of a flowed field difference contains

 int_0^1 sum_e D_e X(U_r)[velocity_e] dr.

D_e X(U_r) is random and correlated with the aligned increment.
Equations(0.1)--(0.2) cannot be applied with those coefficients.
Nor is U_r distributed by the original Wilson law. The original
TURN64 weighted-geodesic inequality remains valid, but none of the
new Fourier algebra removes these dependencies.

(iii) The Haar Dirichlet estimate in Section6 does not automatically
transfer into an equilibrium-weighted local-gradient bound after
spatial multiplication. Section7 gives a sharp counterexample to
that specific inference. It does not refute every possible estimate
using uniform gradient suprema. Controlling nonlinear Jacobian
variation, or deriving a model-specific conditional quadratic
estimate with the actual spatial potential retained, remains
necessary for the proposed magnetic attachment.

Even the adjacent increment bound, once obtained, would have to be
paired with a lower variance at the fixed physical calibration
g_*. Sending the calibration to the vanishing bare coupling cannot
silently supply a nontrivial fixed-scale witness.

6. A SEPARATE EXACT HAAR ELECTRIC DIRICHLET COMPARISON

For completeness there is a genuine quadratic local-gradient
comparison for the unsandwiched electric convolution itself.
Use the left invariant Pauli/2 derivatives D_(e,c), so the spin-j
Casimir is j(j+1). For every F in H1 of the slice Haar space,

 <F,(I-Q)F>_Haar
   <= sum_e (2/beta_e) sum_c ||D_(e,c)F||_Haar^2.     (6.1)

Proof. Write U=exp(i theta n.sigma). In spin j,

 chi_j(U)/(2j+1)=(2j+1)^-1 sum_(m=-j)^j cos(2m theta).

For every integer k, 1-cos(k theta)<=k^2(1-cos theta).
Since 2m is an integer and the mean of m^2 is j(j+1)/3,

 1-chi_j(U)/(2j+1)
            <=(4/3)j(j+1)h(U).

Integrating against q_beta and using the directly proved TURN64
bound E_q h<=3/(2 beta) gives

 1-I_(2j+1)(beta)/I_1(beta)<=2j(j+1)/beta.             (6.2)

The positive electric eigenvalues lie in(0,1]. Thus
1-product_e r_(j_e)<=sum_e(1-r_(j_e)). Peter--Weyl
and the Casimir identity prove (6.1), first on finite sums and
then on H1 by spectral approximation.

In particular (6.1) is the adjacent increment estimate for the
Haar stationary electric process. Its gauge-invariant restriction
also describes the constant-M ground-state case. It has not been
identified with the finite-period physical slice marginal for
M=1, which can have a nonconstant diagonal gauge-projected density.
It also does not pass unchanged through arbitrary nonconstant M.

7. AN EXACT OBSTRUCTION TO A WEIGHTED-GRADIENT SHORTCUT

The following counterexample concerns the proposed inference

 positivity of Q and M, plus the electric bound(6.1)
   => d(F) <= C_beta E_pi sum_c|D_c F|^2             (7.1)

with C_beta independent of the spatial multiplication. This
inference is false, even with analytic positive multipliers and
a bounded analytic gauge-invariant observable.

Work on a single SU2 loop with conjugation as its residual gauge
group. Fix beta>0, keep the actual Wilson electric q_beta, and put

 psi_k(U)=cosh(k u0)/int cosh(k w0)dW,
 a_k(U)=(Q psi_k)(U),
 M_k(U)=sqrt[psi_k(U)/a_k(U)],
 Omega_k(U)=sqrt[psi_k(U)a_k(U)].

All these functions are positive, real analytic and conjugation
invariant. For each finite k, M_k and its inverse are bounded.
Set T_k=M_k Q M_k. Then

 T_k Omega_k=Omega_k.                                 (7.2)

Its ground-state transform is the Markov kernel

 P_k(U,dV)=q_beta(UV^-1)psi_k(V)dV/a_k(U),             (7.3)

with reversible invariant probability

 pi_k(dU)=psi_k(U)a_k(U)dU/D_k,
 D_k=<psi_k,Q psi_k>.

The transformed Markov operator is an L2 contraction, so1 is the
largest eigenvalue of T_k. Strict kernel positivity makes it simple.
The operator is trace class. One may rescale M_k by a scalar to
have M_k<=1 and hence a nonnegative spatial potential; this changes
no normalized ground-state law or increment below.

Take the analytic class function F(U)=u0=Tr(U)/2. As k->infinity,
psi_k dU converges to (delta_I+delta_(-I))/2. Write

 q_+=q_beta(I)=1/c(beta),
 q_-=q_beta(-I)=exp[-2beta]/c(beta).

By continuity of q_beta,

 D_k -> (q_++q_-)/2,
 Var_pi_k F ->1,
 d_k(F):=(1/2)int pi_k(dU)P_k(U,dV)[F(U)-F(V)]^2
        -> 2q_-/(q_++q_-)
         = 2/(exp[2beta]+1)>0.                        (7.4)

On the other hand, in the same Pauli/2 convention,

 sum_c |D_c F(U)|^2=(1-u0^2)/4.

Since q_min<=a_k<=q_max and q_min<=D_k<=q_max,
the expectation of this bounded function under pi_k tends to0:

 E_pi_k sum_c |D_cF|^2 ->0.                           (7.5)

Equations(7.4)--(7.5) disprove (7.1) for every finite C_beta.
For each k the finite-period transfer law tends to this unique
ground-state law as N->infinity. Thus an alleged bound uniform
in temporal period and in M would give the same contradiction.
The conjugation projection causes no change to these class-function
ground-state statements.

The counterexample keeps a Wilson electric kernel but chooses a
different analytic spatial potential. It does not refute a
comparison that uses specific, quantitatively controlled properties
of the homogeneous four-dimensional Wilson spatial action.

Its mechanism is concrete: the exact transfer kernel makes a
positive-probability nonlocal jump between two narrow wells, while
a local derivative vanishes at both wells. An exact differential
Hamiltonian has a local double commutator; the logarithm of this
finite-step transfer matrix cannot be replaced by that local form.

There is no conflict with the new deterministic bound(0.2):
F=u0 is linear in the ambient quaternion, and(0.2) bounds d_k by
1/(2beta), using a fixed ambient coefficient, not the arbitrarily
small equilibrium average of its tangent gradient.

8. CHANGED NEXT MATHEMATICAL ACTION

The normalized Wilson energy estimate is now complemented by an
exact quadratic aligned-link source inequality. This removes the
need to estimate such deterministic linear increments by their
absolute sums. A physical magnetic attachment must nevertheless
retain the actual nonlinear coordinate/frame dependence.

The precise remaining task is to prove a model-specific estimate
for that dependence (or exhibit a bounded gauge-invariant
observable in the class(4.1) with a physical, nonvanishing variance).
Neither positivity, the Bessel spectral ordering, nor the local
Haar Dirichlet inequality alone supplies it.

No continuum limit, cutoff-uniform magnetic variance, or complete
nontrivial YM4 existence theorem is claimed here.
