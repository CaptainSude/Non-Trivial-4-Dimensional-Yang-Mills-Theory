2. A UNIFORM HESSIAN LOWER BOUND FOR THE COMPACT CONDITIONAL LAW

Define
 L(z)=log Z(z),    Z(z)=integral_(S^3) exp(z dot q)domega(q).
Differentiating the compact integral gives

                  Hess L(z)=Cov_(mu_z)(q).             (2.1)

There is a numerical c>0 such that, for every real z in R^4,

                 Hess L(z) >= c(1+|z|)^-2 I_4.         (2.2)

Here is a direct proof, independent of the Fisher/Bochner proof in
TURN45. Write z=kappa n, kappa>=0, |n|=1. For kappa<=4, the density
of mu_z relative to Haar is at least exp(-8). Thus, for every unit a,

 Var_(mu_z)(a dot q)
   =inf_c E_(mu_z)(a dot q-c)^2
   >=exp(-8) inf_c E_Haar(a dot q-c)^2
   =exp(-8)/4.

For kappa>=4 put u=1-n dot q. Its unnormalized density is

       exp(kappa) exp(-kappa u) sqrt(u(2-u)), 0<u<2.

The normalizer is at most C exp(kappa) kappa^-3/2. Each of the
intervals

 I_1=[1/(4kappa),1/(2kappa)],
 I_2=[1/kappa,2/kappa]

has unnormalized mass at least c exp(kappa) kappa^-3/2. This follows
by setting v=kappa u and using1<=2-u<=2 on these intervals.
Hence their normalized probabilities are bounded below by a
positive numerical constant. For two independent copies u,u',
the event u in I_1,u' in I_2 has a gap at least1/(2kappa).
The identity Var(u)=E[(u-u')^2]/2 proves

                 Var(n dot q)>=c kappa^-2.            (2.3)

For any unit vector r orthogonal to n, transverse rotation symmetry
gives

 E(r dot q)=0,
 Cov(n dot q,r dot q)=0,
 E(r dot q)^2=(1/3)E[1-(n dot q)^2]>=c/kappa,           (2.4)

where the last inequality follows already on I_2. Decomposing an
arbitrary unit a into its longitudinal and transverse parts proves
Var(a dot q)>=c kappa^-2. Together with the bounded-kappa case,
this proves(2.2).

The quadratic power is essential: the longitudinal concentration
of a compact Wilson conditional distribution has variance of order
kappa^-2. No uniform strictly positive covariance independent of
