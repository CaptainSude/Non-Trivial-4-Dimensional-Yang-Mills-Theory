HAMILTONIAN COMPARISON AND INTRINSIC LOCAL NORMALIZATION
25 October 2026. Real comparison theorem and finite-domain construction.

This follows the old-density A,C registration. It does not interpolate
background gauge fields. The original full law is never replaced by a
boundary reference; that reference is used to DEFINE local source
coefficients and compare them with the original normalized expectation.

1. COMPLETION COMPARISON ON THE SAME ORIGINAL PRODUCT

Let H0,H1 be two REAL Hamiltonians on the SAME original product K,
each having the complete uniform weighted Hessian gap and the same
fixed decay constants. Let f be C1 and common to both. Set

  H_s=(1-s)H0+sH1,   Q_s=exp(-H_s)dx/Z_s, 0<=s<=1.

The Hessian gap, its weighted version, and the product boundary remain
valid by convexity. The product-ball one-form argument therefore gives
the SAME decaying gradient-covariance kernel for every s. Exactly,

  d/ds Q_s f=-Cov_(Q_s)(f,H1-H0).                    (1)

There is no path of fields or currents and no requirement that two
global regular configurations lie in a common convex field chart.
An extensive parameter-only difference has zero x-gradient and drops
out of (1); its actual original outer scalar remains untouched.

Let Y be a region containing a source seed I. Suppose

  sup|grad_i f|<=a_i,
  sup|grad_k(H1-H0)|<=B_R exp[-mu dist(k,Y^c)],      (2)

where dist(k,Y^c)=0 outside Y. From(1) and the registered covariance,

 |Q_1 f-Q_0 f|
 <=C B_R sum_i a_i exp[-mu' dist(i,Y^c)]            (3)

for a fixed smaller mu'>0. The proof uses the triangle inequality
dist(i,Y^c)<=d(i,k)+dist(k,Y^c), reserves an exponent to sum k, and
integrates s. There is no sum over the number of exterior sites.
If a_i is supported near I or has a weighted source-root sum A_f,
the right side is at most C B_R A_f exp[-mu''dist(I,Y^c)].

For sources f0,f1 which are not identical, add
sup|f1-f0| to this bound. Comparing the source maps is a separate
complete-word tail estimate; it is not a consequence of convexity.

2. TWO GLOBALLY ADMITTED COMPLETIONS

Suppose two admitted real backgrounds/currents agree on the complete
region Y, including any sampled endpoint frames used in the density.
Every old interaction coefficient and every complete precision word
whose read is contained in Y is identical. The remaining words touch
Y^c. The old carrier majorants and the complete precision tree bound
then give(2), with

  B_R=C[R+gR^2+g(1+E0)].                            (4)

Both current branches, private C/h factors and all coefficient reads
are included. For a noise index outside Y the ordinary full force
bound gives the same estimate with exponential1. For an index inside,
any differing carrier/word must connect it to Y^c, giving the displayed
tail. For a framed weight, differing frame endpoints outside Y are
local insertions at those endpoints and obey the same bound.

Thus the normalized comparison is independent of topology or a
field-space interpolation. If the source noise gradient carries g,
the factor g B_R is uniformly bounded on the registered weak cut.
This provides the useful normalized locality estimate without an
extensive supremum of H1-H0.

It is approximate completion-independence, not exact locality. To
obtain an ACTUAL function of b|Y alone, one still needs an intrinsic
local reference or an admissible specified completion. Section3
describes the intrinsic option and its exact native inputs.

3. INTRINSIC FINITE-DOMAIN NORMALIZED REFERENCE

Choose a nonwrapping carrier Lambda with all required native collars,
and a compatible union A of complete current constraint blocks lying
inside Lambda with the fixed native Dirichlet buffer. Its free
coordinates I_A must be the registered subset of the original free
coordinates, with the same individual original balls. This compatibility
is part of the native private-pivot boundary registration.

Input b_Lambda consists ONLY of field/current data on Lambda (and
endpoint frames there if that formulation is used). Use strict direct
caps small enough that restriction to each retained complete carrier
Y inside Lambda belongs to its full B109 independent-pair domain by
the smaller-direct criterion. This is an OWN domain of the local
data, with no prescribed exterior field. It is a strict direct
subdomain, not an assertion about every larger native orbit domain.

Let D_A^Lambda be the native local Dirichlet free precision with all
its actual pivot/soft-gauge conventions. Its required inputs are:

 (D1) holomorphy on that local data domain, real symmetry and a uniform
      compact-reference gap, surviving the small independent-current
      perturbation;
 (D2) two-ended exponentially local kernels, including both current
      corrections and literal free-coordinate injection;
 (D3) comparison with the global precision on I_A: their difference
      has a complete tree bound reaching Lambda^c.

B99's intrinsic Dirichlet construction and Theorem3.14 are the primary
route to D1--D3. The local geometry, common free-coordinate set and
soft-gauge version must actually be registered; simply taking a
principal matrix block would not remove the background read outside
Lambda. A complete-word truncation of that block would be local but
would need its own gap proof. Neither shortcut is assumed here.

More precisely, B99 printed428 gives its physical gap under BOTH
(3.35),(3.36), on the native domain sequences of SectionE with the
stated boundary cushion. It is not itself an independent-J gap.
The latter needs the old complete precision small-current perturbation
registered separately. Theorem3.14 and its following hidden-H tree
expansion supply domain-change words only for matching native operators
and domain sequences. See intrinsic-carrier-local-response-comparison-
20261025.txt and finite-prefix-two-environment-native-audit.txt; neither
statement identifies an arbitrary clipped inverse with D_A^Lambda.

Using the SAME old B116 coefficient family p_Y, define

  H_Lambda(x_A;b_Lambda)
    =(1/2)<x_A,D_A^Lambda x_A>
      +sum_(complete Y contained in Lambda) p_Y(b_Y,x_A extended by0).
                                                               (5)

Zero here is the fluctuation field in the INTERNAL buffer Lambda\A,
not a frozen exterior gauge configuration. Coefficients are defined
on their own complete Y by the imported B116 theorem. Their original
signs and constants are retained; constants in x may instead be kept
as the identical outer scalar. All retained old ages were already
summed into that coefficient family. No E0-per-date sum is used.

Absolute carrier bounds imply the same interaction force/Hessian
bounds after this deletion and zero evaluation. If D1--D3 hold, (5)
therefore has the uniform weighted gap and the product-ball covariance
bound on K_A. For real admitted data its normalized integral is positive.
For a local source f supported in an interior seed I subset A, define

  Q_Lambda f(b_Lambda)
    = integral_(K_A) f(b_I,x_I)e^(-H_Lambda) dx_A
       /integral_(K_A)e^(-H_Lambda) dx_A.            (6)

Equation(6) is intrinsically local: both numerator and denominator
use only b_Lambda. There is NO frozen exterior reference in its read.
It is a normalized finite-domain reference, not claimed to equal the
original global conditional exactly.

4. COMPARISON WITH THE ORIGINAL GLOBAL LAW

Embed(5) in the original full K by adding any fixed decoupled positive
quadratic Gaussian Hamiltonian on the exterior free coordinates. Its
normalized exterior factors cancel for f supported in I. Thus its
expectation is exactly (6). Choose the fixed exterior quadratic gap
comparable to the interior one. The embedded law has the same uniform
weighted covariance control.

All interactions whose complete carrier is inside A agree with the
original global interactions. Missing or zero-evaluated ones touch A^c.
The precision comparison D3 and the missing quadratic cross terms
also have a tail from interior sites to A^c. Consequently(2) holds
with Y=A and B_R as in(4), up to the fixed buffer adjustment. Apply
the affine-Hamiltonian comparison theorem to obtain

  |Q_global f-Q_Lambda f|
    <=C B_R A_f exp[-mu dist(I,A^c)].               (7)

The original global variables, cuts, scalar mass and normalizer have
not been changed. They appear on the left as the original expectation.
The auxiliary exterior Gaussian is only a comparison device for the
second term. No physical history mass is assigned to it.

5. LOCAL NORMALIZED COEFFICIENTS AND COMPLEX DOMAINS

For nested compatible carriers Lambda_n with active regions A_n,
define q_n=Q_(Lambda_n)f and f_n=q_n-q_(n-1). With compatible real
local domains, (7) gives exponentially small f_n, including actual
source-gradient and B_R factors. Each f_n is a function of its OWN
Lambda_n data. At the finite terminal carrier covering the whole
torus use the actual periodic law; the telescope then ends exactly
at the original normalized expectation. This last comparison retains
the native finite-volume boundary/topology conventions.

The local Hamiltonian has a holomorphic continuation and the same
packet phase proof once D1--D3 and the old coefficient domains are
registered. Its normalized ratio is zero-free on its OWN small
ell2 packet tube around admitted real local data. For h_Lambda
local packets this contains a sup tube of radius c/sqrt(h_Lambda),
intersected with the actual direct-domain reserves. This is a
quantitative carrier-dependent neighborhood, not the ENTIRE original
fixed complex native domain. The real-to-complex propagation lemma
can use this actual radius and retain its polynomial read-size cost.

For f=f(Phi_new), the new source map is generally nonlocal. Then (6)
needs an intrinsic finite-domain source map as well, with its own
uniform admission and a comparison sup|f_global-f_Lambda| paid by
complete response/current/return words. The one-step operator and
component-separation estimates suggest that construction, but they
do not by themselves register every multiscale finite-domain prefix
and boundary frame. This is a separate explicit interface, not
erased by the density comparison.

6. WHAT IS PROVED AND WHAT STILL NEEDS NATIVE REGISTRATION

Equations(1)--(4) are a proved normalized completion-comparison
principle from the registered old A,C/complete-read estimates. They
avoid all interpolation in gauge-field space.

Equations(5)--(7) give a concrete intrinsic local normalized source
construction conditional on D1--D3, compatible original free blocks,
and the stated local direct domains. Old B116 carrier functions
already have own-domain declarations. Remaining native work is the
Dirichlet soft-gauge/free-coordinate registration and, for propagated
sources, the intrinsic source-map/prefix boundary registration.
There is no claim that the normalized own-domain problem has been
solved merely by writing a principal Gaussian covariance block.
