1. THE ABSTRACT FINITE LEMMA

Let I be a finite bounded-density lattice index set, with fixed-dimensional
Euclidean block variables x_i and K_S=product_i B_S. The radii can also
vary provided each ball is centered and the premises below are uniform.
For fixed actual background beta, set

 H_t(x)=1/2 <x,D x> + t P(x),  0<=t<=1,
 mu_t(dx)=Z_t^-1 1_(K_S) exp(-H_t(x)) dx.                 (1)

Pure background constants are kept in the original outer mass; they
can be removed from (1) because its ratio is unchanged. Suppose:

 (i) all H_t have the SAME uniform strongly convex lower bound c0>0
 and complete weighted-Hessian/accretivity bounds on K_S and a strict
 surrounding chart;

 (ii) for some summable spatial kernel a(j,k), with uniform row sums,

 |grad_j P(x)| <= C_P g sum_k a(j,k)(1+|x_k|^2);           (2)

 (iii) the weighted L2 covariance consequence of (i) holds in the form

 |Cov_(mu_t)(u.x_i,P)|
 <= C_cov [sum_j exp(-2nu d(i,j))
                         ||grad_j P||_(L2(mu_t))^2]^(1/2), (3)

 for unit block vectors u and a fixed nu>0. Its constants are uniform
 in t, S, I. The product-ball Neumann proof is independently recorded
 in product_ball_L2_covariance_and_small_mean_20261024; its boundary form is
 nonnegative, and a spatial scalar weight commutes with every block
 face's tangential projection. This is an L2 estimate, not the former
 supremum-gradient estimate.

Uniform convexity implies the usual centered linear-tilt bound

 E_t exp[u.(x_i-E_t x_i)] <= exp(|u|^2/(2c0)).             (4)

Indeed a real linear tilt preserves the Hessian and convex support;
apply the convex-cut variance inequality to that tilted law, then
integrate its logarithmic moment-generating function twice. Fixed
block dimension therefore gives E_t|x_i-E_t x_i|^4<=C(c0).

Put m_i(t)=E_t x_i and M(t)=max_i |m_i(t)|. Minkowski and (4) imply

 ||1+|x_k|^2||_2 <= C(1+M(t)^2),
 ||grad_j P||_2 <= C g(1+M(t)^2).                         (5)

The exact fixed-product normalized derivative is
m_i'(t)=-Cov_t(x_i,P). Use (3), then the summable spatial square row:

                 |m_i'(t)| <= A g(1+M(t)^2).             (6)

At t=0, every mean is zero because the quadratic density and product
are invariant under the GLOBAL sign x -> -x. Separate coordinate
sign invariance is unnecessary. There are finitely many smooth means,
so M is locally Lipschitz and its upper derivative obeys the same
inequality almost everywhere. Hence

 arctan M(t) <= A g t,
 M(t) <= tan(A g t) <= 2 A g,  if A g<=pi/4.              (7)

This proves (A). No integration by parts against x through a cut face
was used in the scalar mean calculation. The convex Neumann boundary
enters only the already stated weighted covariance premise.
