FULL-ACTION REFERENCE: GAUGE REDUCTION, CHART OBSTRUCTION,
AND A FIXED-FOREST ALTERNATIVE -- TURN59
20 October 2026. gauge47.

RESULT

Gauge invariance does simplify a full soft denominator, provided the
action is actually defined on the entire integration region. For a
globally defined coarse Wilson action, Section2 proves a full
untruncated complex plaquette-source neighborhood with explicit
polynomial dimension/width cost. This is a concrete candidate
reference result, not an identification of the native reconstructed
fine action.

The proposed smooth weak-chart workaround has a sharp limitation:
orbit averaging a nonzero smooth product link cutoff supported in
a proper weak ball gives a current profile which is NOT analytic at
the flat physical datum, already on two links. Smoothing the hard
cut does not fix this issue. Section3 proves this by positive
spectral moments, without assuming a particular boundary shape.

There is a different exact fixed-chart construction. Fix a construction
forest F, extend the chart cut invariantly by its unique axialization,
and keep F as part of the reference specification. This has an exact
weak plateau and an exterior-holomorphic fixed-coordinate denominator
on its admitted action domain. Section4 verifies the Haar measure,
gauge covariance, and parameter dependence. It is NOT independent of
the choice of construction forest and does not supply the missing
native G0,T0 law or initial R identity.

1. WHAT THE GAUGE IDENTITY DOES AND DOES NOT DO

Use the anchored finite graph of TURN58, with integrated links E,
m nonanchor vertices, and real reference t. Put

 psi_t(U)=exp[-lambda sum_e q(U_e t_e^-1)],
 bar_psi_t(U)=int_H psi_t(U^h)dh,
 phi_t=bar_psi_t/w_lambda^m,
 q(V)=1-Tr(V)/2,    w_lambda=int_SU(2) exp[-lambda q(V)]dV.

For the SAME globally defined invariant weight W_e(U), full Haar
integration and exact anchored tree factorization give

 int_slice phi_t(U) W_e(U)dU_slice
   =w_lambda^-m int_full psi_t(U) W_e(U)dU
   =w_lambda^-m int_full exp[-lambda sum q(v_e)]
                                      W_e(vt)dv.       (1.1)

The identity includes the actual fixed exterior conventions. A
conditional coarea cannot be dropped if the true measure is not
the full Haar link product/slice used here.

When W_e=exp[-S_e/g^2], subtracting S_e(t) factors out exactly
exp[-S_e(t)/g^2]. It does not prove the centered action nonnegative
on the full compact group. A stationary point and a local physical
gap alone are insufficient for that global assertion.

Most importantly, the right side of(1.1) is not defined by a local
reconstruction which exists only for weak v. Since psi_t is strictly
positive everywhere, the undefined part cannot be discarded as a
small integrand. Smallness is an estimate of a defined expression.

This separates the current task from the following earlier results:

 * matched_convex_local_score_turn10 proves real convex marginal/score
   statements under GLOBAL Hessian hypotheses. It neither provides a
   compact gauge-field global extension nor a complex normalizer.
 * finite_g_local_chart_turn42 constructs finite-order stable entire
   polynomial approximants on an explicitly fixed first-step chart.
   Its equality, gauge-covariance and all-scale limitations are
   retained. It is not an exact extension of the native action.
 * native_reference_score_attempt_turn15 concerns the ACTUAL fixed
   relative B window on an admitted native branch. It already cancels
   the large background scalar and bounds real centered moments.
   Its admitted complex exterior path, reconstruction, and fixed
   window are essential. It does not give unrestricted soft tails.

2. A GENUINE FULL COARSE-WILSON DENOMINATOR

This subsection is an explicit globally defined model, not a generic
convexity premise assigned to the native action.

Take a finite bounded-incidence plaquette graph, with each plaquette
a word of at most four integrated or fixed-identity links. Its
anchored gauge group preserves those fixed links. Set

 S(U)=sum_p q(U_p)>=0,     t_e=I,
 Q(U)=sum_(e in E) q(U_e),
 beta=g^-2,      lambda=(gW)^-2,
              W>=1,       0<g<=1,       gW<=1.

Let n=|E|, and let D bound the number of plaquette occurrences of
one integrated link, including fixed finite multiplicity. Define

 Z(z)=int_full exp[-X(U)-g^-2 sum_p z_p q(U_p)]dU,
 X(U)=g^-2 S(U)+lambda Q(U)>=0.                         (2.1)

All products are compact SU(2) integrals, so Z is entire in the
finite collection of complex plaquette multipliers z_p. The action
and integrand are genuinely defined on the whole integration space.

The corresponding soft orbit reference in any anchored slice has
partition w_lambda^-m Z(z), by(1.1); the factor does not depend on z.

Here is a bound on the ACTUAL full baseline distribution. Restrict
every variable link to dist(U_e,I)<=c g with fixed0<c<=1/4.
For a plaquette, bi-invariance and q(V)<=dist(V,I)^2/2 give

 q(U_p)<=2 sum_(e occurs in p) dist(U_e,I)^2.

Thus on this product ball,
g^-2 S<=2D c^2 n and lambda Q<=c^2 n/2. Its Haar volume is
at least (c_1 g^3)^n with c_1>0 depending only on c.
Consequently

                       Z(0)>=(c_D g^3)^n               (2.2)

for a fixed c_D>0 depending only on D,c.

At half temperature we may discard the nonnegative Wilson action:

 int exp[-X/2]dU
      <=w_(lambda/2)^n
      <=[C_H (lambda/2)^-3/2]^n
      =[(2^(3/2)C_H)g^3 W^3]^n.                       (2.3)

The all-positive-s Haar upper bound from TURN58 is used here.
With respect to dmu_X=Z(0)^-1 exp(-X)dU, (2.2)--(2.3) imply

 E_mu_X exp(X/2)<=exp(K_full),
 K_full=max(1,n log[C_D W^3]),                          (2.4)

where C_D can be enlarged to be at least e. Constants depend on
bounded plaquette incidence, not on g or the number of links.
In particular K_full=O(1+n(1+log W)).

For 0<=s<=1/2, Holder interpolation gives
E_mu_X exp(sX)<=exp(2K_full s). If r=max_p |z_p|, then

 |g^-2 sum_p z_p q(U_p)|<=r X.

Hence, for r<=1/2,

       |Z(z)/Z(0)-1|<=exp(2K_full r)-1.                (2.5)

In particular r<1/(16K_full) gives a zero-free domain with
|Z(z)/Z(0)-1|<1/4 and a bounded holomorphic logarithm.
All original scalar factors, including w_lambda^-m, are retained.
No global convexity or removal of a gauge-null mode was used:
nonnegativity of the actual Wilson action and the full link soft
weight supplied the real moment estimate.

This is a full-action improvement over an orbit-profile-only bound,
but in a deliberately different and explicit model. The parameters
are plaquette multipliers, not arbitrary native exterior fields t(e).
Replacing the native reconstructed reference action by this coarse
Wilson action would still require the actual fine/coarse action
cancellation, source domains, and original-law identity. No such
replacement follows from(2.5).

3. SMOOTH COMPACT LINK CUTS STILL FAIL AFTER ORBIT AVERAGING

The following obstruction prunes a particular workaround, not every
invariant chart construction.

Let f be a nonzero real C-infinity function on SU(2), supported in
{dist(U,I)<=delta} with delta<pi/2. It may be a smooth plateau
cutoff multiplied by exp[-lambda q(U)]. On two parallel links
p->q, anchor p and leave q free. For the product cutoff f(U_1)f(U_2),
the orbit average in the slice U_1=I,U_2=h_s=exp(i s sigma_3) is

       C(s)=int f(h)f(h_s h)dh = <f,L_s f>_L2,           (3.1)

where L_s is a unitary circle action of period2pi.

CLAIM. C has no real-analytic germ at s=0.

Proof. Let P_k be the spectral projection of this circle action
with frequency k in Z, and a_k=||P_k f||_2^2>=0. Then

              C(s)=sum_k a_k exp(i k s).

Smoothness of f gives every finite spectral moment, and

      (-1)^j C^(2j)(0)=sum_k k^(2j) a_k.                (3.2)

If C were analytic near0, its complex germ on a smaller disk would
give |C^(2j)(0)|<=M(2j)! R^(-2j). For0<r<R, positivity and Tonelli
would therefore give

 sum_k a_k cosh(rk)
   =sum_(j>=0) r^(2j)/(2j)! sum_k k^(2j)a_k
   <=M/[1-(r/R)^2].

Thus sum_k exp(r|k|)a_k<infinity. Disintegrate Haar measure over
the left circle orbits. The spectral projections are exactly the
Fourier modes on those orbits, and the last sum is the integral
of their exponentially weighted squared Fourier coefficients.
For almost every orbit, the Fourier coefficients therefore define
an analytic periodic function in a strip |Im s|<r/2. Since f is
smooth, that function agrees with its actual restriction to the
real orbit.

On any orbit which intersects the support ball, translation by pi
sends the intersecting point h to -h. Its distance from I is
pi-dist(h,I)>delta. Thus each such orbit has an open interval on
which f vanishes. An analytic orbit restriction must vanish
identically. This contradicts f nonzero in L2. QED.

The argument does not merely replace the TURN58 hard cusp by a
claim of slow smoothness: it rules out an analytic germ for every
nonzero smooth product cutoff with this proper weak support.
A positive analytic current Wilson weight and an exterior-only
normalizing scalar cannot make C analytic; division by that
nonvanishing analytic factor would make C analytic again.

This is a statement about the CURRENT profile. A fully integrated
fixed-domain parameter integral can still be holomorphic. The
argument also does not apply to every possible nonproduct cut,
or to the fixed-forest invariant extension constructed next.

4. AN EXACT INVARIANT EXTENSION OF ONE FIXED CHART CUT

Orbit averaging the cut is not the only way to make it invariant.
The distinction below is useful because the native reference is
already specified on a particular axial slice.

Fix a construction forest F with one anchor per component. For each
real U,t, let a_(F,t)(U) be the unique anchored gauge transformation
such that

              pi_(F,t)(U):=U^(a_(F,t)(U)),
              pi_(F,t)(U)_e=t_e for e in F.              (4.1)

For an edge parent->child its recursive equation is

              a_child=t_e^-1 a_parent U_e.

The reverse orientation is obtained by inversion. These finite
matrix products define a global real-analytic map, and a holomorphic
map on the corresponding SL(2,C) coordinates. There is no logarithm
or branch choice in this axialization.

Let y_e=pi_(F,t)(U)_e t_e^-1 on the chords e notin F. Choose a
nonnegative smooth class function chi(y) on these relative chords,
with a plateau equal to1 on a smaller product ball and support in
a larger, admitted product ball. Define

                    chi_tilde_(F,t)(U)=chi(y).          (4.2)

For anchored h, uniqueness gives
a_(F,t)(U^h)=a_(F,t)(U)h^-1, hence pi_(F,t)(U^h)=pi_(F,t)(U).
Therefore(4.2) is exactly invariant. It has the desired plateau
on the corresponding physical chart.

For a joint change of frames k, transforming U,t and all external
data together, uniqueness gives

 pi_(F,t^k)(U^k)=pi_(F,t)(U)^k.

The chord relative variables transform by conjugation at their
initial endpoints. Choosing chi invariant under these conjugations
proves joint frame covariance, including nontrivial boundary frames.
The forest itself and its roots are carried with the geometry.

Now suppose the ACTUAL local action S_e is defined and invariant on
the gauge saturation of this supported chart. Define the real
weight there by chi_tilde exp[-S_e/g^2], and set it to zero outside.
No value of S_e outside that saturation is used. Exact forest Haar
factorization gives

 int_full chi_tilde_(F,t)(U) exp[-S_e(U)/g^2]dU
    =int_chords chi(y) exp[-S_e(V_F(y,t))/g^2]dy,         (4.3)

where V_F(y,t)_e=t_e on F and V_F(y,t)_e=y_e t_e on chords.
This is the same fixed relative slice integral, with its complete
Haar factors if one subsequently uses Lie-algebra coordinates.

If t=t(e) and S_e(V_F(y,t(e))) are holomorphic in exterior parameters
on ONE common disk for every y in supp chi, with a uniform bound
on this compact support, the right side of(4.3) is holomorphic.
The cut is fixed in y; no differentiation of a moving hard boundary
is required. Real positivity gives local nonvanishing; a quantitative
uniform zero-free radius still needs actual coefficient estimates.
The admitted fixed-window native estimates of TURN15, and the
separate TURN59 fixed-window work, address precisely such estimates.

On the inner plateau, the cut is identically1, so the current density
there is the actual analytic action density. This construction
therefore avoids the autocorrelation obstruction of Section3:
it defines an invariant cut by a fixed orbit coordinate, rather
than averaging a product cut over vertex gauges.

Three restrictions are essential.

 (a) F is part of the definition. Changing this CONSTRUCTION forest
     generally changes chi_tilde and the physical reference law.
     The TURN57 theta counterexample remains valid. For the SAME
     fixed invariant weight, one may use another integration slice,
     but that is a different operation from changing(4.2).

 (b) Holomorphy requires the complete actual action and its derived
     background/current domain on the entire supported family.
     The fixed-chart formula does not manufacture that domain.
     Replacing t(e) by an uncontrolled or discontinuously selected
     reference invalidates the claimed common analytic disk.

 (c) A deterministic local F can define a new finite-graph reference
     and can be compared across identical finite graph data. This
     does not prove it is an allowable complete native G0,T0 pair,
     or that the coupled native action/history estimates are uniform
     for the new choice. The initial positive R identity remains a
     separate source attachment.

5. WHY AN ARBITRARY ACTION EXTENSION IS NOT A FREE ALTERNATIVE

A holomorphic extension agreeing exactly with a local holomorphic
action on an open set is constrained by the identity theorem.
An arbitrary bump splice cannot preserve that equality and global
holomorphy. A smooth real splice can be constructed in many finite
settings, but its exterior analyticity, covariance and estimates
are additional facts, not consequences of the existence of a bump.

Even a perfectly analytic global extension can have deeper minima
outside the original chart. Local stationarity and a local Hessian
gap do not exclude them. For example, on a single SU(2) physical
loop let q=1-Tr(U)/2 and

                       S(U)=2+q-q^2.

It is nonnegative, has a strict local minimum S(I)=2 with positive
quadratic term, and has S(-I)=0. Subtracting S(I) therefore yields
a negative well away from the admitted chart. A soft penalty with
lambda=g^-2/W^2 and sufficiently large fixed W does not prevent
that well from dominating at weak g. This example is not a claim
about the actual Wilson minimum; it shows the insufficiency of
using only the local chart facts to justify an arbitrary extension.

6. IMPLICATION FOR THE CURRENT PROOF ROUTE

The full soft-action route is viable in an explicit globally
defined coarse Wilson model, as Section2 proves. It is not yet
attached to the native reconstructed fine action. In particular,
the Section2 source variable is not the native exterior datum.

For an action available only on an admitted local chart, keeping
that chart has a mathematically coherent alternative: the exact
fixed-forest extension(4.2)--(4.3). It preserves the original chosen
slice integral and a weak plateau, while retaining the forest as
part of the specification. It does not require the unrestricted
soft tails or solve their full-action problem. It also does not
remove the native choice/identity gate by renaming a new forest.

The product smooth-cut orbit average is the shortcut eliminated
here. No general impossibility of modified references or of a YM4
construction is inferred. At present the unchanged native counted
route has fewer new action-extension obligations. A modified route
must justify a specific replacement and its complete estimates;
the finite-graph constructions are options, not completed native
renormalization theorems.
