EXPLICIT ALL-ORDER EXTENSION ON FLAT-DIAGONAL SCHWARTZ TESTS
18 October 2026. Working proof, not a claim of continuum construction.

1. INPUT AND PRECISE CONCLUSION

Write D_n=union_{i<j}{x_i=x_j} in (R^4)^n and
d(x)=min_{i<j}|x_i-x_j|_infinity. Suppose compatible off-diagonal
correlations T_n are given on tensor tests. Assume fixed A,B,c,rho>0
such that, whenever the f_i are supported in pairwise disjoint boxes
of radius at most rho/n and mutual distance at least d>0,

 |T_n(f_1 tensor ... tensor f_n)|
 <= A^n (n!)^B (1+d^-1)^(cn) product_i ||f_i||_{C^1}.       (1)

The constants do not depend on placement. Compatibility means that
the multilinear functionals agree under restriction and finite
partitions; it holds for limits of the actual correlation functionals.
Increasing constants covers n=1, separately treated by unit-scale
partitions. It suffices to prove the result for n>=2.

Let S_flat be the closed subspace of Schwartz functions F for which
every partial derivative vanishes on D_n. Put

 p_m(F)=max_{|alpha|<=m} sup_x (1+|x|_infinity)^m |D^alpha F(x)|.

There are fixed A_1,B_1,C_1 such that T_n has a unique continuous
extension from compact tests away from D_n to S_flat and

 |T_n(F)| <= A_1^n (n!)^B_1 p_{ceil(C_1 n)}(F).             (2)

For example, let k=ceil(c)+7. The proof below permits C_1=k+6 and
B_1=B+k+40, after increasing A_1. Constants are deliberately loose.
No abstract dimension-dependent kernel theorem is used.

SCOPE: (2) is the regularity bound on the flat-diagonal Schwartz
space. Hahn--Banach supplies extensions to the full Schwartz space
with the same bound, but does not choose contact terms consistent
with every other OS axiom. A reconstruction theorem formulated on
S_flat may consume (2) directly. If full-Schwartz Schwinger functions
are required, their compatible contact extension remains separate.

2. A DYADIC CONFIGURATION PARTITION WITH CONTROLLED DERIVATIVES

Fix a large numerical K, say K=100. Choose a nondecreasing smooth
chi on [0,infinity), zero on [0,1], one on [4,infinity), taking
values in [0,1], with derivative bounds

 ||chi^(m)|| <= C^(m+1) (m!)^2.

Such a fixed Gevrey-2 transition follows from exp(-1/t), with its
standard elementary differentiation estimate. Constants below may
depend on this one transition, but never on n. Set

 ell_0=min(1,rho/10)/n,       ell_s=2^-s ell_0,
 H_s(x)=product_{i<j} chi(|x_i-x_j|_2^2/(K n ell_s)^2),
 h_0=H_0,                   h_s=H_s-H_{s-1} (s>=1).

Then h_s>=0 and sum_{s>=0}h_s=1 off D_n. At any fixed point only
a fixed number of h_s are nonzero, apart from the single far term.
Where h_s is nonzero, all pairwise Euclidean distances exceed
K n ell_s. For s>=1 at least one pair has Euclidean distance at most
4K n ell_s. Equivalence with the four-dimensional sup norm changes
only K by a fixed factor. Thus these are capped Whitney scales.

Choose a one-dimensional nonnegative smooth theta supported in
[-1,1], with sum_{m in Z}theta(t-m)=1 and bounds
||theta^(m)||<=C^(m+1)(m!)^2. It can be constructed by dividing a
fixed Gevrey bump by its periodized sum; this denominator has a
fixed positive lower bound and at most a fixed number of terms.
In N=4n dimensions let

 theta_{s,m}(x)=product_{a=1}^N theta(x_a/ell_s-m_a), m in Z^N.
 F_{s,m}=F h_s theta_{s,m}.

Their sum is F off D_n. Discard terms that are identically zero.
The product boxes containing a remaining term, enlarged from radius
ell_s to radius 2ell_s in each four-dimensional block, remain
pairwise disjoint. Indeed a point where h_s theta_{s,m} is nonzero
witnesses separation >=K n ell_s, whereas enlargement moves the
points by only a fixed multiple of ell_s. Their radii are <=rho/n.
Their mutual distance d_{s,m} is at least a fixed multiple of
n ell_s, and hence at least a fixed multiple of ell_s.

Here is the dimension bookkeeping. The product defining h_s times
theta_{s,m} contains at most C n^2 factors, each bounded by one.
For an ordered derivative of total order u, distribute its u
individual differentiations among these factors: there are at most
(C n^2)^u choices. The derivative factors obey a product bound
C^u (u!)^2 ell_s^-u because product_j u_j! <= u!.
Undifferentiated factors cost one, not C. The subtraction defining
h_s costs only two. Therefore, for 0<=u<=6n,

 ell_s^u ||D^alpha(h_s theta_{s,m})||_infinity
 <= C^n n^(30n),                                         (3)

uniformly s,m. This loose exponent also includes the product rule
used below. In particular it does not contain C^(n^2), nor an
overlap constant raised to O(n). Using an arbitrary Whitney
partition and bounding derivatives of its reciprocal by its
dimension-dependent overlap would not justify this estimate.

3. LOCAL TENSOR FOURIER EXPANSION

Scale y=x/ell_s-m. The function
G(y)=F_{s,m}(ell_s(m+y)) has support in [-1,1]^(4n).
Expand it periodically on [-3,3]^(4n). For each four-dimensional
block apply (1-Delta_{y_i})^3. Its Fourier coefficients satisfy

 |a_{k_1,...,k_n}|
 <= C^n product_i(1+|k_i|^2)^-3
       max_{|alpha_i|<=6} ||D_y^alpha G||_infinity,         (4)

where k_i in Z^4. The maximum uses total derivative order <=6n.
There is only a fixed number of terms per block in the differential
operator, so its expansion contributes C^n.

Choose a fixed four-dimensional cutoff eta equal to one on [-1,1]^4
and supported in (-2,2)^4. The Fourier series represents F_{s,m}
globally as a sum of tensor products

 product_i eta(x_i/ell_s-m_i)
             exp(i pi k_i.(x_i/ell_s-m_i)/3).

Each factor has C^1 norm <=C ell_s^-1(1+|k_i|). The fixed sum

 sum_{k in Z^4}(1+|k|^2)^-3(1+|k|)

converges. Consequently (1), (3), and (4) give

 |T_n(F_{s,m})|
 <= A_2^n (n!)^B n^(30n) ell_s^[-(c+1)n]
       max_{|alpha|<=6n} ell_s^|alpha|
                    sup_{support F_{s,m}} |D^alpha F|.     (5)

One may harmlessly replace the support in (5) by the bounded
neighborhood where derivatives of h_s theta_{s,m} are supported.
The same separation/nearest-collision bounds hold there by closure.

This also constructs the off-diagonal distribution without invoking
a kernel theorem. On any compact product rectangle away from D_n,
the tensor Fourier series converges absolutely in the norm used in
(1), and finite tensor sums are dense in the required finite-order
smooth topology. Two such constructions coincide on overlaps,
since they agree on tensor tests; a finite product partition proves
the equality for general tests supported in the overlap. Thus the
local definitions glue to the given compatible off-diagonal T_n.

4. FLATNESS AND SPATIAL DECAY

Let M=(k+6)n, where k=ceil(c)+7. For s>=1 and a point in the
support of a differentiated h_s theta_{s,m}, a pair is separated
by at most C n ell_s. Move one of those two four-dimensional
coordinates onto the other, keeping every other coordinate fixed.
The resulting point lies on D_n. Taylor's formula along this line,
using vanishing of every derivative of F on D_n, gives, for
|alpha|<=6n,

 |D^alpha F(x)|
 <= (C n ell_s)^(kn)/(kn)!
       max_{|beta|=kn, y on the segment}|D^(alpha+beta)F(y)|.

The notation absorbs the at most 4^(kn) directional-derivative
expansion into C^(kn). Derivatives used have order <=M.
The displacement is at most C n ell_0, a fixed constant independent
of n. Also x differs from the grid center ell_s m by at most
ell_s. Hence the Schwartz weight at y is comparable to the weight
at that center, with comparison C^M, and ell_s^|alpha|<=1. Discarding
the beneficial factorial yields

 max_{|alpha|<=6n} ell_s^|alpha| sup |D^alpha F|
 <= C^n n^(kn) ell_s^(kn)
              (1+|ell_s m|_infinity)^-M p_M(F).            (6)

For s=0 no flatness is needed. The same quantity is bounded by

 C^n (1+|ell_0 m|_infinity)^-M p_M(F).                     (7)

5. EXPLICIT CELL AND SCALE SUMS

For N=4n, M=(k+6)n>N, and 0<ell<=1,

 sum_{m in Z^N}(1+|ell m|_infinity)^-M <= C^N ell^-N.       (8)

To see this without a dimension-hidden integration constant, count
points in sup-norm dyadic shells. The jth shell has at most
(C 2^j/ell)^N points and weight at most 2^-jM. The resulting
geometric series sum_j 2^-j(M-N) is uniformly bounded. The central
shell obeys the same estimate. This proves (8) with C independent n.

Combining (5),(6),(8), all s>=1 terms sum to at most

 A_3^n (n!)^B n^[(k+30)n] p_M(F)
                 sum_{s>=1} ell_s^[(k-c-5)n].             (9)

The exponent k-c-5>=2, so the scale sum is bounded by one after
an unimportant fixed factor. For s=0, (5),(7),(8) give

 A_4^n (n!)^B n^(30n) ell_0^[-(c+5)n] p_M(F)
 <= A_5^n (n!)^B n^[(c+35)n] p_M(F).                     (10)

Since n^(Dn)<=e^(Dn)(n!)^D, (9)--(10) imply (2) with the constants
stated in Section 1. Every dimension-dependent loss has been
displayed: n^2 cutoff factors differentiated O(n) times, O(n)
Gevrey derivative factorials, kn-order flat Taylor loss, and the
ell^-4n configuration-cell count. Each is n^{O(n)}.

6. CONVERGENCE, CANONICITY, AND WHAT THIS DOES NOT PROVE

The series sum_{s,m} T_n(F_{s,m}) is absolutely convergent by
(9)--(10), defines the claimed extension, and obeys (2). Compact
smooth tests away from D_n are dense in S_flat in its Schwartz
topology. An explicit approximation first cuts off at spatial
infinity, then multiplies by H_s and lets s tend to infinity.
The derivatives of H_s grow only as a fixed power of ell_s^-1
for each fixed n and derivative order. Flatness supplies an
arbitrarily higher power, so every fixed Schwartz seminorm tends
to zero on the removed collision neighborhood. The spatial cutoff
is treated by Schwartz decay. Each approximation is compact and
avoids D_n, hence uniqueness follows from continuity.

If an actual regulator family satisfies (1) for ALL tiny boxes and
separations at each fixed regulator, the same proof gives a uniform
bound (2). This stronger premise must not be inferred when the
source producer requires the mesh to be smaller than the test box
or separation. In that case first extract continuum limits on each
fixed positive-separated tensor box (and a countable dense class),
then apply the present proof to those continuum correlations. The
canonical S_flat extension is a continuum construction; it does
not, without additional small-separation estimates, assert direct
convergence of bare-regulator evaluations on every flat test.
No separate dimension-dependent distribution theorem is required.
Permutation symmetry and Euclidean covariance pass on S_flat if
they hold on that dense off-diagonal class. Reflection positivity
passes for any OS test class whose tested quadratic combinations
remain in the admitted domain and for which the regulator identity
is available. The present argument does not supply that domain
identification, contact terms, clustering, or nontriviality.

The source producer still has to prove the uniform product estimate
(1) for the correctly renormalized and centered actual observables.
This note closes its all-order flat-Schwartz regularity consumer;
it does not assume that producing (1) completes Yang--Mills existence.
