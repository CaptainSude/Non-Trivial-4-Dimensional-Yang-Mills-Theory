RESULT

There is a concrete alternative to the thin transported-curvature
probe: spatially flow each time slice, form a gauge-invariant local
MAGNETIC curvature square, smear that scalar, and take a bounded
positive exponential. No color connector between different points
appears in this definition.

The finite-lattice gauge invariance and positive-time support are exact.
The free Maxwell benchmark is well defined and has a strictly positive
centered reflection covariance, with an explicit determinant lower
bound proved below. This is a viable target for an actual original-law
comparison, not a proved interacting comparison or YM4 construction.

There is also a precise obstruction to the simpler unflowed proposal.
In four-dimensional free Maxwell theory, centering the ordinary
regularized action-density square does NOT remove its self-variance:
it diverges as epsilon^-4 after a fixed smooth spacetime smear.
The separated reflected covariance nevertheless has a finite positive
limit. Multiplicative normalization sufficient to bound that variance
kills the separated covariance. Thus ordinary L2 tightness of this
centered random square is the wrong requirement for that unsmoothed
probe. This statement does not deny the operator-valued Wick composite
or renormalized separated Schwinger functions. In fact Section3A
proves that compactly regularized squares form a nonzero OS-Cauchy
family in the free theory. The unsmoothed scalar route therefore
remains viable at the level of reflection reconstruction, although
its actual interacting Schwinger-function estimates are unproved.

2. AN EXACT ORIGINAL-LATTICE OBSERVABLE

At lattice spacing a, let V^s_i(t,x;U) be the spatial Wilson flow
of the ORIGINAL spatial links at physical flow time s>0. Its lattice
flow parameter is s/a^2. Each time slice is evolved independently,
using its spatial plaquette action. Initially V^0_i=U_i.

On any finite spatial torus this is a globally defined smooth ODE
on a compact product of groups. For arbitrary site gauge maps
g(t,x), the spatial action and force are equivariant. Uniqueness
therefore gives

             V^s(U^g)=V^s(U)^g.                         (2.1)

Time reflection commutes with the flow because it merely permutes
the independent time-slice equations. In particular a function of
flowed fields at t>0 is a function of ORIGINAL links at t>0.
There is no flow across the reflection plane.

For SU(2), put

 h(P)=1-(1/2)Re Tr P,
 Q_(a,s)(f)=8 sum_x f(ax) h(P^s_12(x)),
 B_(a,s,u)(f)=exp[-u Q_(a,s)(f)],                         (2.2)

where f>=0 is smooth with compact support in t>delta>0 and u>0.
The plaquette P^s_12 is entirely spatial. Thus Q>=0 and
0<B<=1 for every original field. Both are gauge invariant without
any color alignment, gauge fixing, or thin connecting line.

On the usual finite geometry admitting the Wilson time reflection,

 E[(theta B)B]-(E B)^2 >=0.                              (2.3)

Proof: B is a bounded element of the original positive-half algebra.
Apply original Wilson RP to B-E B. The deterministic flow has not
changed the measure. Equivalently it produces a bounded slice
multiplication operator in the original transfer-matrix Hilbert space.

The normalization in(2.2) is the natural CONNECTION convention:
if U_i=exp[i a mathcalA_i^c sigma_c/2], then

 h(P_12)=a^4 |mathcalF_12|^2/8+o(a^4),
 Q_(a,s)(f) -> integral f |mathcalF^s_12|^2              (2.4)

on smooth classical fields. If mathcalA=g_* A in a Gaussian reference,
that reference quadratic observable is g_*^2 times the canonical
one used below. It is equivalent to replace u by u g_*^2.

No division by the bare g_a^2 is silently made. Along an interacting
tuned trajectory, matching the natural flowed connection to a
canonical field and the coupling at scale s^-1/2 is a renormalized
physical comparison. A bare-field Taylor expansion does not prove it.

Spatial support requires a separate qualification. Full spatial flow
depends on the whole time slice. Finite-volume RP remains exact.
Flow restricted to a spatial box also preserves gauge equivariance
and time support, but has a different boundary-dependent Gaussian
kernel. The whole-space benchmark below is not automatically the
benchmark for that box. Passing to a spatial thermodynamic limit or
obtaining a uniform influence-tail estimate is an additional task.

3. WHY CENTERING THE UNFLOWED ACTION DENSITY IS INSUFFICIENT

Here is a fully explicit Gaussian calculation. Take n independent
canonical Maxwell colors on R4. Curvature is defined from a Gaussian
potential with covariance delta_(mu nu)/p^2; its covariance is gauge
independent. Use six components indexed by mu<nu.

Let rho_epsilon have Fourier transform exp[-epsilon^2 p^2/2],
and F_epsilon=rho_epsilon*F. The curvature covariance matrix has
Fourier transform

          G_epsilon(p)=exp[-epsilon^2 p^2] P(p),         (3.1)

where P(p) is the orthogonal rank-three projection on exact two-forms:

 P_(mu nu,rho sigma)(p)
  =[p_mu p_rho delta_nu,sigma-p_mu p_sigma delta_nu,rho
    -p_nu p_rho delta_mu,sigma+p_nu p_sigma delta_mu,rho]/p^2.

For a real nonzero smooth compact f, define the centered scalar

 Q_epsilon(f)=sum_(c=1)^n sum_(mu<nu)
        integral f(x)[F^c_(epsilon,mu nu)(x)^2
                         -E F^c_(epsilon,mu nu)(x)^2]dx.

Wick's formula gives

 Var Q_epsilon(f)
   =2n integral dxdy f(x)f(y)||G_epsilon(x-y)||_F^2.

Scaling G_epsilon(z)=epsilon^-4 G_1(z/epsilon), followed by
translation continuity in L2, yields

 Var Q_epsilon(f)
   ~ [3n/(32pi^2)] epsilon^-4 ||f||_2^2.                 (3.2)

For completeness, the integrable domination follows from
|integral f(x)f(x-epsilon z)dx|<=||f||_2^2. Parseval and
Tr P(p)^2=3 give

 integral ||G_1(z)||_F^2 dz
    =3 integral d^4p/(2pi)^4 exp[-2p^2]
    =3/(64pi^2),

which proves the constant in(3.2). For a single F_12 square,
the analogous coefficient is n/(96pi^2), since the angular
average of[(p_1^2+p_2^2)/p^2]^2 on S3 is1/3.

By contrast, take f>=0 supported in t>delta and reflect its
support. Away from zero, the unsmoothed covariance matrix is

 G(z)=[1/(pi^2 |z|^4)] wedge^2(I-2 zhat tensor zhat).

The indicated reflection on two-forms is orthogonal, so
||G(z)||_F^2=6/(pi^4 |z|^8). The supports are separated by
at least2delta. Smooth convergence away from the diagonal gives

 lim_(epsilon->0) Cov(Q_epsilon(theta f),Q_epsilon(f))
   =[12n/pi^4] integral dxdy
          f(x)f(y)/|theta x-y|^8
   =A_f,                     0<A_f<infinity.             (3.3)

For the conventional energy (1/2)sum_(mu<nu,c)F_mu nu^2,
both covariance constants are divided by4.

Consequently any deterministic multiplicative factor z_epsilon
which keeps Var[z_epsilon Q_epsilon(f)] bounded satisfies
z_epsilon=O(epsilon^2), and its reflected covariance tends to0.
Subtracting another deterministic constant cannot fix this.

This is a diagnostic for ordinary random-variable L2 convergence.
It does not forbid renormalized composite Schwinger distributions,
operator-valued Wick fields, or separated-observable algebras.
The four-dimensional mollifier is used only for this calculation;
it does not preserve exact positive-half support at finite epsilon.
Nor is it proved here that every nonlinear bounded function of the
unsmoothed square must be trivial. The exact obstruction is(3.2)--(3.3).

3A. A NONZERO OS LIMIT DESPITE THE ORDINARY VARIANCE DIVERGENCE

There is a useful positive complement to the preceding diagnostic.
It is a new consequence of the explicit free covariance, not an
imported nonperturbative Yang--Mills statement.

Now choose a real nonnegative radial smooth mollifier rho supported
in the unit ball, with integral1, and put
rho_epsilon(x)=epsilon^-4 rho(x/epsilon).
Let f>=0 be nonzero, smooth and compactly supported in t>delta>0.
Define Q_epsilon(f) as above with this compact mollifier.
For epsilon<delta, it reads only the original Gaussian field in
the positive half-space. The mollifier commutes with the tensorial
time reflection.

Write r_0=-1 and r_i=1 for spatial indices. Reflection acts on
curvature by

             (theta F)_(mu nu)(x)
                     =r_mu r_nu F_(mu nu)(theta x).

The square of each sign is1, so the scalar sum of curvature
squares, and its translation-invariant centering, transform as
an ordinary reflection-even scalar. There is no missing electric
minus sign in the following scalar Wick contraction.

For real random variables in the positive-half algebra use
(A,B)_OS=E[(theta A)B]. At every fixed positive cutoff Q_epsilon
has finite ordinary L2 norm. Reflection positivity extends from
bounded positive-half functions to Q_epsilon by ordinary-L2
truncation, since |E[(theta A)B]|<=||A||_2||B||_2.

For two cutoffs epsilon,eta<delta/2, their mixed reflected product is

 (Q_epsilon(f),Q_eta(f))_OS
   =2n integral dxdy f(x)f(y)
       sum_(a,b)[(rho_epsilon*rho_eta*G)_(a,b)
                                      (theta x-y)]^2.  (3.4)

Here a,b index the six curvature components. The radial real rho is
even, so no omitted reflected convolution kernel occurs. The mixed
covariance includes the full curvature distribution G, including
any contact part, before it is evaluated on the separated supports.
Those supports and the compact convolution remain a positive
distance from the singularity at0. Thus the convolution converges
uniformly there to the smooth kernel G, and bounded convergence gives

             (Q_epsilon(f),Q_eta(f))_OS -> A_f>0         (3.5)

as epsilon,eta->0, with the SAME A_f in(3.3).
In particular,

 ||Q_epsilon(f)-Q_eta(f)||_OS^2
  =(Q_epsilon,Q_epsilon)_OS+(Q_eta,Q_eta)_OS
                           -2(Q_epsilon,Q_eta)_OS ->0.  (3.6)

The OS Hilbert-space completion therefore contains a nonzero limit
with norm squared A_f. This is a limit of vectors in the reflection
Hilbert space; it is not asserted to be an ordinary-L2 random-variable
limit on Euclidean configuration space.
Because each Q_epsilon is centered, its OS inner product with the
vacuum1 is zero; the nonzero limiting vector is orthogonal to the vacuum.

Indeed the compact regulator has the same power divergence:

 Var Q_epsilon(f)
   ~ c_(rho,n) epsilon^-4 ||f||_2^2,
 c_(rho,n)=6n integral d^4p/(2pi)^4 |rho_hat(p)|^4>0.    (3.7)

The scaling/Parseval argument for(3.2) proves(3.7), using the rank-three
projection and the covariance multiplier |rho_hat(epsilon p)|^2.
The numerical constant depends on the mollifier; the power and the
separated OS limit do not.

Thus the earlier failure of ordinary-L2 tightness does not require
abandoning the unsmoothed scalar as an OS/Schwinger-function route.
It requires the correct type of estimate. For interacting YM4 one
would still need the properly renormalized mixed separated cutoff
correlations, their uniform bounds and consistency, and the rest of
the reconstruction hypotheses. None is supplied here by the Gaussian
calculation or the existing native auxiliary count.

4. SPATIAL SMOOTHING FIXES THE MAGNETIC FREE-FIELD PROBLEM

For canonical Maxwell curvature define

              F^s_12(t,x)=exp[s Delta_space] F_12(t,x).

The magnetic curvature obeys this equation under the linearized
spatial Yang--Mills flow, including the ungauge-damped formulation:
the pure longitudinal mode drops out upon taking the curl.

Its real one-color covariance is

 K_s(t,x)=integral d^3k/(2pi)^3
    exp(ik.x) exp[-2s k^2]
    [(k_1^2+k_2^2)/(2|k|)] exp[-|k| |t|].               (4.1)

Integrating the Euclidean frequency produced exp[-|k||t|]/(2|k|).
In particular

                    K_s(0,0)=1/(48pi^2 s^2).            (4.2)

Indeed the angular average of k_1^2+k_2^2 is(2/3)k^2,
and integral_0^infinity k^3 exp[-2s k^2]dk=1/(8s^2).
For fixed s>0 the covariance is bounded and continuous in
time and space. There is no remaining pointwise magnetic
variance divergence, even at equal Euclidean time.

The qualification MAGNETIC is essential. Spatial smearing
does not damp the temporal frequency omega. Already in a
Gaussian heat reference, an electric time derivative has a
factor omega^2/(omega^2+k^2), which approaches1 as |omega|
increases. It retains temporal contact noise at equal time.
Thus one may not substitute the full electric-plus-magnetic
action density into(4.2) merely by citing spatial flow.

For nonzero f>=0 smooth and compactly supported in positive time define

 Q_s(f)=sum_(c=1)^n integral f(x)|F^c_12(s,x)|^2 dx.

This nonnegative random variable requires no Wick subtraction,
and

 M:=E Q_s(f)=n K_s(0,0)||f||_1<infinity.                (4.3)

It has finite moments of every order. One way to see this is
to regard sqrt(f)F^s as a Hilbert-valued Gaussian with finite
expected norm square. Its covariance is positive trace class.

5. EXACT BOUNDED QUADRATIC REFLECTION BENCHMARK

Use H=L2(support f,dx). Let C and R have kernels

 C(x,y)=sqrt(f(x)) K_s(t_x-t_y,mathbf x-mathbf y)sqrt(f(y)),
 R(x,y)=sqrt(f(x)) K_s(t_x+t_y,mathbf x-mathbf y)sqrt(f(y)).

C is the covariance on positive times and R is the reflected
cross covariance. Both are positive. Positivity of R follows
directly from(4.1): its time factors are exp[-|k|t_x]
exp[-|k|t_y], giving a square after integrating against a test.
The joint covariance of the positive and reflected fields is

                         [ C  R ]
                         [ R  C ].

Thus C+R and C-R are positive. Also
Tr C=K_s(0,0)||f||_1 and R is trace class. It is nonzero:
K_s(2t,0)>0 and f is nonzero on a positive-measure set.

Put B=exp[-u Q_s(f)], u>0, and

 A=I+2uC,
 D=2u A^(-1/2) R A^(-1/2).

Then 0<=D<=I-A^-1, so ||D||<1. Finite-dimensional Gaussian
integration followed by trace-class approximation gives

 m=E B=det(A)^(-n/2),
 q=E[(theta B)B]
   =det(I+2u(C+R))^(-n/2) det(I+2u(C-R))^(-n/2)
   =m^2 det(I-D^2)^(-n/2).                              (5.1)

No commutation of C and R is assumed: factor A^(1/2) on
both sides of each I+2u(C+-R). The remaining operators
are I+-D, whose Fredholm determinants multiply to
det(I-D^2).

Let

 A_s(f):=Cov(Q_s(theta f),Q_s(f))
       =2n ||R||_HS^2
       =2n integral dxdy f(x)f(y)
             K_s(t_x+t_y,mathbf x-mathbf y)^2 >0.        (5.2)

The strict positivity can also be seen near equal spatial
points in the open set where f is positive. The Gaussian
bounded centered reflection norm has the explicit lower bound

 D_G:=q-m^2
   >= [u^2 exp(-2uM)/(1+2u||C||)^2] A_s(f) >0.          (5.3)

Proof: -log det(I-D^2)>=Tr D^2, hence
det(I-D^2)^(-n/2)-1 >=(n/2)Tr D^2.
Also
Tr D^2>=4u^2||R||_HS^2/||A||^2,
and det(A)^(-n)>=exp[-2unTr C]=exp[-2uM].
Combining gives(5.3). Every quantity is finite at fixed
physical s,f,u. For example u=1/M gives a concrete positive
margin without any ultraviolet cutoff.

For a free connection of covariance g_*^2 times the canonical
one, replace C,R by g_*^2 C,g_*^2 R, equivalently replace u
by u g_*^2. This is a fixed physical reference parameter.

This quadratic field is non-Gaussian even when its underlying
connection is Gaussian. Therefore its positive reflection norm
proves only a nonzero physical state in that benchmark. It must
not be advertised as proof of interacting nonabelian dynamics.

at fixed physical s,f,u, with the SAME normalization and

 E_a[(theta B)B]-(E_a B)^2 >=D_G-3eta.                  (6.2)

This requires no exponential moment of an unsmoothed local

However, neither(6.1), uniform nonlinear spatial-flow control,

7. DECISION

The unsmoothed centered scalar density should not be pursued by
requiring a cutoff-uniform ordinary L2 variance; the exact free
calculation shows why that would remove its separated signal.

The compact-mollifier calculation nevertheless constructs a nonzero
free OS limit of the unflowed centered scalar. An interacting route
through renormalized separated Schwinger functions remains distinct
from a route through ordinary Euclidean random-variable L2 bounds.

The spatially flowed magnetic scalar is a concrete, bounded,
gauge-invariant alternative with exact time support and an
explicit nonzero free reflection benchmark. It avoids the
specific thin-line issue of the transported-color probe.
The next mathematical task is its normalized original-law
comparison(6.1), with a correctly identified physical field
normalization and a controlled nonlinear spatial flow.

No inspected primary source already proves that comparison.
No full nontrivial YM4 existence result is claimed.
