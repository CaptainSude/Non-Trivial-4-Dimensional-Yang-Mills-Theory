SEPARATED GAUSSIAN ACTION DEFECTS: A CONTACT-FREE COMPARISON THEOREM
19 October 2026. Root mathematical calculation for the strategic reset.

This is a free Gaussian theorem, not an interacting Yang--Mills theorem.
Its purpose is to test a weaker target than ordinary local F^2 moments
or a complete norm for every source descendant. It proves that the target
can remain finite at the correct dimension-four normalization even where
the ordinary variance diverges. Determinant identities used below are
standard; no claim of novelty in the literature is made.

1. FINITE GAUSSIAN SETUP

Let Y be a finite real centered Gaussian vector with covariance C>=0,
||C||<=1. A singular C is permitted. Let f,h be nonnegative diagonal
weights with DISJOINT supports and max(||f||,||h||)<=M. Write

 X=sqrt(f)Y,  W=sqrt(h)Y,
 A=Cov(X), B=Cov(W), D=Cov(X,W).

Delete zero-weight coordinates. Their joint covariance is

                     [ A   D ]
              Ctilde=[       ],       ||Ctilde||<=M.
                     [ D*  B ]

The bound follows by writing Ctilde=R C R*, where R*R=f+h<=M I.
Define Z(s,t)=E exp(s||X||^2+t||W||^2) and the cross-pressure

 G(s,t)=log Z(s,t)-log Z(s,0)-log Z(0,t).               (1.1)

Take the analytic logarithm equal to zero at (0,0). Centering either
quadratic source changes only a single-variable term and leaves G fixed.
The same is true of arbitrary additive source-vacuum counterterms
e(s)+e'(t). This algebra does not remove field-dependent counterterms.

2. EXACT DETERMINANT AND UNIFORM BOUND

For |s|,|t|<=rho with rho M<1/4, Gaussian integration and a block Schur
complement give

 exp G(s,t)=det(I-K(s,t))^(-1/2),
 K(s,t)=4st (I-2tB)^(-1) D* (I-2sA)^(-1) D.          (2.1)

This derivation does not introduce square roots of complex s or t.
Put q=2rho M and kappa=q/(1-q)<1. Then

 ||K|| <= kappa^2,
 ||K||_1 <= 4|st| (1-q)^(-2) ||D||_HS^2.

Consequently the trace series is absolutely convergent and

 G(s,t)=(1/2) sum_(n>=1) Tr(K^n)/n,
 |G(s,t)| <= [2|st|/(1-4rho M)] ||D||_HS^2.          (2.2)

Indeed |Tr K^n|<=||K||_1 ||K||^(n-1), and
(1-q)^2(1-kappa^2)=1-2q=1-4rho M.

For real s,t>=0, K is similar to H*H, where

 H=2 sqrt(st) (I-2sA)^(-1/2) D (I-2tB)^(-1/2).

The trace series is then nonnegative. Both inverse diagonal blocks
are >=I, so Tr K>=4st||D||_HS^2. Hence

 2st||D||_HS^2 <= G(s,t)
              <= [2st/(1-4rho M)]||D||_HS^2.          (2.3)

In particular a nonzero off-diagonal covariance gives a strictly
positive cross-pressure. Also

             partial_s partial_t G(0,0)=2||D||_HS^2. (2.4)

Neither bound contains Tr A, Tr B, Tr A^2 or Tr B^2. Those are precisely
the potentially divergent one-region terms that spoiled an ordinary
L2 estimate of a dimension-four quadratic source in turn29.

3. A PRECISE LIMIT CRITERION

Suppose the cutoff spaces are embedded into fixed real Hilbert spaces,
and the following hold:

 (i) A_a,B_a converge strongly to A,B, are self-adjoint, and their norms
     are uniformly <=M;
 (ii) D_a converges to D in Hilbert--Schmidt norm;
 (iii) the full covariance block norm is uniformly <=M.

Then G_a(s,t) converges locally uniformly on rho M<1/4 to (2.1), with
the Fredholm determinant of the trace-class K. To prove this, use the
uniform Neumann series for the two diagonal resolvents. Strongly
convergent bounded operators multiply Hilbert--Schmidt convergent
operators in Hilbert--Schmidt norm; the product of the two resulting
Hilbert--Schmidt factors converges in trace norm. The trace series
above has a uniformly summable tail. Finite partial sums converge,
which proves both the limit and local uniformity on smaller polydiscs.

This is a theorem with explicit operator convergence hypotheses, not
an assertion that arbitrary approximate covariance kernels satisfy them.
For an independent continuum test, start directly with the bounded
continuum curvature projection and compress it by finite-rank projections
on the two support Hilbert spaces. Strong diagonal and Hilbert--Schmidt
off-diagonal convergence then follow directly. This gives the continuum
cross determinant without an unproved lattice-to-continuum interchange.

4. WHY FOUR-DIMENSIONAL MAXWELL CURVATURE FITS THE CROSS NORM

For the cutoff Maxwell curvature in turn29, C is the orthogonal
projection onto exact lattice two-forms. In the continuum on R^4 its
Fourier multiplier, away from p=0, is

                  P(p)= (p wedge)(i_p)/|p|^2

on two-forms. It is an orthogonal projection of operator norm one.
Its kernel off the diagonal is a finite tensor combination of second
derivatives of the Laplace Green function, bounded by C|x-y|^-4.
There is also a distributional diagonal contribution, which has no
effect between disjoint supports.

For smooth f,h with compact supports at distance d>0,

 D=sqrt(f) P sqrt(h),
 ||D||_HS^2=integral f(x)h(y)|P(x,y)|_HS^2 dx dy
             <= C d^-8 ||f||_1 ||h||_1 < infinity.   (4.1)

Choose regions and components for which the off-diagonal kernel is
not zero; then this norm is strictly positive. The exact determinant
theorem therefore supplies a finite and nonconstant separated defect
pressure. It does not require the same-region Wick square to be an
ordinary square-integrable Euclidean random variable.

For the canonical lattice discretization, the corresponding sufficient
kernel input is the usual off-diagonal scaling
C_a(p,q)=a^4 P(x_p,x_q)+o(a^4) on separated compact sets, with the
uniform bound C a^4 d^-4, together with strong projection convergence.
These imply the hypotheses in Section3 by Riemann sums and dominated
convergence. The finite-rank continuum compression above already proves
the free comparison independently of that discretization lemma; a new
Yang--Mills argument may not assume its interacting counterpart.

The source Q_a(f)=sum_p f_p(Y_p^2-C_pp) has ordinary variance comparable
to a^-4 for nonnegative f, as proved in turn29. Equations(2.2)--(4.1)
show that its cross-pressure has a different, finite scale. An a^2
rescaling that would kill the separated physical signal is unnecessary.

5. RELATION TO REFLECTION POSITIVITY

For a reflection-positive law and a positive-half source F, let h be
its reflected partner. The normalized half observable

                  V_t=exp(tF)/E exp(tF)

has centered reflected norm

                <Theta(V_t-1)(V_t-1)>=exp G(t,t)-1.  (5.1)

The free theorem gives upper and positive lower bounds for this norm
when D is nonzero. It is consistent with divergent ordinary L2 norms;
the two pairings are different. The exact RP/conditional-boundary
argument and its four-real-pressure refinement are in
reflection_pressure_route_turn30.txt.

Finite cutoff V_t is a legitimate local function. Convergence of this
one reflected pairing does not alone reconstruct a local quantum field
theory. A common compatible family of mixed products, translation and
rotation regularity, and the requisite nontriviality/identification
remain necessary. No Gaussian comparison can establish a nonabelian
interaction by itself.

6. A DIFFERENT YANG--MILLS PRODUCER TO TEST

For the actual Wilson measure, consider the same cross-pressure with
two separated local scalar coupling deformations. Seek a uniform bound
on that ENTIRE difference, with local one-region vacuum contributions
cancelled BEFORE taking absolute values. The scalar deformation should
use the controlled running-coupling coordinate, rather than an arbitrary
21-component tensor calibration.

This target concerns only contributions communicating between regions.
It may permit a smaller proof than propagating a strong analytic norm
for every geometry-labelled one-source descendant. That possibility is
not a deduction from source-free ultraviolet stability. Local coupling
renormalization, interface effects, and source-dependent field terms
still have to be controlled. Nor does the cross ratio manufacture a
nonzero lower bound; one needs an actual interacting comparison or a
boundary-response estimate.

The public repository's E(w)+log h representation already embodies
local vacuum cancellation and its scalar coupling normalization. This
note is a useful explicit free benchmark and a weaker-target formulation,
not a claim to have supplied that representation's missing proof inputs.

No complete four-dimensional Yang--Mills existence theorem is asserted.
