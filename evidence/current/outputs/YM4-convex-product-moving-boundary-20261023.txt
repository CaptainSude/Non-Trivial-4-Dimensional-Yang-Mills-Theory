CONVEX PRODUCT CUTOFFS: UNIFORM LOCAL TAILS AND MOVING-BOUNDARY RESPONSE
23 October 2026. Root finite proof. Source archive unchanged.

RESULT AND SCOPE

A complete weakly nonlinear, centered massive finite-dimensional law
can be restricted to arbitrary sitewise convex sets containing a large
centered ball without losing uniform local tail control. The proof uses
sequential conditioning and a spatially summable conditional-mean
response. It never divides by the probability that ALL cuts pass.
It also gives a normalized, spatially local estimate for moving cutoff
boundaries. These results apply to the first full-Omega law reconstructed
in first_full_omega_mixture_domain_20261023.txt, including ALL its current
success/failure atoms. They do not sum the earlier Omega records, prove
regularity of the next effective action, or close a later reset history.

1. PRECISE FINITE HYPOTHESES

Let I be a finite lattice index set with metric d and fixed block dimension
m. Assume V_nu=sup_i sum_j exp[-nu d(i,j)] is bounded independently of I,
including its torus convention. Fix R>=r>=2, R/r<=C_radius. Let H be C^2
on a neighborhood of the CLOSED product B_R^I, and decompose

             H(x)=1/2 x^T D x+P(x),       D=D^T.               (1)

Additive constants are immaterial. For H_s=1/2 x^T D x+sP, 0<=s<=1,
require the following pointwise bounds on this WHOLE product:

 H_s'' >= c0 I;
 Re <z,W H_s'' W^-1 z> >= c_nu |z|^2;
 sup_i sum_j exp[nu d(i,j)] ||(H_s'')_ij|| <= M_nu,
 sup_j sum_i exp[nu d(i,j)] ||(H_s'')_ij|| <= M_nu;             (2)
                       sup_i |grad_i P|<=b.                   (3)

Here c0,c_nu>0 and W is ANY positive site-block scalar weight whose log
is nu-Lipschitz. Bounds also apply to principal restrictions; (2) for
the full matrices implies these restricted bounds by zero extension.
A slightly smaller nu may be chosen once to secure strict constants.
No arbitrary retained exterior force is allowed in (1),(3): it would
enter P and must meet (3). This is why the complete first full-Omega
integration, which integrates ALL its current noise, is useful.

Every factor K_i is a closed convex body satisfying

                       B_r subset K_i subset B_R.            (4)

Define Q_K by the EXACT density Z_K^-1 1_(product K_i) exp[-H].
Its normalizer need not be close to that on B_R^I. Constants below
depend only on m,c0,c_nu,M_nu,nu,V_nu,C_radius, never on |I|.

2. WHY THE WEIGHTED COVARIANCE TOOLS SURVIVE THESE CUTS

The earlier convex-product covariance proof works for the factors (4),
not only for round balls. For smooth convex factors solve the scalar
Neumann Poisson problem for the full log-concave law. Its differentiated
equation is (L tensor 1+H'') grad u=grad G. On a product boundary the
second fundamental form acts only inside the boundary site's coordinate
block and is nonnegative. A site-scalar W preserves its normal/tangent
subspaces and commutes with that boundary form. The weighted integration
by parts thus has the same nonnegative boundary term. Taking its real
part and using (2) gives

 |Cov_Q(F,G)| <= c_nu^-1
                ||W^-1 grad F||_L2(Q) ||W grad G||_L2(Q).      (5)

With W=1 the ordinary Poincare bound uses c0^-1. Approximate each convex
factor by smooth convex bodies and pass to the normalized finite
integrals; all constants are unchanged. Product corners cause no extra
negative boundary term. The same argument applies after fixing any
subset of blocks, since the remaining domain is a convex product and
its action has a principal Hessian. An arbitrary affine tilt of H also
preserves (2). These are structural facts about these particular laws,
not an assertion that arbitrary conditioning preserves covariance bounds.

For later use let F depend only on x_S and b_F=osc(F)/2. Projecting a
smooth G onto x_S, differentiating its complementary conditional ratio,
and applying (5) gives the bounded-local consumer

 |Cov_Q(F,G)| <= b_F C_loc/sqrt(c0)
            ||exp[-nu d(.,S)] grad G||_L2(Q),
                      C_loc=1+M_nu/c_nu.                      (6)

Indeed the gradient of E[G|x_S] is E[grad_S G] minus the complementary
covariance with H'_S. The two terms are bounded by 1 and M_nu/c_nu
times the weighted full gradient norm; Poincare and sd(F)<=b_F finish.
This slightly enlarged constant avoids optimizing the previous square
root constant. No derivative of bounded F is required.

3. CONDITIONAL MEANS AND SINGLE-CUT CHANGES

Hold every factor except the one at j fixed and integrate the complement
conditional on x_j=z. For a unit coordinate direction at i!=j,

 D_v E[x_i|x_j=z] = -Cov_(I\{j})(x_i, v dot H'_j).             (7)

The weighted column bound in (2) and (5), centered at i, give

 Lip_z E[x_i|x_j=z] <= A exp[-nu d(i,j)],
                      A=max(1,M_nu/c_nu).                     (8)

The formula for i=j is the identity map and obeys (8). Fixed color
dimension factors may enlarge A. The entire z domain B_R is convex,
so integrating this derivative gives oscillation at most
2 A R exp[-nu d(i,j)]. This remains valid when all the OTHER factors
are already arbitrary sets (4).

If j is still unrestricted, let E={x_j in K_j} and p=Q(E)>0. For any
integrable conditional mean h(z), exact conditioning gives

 Q(h|E)-Qh=(1-p)[Q(h|E)-Q(h|E^c)].                             (9)

If p=1 the change is zero. Combining (8),(9) yields

 |Q(x_i|E)-Q x_i| <= 2 A R exp[-nu d(i,j)] Q(E^c).             (10)

No inverse p occurs. This identity is valid even if the probability of
the intersection of all previously imposed cuts is extremely small.

For bounded local F, if j is outside S, apply (6) on the complementary
law to H'_j and use the weighted column bound in (2). This gives

 Lip_z E[F|x_j=z] <= C b_F exp[-nu d(j,S)]

with C=C_loc M_nu/sqrt(c0). If j lies in S use its trivial oscillation
2b_F. Consequently, since R>=1, in either case

 osc_z E[F|x_j=z] <= C_F b_F R exp[-nu d(j,S)],                 (11)

where C_F is fixed independently of F,S,I. Measurable bounded tests
are admitted by the conditional-ratio proof/approximation. In later
moving-domain applications the actual original trace is smooth anyway.

4. VOLUME-UNIFORM MEAN BOOTSTRAP

First put EVERY factor equal to B_R and interpolate H_s. At s=0 the
domain and Gaussian density are invariant under x -> -x, so every
block mean is zero, even when D has off-diagonal couplings. By (5),(3),

 |d_s E_s x_i| = |Cov_s(x_i,P)| <= C_mean b,
       C_mean=c_nu^-1 [sup_i sum_j exp(-2nu d(i,j))]^(1/2),    (12)

up to the fixed color conversion. Thus the baseline mean is at most
C_mean b. Require

                         C_mean b <= r/8.                     (13)

For every product domain and any block linear direction v, affine tilts
preserve Hessian >=c0. Applying Poincare to the tilted log moment
generating function and integrating twice proves

 Q exp[t v dot (x_i-Qx_i)] <= exp[t^2 |v|^2/(2c0)].            (14)

In particular, if |Qx_i|<=r/4, a union over m orthonormal directions
gives

 Q(|x_i|>=r/2) <= epsilon_r,
                  epsilon_r=2m exp[-c0 r^2/(32m)].             (15)

Order I arbitrarily. Starting from the baseline, replace B_R by K_j
once at each site. If all current means have norm at most r/4, (15)
applies. A new bad event x_j notin K_j is contained in {|x_j|>=r},
so its probability is at most epsilon_r. Summing (10) over the cuts
already imposed gives, for EVERY output block i,

 |mean_i - baseline_mean_i| <= 2 A R epsilon_r V_nu.           (16)

Assume also

                      2 A R epsilon_r V_nu <= r/8.            (17)

Induction now proves the required mean bound at every intermediate
stage, and hence at the final arbitrary product (4). There is no
circular use of the final tail: each step uses only the previous
stage, and (16),(17) prove that the next stage stays admissible.

It follows that EVERY such product satisfies (15), uniformly in volume
and in its shape. This includes a comparison in which some factors
are native success balls and others are actual old SU(2) intersections.

As a useful separate corollary, (9),(11),(15), summed over the sites,
give for any bounded local F

 |Q_K F-Q_(B_R^I) F| <= C b_F R epsilon_r |S|.                 (18)

Here sum_j exp[-nu d(j,S)]<=|S| V_nu. Comparing two arbitrary product
cuts through B_R costs twice this constant. This is local total-
variation control after taking the supremum over tests. It does not
assert total variation control of the full high-dimensional laws.

5. NORMALIZED RADIAL BOUNDARY PRESSURE

Because K_j contains B_r, it has a radial function q_j(n) in [r,R]
for n on the unit sphere. Convexity and a common inner ball imply the
needed Lipschitz regularity of q_j. For smooth H, set

 B_j = Z_K^-1 integral_(product K_i,i!=j) dy integral_(sphere)
       q_j(n)^(m-1) exp[-H(q_j(n)n,y)] dn.                    (19)

This is RADIAL pressure; no surface-normal conversion is being omitted.
The derivative of a radial-domain integral is naturally expressed with
this density and the radial boundary speed.

By the unweighted row bound and (3),

 sup_(x in B_R^I) |grad_j H(x)| <= M_nu R+b =: G.              (20)

Choose delta=min(r/2,1/(1+G)). In the interior radial shell
q_j(n)-delta <= u <= q_j(n), the Jacobian u^(m-1) is at least
2^(-(m-1)) q_j(n)^(m-1), and exp[-H(un,y)] is at least
e^-1 exp[-H(q_j(n)n,y)]. Hence its normalized probability is at least
c_m delta B_j. The shell lies in {|x_j|>=r/2}, whose FULL Q_K
probability is bounded by (15). Therefore

                 B_j <= C_m delta^-1 epsilon_r
                           <= C_boundary R epsilon_r.        (21)

The last step uses r>=2, (13), R/r bounded, and fixed Hessian constants.
There is no claim of a uniform tail conditional on arbitrary y; only
the full normalized marginal tail from Section4 is needed. No global
partition function lower bound or sum of all surface areas enters.

6. EXACT MOVING-DOMAIN IDENTITY AND ITS LOCAL BOUND

Let t vary on a finite interval. H_t and F_t are C1 on the fixed
containing product, with uniform hypotheses (1)--(4),(13),(17).
Let each q_j(t,n) be Lipschitz in t, uniformly in n. The actual K_j(t)
can have moving intersection corners. Define

                p(t)=Q_(K(t),H_t) F_t,      S_t=dot H_t.

Finite radial-coordinate integration and the ordinary Leibniz rule,
applied a.e. in t and n, give

 dot p = Q dot F-Cov_Q(F,S_t)
  +sum_j Z^-1 integral dy integral dn q_j^(m-1) exp[-H(q_j n,y)]
                              [F(q_j n,y)-QF] dot q_j.        (22)

This is the derivative of the ORIGINAL normalized ratio. The final
term includes the boundary contribution to the denominator. Additive
background constants in H cancel only in the covariance; their outer
mass is not removed. Since I is finite and the factors have a uniform
inner ball, the quotient is absolutely continuous; the a.e. identity
integrates along the entire admitted path. Smooth approximation is an
alternative, but a nonexistent globally smooth active center is not.

For each j the radial speed is independent of the OTHER noise y.
Conditioning those coordinates therefore replaces F(q_j n,y) by
h_F(q_j n)=E[F|x_j=q_j n]. Its difference from QF is bounded by its
oscillation over K_j; this is at most (11). Using (21),

 |boundary contribution|
 <= C b_F R^2 epsilon_r
                  sum_j exp[-nu d(j,S)] sup_n |dot q_j(n)|.   (23)

For j in S the sharper factor is R epsilon_r, but (23) suffices.
Combining (6) with (22),(23) yields the complete local response

 |dot p| <= Q|dot F| + b_F C_loc/sqrt(c0)
                        ||exp[-nu d(.,S)] grad_x dot H||_L2
       + C b_F R^2 epsilon_r
                    sum_j exp[-nu d(j,S)] sup_n |dot q_j|.    (24)

All terms act on the same actual Q, with all current cut factors.

9. WHAT THIS DOES NOT CLOSE

The exact outer full-Omega mass remains
 J_Omega(beta) exp[-g0^-2 Action(U1)] Z_Omega(beta). Other Omega
records and their original mass remain. A normalized current mixture
has been handled in this specific sector, not the entire native law.

The moving intersections can have true corners as beta varies. Estimate
(31) is not a uniform second-derivative theorem for log Z_Omega, and
does not by itself put the NEXT effective action in the same smooth
weighted-Hessian class. Thus replacing the first all-success normalizer
in the prior two-step proof by Z_Omega is not yet justified. The exact
new next law must first be matched and its complete coercivity proved.

At later births, held histories and current exterior may shift means
by O(R), and inherited action channels may fail (3). The mean bootstrap
cannot be transplanted there by suppressing a linear force or changing
centers without retaining the translated masks and compensator.
No continuum construction, nontriviality, or interaction conclusion
follows from this finite first-step advance alone.
