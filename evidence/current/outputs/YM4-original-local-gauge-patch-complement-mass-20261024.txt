RESULT

The original Wilson exponential moment gives prescribed coarse-
curvature failure bounds with the ORIGINAL normalization, summed
over all prior records. It also gives actual eligible P1-failure
bounds via complete fine-plaquette witnesses. Neither requires a
global all-good probability or conditional independence.

There is a sharper elementary gauge-patch estimate than using the
earlier safe n^2 filling bound: coordinate-tree loops in a filled
side-n coarse box have fillings of size O(n). Direct use of the
Wilson moment then gives, at a coarse bond-angle radius b,

 P(complete coarse patch admitted AND chosen tree patch fails)
 <=min{1,C(1+n)^4 exp[C_L(1+n)-c_L beta b^2/(1+n)]}.       (A)

For b a fixed multiple of epsilon0 or delta0, beta b^2 is of order
r^2 with fixed hierarchy constants. Estimate(A) is useful in a
small enough n=O(r) range. It does NOT price the buffers already
chosen for rare-times-h^4 boundary errors, where n is at least a
fixed multiple of r^2. On those buffers the displayed upper bound
becomes uninformative. This is a limitation of the proved estimate,
not a lower bound or a claim that the patch usually fails.

A separate registration, native_local_source_epsilon_box_
registration_20261024, establishes that the source family with
actual guards HELD successful can use b=c min(epsilon0,epsilon1).
The current original-law guard-pressure proof requires its smaller

1. THE LAW AND IMPORTED MOMENT

Let mu_beta be the full original finite periodic SU(2) Wilson law
with normalized link Haar, homogeneous beta>0 and nondegenerate
periods. Put h_p=1-Tr(U_p)/2. The proved finite-volume input is

 E_mu exp[(beta/4)sum_(p in E) h_p]<=2^(3|E|/4)             (1)

for every deterministic finite set of distinct plaquettes E.
Its exact original Z_beta is in the denominator. Here beta=C_W/g0^2
with the actual fixed normalization C_W retained. This note imports
finite_volume_wilson_energy_turn64; it does not reprove its finite
transfer argument or claim an arbitrary-exterior conditional version.

Use the exact first joint law on its actual admitted coarse rows,
with the original P0/P1 partitions, positive present-set averaged-
gauge insertion and Q1 partition. B109's averaged-contour map is
only specified on its admitted clustered-contour neighborhood. No
global measurable equivariant extension on rough configurations
is assumed here. Write A_c for admission of ALL four coarse rows
and their complete native reads for a candidate coarse plaquette c.
An undefined or ineligible row is assigned to the separate prior-
record complement; its putative curvature is never evaluated.

The partitions sum pointwise to1 and each inserted gauge factor
has its native normalized mass. Fine transformations used in that
insertion fix the coarse centers, so the defined coarse values are
unchanged. The expectation of an admitted coarse event, and of its
gauge-invariant fine curvature witnesses, is therefore bounded
under the same original law after summing all records. No number
of records multiplies (1). The random presence of a gauge center
is not replaced by an independent formal bit.

All results below are annealed with this original normalization.
They may be disintegrated afterward to get Lq bounds on conditional

2. A COMPLETE LOCAL FINE WITNESS FOR COARSE CURVATURE

For each elementary coarse plaquette c, let T_c be a deterministic
finite fine-plaquette set containing every averaging contour and
a filled fixed-L block neighborhood of its four coarse rows.
It has |T_c|<=v_L and its translates have overlap <=B_L, where
v_L,B_L depend on fixed L and the native averaging convention.

There are fixed t_L>0 and C_L such that

 max_(p in T_c)||U_p-1||<=t<=t_L
       implies ||V_c-1||<=C_L t.                         (2)

This is the local averaging regularity behind the explicit B119
printed246 consequence of Proposition1 [12], ||V_c-1||<2L^2 epsilon0
on a prior-good coarse plaquette. The constant 2L^2 need not be
retained for the present weaker complete-read estimate.

A direct verification uses only the native definitions. On the
fixed finite filled fine neighborhood choose a tree gauge. Every
non-tree fine link is a loop filled by at most C_L plaquettes, so
all transformed fine links are C_L t-close to1. Every native path
product, endpoint average M and anchored logarithm is then in its
fixed analytic chart. B109(0.5)--(0.12), including M's first jet,
bound the four coarse links and their plaquette by C_L t. Coarse
plaquette norm is unchanged by the induced gauge at coarse centers.
Thus no averaged-gauge SMALLNESS event is needed for(2). The full
coarse-averaging read and its filling are essential.

On A_c the estimate can be expressed as

 ||V_c-1||<=C'_L sum_(p in T_c)||U_p-1||.                 (3)

Indeed (2) proves this when all fine plaquettes are <=t_L. If one
exceeds t_L, choose C'_L>=2/t_L and use ||V_c-1||<=2 ON A_c.
Only an already defined admitted unitary value is used in that
case. Outside A_c, (3) is not a statement about a coarse value.

3. PRESCRIBED COARSE FAILURES AND THEIR LOCAL PRESSURE

For 0<tau below a fixed constant, let
E_c(tau)=A_c intersect {||V_c-1||>=tau}. By(2), E_c has a fine witness in T_c
with ||U_p-1||>=tau/C_L. For distinct coarse plaquettes J, choose
one witness for each c in J. The distinct witness union has at
least |J|/B_L elements. Using ||U_p-1||^2=2h_p, (1), and at most
v_L^|J| witness choices gives

 P_mu(all c in J fail at tau)<=rho_c(tau)^|J|,
 rho_c(tau)=min{1,C_L exp(-c_L beta tau^2)}.               (4)

Constants include the exact fixed read overlap. There is no
conditioning on P0=P1=empty or on every coarse plaquette passing.
For a deterministic finite coarse plaquette set S, finite product
expansion gives, for u>=0,

 E exp[u sum_(c in S)1_(E_c(tau))]
   <=exp[|S|rho_c(tau)(exp u-1)].                         (5)

Undefined/ineligible rows remain separate prior-record defects.
In Sections5--7 every tree-patch failure estimate is likewise for
failure intersected with admission of its COMPLETE coarse patch;
to bound the whole certificate complement one must ADD the actual
prior-ineligibility estimates from Section4. No global rough map
is being silently selected. This is a volume-independent local
pressure with the original
source-zero normalizer. Its usefulness depends on the ACTUAL tau;
one cannot keep the epsilon0 exponent after lowering the threshold.

5. OPTIMIZING THE DETERMINISTIC TREE FILLING

Let the complete W fit in a filled coarse box {0,...,n}^4, with no
wrapping cycle. Use paths from0 which first move in coordinate1,
then2, then3, then4. These are consistent paths of a rooted tree.
For a positive bond (x,x+e_mu), its fundamental tree loop is obtained
by commuting that one mu-step through the later-coordinate path
segments. Each commutation inserts a mu/nu plaquette; the number is

             sum_(nu>mu)x_nu <=3n.                       (8)

Hence the earlier safe A_W<=C n^2 can be sharpened, for this filled
box and this particular tree, to A_W<=C n. The resulting sufficient
curvature criterion for its bond-angle box of radius b is

                   max_W curvature < c b/(1+n).         (9)

For this deterministic criterion, (4) and a union bound give

 P(complete coarse patch admitted AND criterion(9) fails)
 <=min{1,C(1+n)^4 exp[-c beta b^2/(1+n)^2]}.              (10)

Because (9) is SUFFICIENT, it also bounds the probability of failure
to admit the chosen tree gauge (and of failure to admit any gauge),
in each case intersected with COMPLETE coarse-patch admission.
Its complement is not an equivalent characterization of either
failure event, and no probability lower bound follows.

A uniform O(curvature) potential bound independent of n is not
available deterministically. For an Abelian constant-curvature
field with n^2 kappa small, a square of side n has holonomy distance
at least c n^2 kappa. If every link in some gauge were a-close to1,
the boundary product estimate would give at most4n a. Thus some
link must have size at least c n kappa. This explains a genuine
worst-case n factor, but says nothing about its Wilson probability.

6. A STRONGER ORIGINAL-LAW TREE-ANGLE TAIL

One need not first require every plaquette to meet(9). For each
coarse bond's O(n)-plaquette tree filling, use(3). Its complete
fine-plaquette union E_b has cardinality <=C_L(1+n); fixed local
overlaps and multiplicities have bounded constants. The exact
unitary product bound gives

 ||V^k(b)-1|| <=C_L sum_(p in E_b)||U_p-1||
             <=C_L sqrt[(1+n)sum_(p in E_b)h_p].         (11)

For b below a fixed small group radius, failure of the desired
principal-angle box implies a bond norm >=c b. Hence that failure
at a specified bond implies sum_(E_b) h_p>=c_L b^2/(1+n).
Exponential Markov with(1), followed by the O((1+n)^4) bond census,
proves the displayed admitted-patch bound(A). Undefined or ineligible
coarse patches are handled by the separate prior-defect estimates.

This is a direct stochastic consequence of the original moment,
stronger in its useful range than the maximum-curvature witness
bound(10). It keeps the entropy cost C_L(1+n) from the ACTUAL
filling's moment bound. Omitting that cost would falsely turn an
absolute-energy estimate into cancellation of curvature components.
It does not use an arbitrary-exterior normalized conditional law.

7. THE CRITICAL BUFFER RANGE AND WHAT IS STILL MISSING

Write R_b=b/g0 and beta=C_W/g0^2. The bound(A) for failure
intersected with complete coarse-patch admission is

 min{1,C(1+n)^4 exp[C_L(1+n)-c_L R_b^2/(1+n)]}.           (12)

For n<=c_* R_b with c_* sufficiently small (depending on the fixed
constants), its exponent is <=-c R_b^2/(1+n); the remaining
polynomial census is explicit. For b=c min(epsilon0,epsilon1)
or b=c_delta delta0, R_b is a fixed hierarchy multiple of r at
the registered first adjacent-grade trajectory. Thus there is a
useful finite small-patch range, but not a rare bound uniform over
all growing patch sizes.

The current rare-times-h^4 boundary prescription has

 D(s)=ceil[Kbuf(r^2+s)^2/r^2]+d_read >=Kbuf r^2.

Its complete W contains this physical active buffer; with fixed
first-grade scale conversion, n>=c D(s). Then even at s=0,
R_b^2/(1+n) is bounded while the positive filling cost is of order
r^2. The bound(12) is1 after truncation. The earlier n^2-area route
would be weaker still, with exponent r^2/n^4 for its maximum-
curvature event. Increasing the fixed epsilon0/delta0 hierarchy
does not cure these asymptotic powers.

This is NOT a proof that the actual gauge-patch complement has
large mass. It proves that the present original absolute-energy
moment plus deterministic fillings does not price that complement
on the required buffers. A stochastic gauge-potential/flux estimate
with actual cancellation, or a different local source comparison
which needs a much smaller patch, is a genuinely additional input.
Signed/transported curvature concentration does not follow from
(1) by replacing its energies by signed components. Native later
current-image bounds could supply admission on selected histories,
but they are not a probability estimate for the original complement.

The local source domain can use O(epsilon0) as separately registered.
The present guard-pressure bound nevertheless holds only on its
O(delta0) gauge-patched lane. Extending it to the larger source box,
or paying the intervening complement, remains separate. One must
not combine the larger source domain with the smaller-lane guard
mass as though their hypotheses coincided.

For actual original marks, boundedness gives, for any failure event E,
E[|c_a f_p h_p|1_E]<=2 alpha_p P(E), and analogous raw pair caps
with ONE probability factor. On a prior-good source read its sharper
