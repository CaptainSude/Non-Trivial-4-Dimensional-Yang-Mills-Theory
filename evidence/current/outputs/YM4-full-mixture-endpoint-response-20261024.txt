RESULT

There is a response estimate that vanishes with the determining-data
displacement without differentiating the nonsmooth correction's score.
It uses BOTH its localized first-background derivative and its current-
noise localization. For a source with a gradient arm A_F, the correction
cost is

 C A_F K_g (T s)^a (g1 R1)^(1-a) exp[-lambda_a d(P,q)],        (R)

for any fixed 0<a<1, at a correspondingly smaller fixed spatial rate.
Here s is the size of one determining-coordinate change, T its actual
reconstruction/minimum response price, and g1 R1 is the physical current
noise amplitude. The complete smooth-reference score and direct-source
terms are retained separately. In particular the actual first source
has A_F=C g1 L_p^Omega; (R) keeps that coupling factor.

The proof is an exact endpoint interpolation of normalized laws and
localization of the ENDPOINT DIFFERENCE ell_b(B_theta')-ell_b(B_theta).
It does not differentiate D_theta ell_b, assume a localized mixed score,
or turn a value norm into an ordinary derivative bound. After a separately
matched next finite-read reconstruction with derivative O(g2), it gives
a useful hybrid-metric source profile, with an explicit g2^a factor.
The comparable adjacent-coupling case restores a product-sized small
factor in this correction. No uniform many-scale iteration follows.

1. COMMON FIXED FIBER AND THE ACTUAL AVAILABLE INPUTS

Let x lie in the SAME finite product K=product_i K_i, with each K_i
convex, containing a chosen x_i*, and diameter <=C R1. Geometry, pivot
labels and real free-coordinate conventions remain fixed. Let theta,
theta' differ in one determining block q by s=|theta'_q-theta_q|.
All intermediate physical determining values and the entire x product
must remain admitted. Write the exact normalized family as

 mu_theta(dx)=Z_theta^-1 1_K(x)
                exp[-H_theta(x)+V_theta(x)]dx,
 V_theta(x)=sum_b ell_b(B_theta(x)).                          (1)

H_theta is the COMPLETE smooth reference Hamiltonian, including every
inherited normalizer, coarea factor and gauge/Jacobian contribution in
its declared exact scalar convention. It has the uniform complete
weighted gap and weighted absolute rows required by the finite consumer.
The two endpoint reference Hamiltonians have those bounds on all K.

Each V_theta has the exact small supported value interaction supplied
by the earlier full-Omega construction, uniformly on the determining
family. Supports can be enlarged once to a common list because the
current geometry and finite reconstruction reads are fixed. Its norm
is <delta_* with reserve. Its scalar terms are retained in Z_theta.

The first correction terms ell_b are the SAME exact one-factor
normalizer increments, not newly assumed local functions. On the
admitted first-coarse Cartesian chart the proven estimate is

 |D_(beta_r) ell_b|<=K_g exp[-eta d(b,r)].                     (2)

It is an a.e. derivative with an integrated Lipschitz version; no second
derivative is asserted. The available bound is
K_g=C[sqrt(epsilon_r)rho_*^-1 B0(R0)+epsilon_r R0^2/g0].
All statements below keep this actual price.

The native reconstruction B_theta(x)=beta(Phi1(x;theta)) must provide
the following first-order comparisons at this fixed family:

 (i) |partial_(x_i) B_theta,r|<=C g1 for r in its fixed current
     collar of i, and zero otherwise, uniformly in theta,x;

 (ii) |B_theta',r(x)-B_theta,r(x)|
                  <=T s exp[-sigma d(r,hat q)],              (3)

with fixed metric/grade conversions into hat q. The constant T contains
the ACTUAL native minimum, current, coefficient and coordinate-response
prices, in the declared determining coordinates. No fixed physical
radius is substituted for a shrinking one without its conversion.
Both endpoint images and the straight first-coarse beta segments used
with (2) must lie in its admitted Cartesian chart.

For the complete reference difference Delta H=H_theta'-H_theta,
the additional input is a genuine mixed-score comparison

 sup_K |partial_(x_i) Delta H|
                     <=B_H s exp[-sigma_H d(i,hat q)].       (4)

This may be proved by integrating the complete reference mixed derivative
along an admitted determining path. It is not inferred from the pure

For the source family, assume the already available profiles

 sup_K |partial_(x_i) F_theta|
                       <=A_F exp[-nu_F d(i,P)],

The same bound for F_theta holds on all endpoint families used below.
The theorem needs only these first-order inputs and the small value norm.

2. EXACT ENDPOINT INTERPOLATION, WITH EVERY NORMALIZER

Define auxiliary measures on the SAME K by

 H_t=(1-t)H_theta+t H_theta',
 V_t=(1-t)V_theta+t V_theta',
 mu_t(dx)=Z_t^-1 1_K exp[-H_t+V_t]dx,       0<=t<=1.           (6)

The smooth reference's complete weighted Hessian bounds persist by
convex combination. The value interaction norm of V_t stays below the
same smallness threshold, with its common supported list and its exact
scalar part. Thus the finite spatial consumer applies to EVERY mu_t.
No claim that (6) is a physical intermediate RG kernel is needed.

Put Delta V=V_theta'-V_theta and hold F_theta fixed inside the integral.
Exact finite differentiation of (6) gives

 mu_theta' F_theta'-mu_theta F_theta
 =mu_theta'(F_theta'-F_theta)
    +integral_0^1 Cov_(mu_t)(F_theta,-Delta H+Delta V)dt.       (7)

The covariance in (7) is the derivative of the ORIGINAL normalized
ratio; there is no omitted derivative of Z_t. Parameter-only scalars
cancel in that covariance, but remain in Z_t and the raw outer mass.
The finite product and finite-volume local norm justify differentiation.
Countable supported sums follow by absolute convergence in each finite
volume. No nonsmooth theta derivative of Delta V is taken.

From the two-profile covariance consumer, (4),(5) imply

 |Cov_(mu_t)(F_theta,Delta H)|
        <=C A_F B_H s exp[-lambda_H d(P,hat q)].              (8)

This is the COMPLETE reference term. It is not dropped because V_theta
is small, nor replaced by a classical quadratic score alone.

3. TWO INDEPENDENT BOUNDS FOR THE CORRECTION DIFFERENCE

Set G_b(x)=ell_b(B_theta'(x))-ell_b(B_theta(x)). From (2),(3) and
one exponential convolution, after a strict rate reserve,

 ||G_b||_infinity
                <=C K_g T s exp[-sigma_0 d(b,hat q)].        (9)

Separately, the finite current read in (3)(i) and (2) give

 sup_K |partial_(x_i) ell_b(B_theta(x))|
                       <=C K_g g1 exp[-eta_0 d(i,b)],        (10)

and the same estimate at theta'. Hence G_b has twice this gradient
majorant. Unlike (9), (10) does not vanish with s. BOTH estimates are
needed; neither is a localized derivative of the other.

Let x^(b,n) equal x on the current coordinate ball of radius n around b
and x* elsewhere, with a fixed collar included; x^(b,-1)=x*. These are
all in K. Define the exact supported endpoint increments

 G_(b,n)(x)=G_b(x^(b,n))-G_b(x^(b,n-1)).                      (11)

Their supports are actual current-coordinate balls, of size
C(1+n)^d and diameter <=2n+C. The endpoint reconstruction still lies
in the first physical chart, by the whole-K admission, even though a
partially replaced x need not equal the original x configuration.

Bound (9) gives twice its supremum for each increment. Bound (10),
integrated along the changed shell with coordinate displacement <=CR1,
gives a second cap. Thus

 ||G_(b,n)||_infinity
 <=C K_g min{T s exp[-sigma_0 d(b,hat q)],
              g1 R1 (1+n)^(d-1)exp[-eta_0 n]}.              (12)

At n=0 use a harmless adjusted fixed collar constant. This second cap
comes from actual current noise amplitude g1R1, not the size of an
unrelated background extension. For finite volume the telescoping ends
at its diameter, exactly:

 Delta V(x)=sum_b G_b(x*)+sum_(b,n)G_(b,n)(x).                (13)

The first sum in (13) is a real parameter-dependent scalar. It is kept
in the interpolated partition function in (6), and only cancels inside
the covariance in (7). This is not an approximation of the normalizer.

4. SPATIAL HOLDER RESPONSE WITH A PHYSICAL NOISE FACTOR

Fix 0<a<1. Use min(u,v)<=u^a v^(1-a) in (12). For a sufficiently
small lambda below the consumer rate, nu_F, a sigma_0, and
(1-a)eta_0, the bounded-local/profile covariance theorem gives

 |Cov_(mu_t)(F_theta,G_(b,n))|
 <=C A_F |supp G_(b,n)| ||G_(b,n)||_infinity
                            exp[-lambda d(P,supp G_(b,n))].

Use d(P,supp)>=d(P,b)-n-C. The n sum is a fixed polynomial times
exp[-((1-a)eta_0-lambda)n] and converges. Sum b by the exponential
convolution of exp[-lambda d(P,b)] and exp[-a sigma_0 d(b,hat q)].
With one additional fixed spare exponent this proves uniformly in t

 |Cov_(mu_t)(F_theta,Delta V)|
 <=C_a A_F K_g (T s)^a (g1 R1)^(1-a)
                                  exp[-lambda_a d(P,hat q)]. (14)

Fixed root cardinalities and grade conversion factors are included in
C_a. For extended roots they must be retained in the corresponding
convolution count. Constants diverge when a approaches one or when an
exponent reserve is removed; a is fixed before the weak floor.

Combining (5),(7),(8),(14) yields the finite response theorem

 |mu_theta' F_theta'-mu_theta F_theta|
 <=C [D_F s+A_F B_H s
          +A_F K_g (T s)^a(g1R1)^(1-a)]
                                  exp[-lambda_a d(P,hat q)]. (15)

In particular it vanishes with s. The statement is for a one-block
determining change; a finite collection can be changed sequentially
with its explicit sum of s_q and s_q^a. No dimension-free l2 tangent
norm is asserted for the Holder part merely from this argument.

6. WHAT HAS AND HAS NOT BEEN MATCHED

The first ell_b derivative (2), fixed-current reconstruction derivative
(3)(i), actual source price in (16), small value interaction and exact
two-level normalizer convention are established in the preceding notes.
The endpoint consumer above is fully proved from these and the precise
family inputs (3)(ii),(4),(5). It introduces no new mixed derivative of
the nonsmooth correction.

For an actual varying V2 family, (3)(ii) must be supplied by the native
minimum response together with variation of C1, h_piv and Dtilde1;
holding those maps fixed while varying V2 is invalid. Estimate (4) is
the complete SMOOTH reference mixed score, including the first exact
F_s and coarea pullbacks. The independent smooth-reference task is
checking those inputs. A pure fluctuation Hessian alone does not prove
them. Likewise the next Phi2 whole-cut physical admission, exact
outer factors, changed geometry and masks require their own match.

The fixed cut in (1),(6) is indispensable. Moving K, a changed pivot
list, a different gauge chart, or an outer event crossing adds terms
that (7) does not contain. Such cases are excluded here, not silently
treated as a score of H. All source-free scalars remain in the exact
raw masses even when they disappear from a conditional covariance.

Small oscillation alone cannot imply C1 response: delta sin(theta/epsilon)
h(x) already has unbounded theta derivative as epsilon tends to zero.
The localized first ell_b bound used here excludes that particular
large first derivative, but supplies no localized SECOND derivative.
That is why (14) is obtained from two endpoint caps rather than an
unproved local D_theta score.

Even the localized first bound (2) does not by itself force an ordinary
SPATIALLY localized score. To see the distinction, let a determining
coordinate q=b be distance D from one noise coordinate i, set
d_D=exp(-eta D), and use the abstract positive correction
 ell_b(beta)=K_g d_D[1+sin(beta_q/d_D)h(beta_i)]
with |h|<=1 and |h'|<=1. Its first beta_q derivative is bounded by K_g;
its first beta_i derivative is bounded by K_g exp(-eta D), exactly as
in (2). Its value is <=2K_g d_D and its long-read value norm is small
at any exponent below eta. With beta_q=theta and beta_i=g1 x_i,
the normalized theta response at theta=0 contains
K_g Cov(F,h(g1 x_i)), with NO factor exp(-cD). The resulting example
meets these first-derivative/value inputs, although it is not asserted
to satisfy every additional native normalizer curvature property.
It pinpoints why one cannot take a derivative limit of (15) and claim
the same spatial exponent: a mixed response or additional smoothing
would be a substantive new input.

Finally, this proof spends exponents and produces a Holder modulus.
It does not establish that a later re-localized raw normalizer has a
uniform local C^a interaction norm without further loss. A fixed
physical source gain in (18) does not by itself close all subsequent
normalizers or give a UV-depth-uniform recursion. A stable mixed
spatial/parameter regularity class or a genuine smoothing argument
would still be needed for that stronger iteration claim.
