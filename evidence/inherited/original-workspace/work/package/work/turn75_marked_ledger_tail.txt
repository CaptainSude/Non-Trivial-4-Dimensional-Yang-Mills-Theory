TURN75 -- FIXED SOURCE-JET LOSSES IN THE COMPLETE NATIVE DATED LEDGER
20 October 2026. tail47.

RESULT

A fixed power of g_j^(-1) at each genuinely raw-owned marked incidence
can be absorbed into ONE fixed enlargement of the native dated cost.
Rerunning the TURN54 scaled-credit forecast then pays all later aging,
renewal and merger costs. No new primitive credit is created and no
small integrated mass is used as a pointwise density.

This is a useful weighted complete-history consequence, proved below.
Its required attachment is stronger than a list of one-step coefficient
norm bounds: the marked calculation must retain a complete history
majorant, a chargeable owner for each loss, and the actual source/read
slots through all subsequent native uses. The isolated raw action-role
module in TURN53 has this structure at its stated input scope. The
entire boundary/regular source trajectory has not yet been shown to
retain it. In particular this lemma does not pay unowned bulk losses.

1. THE PRECISE NATIVE LEDGER INPUT

Use the complete-history scalar majorants and the fixed-constant
scaled-credit theorem of native_tagged_history_propagation_turn54.txt,
Sections 1--4 and 7. Its primary input is Large-II (1.79)--(1.89),
printed pp383--387, including the collective positive-factor module,
the prospective K_j account, and the actual counting convention.

For a complete admitted continued history h, let I(h) be its dated
raw component incidences (j,Z), with all inclusion/multiplicity roles
retained. In dimension four put

 q_j(Z)=1+d'_j(Z),
 W_j(Z)=M^4 R_j^5 q_j(Z),
 W(h)=sum_((j,Z) in I(h)) W_j(Z).                        (1.1)

d'_j counts the native MR_j cells, not the regular M-cell tree size.
The 1 includes nonempty singleton and exceptional constant costs.
The source's literal cost can use d'_j; the already retained enlarged
cost convention bounds these exceptions by q_j. They must not be
forgotten when comparing a nonzero derivative cost to a one-cell set.

Let B(h)>=0 be the sum of ORIGINAL primitive credits: fresh components
have the c_b P_j^2 q_j plus root-token credit; genuine preparatory
renewals have their P_j credit. Released root tokens at mergers are
not new terms in B. Native notation here is P_j=p0(g_j).

At the indexed, not-yet-counted level write the complete positive
bound schematically as

              a_h <= exp[C_0 W(h)-B(h)] times R_h,       (1.2)

where R_h denotes the remaining fixed weaker walk/support factors
and any base measures or positive weights retained by the native
proof. This notation abbreviates the actual dated sum; it does not
replace unweighted earlier lattice volumes by terminal volume.

The accepted TURN54 theorem permits the following choice ONCE:
fix C_1>=C_0 and lambda in [lambda_0,1), then choose native margins and
the weak floor so that the same complete census closes for

              exp[C_1 W(h)-lambda B(h)] R_h.            (1.3)

The choice must satisfy the quantitative prospective-cost conditions,
including P_j/R_j^6 sufficiently large in dimension four. A sufficient
power hierarchy is P(g)~(1+|log g|)^p, R(g)~(1+|log g|)^r with p>6r,
before the weak floor. This note does not repair an already frozen
hierarchy violating those assumptions by merely raising the floor.

The residual terminal spatial allowance is included in C_1 before
the forecast if a terminal bound exp[-kappa_c d_k(X)] is wanted.
All numerator/denominator roles of the actual continued integral
remain in the native positive majorant. R_h is never inferred from
the positivity or smallness of a final normalized coefficient.

2. ELEMENTARY COST COMPARISON AT FIXED JET ORDER

Fix the total source degree m, for example m=2. Let
ell_j=1+|log g_j| and assume R_j>=c_R ell_j^r with r>1, as in the
proposed native hierarchy. A typical normalized
one-step source-jet loss has the form

 L_j <= C_m g_j^(-A_m) (1+ell_j)^(B_m),                 (2.1)

where C_m,A_m,B_m are fixed, independently of date and ultraviolet
depth. Cauchy rho_j^(-2), rho_j=c g_j^P, is of this form. Source
coefficient norms and dated preparation sizes are not silently set
to one; (2.1) describes the additional normalized response factor.

For g_j<=1, after replacing L_j by max(1,L_j),

                  log L_j <= C'_m ell_j.               (2.2)

Suppose a decorated history assigns each such response operation v
to an EXISTING owner (j,Z) in I(h). Require, for instance,

number of operations assigned to (j,Z)
                         <= K_m M^4 R_j^4 q_j(Z).      (2.3)

K_m is fixed. A more general weighted incidence hypothesis giving the
same next inequality is equally sufficient. Then

sum_v log L_(j(v))
    <= K_m C'_m sum_(j,Z) ell_j M^4 R_j^4 q_j(Z)
    <= Delta C W(h),                                   (2.4)

 Delta C=K_m C'_m sup_(admitted g) ell(g)/R(g) < infinity.

For r>1 the displayed ratio tends to zero at weak coupling. This
allows a fixed number of response operations per CURRENT physical
cell in the raw owner's native volume, not just one per component.
The source's actual domain-volume conversion and role multiplicity
must establish (2.3); it is not inferred for an arbitrary coefficient
merely because its support meets Z.

For the narrower one-operation/fixed-q case, where the right side of
(2.3) can be replaced by K_m q_j(Z), the sharper cost ratio is
ell(g)/[M^4 R(g)^5], and 5r>1 already suffices for decay of that ratio.
The native-volume version above is the relevant robust condition when
all current-cell roles are counted. No large-field probability is
being estimated in (2.4); it compares deterministic costs on the same
complete history.

Fixed combinatorial constants per marked operation are included in
C_m. Summing possible marked decorations requires its own absolute
bound. One permissible version is

 sum_(decorations d of h) prod_(v in d) L_(j(v))
                             <= exp[Delta C W(h)] A_in, (2.5)

for the prescribed fixed source anchors and initial jet amplitude
A_in. For example (2.3) with a fixed number of local choices per
charged incidence yields this by increasing Delta C. Merely saying
there are two source labels does not prove (2.3) or (2.5): each label
can spread to many carrier terms. Those sums must be controlled by
the actual rooted catalogue and geometry, or retained explicitly.

Original fine-source placement sums and calibration factors remain
in A_in or in their declared rooted norm. This lemma does not remove
an a^(-4) source count by calling it a fixed jet, and does not spend
the TURN73 initial beta_0^(-K) gain repeatedly.

3. WEIGHTED COMPLETE-HISTORY THEOREM

Assume the marked calculation has the SAME complete continued-history
representation as (1.2), and its absolute coefficients satisfy

 sum_d |a_(h,d)^marked(F)|
 <= A_in exp[Delta C W(h)]
                exp[C_0 W(h)-B(h)] R_h ||F||infinity.    (3.1)

F is a bounded terminal test on the admitted native domain. All later
native multipliers, original reads, and integrations are already part
of the complete h in this assertion; F is not an arbitrary unbounded
later physical source. The parameter-dependence and tests must match
the source's allowed module. No sign or positivity of marked
connected coefficients is assumed: their absolute sum is bounded.

Choose C_1=C_0+Delta C before running the scaled forecast. Since
B(h)>=0, the right side of (3.1) is at most

 A_in exp[C_1 W(h)-lambda B(h)] R_h ||F||infinity.        (3.2)

Apply the ONE native complete-history census with C_1. Its terminal
raw-component consequence, with the reserved spatial allowance, is

 |T_(k,X)^marked F|
 <= C_m A_in exp[-2lambda c_beta P_k-kappa_c d_k(X)]
                                                   ||F||infinity. (3.3)

This is the same kind of raw-operator bound as TURN54(7.2), now without
a winding tag and with fixed source-jet losses included in the costs.
The constant and the weak floor depend on m and the declared marked
catalogue constants, but not on the number of future raw dates.

PROOF. Equations (2.4) or (2.5) give the first enlargement. Inequality
exp(-B)<=exp(-lambda B) gives (3.2). TURN54 Section 3 proves the
forecast for precisely that fixed enlarged dated cost by its fresh
birth, no-new-field, renewal and merger induction. The complete
positive census and terminal root/spatial reserve give (3.3).
This argument never removes a fraction from an already pledged K_j.
The primitive credit is weakened once in defining the alternate
majorant; it is not weakened afresh after every marked step.

At a stage where the native proof has already replaced a subfamily
sum by its counting cost and geometric supremum, use the same weighted
supremum convention. Do not sum an already-counted envelope a second
time. A marked-label sum that was not included there must first be
paid in (2.5), with its actual incidence information retained.

If a previously designated geometric/walk tag is also present, split
B=B_prefix+B_future before dropping the tag. The exact inequality

 exp[-B] <= exp[-(1-lambda)B_prefix] exp[-lambda B]

leaves its ONE extracted factor outside (3.2). Repeated detections
of the same old winding do not create extra factors. Any walk
exponent reserve still has to remain sufficient after the marked
localization; (2.4) itself does not create spatial decay.

4. WHAT THE RETAINED SOURCE MODULE ALREADY ATTACHES

Read literally native_full_state_cover_turn53.txt, Sections 13A--C,
and native_marked_comparison_audit_turn53.txt, Sections 5--6.
They retain the full dated positive history majorant and the actual
typed input domains, rather than only T_X1 at the endpoint.

For an actual action-role insertion on a fixed raw history their
bound is

 |Delta_X(h)| <= E_j v_j(X),
 |exp(lambda Delta_X(h))|
                       <=exp[|lambda| E_j v_j(X)].       (4.1)

It is pointwise before the raw integrations. Normalizing the input
size E_j and restricting |lambda|E_j<=rho_j=c g_j^P spends a fixed
part of the local dated budget. Cauchy on the same fixed-domain
history integral then gives a loss C E_j/rho_j. For two marks the
coefficient polydisc used in TURN74 Section 6 gives C rho_j^(-2)
times the two input sizes. The source-independent reference
denominators and the actual complex-to-real native comparison
remain inside this estimate; no logarithm of a small raw mass is used.

Thus an INDIVIDUAL raw action-role step with these admitted inputs
has exactly the normalized polynomial loss needed in (2.1), with
its owner X explicit. The continued-history formulation allows the
same assertion at its actual later incidence, if all the subsequent
native integrand roles and reads remain in that admitted module.
This is stronger than terminal mass smallness and is the correct
starting point for (3.1).

TURN74's complete two-jet algebra also keeps scalar and cross terms:
Z'=KZ+Cov_K(X,Y). In an absolute expansion a mixed collision can
cost two inverse radii instead of one; m=2 includes this fixed change.
It does not identify every boundary coefficient with a raw insertion

5. THE REMAINING ATTACHMENT FOR A FULL BOUNDARY SOURCE TRAJECTORY

The currently retained statements do NOT automatically prove (3.1)
for every source emitted into the native boundary file. In particular:

(a) Original typed domains and full continuation.
The coefficient module requires the complete admitted analytic/noise
domains and actual live real-A variables. A bound on a physical graph
or on a terminal integral does not give a uniform bound against later
field-dependent native uses. One must either retain (4.1) in every
action role or prove the corresponding history-resolved domination.
A bare estimate ||delta T_X||<=small on one final test is insufficient.

(b) Ownership, including mergers and relative support.
Every inverse-radius factor to be paid must have an owner among the
raw incidences counted by the SAME I(h), with a bound such as (2.3).
A boundary coefficient may touch several retained components, and
its charged relative-tree connectors may lie outside them. Its
connected activity must keep each raw mass once and retain the
interior/relative support dictionary. "It touches a large-field
region" alone is not the multiplicity or complete-continuation proof.
No small component mass may be multiplied again to pay each mark.

(c) Dated preparation factors have not disappeared.
The actual source size in TURN53(13.1)/TURN74(6.1) contains

 L^[D(j-i)] (1+j-i)^q (2+gamma_i+gamma_j)^A epsilon_i.     (5.1)

The local Cauchy cost rho_j^(-m) is only one part of this expression.
A factor L^[D(j-i)] can be amortized over charged raw dates if a
proved ownership path covers the intervening time. It is not a fixed
power of current g_j merely because the source has a bad ancestor.
Its actual old-cell counts, role multiplicities and ordinary support
preparation must still be bounded or retained. Equation (2.4) makes
no claim about a sum of such factors without this extra accounting.

(d) Regular output after healing is different.
The native split before Large-II(1.100) assigns a coefficient to bulk
only when its complete domain avoids the surviving retained region.
Once a source is in such a regular channel there may be no raw owner
at its subsequent dates. Those losses require the actual regular
transport/contraction estimate. A previous raw birth is not a license
to charge every future bulk step to the old primitive credit.

(e) Positive full-history control is not a source-law conclusion.
Even a proof of (3.3) for a broad class would be a raw source
contribution bound, with original anchors and normalization retained.
One still has to assemble all regular/raw mixed channels, normalized
connected scalars and the final outer conditional-mean covariance.
It is not an event probability for a signed source decoration and
not a complete original-law two-source or nontriviality theorem.

These are precise remaining attachments. They do not undermine the
scalar cost comparison of Sections 2--3; they determine whether the
actual full source recursion qualifies for that comparison.

6. WHY ONE BAD ANCESTOR DOES NOT PAY UNOWNED LATER LOSSES

An elementary norm-level example isolates the issue. Let a source have
one original raw birth with primitive credit B_0 of order
(log gamma_0)^(2p), and raw cost W_0 of order (log gamma_0)^(5r).
Suppose it immediately heals, and then n ordinary steps each have only
the available upper norm loss g_j^(-A), A>0, with
gamma_j=Gamma+n-j. The product's logarithm is

 (A/2)sum_(j<n)log(Gamma+n-j)=O(n log n),

and is bounded BELOW by c_A n log n for large n. Neither the single
B_0 nor Delta C W_0 pays this product for any fixed p,r,Delta C.
One can realize these norm multipliers by scalar linear maps; the
example tests the inference from the stated norm bounds, not the
physical conditional expectation or Yang--Mills dynamics.

If instead the history genuinely remains raw, its repeated no-new-
field dates, maturation renewals and mergers belong to I(h) and B(h).
The complete forecast pays their enlarged costs as in Section 3.
Deleting those dates or their required renewal tests and then quoting
only an old terminal mass would lose the very mechanism that works.

7. DISPOSITION

The elementary weighted complete-history consequence is proved:
fixed-degree, incidence-owned polynomial inverse-coupling losses can
be paid by a once-enlarged native cost and a once-weakened primitive
ledger. For isolated admitted raw action-role marks the source module
has the required local structure.

The decisive remaining check is a structural, history-resolved one:
does every proposed boundary source operation retain an owner and the
same absolute continued-history envelope, including preparation,
relative geometry and source roles? The present files have not proved
that assertion for the full emitted original source. This note neither
assumes it nor replaces it by posterior L2 contraction, terminal mass
smallness, or a selected-law theorem.
