RESULT AND SCOPE

The original logarithmic first-date reserve supplies an age-resolved
bound for ONE large-field encounter of an admitted born zero21 source.
Its decay is faster than every inverse power of age; exponential decay
in age is neither asserted nor needed for a fixed finite source order.
The deduction uses the complete original preparation/row bound and
one-mark unnormalized healed-output theorem already identified in
homogeneous_first_contact_turn34 and marked_healed_output_turn28.

A separate, unconditional positive renewal lemma shows that an
order-independent feedback kernel of mass less than one and with all
polynomial moments has a resolvent with all polynomial moments, on ONE
weak-coupling floor. One must not ask its polynomially weighted norm to
be less than one separately for each source order.

Applying the renewal lemma to the FULL nonlinear recurrent native
source system still requires a same-class filing and first-encounter
decomposition. This note does not supply that missing native identity,
nor all mixed contacts, terminal normalized bounds, or YM4 existence.

1. DATES, NORMS, AND THE EXISTING ONE-MARK INPUT

Dates increase toward the infrared. At block-spaced dates assume the
same supplied body trajectory, with bounded primitive oscillations,

 gamma_k+c(k-n)-C0 <= gamma_n <= gamma_k+C(1+k-n), n<=k,
 gamma_k>=Gamma,                 gamma_n=g_n^(-2).       (1.1)

These inequalities hold for EVERY pair n<=k in the interval, so they
include monotonicity up to a fixed additive oscillation. A lower bound
by Gamma+c(k-n) alone would not compare gamma_b with gamma_n and would
be insufficient for (2.1). The lower drift is an additional input here;
the before-census one-mark bound of turn28 did not need it. Fixed
collar offsets can be included by shifting ages a fixed number of steps
and changing constants. They cannot depend on the UV cutoff. Native R
maturity is DIFFERENT: its delay can be O(log_L(M R_i)), hence unbounded
as the old birth i moves into the UV. No maturity delay is hidden in C0.

Let Z_i be one complete born own-invariant zero21 source, with its
vacuum and clover companions accounted for separately. Normalize its
actual dated core/tag norm by u_i. The existing original near/nested
preparation, AFTER its actual fine-root census, gives at proper row n

 C u_i gamma_i^A (1+n-i)^a exp[-sigma(n-i)] D_n(H),       (1.2)

for n>=i, with fixed A,a,sigma>0. A contains the actual finite field-
radius price, not an inverse running radius assigned to a fixed-radius
core. The exterior/first-used arms retain their native positive kernels.
The finite-box correction has faster age decay and can use (1.2).
This is the COMPLETE structured preparation premise, not just analytic
inclusion, and not an estimate for the unextracted marginal part.

For a reached history H, let b(H) be its original first component
date. Every charged row satisfies b<=n+C0. Its native positive lifetime

             exp[-delta Q(H)-eta p0(g_b)],
             p0(g)=A0(log gamma)^p, p>1.                (1.3)

It still retains the entire future continuation credit, its own small

Here Gamma is chosen above a fixed threshold so the displayed tail is

3. POSITIVE RENEWAL WITH ALL MOMENTS ON ONE FLOOR

THEOREM. Let K(t)>=0, t in nonnegative integers, with

 rho=sum_t K(t)<1,    M_P=sum_t (1+t)^P K(t)<infinity

for every fixed nonnegative integer P. Define convolution in age and

             R=delta_0+sum_(l>=1) K^{*l}.               (3.1)

Then sum_t R(t)=1/(1-rho), and R has every polynomial moment. The
condition rho<1 is imposed ONCE, independently of P.

PROOF. If rho=0 the assertion is immediate. Otherwise let X_1,...,X_l
be independent nonnegative integer variables with law K/rho. For P>=1,

 (1+X_1+...+X_l)^P
       <= [sum_(j=1)^l (1+X_j)]^P
       <= l^(P-1) sum_(j=1)^l (1+X_j)^P.

Thus

 sum_t (1+t)^P K^{*l}(t) <= rho^(l-1) l^P M_P,

and the sum over l converges. P=0 is the geometric series. This proves
the theorem without requiring sum_t(1+t)^P K(t)<1.               QED

The same domination applies to a causal nonautonomous operator kernel
L_(k,i) on a fixed family of Banach spaces if, after declared norm
identifications,

                 ||L_(k,i)|| <= K(k-i).                 (3.2)

Time-ordered products are bounded by the convolution powers. For
zero-age terms the Neumann series also covers a bounded implicit
diagonal term; a strictly causal system simply uses K(0)=0. An exact
recurrence or a finite causal majorant is required. An inequality
u<=f+Lu for an arbitrary infinite sequence alone permits extra boundary
data and is not a uniqueness theorem.

4. POLYNOMIAL COUPLING FORCING AND FINITE SOURCE DEGREE

At finite UV cutoff let the actual recurrence satisfy (3.2), with its

                    ||f_i|| <= C_P gamma_i^P.

The drift bound gives gamma_i/gamma_k<=C(1+k-i), so (3.1) yields

 ||u_k|| <= C_P gamma_k^P sum_t R(t)(1+t)^P
                       <= C'_P gamma_k^P.               (4.1)

All constants are uniform in the UV starting date on the SAME floor
rho<1. A separate fixed-order smallness assumption for each P is
unnecessary. A finite-degree nonlinear system has the same conclusion
inductively if its highest degree is acted on by this ONE source-zero
linear operator and all other terms are already bounded lower-degree
forcing. This is the usual exact coefficient triangularity, not a
bound for all mixed contacts without proving their forcing estimates.

For regular transport with a positive propagator G having EVERY

with its full regular
propagation before the next encounter. The effective kernel is bounded
by G*K_Gamma. Its mass is ||G||_1 ||K_Gamma||_1, which can be made less
than one at one Gamma if ||G||_1 is uniform there. Its every polynomial
moment follows from those of G and K_Gamma; mere summability of G
would not suffice for this latter conclusion. This is a first-encounter
decomposition: static continuations of one live encounter are not new
independent insertions of K_Gamma.

These are finite, concrete producer tasks. The renewal lemma removes

6. ARITY LIMITATION AND A POWER-TAIL COMPARISON

The original logarithmic tail provides every fixed moment but no
common factorial bound of the needed type by this argument. Integrals
of u^P exp[-d(log u)^p] have logarithm of order P^{p/(p-1)}, rather
than O(P log P). Hence a polynomial source-radius loss per degree,
combined only with (2.4), can still give superfactorial arity bounds.
The renewal theorem cannot convert that input into a Gevrey theorem.

If a genuinely revalidated native hierarchy instead gave
h(u)<=C exp[-c(Gamma+u)^epsilon], 0<epsilon<=1, the conclusion would
improve. Choose a small d>0 so the weighted kernel mass
sum_t exp(d t^epsilon)K(t)<1; this is possible at a sufficiently weak
ONE floor for a uniformly strengthened tail. Subadditivity
(s+t)^epsilon<=s^epsilon+t^epsilon makes that weight submultiplicative,
so the renewal has finite exp(d t^epsilon) moment as well. Optimizing
t^P exp(-d t^epsilon) gives C^{P+1}(P!)^{1/epsilon}, up to a fixed
integer enlargement. This is only a comparison lemma: turn34's finite
list of power-cutoff checks is not a validation of the full native
hierarchy. No cutoff replacement is made here.

STATUS

One-mark age information can be retained through the first encounter
instead of being thrown away at healing. The resulting positive memory
kernel has arbitrarily many polynomial moments and small total mass at
one weak floor. A rigorous renewal principle explains how such memory
could close recurrent fixed-order bounds, once the exact native
