Exact covariant lattice commutator at Raw I111650 (*)
18 October 2026; minimal_route

This fills only the coefficient-count step in Raw I Lemma
4.25:lem-cut-layer-partner. It does not prove the background layer
theorem, the mixed-scale cover hypotheses, or global carrier entry.

Write h=eta, (T_mu B)(x)=R_{x,mu} B(x+h e_mu), and
T_mu^-1 B(x)=R_{x-h e_mu,mu}^-1 B(x-h e_mu). Then
nabla_mu=(T_mu-I)/h and nabla_mu^*=(T_mu^-1-I)/h.
Use the invariant bilinear Lie-algebra pairing, so these are indeed
transpose operators even for the stated complex adjoint transport.
Let tau bound both transports, a_mu=partial_mu f and
b_mu=partial_mu^- f. All products by scalar functions act at the
basepoint. Exact operator identities give

 [nabla_mu^* nabla_mu,f] B
 = -(partial_mu^- partial_mu f) B
   - a_mu nabla_mu B - b_mu T_mu^-1 nabla_mu B.             (1)

For distinct mu,nu the product rule gives

 [nabla_mu^* nabla_nu,f] B
 = a_nu nabla_mu^* T_nu B
   -(partial_mu^- partial_nu f) T_mu^-1 T_nu B
   -b_mu T_mu^-1 nabla_nu B.                              (2)

The potentially troublesome first term uses the exact identity

 nabla_mu^* T_nu B
 = -T_mu^-1 nabla_mu B
   +T_mu^-1 nabla_nu B - nabla_nu B.                      (3)

No commutation of T_mu and T_nu, and no differentiation of the
transport coefficients, has occurred. Identity (3) follows by
substituting T_nu=I+h nabla_nu and T_mu^-1-I
=-h T_mu^-1 nabla_mu. Its gradient bound is (1+2tau)||nabla B||.

For the nu component of D_W^*D_W, each mu!=nu contributes
nabla_mu^* nabla_mu B_nu - nabla_mu^* nabla_nu B_mu.
Consequently (1) costs (1+tau)s_1 ||nabla B||+s_2||B||;
(2) costs (1+3tau)s_1||nabla B||+tau^2 s_2||B||.
The total per mu is therefore

 (2+4tau)s_1||nabla B||+(1+tau^2)s_2||B||.

Every field value and covariant gradient used is based at x or x-h e_mu;
the transported field value in (2) is at x-h e_mu+h e_nu. All lie in
the stated Chebyshev-one stencil. Backward scalar differences are
forward differences at x-h e_mu, also inside that stencil.

Since tau<=5/4, 1+tau^2 <= 2+4tau. Summing over d-1 choices gives
exactly c_d=(2+4tau)(d-1), the coefficient asserted at the starred
step. The subsequent cutoff-difference and scale-comparison argument
then yields the printed c_zeta=2c_d, under its stated cover and collar
hypotheses. This calculation is valid for arbitrary noncommuting
transport matrices with the given inverse-norm bound.
