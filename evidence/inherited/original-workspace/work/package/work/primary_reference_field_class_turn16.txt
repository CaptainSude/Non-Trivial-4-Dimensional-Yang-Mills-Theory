primary homogeneous constraint chart: for the reference before II(1.10),
the fine field is an AUXILIARY lift of current Vk, not an independently
specified fine boundary of the local minimum.

2. What the original domain actually asks for

On X at the indicated fine spacing, B109 first treats (U,J) as an
independent pair, U group-complex-valued and J Lie-algebra-complex-valued.
Both transform in the stated gauge-covariant way. Its domain is the
union of complex gauge orbits of pairs admitting the following data:

(i) A real compact-group factor with small plaquette curvature and a
    small potential and first differences in a real gauge on each of
    the prescribed local cubes.
(ii) A complex relative exponential factor, with small potential and
     first covariant differences relative to that real factor.
(iii) The curvature of the full field and the independent current are
      bounded by their prescribed constants.
(iv) For the maximal native nested determining family on X, and each
     initial subsequence, the corresponding axial-gauge minimum formed
     from the actual averaged datum has both curvature and PHYSICAL
     current bounds on the two-layer inset. The same derived tests are
     imposed on the real factor.

Thus (iv) is not merely stationarity of U, and (iii) does not set J=0.
The special fine-field section is J=J_W(U), the Wilson first variation
in the source convention. Fine boundary/exterior slots are not erased:
the minimum tests use the declared native domains and their supplied
boundary data. The two-layer inset is part of the definition, not a
license to choose unrelated boundary fields.

The paragraph after (1.16) states a SUFFICIENT SUBCLASS: sufficiently
smaller constants in (i)--(iii) imply (iv). Its proof uses the bound on
the averaged datum and CMP102 Proposition9. It then explicitly treats
the physical-current section, requiring the corresponding current
bound also for the real factor. This is the route applicable to a fine
field perturbation below.

The reference paragraph before B122-II (1.10) substitutes V_k=M^k(U)
with U in the original complex regular field class. It does not impose
an arbitrary new independent mixed-current rider there. For safety the
present sufficient admission uses the physical-current section of the
original pair class. It does not identify every later occurrence of
the same shortened U-notation with every graded or arrested domain.

B119 (2.34)--(2.39) separately describes multiscale original-domain
conditions for the induction. They include tiered curvature/current
tests and local gauge bounds. A reference-input theorem must not be
strengthened to that complete mixed-history setting merely because
both domains use the letter U.

3. What CMP102 Proposition9 adds, and what it does not

The actual minimum in axial gauge extends analytically in relative
complex determining data. In its stated Landau representative, its
logarithmic response H(B) obeys the earlier field estimates, and its
functional derivative obeys (190). These include covariant second
derivative/current-type control and a Laplacian bound. Their kernel
scales have output length to power -3 and source length to power -d,
times the displayed exponential distance factor.

The theorem also specifies regularity assumptions on its external
background. It therefore supplies more than just a C1 map, but it does
not say that an arbitrary graded-tower minimum is homogeneous on its
artificial rim. In particular it does not remove the anchor mismatch
in Section4 of native_reference_score_attempt_turn15.txt by itself.

4. Compact C2 perturbations preserve the smaller fine-field subclass

Here is a direct local admission lemma, independent of minimization.
Use units in which the chosen current block has size one and fine
spacing eta. Assume a REAL fine anchor U_* has strict margin in the
smaller (i)--(iii) subclass, including its actual current, on the ENTIRE
target domain X (Z for the reference application). Separately choose
one real coherent gauge on the complete protected perturbation/read
patch. The global smaller-subclass condition is essential for invoking
the primary sufficient criterion for every derived minimum on X.
Let a(z) be holomorphic for |z|<r, real for real z, supported with its
full Wilson stencil strictly inside the patch, and satisfy

     ||a(z)||_infty + ||nabla_eta a(z)||_infty
             + ||nabla_eta^2 a(z)||_infty <= C_a |z|.       (4.1)

Set U(z)=exp(i eta a(z))U_*. The fixed real factor is U_*. Thus the
complex-factor condition follows directly from (4.1), and the real
factor's derived tests have not changed. The plaquette difference is
bounded by C|z| in physical curvature units.

The actual current difference is bounded by C|z|, uniformly in eta.
There is a direct PRIMARY identity for this step: B109(3.11), p272,
states J_W(exp(i eta a)U)-J_W(U)=D_U*D_U a+F(U,a), where F is local
in U, its plaquette, a and its first covariant derivative, and F(U,0)=0.
Its native analytic bounds are uniform in eta. The coherent C1 bound
on the anchor controls D_U*D_U a by the ordinary C2 norm of a. Thus
this displayed source identity proves the required current bound
without differentiating the anchor curvature. Equivalently, one can
see the same bound from the Wilson first variation: its derivative
is the covariant second-difference Hessian acting on a, plus curvature
times a and bounded coordinate/parallel-frame terms. In one coherent
weak gauge the bound is

 ||D J_W(U)[a]||_infty <= C[||nabla_eta^2 a||_infty
   +(1+||A_U||_infty)||nabla_eta a||_infty
   +(||nabla_eta A_U||_infty+||A_U||_infty^2
                   +||J_W(U)||_infty)||a||_infty].         (4.2)

All terms are local finite-difference/product identities; the second
derivatives fall on the perturbation, not on the anchor potential.
Integrating along t a and using Gronwall absorbs the current term.
This gives the asserted uniform difference bound on a fixed weak ball.
The same argument is holomorphic with absolute norms for complex z.

Choose r below a fixed multiple of the smallest original margin divided
by C_a. The primary B109 smaller-constants criterion then proves ALL
derived-minimum conditions (iv) for U(z). No new minimum output or
independent-current projection has been used. The fixed exterior is
literally unchanged, since the perturbation and its full stencil vanish
before that exterior. The complete gauge patch, and not an unrelated
cover by several gauges, is essential.

5. Exact-coordinate construction; separated from admission

To turn Section4 into a lift of ONE specified current link, it remains
to produce a(z) satisfying (4.1) and the exact M^k constraints. At the
flat adjusted-average level this is elementary. Two smooth interior
bumps per direction have linearly independent affine-hat moments for
the incoming/outgoing incident rows. The resulting 2d dim(g) matrix is
uniformly invertible under refinement. Smooth bumps supply C2 rather
than merely C1 bounds. Control ALL incident rows, not only the target.

Explicitly put N=L^k=2m+1 and label the longitudinal fine tail sites of
the centered owner block by j=-m,...,m. In the normalized k-step
straight-string average, their outgoing and incoming row weights are
(j+m+1)/N and (m-j)/N. They sum to one. This identity follows either by
counting translated length-N strings or by multiplying the one-step
uniform-average generating polynomials. Choose two fixed nonnegative
smooth longitudinal bumps supported in [1/8,3/8] and [-3/8,-1/8], and
one common smooth transverse bump supported strictly inside the owner
block. Take the longitudinal bumps strictly positive at EVERY point of
the respective open interval (the usual smooth exponential bump), and
the transverse factor strictly positive on (-3/8,3/8). Thus the positive
longitudinal bump includes 2/11 and 3/11. Normalize their discrete masses.
The corresponding outgoing
weighted means differ by at least 1/4: every point in the first support
has normalized longitudinal coordinate at least 1/4 greater than every
point in the second. The incoming means are their complements. For
N>=L>=11 every longitudinal interval contains fine sites, so every
discrete mass is positive. Riemann-sum convergence supplies a uniform
positive lower bound for all large N; the finitely many remaining N
have a positive minimum. The same argument applies transversely. The
two masses are also uniformly bounded above. The determinant is therefore bounded away from zero
uniformly in k. Multiplying the fixed smooth shapes by its inverse
gives uniform physical C2 bounds. At k=0 the map is the identity on
current links; use the target fine-link coordinate directly instead
of a fictitious interior continuum bump, with its enlarged protected
Wilson stencil. Strict owner-cell interior support is not claimed in
that k=0 case. It has its own finite uniform constants.

The ordinary average is not identical to that adjusted average. Its
flat endpoint-contour terms add a coarse gauge gradient d_c chi_k(a).
For a bubble in one block, chi_k is supported at that block's coarse
vertex. A compact smooth fine gauge parameter lambda with the opposite
value there and zero at the other coarse vertices cancels this gradient,
because ordinary gauge covariance gives

                  Q_ord d lambda = d_c lambda|coarse.

This repairs the flat right inverse and keeps compact support. It is
not permissible to omit this correction and declare the straight-string
matrix to be the literal ordinary averaging matrix.

The nonlinear finite-dimensional inverse follows when the actual iterated
ordinary averaging has the uniform smooth-bubble comparison

  ||D M^k(U_*) E_ord - D M^k(1) E_ord|| <= C a_*             (5.1)

and a uniform holomorphic second derivative on the fixed finite bubble
ball, independent of k. Then finite-dimensional inversion gives a(z)
with (4.1), exact prescribed target row, every other affected row fixed,
and all unaffected rows unchanged by endpoint-block locality.

Section7 proves (5.1) from the literal ordinary one-step averaging and
its gauge covariance. It uses a bounded-potential holomorphic map and
Cauchy; it does not propagate C2 norms through every intermediate step.
C2 is needed only for the finitely many INPUT bubble shapes. Merely
multiplying a fixed one-step constant would yield C^k and is invalid.

This closes the compact construction for an anchor with strict smaller-
subclass margin throughout the target X, a small coherent gauge on the
COMPLETE finite dependency collar, and protected variable support. It is not an admission theorem
for arbitrary overlapping weak gauges or independently supplied mixed
current riders.

Unchanged exterior field values do not imply unchanged derived minima.
If the anchor outside the patch is only in the larger admitted class,
the global smaller-constants implication cannot be used there. That
weaker anchor would need a separate response estimate for all derived
tests. Alternatively restrict the target X itself to the protected patch.

             |z| < c min(r_geom, a_margin/(C_E(1+C_J))).

For either a certified exact-coordinate lift or any already specified

  sd_s(partial_z log w) <= C/r,
  |Cov_s(f(Phi),partial_z log w)| <= C (g/r) Lip(f).

The reference cutoff, geometry, gauge slice and relative Haar variables

7. Uniform ordinary iterate: proof from B109(0.8),(0.11),(0.12)

This section supersedes the conditional status of (5.1) in the first
version of this note. It is an independent finite-dimensional argument
from the actual primary ordinary average M. It does not identify the
auxiliary corrected map below with the source's background-dependent Q.

Fix the primitive odd L. At ONE step let U(y,x) be the averaged contour
variable of B109(0.11). On a sufficiently small complex link-angle ball
define

 a(V;y)=exp[ average_(x in B(y)) log U_V(y,x) ],
 C(V)(c)=a(V;c_-)^{-1} M(V)(c) a(V;c_+).                    (7.1)

The one-step formulas involve finitely many links at fixed L and are
holomorphic on a fixed ball. At V=1 the first differential of log M is
the translated straight-string mean plus the averaged incoming endpoint
contour minus its outgoing counterpart. This follows by differentiating
the products/logarithm in (0.12), using the arithmetic-mean derivative
(0.8) for (0.11). The two endpoint terms cancel in (7.1). Hence, in
physical units, the first differential of C is Q_st, a positive normalized
straight-string average with sup operator norm exactly one.

For a fine spacing h set

 F_h(A)=(i L h)^{-1} log C(exp(i h A)).

A fixed-dimensional Taylor/Cauchy estimate gives

 F_h(0)=0, DF_h(0)=Q_st,
 ||DF_h(A)||_(infty->infty) <= 1+C_L h||A||_infty,         (7.2)

on h||A|| below a fixed radius. The factor h is genuine: a term of
degree n in the dimensionless link angles contributes h^(n-1) after
division by Lh. The finite-dimensional norm is the complete link sup
norm; its dimension depends on L only.

Starting at eta=L^-k and h_t=L^t eta, iterate C with reference1 at every
step, C_t=exp(i h_t A_t). Equation(7.2) gives

 a_(t+1)<=a_t+C_L h_t a_t^2,
 sum_(t<k)h_t=(1-eta)/(L-1).

For sufficiently small a_0, a standard bootstrap gives a_t<=2a_0
simultaneously for all k and all t<=k. This is valid for complex fields.

The ordinary average is exactly gauge covariant by the product formula
and similarity covariance of its analytic logarithms/group means.
Define g_0=1 and, at each surviving coarse vertex,

              g_(t+1)=g_t a(C_t).

Then induction gives the EXACT ordered identity

                     M^t(U)=C_t^{g_t}.                    (7.3)

Each elementary contour has fixed length at fixed L, so
||log a(C_t)||<=C_L h_t a_0. Ordered products and inverses therefore
satisfy ||g_t-1||+||g_t^-1-1||<=C_L h_t a_0. Combining with C_t gives

 ||(i h_t)^-1 log M^t(exp(i eta A))||_infty<=K_L||A||_infty. (7.4)

All logarithms stay on their original branch after one fixed reduction
of a_0. The maps are holomorphic on that same complex sup ball, uniform
in t,k and compatible volume. Neither the source Q nor physical-current
estimates enter this purely averaging argument.

At final h_k=1, Banach-valued Cauchy applied to (7.4) bounds the first
two derivatives of P(A)=(1/i)log M^k(exp(i eta A)) uniformly. For a
coherent anchor U_*=exp(i eta A_*), the relative exponential family is
represented by the fine potential

 B(a,A_*)=(i eta)^-1 log[exp(i eta a)exp(i eta A_*)].

This BCH map and its derivatives are uniform in eta; B(0,A_*)=A_* and
D_aB(0,A_*)=I+O(eta||A_*||). The relative output logarithm at M^kU_*
has another fixed analytic BCH coordinate change. Consequently its
derivative on the fixed bubble space differs from the flat derivative
by O(||A_*||), proving (5.1) with a depth-independent constant. The
second derivative on that bubble space is uniformly bounded as well.

The support statements used in Section5 can also be checked directly
from (0.12): a row uses its two endpoint blocks; internal endpoint
contours stay in the owner block. Nested aligned block composition
preserves this property. In particular g_k(y), and its flat differential
chi_k, read only the fine block owned by y. On a bubble in one final
block, Q_ord,k=Q_st,k+d_c chi_k, and chi_k vanishes at every other final
vertex. A smooth compact gauge bump with the prescribed value at y
therefore gives the exact flat endpoint correction in Section5, with
uniform C3 norm for the gauge parameter and uniform C2 norm for its
fine gradient. The finite nonlinear inverse changes only the finitely
many incident output rows.

Differentiating that exact inverse at z=0 gives compact exact constraint
duals on this protected ONE-BLOCK ordinary-average fiber, with uniform
C1/C2 bounds. These may be paired with an actual stationary current to
recover its multiplier from the Wilson first variation. This observation
does not upgrade the result to a heterogeneous tower: finer retained
rows can read the same support, and must be separately controlled before
any such dual is used in a mixed constraint family.

8. Reference boundary dictionary: an auxiliary global lift is sufficient

There is a simpler source-attached option for a globally homogeneous
regular anchor. B119(1.2)--(1.3), p246, defines its local test minimum
from the copied field Q^1 V; the text explicitly states dependence on
V restricted to the enlarged test cube. B119(2.13)--(2.16), pp256--257,
repeats this construction with k-block copies. The complete determining
datum, including the low-tier rim, is the averaged datum of that copied
CURRENT field. This differs from the arbitrary heterogeneous retained
datum V_j in (2.10)--(2.12).

B122-II(1.3), p357, specifies the reference free-core minimum by the
constraint datum M_(B_k(Z))(Q^k V_k) on Z outside Lambda. Thus its fixed
rim/fine-boundary entries are functions of CURRENT V_k; no separately
supplied earlier fine field is present in this displayed reference
problem. The paragraph before(1.10), p358, replaces precisely V_k by
M^k(U) in that function. It does not substitute U itself as the rim
field of the local variational problem.

Suppose an auxiliary homogeneous path U(z) is in the original fine-field
class and realizes M^k(U(z))=V_k(z) on the COMPLETE current read, with
only the chosen collar coordinate varying. Evaluating the source
analytic extension on U(z) therefore gives the EXACT same reference
weight as evaluating the original reference rule on V_k(z). Its copied
boundary data, the fixed exterior, and their derivatives are those of
V_k(z), even if U(z) changes on fine links outside Z. No D_eP term is
discarded: all dependence of the local reference minimum is still
differentiated through its actual V_k argument.

For a coordinate whose copied determining footprint misses the fixed
boundary/rim, those boundary entries remain literally fixed. If the
coordinate reaches the rim, their induced variation is part of the
reference derivative, not an unnoticed independent boundary parameter.
The geometry and gauge slice remain fixed in both cases, and the path
must stay in their admitted branch.

This dictionary permits the primary homogeneous arbitrary-regular-U
constraint chart, checked separately in
work/protected_native_current_admission_turn16.txt, to supply the path.
It need not take the graded reference minimum U0 as its anchor. Hence
the extra graded-anchor premise H_min in turn15 is sufficient but not
necessary for this fixed-reference score problem.

Precise limit: the bypass is not applicable to a different function
which genuinely reads independent fine exterior variables, retained
old-tier V_j, moving branch selectors, or a change in the determining
geometry. Nor does an anchor known only on an arbitrary collection of
gauge patches automatically admit a global homogeneous extension. The
compact construction covers one complete protected coherent patch;
the homogeneous-chart option assumes its declared homogeneous anchor.
