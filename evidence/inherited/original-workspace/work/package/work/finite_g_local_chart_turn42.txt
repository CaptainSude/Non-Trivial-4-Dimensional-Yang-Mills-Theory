RESULT

There is a concrete finite-g reference beyond the classical saddle and
leading determinant. For every FIXED integer p>=1, the exact first-step
constraint chart yields an entire polynomial fluctuation interaction,
with exponentially weighted connected coefficient kernels and a positive
degree-(2p+2) stabilizer. It is integrable under the actual constrained
Gaussian AND under its positive forest covariance weakenings. On real
weak data its normalized law approximates the intrinsic energy-window
reference with error

 C_p N g^(2p) + C exp[C N-c g^(2a-2)]
                  + C exp[C N-c(Lambda^2-S_min/g^2)],   (0.1)

where 0<a<1 is fixed, N<=C_L R^4, and N g^a is sufficiently small.
Thus every polylogarithmic box is allowed. The last term is the ACTUAL
energy-window tail, not an artificial fixed-chart exp(-c/g^2) tail.
Increasing the window constant once for a chosen p makes it pay any
specified fixed power of g. All Gaussian and Haar/coarea normalizers are
retained.

This proves an actual stable analytic approximate reference at each fixed
order, not a convergent all-order expansion or an iteration-invariant
Yang--Mills interaction class. It does not assert gauge covariance of a
finite Taylor truncation under nonlinear changes between coarse frames.

2. AN EXACT AMBIENT EXTENSION OF THE NONLINEAR FIBER CHART

For b in the FULL fine chord space, solve

 x(b,u)=x_*(u)+P b+E tau(b,u),
 F_e(x(b,u))=F_e(x_*(u)),       tau(0,u)=0.             (2.1)

Let A_*=DF_e(x_*)E. On the common ball A_* is close to I in a weighted
row/column norm. Its inverse has an exponentially weighted kernel. The
implicit equation is explicitly

 tau=-A_*^-1 DF_e(x_*)P b
      -A_*^-1 [F_e(x_*+P b+E tau)-F_e(x_*)
                            -DF_e(x_*)(P b+E tau)].   (2.2)

For ||b||_infinity<r_0 this is a uniform analytic contraction after
separating its displayed linear term. r_0 is independent of N. On b in K,
P b=b, and (2.1) is exactly the original fiber parameterization with
B=B_*+b. Off K it is an explicitly chosen analytic extension; it is not
another integration variable or an altered coarse constraint.

For completeness, the positive spatial coefficient estimate follows
directly from (2.2). Retain all input and output legs in the weighted
tree norm of analytic_tree_norm_turn41. The maps P,E,DF and A_*^-1 have
bounded one-leg weighted sums, and the local nonlinear rows have common
geometric Taylor bounds. Every coefficient in the implicit recursion is
a tree contraction. Its norm is bounded by the corresponding product,
because the tree can be rooted at ANY external leg. The scalar majorant

 w(t)=a t+k C^2(w+t)^2/[1-C(w+t)]

therefore bounds the recursion on a common radius. This proves a common
geometric bound on the absolute coefficient tensors of x(b,u), including
their physical positions, with one reserved exponential fraction for
tree-hull assignment. No separate radius or constant is chosen for each
source order. This is the same elementary tree argument as the saddle
theorem, applied to the literal LOCAL constraint equation (2.2).

Define on this ambient ball

 f(b,u)=S_e(x(b,u)),
 ell(b,u)=log J(b,u),
 J=c_E j_C(x)/[j_J(v) det(DF_e(x)E)].                  (2.3)

The determinant branch is the one fixed by its positive real center.
The constant c_E and the field-independent output Haar factor are kept.
They do not enter derivatives in b. At real u, J>0 on the chart.

The action has local vertices composed with (2.1). The determinant
logarithm is treated by its convergent local matrix series. A marked
trace coefficient is a cycle; open one bounded inverse edge and apply
the same any-leg tree contraction bound. Thus f-f(0) and ell-ell(0)
have exponentially weighted, connected Taylor kernels on the same
common ball. The vacuum constants f(0),ell(0) remain separate; an
unmarked trace is extensive and is not falsely assigned a rooted bound.

In particular write the homogeneous polynomials, including Taylor
factorials in their definition,

 f(b,u)=s(u)+q_u(b)+sum_(d>=3) F_d(b,u),
 q_u(b)=.5 b^T H_u b,
 ell(b,u)-ell(0,u)=sum_(d>=1) L_d(b,u).                (2.4)

The first field derivative of f is zero: x(b,u) lies in the same fiber
and x_* is constrained critical. On K, H_u is the exact constrained
Hessian in B coordinates and satisfies cI<=H_u<=CI for real u.
The coefficient bounds imply, for real or complex ambient b,

 |F_d(b,u)|+|L_d(b,u)|<=C^d sum_i |b_i|^d.             (2.5)

Proof of this useful scalar consequence: apply weighted AM--GM to every
absolute monomial, |b_i1...b_id|<=d^-1 sum_a |b_ia|^d, and then sum
coefficients with each selected leg fixed. The any-leg positive bound
is precisely what removes a factor N here. Tail versions of (2.5)
hold on a smaller fixed sup ball with geometric constants.

3. THE GAUSSIAN CAN BE REDUNDANT; ITS INTERPOLATIONS NEED NOT LIE IN K

On K the actual Gaussian covariance is the inverse of H_u restricted
to K, embedded into the ambient space. It is positive semidefinite and
singular in the normal directions. Its kernel is exponentially decaying
with uniform row/column bounds. One way to see the latter is to use the
physical constrained covariance of the nonlinear KKT matrix and apply P
on both sides: B=P x exactly. P has finite range. No long orthonormal
basis of K is needed.

Fix the identity-data Gaussian C_0 on K. For u in a sufficiently small
common complex ball, H_u-H_0 is small in operator norm and in the same
weighted coefficient norm. On real data the quadratic adjustment can
either be included in the interaction against C_0 or integrated into
the exact H_u Gaussian; these descriptions retain the determinant ratio.

There is a real trap here. A covariance forest weakening usually leaves
K. For example C=[[1,1],[1,1]] is supported on x_1=x_2. Replacing its
off-diagonal entries by t gives C_t=[[1,t],[t,1]], and
(1,-1)C_t(1,-1)^T=2(1-t). Thus an interaction defined ONLY on ker Q
cannot simply be inserted into a weakened Gaussian expectation.

Equation (2.1) resolves this at the level needed for a Gaussian forest
formula. It defines the interaction on the redundant ambient variables,
and agrees with the physical fiber at the undeformed covariance.
For any positive-semidefinite forest multiplier T with diagonal1,
C_T=C_0 circle T is positive semidefinite and

                  ||C_T||_op<=||C_0||_op.             (3.1)

Indeed 0<=C_0<=||C_0||I and the Schur map by T preserves this order
and fixes I. Its Gaussian can be degenerate; polynomial integration
and finite-order interpolation identities remain meaningful. It is not
asserted that these auxiliary Gaussians are constrained physical laws.

4. A GLOBALLY STABLE ENTIRE POLYNOMIAL INTERACTION

Fix p>=1. Take the exact polynomials of (2.4) through degrees 2p+1
in f and 2p-1 in ell. With xi=b/g define

 V_(p,g)(xi,u)=.5 xi^T(H_u-H_0)xi
      +sum_(d=3)^(2p+1) g^(d-2) F_d(xi,u)
      -sum_(d=1)^(2p-1) g^d L_d(xi,u)
      +D_p g^(2p) sum_i xi_i^(2p+2).                 (4.1)

Homogeneity identifies this with the corresponding truncated action
at b=gxi, divided by g^2, and the truncated log density. The stabilizer
is diagonal in the local ambient chord coordinates. It is entire, real
and positive on real xi, and has a one-site carrier. All other terms
retain their actual exponentially weighted tree kernels from Section2.
No compact holomorphic cutoff is asserted or used.

There are constants D_p<infinity and g_p>0, independent of N, and a
smaller common complex data ball, such that for real xi and g<=g_p

 Re V_(p,g)(xi,u)>=-kappa ||xi||_2^2-C_p g^2 N,
                  2 kappa ||C_0||_op<1.              (4.2)

Proof. Choose kappa sufficiently small first. Small data pay the
quadratic H_u-H_0 term. For each scalar t>=0 and the finite higher-degree
coefficient sum in (2.5), choose D_p so that

 sum_(d=3)^(2p+1) C^d t^d
   +sum_(d=3)^(2p-1) C^d t^d
       <=(kappa/4)t^2+(D_p/2)t^(2p+2).               (4.3)

Such a finite constant exists: near zero the left side is o(t^2),
while at infinity the rightmost degree dominates. The quadratic Haar
term carries g^2 and is paid by choosing g_p small. Its linear term
satisfies g^2 C sum_i|b_i| <=(kappa/4)||b||^2+C_p g^4N.
Apply (4.3) to each |b_i|, absorb the harmless g^2 coefficients, divide
by g^2, and increase D_p once. This proves (4.2), also for complex u
because (2.5) bounds the absolute coefficient values on the common ball.
For real u, V is real. Constants may grow with p; no all-p estimate is
hidden in this finite construction.

It follows that exp(-V_(p,g)) is integrable under EVERY C_T of (3.1),
uniformly in its interpolation parameters at finite volume. In fact

 integral exp[-Re V_(p,g)] dgamma_(C_T)
 <=exp(C_p g^2N) det(I-2kappa C_T)^(-1/2)
 <=exp(C_p' N).                                      (4.4)

Zero eigenvalues contribute1 to the determinant. Complex datum
derivatives on a smaller ball are legitimate by domination and Cauchy;
the finite polynomial field degree and its positive highest term also
give direct domination on compact data sets. This supplies an actual
analytic partition VALUE on a common data ball. It does NOT prove it
zero-free there, nor that its connected logarithm has a convergent
volume-uniform polymer expansion. Equation(4.4) is a stability bound,
not that missing connected estimate.

5. ACTUAL NORMALIZED APPROXIMATION ON EVERY POLYLOG BOX

Fix real weak u. Let mu_chart be the exact positive law in the SAME
fixed small convex K-domain D0 with density exp[-f(b,u)/g^2]J(b,u),

mu_chart. The fixed domain contains B_* in a common inner margin; its

 exp[-s/g^2] J_* (2pi g^2)^(dim K/2)
                    det(H_0|K)^(-1/2) times its expectation. (5.1)

Thus all vacuum, Jacobian and Gaussian factors can be restored exactly.
Using H_u instead merely transfers the exact determinant ratio.

Fix 0<a<1, r_g=g^a, and suppose N r_g is sufficiently small. Let
C_g={||b||_infinity<=r_g}. This intermediate event is used for the
estimate only; it is not part of the polynomial reference law.
On C_g, both exact and polynomial densities relative to the H_u Gaussian
are sandwiched by a quadratic perturbation O(r_g)||xi||^2 and a factor
exp[O(N r_g)]. Indeed the nonlinear action starts at degree3, so (2.5)
gives |f-s-q|<=C r_g||b||^2; the corresponding finite polynomial obeys
the same bound, including its stabilizer for fixed p. The log-density

The missing Gaussian mass of C_g is at most C N exp[-c r_g^2/g^2].
Outside C_g, exact constrained convexity on D0 and the complete bounded

stability proof can leave a positive quadratic margin, giving the SAME

On C_g the difference of the two log densities satisfies the actual

 |U_exact-U_p|
 <=C_p g^(2p) sum_i (|xi_i|^(2p+2)+|xi_i|^(2p)),       (5.2)

where the stabilizer is included in U_p. Integrate
|exp(-U_exact)-exp(-U_p)|<=|U_exact-U_p|exp[-min(U_exact,U_p)]
using the common slightly weakened H_u Gaussian. Each fixed-coordinate
moment is uniformly bounded; summing i costs N, not a fresh volume per

Division by the positive normalizers and the elementary normalized-density
inequality yield

 TV(mu_chart,mu_p)
 <=C_p N g^(2p)+C_p exp[C_p N-c_p g^(2a-2)].           (5.3)

TV is sup-event distance. Constants absorb fixed factors2. The argument
is uniform in the declared real weak data and all positions. For every
polylogarithmic N, N g^a->0 and the second term is smaller than every
power of g. This is stronger than a comparison carrying exp(CN) in
front of the central Taylor error. The shrinking intermediate radius,
not a claim of globally small interaction, is what removes that cost.

The same proof gives a weighted version for any common measurable test
obeying |G(xi)|<=A(1+||xi||_2)^k, k fixed:

 |E_chart G-E_p G|
 <=A C_(p,k) (N+1)^(k/2+1) g^(2p)
       +A C_(p,k)(N+1)^(k/2) exp[C_(p,k)N-c_(p,k)g^(2a-2)]. (5.4)

Use Gaussian moments in the central integral and reserve half the tail
exponent for polynomial weights. Normalization terms use the same
moments; they are not discarded. A physical mark outside the chart must
have an explicitly chosen extension or polynomial approximation before
applying (5.4). No engineering coefficient is absorbed into A.

6. THE INTRINSIC ENERGY WINDOW AND THE TWO DIFFERENT TAILS

Let mu_E be the actual intrinsic-window reference of turn41 on the SAME
weak real fiber. Its weight in mu_chart is
psi(f/(g^2 Lambda^2)). Write E_*=Lambda^2-s/g^2>0.
The upper Hessian bound gives f-s<=C||b||^2 on D0. Hence a nonzero
deficit implies ||xi||^2>=c E_*. The lower Hessian bound, complete
log-J ratio and the already bounded positive normalization give

 epsilon_E=E_chart[1-psi(f/(g^2Lambda^2))]
            <=min{1,C exp(CN-c E_*)}.                 (6.1)

Since mu_E is this weighted probability normalized by 1-epsilon_E,
TV(mu_E,mu_chart)<=epsilon_E. Combining with (5.3) proves (0.1).
Weighted versions follow by the same tail-moment argument and the exact
normalization identity of energy_window_marked_transfer41.

The turn41 Lambda includes S_min/g^2<=C N Z^2 and a chosen multiple
of N+ell. For any fixed p,k, that multiple can be selected ONCE so
(6.1) is below a desired fixed power of g, while all supported fields
remain in the same chart because g Lambda is polylogarithmically small.
If the original window is kept unchanged, retain (6.1) exactly instead
of claiming an arbitrarily high error order.

The artificial fixed-chart boundary is an exp(CN-c/g^2) phenomenon.
Removing the shrinking ENERGY window is only an exp(CN-c E_*)
phenomenon. These estimates are not interchangeable. No data derivative
of psi is taken in this comparison, and psi is not made holomorphic.

7. WHAT THIS CONSTRUCTIVE REFERENCE SUPPLIES AND DOES NOT SUPPLY

Supplied at the stated first bare step:

 * An exact holomorphic ambient extension of the local nonlinear
   constraint chart, agreeing with the physical kerQ fiber.
 * A positive exponentially decaying Gaussian covariance in redundant
   ambient variables, with explicit treatment of forest weakenings that
   leave kerQ.
 * For each fixed p, an entire interaction with actual connected
   coefficient kernels and a volume-independent stability construction,
   on one common weak-data ball, uniformly over those Gaussian weakenings.
 * An actual normalized approximation to the original intrinsic-window
   reference, with arbitrarily high FIXED power accuracy on polylog boxes
   after its stated tail allowance. Bare-law omitted marks are separately
   controlled by energy_window_marked_transfer41; no conditional law is
   silently replaced by that annealed bound.

Still missing is the convergent connected Gaussian forest/cluster bound
for the COMPLETE finite-g logarithm in the needed local analytic norm,
with normalized source derivatives and a reusable nonzero background
domain. Stability (4.4) alone does not prove it. In particular constants
D_p,C_p need not be controlled as p grows; choosing p separately at every
source arity is not an all-arity VALUE theorem. A finite Taylor reference
also need not transform exactly under nonlinear coarse gauge changes,
even though the underlying intrinsic-window measure is gauge invariant.
The construction is a local weak-framed reference, not an asserted global
gauge-invariant replacement action.

The ambient interaction is an auxiliary extension, not an independent
unconstrained physical field model. Complete determinants and vacuum
factors remain in (5.1), and eventual contact/source cancellations must
be performed for the actual normalized quantities. No arbitrary exterior,
later effective measure, recurrent mixed-history domain or all-scale
Yang--Mills conclusion follows from this one-step result.
