GENERATED INTERACTIONS AND THE TRUE FINITE-STEP SOURCE RESPONSE
19 October 2026. TURN45. generated45 producer.

RESULT AND LIMITATION

The actual constant-source Morse estimate of TURN44 survives addition
of an analytic generated action of order one per cell. The action need
not be small in value, and it may have a nonzero linear fluctuation jet
at the OLD classical saddle. Its first two fluctuation derivatives must
obey the stated polynomial bounds on the common analytic domain.

For an unmarked generated action, the additional marked coarse-Hessian
error remains O(g^4 N^4), with the same exponentially small cutoff tail.
For a source-dependent generated action there is an additional EXACT
transported term: its source dependence evaluated at the old classical
saddle. It is retained, not absorbed into an unspecified counterterm.
An explicit fixed marginal projection removes the algebraic freedom in
that split. A flat-point relative quadratic form bound is preserved
without amplification by classical minimizer transport.

These are finite-step statements under explicit domains. They do not
prove the native all-scale interaction class, its irrelevant contraction,
or a cutoff-uniform source identity. A simple example below shows that
unmarked bounds alone cannot give the required marked g^4 estimate.

1. PRECISE INPUT CLASS AND THE ACTUAL INTEGRAL

Retain the complete framed fiber, original charged action f, Haar/coarea
factor J, old classical saddle B_*(u), and frozen REAL energy window of
complex_energy_remainder_turn42 and original_source_route_turn44. All
charged plaquettes, including the original crossing terms, remain in f.
The Morse map is also frozen:

 B=B_*(u)+b(y,u),       f(B,u)=s(u)+(1/2)y^t y,
 A(y,u)=J(B_*(u)+b(y,u),u) det D_y b(y,u),
 A0(u)=A(0,u),         d=dim ker Q<=C N,  a=d/2.

Here ||u||_infinity<r_N=c_u/sqrt(N) and ||y||_2<r_y are inside the
fixed chart of TURN44 Section 3A; r_y>0 is fixed and sufficiently small.
On that chart the first and second y derivatives of log(A/A0) have
operator norms at most C N. The actual window requires the smaller
datum radius rho=c_0 g Lambda/sqrt(N), with E0=g^2 Lambda^2 and
|s(u)|<E0/8. The larger domain belongs to the amplitudes only.

Let W(B,u;zeta) be holomorphic after composition with this chart on

 ||u||_infinity<r_N,  ||y||_2<r_y,  |zeta|<c_s/g^2,

where c_s<1/8 is fixed. Put

 Wtilde(y,u;zeta)=W(B_*(u)+b(y,u),u;zeta),
 W_*(u;zeta)=Wtilde(0,u;zeta).

Assume, uniformly on this common product domain,

 ||D_y Wtilde||<=M1,       ||D_y^2 Wtilde||<=M2.       (1.1)

These are complex operator bounds in Euclidean fluctuation directions.
No bound on the possibly extensive W_* is needed for the normalized
Laplace ratio. To identify the zero-source integral with a positive
conditional law, require W to be real on real fields at zeta=0.

Set

 K=1+N^2+M1^2+M2,       v_*=c_v/K.                   (1.2)

Take c_v sufficiently small, and assume the polylogarithmic regime in
which K and Lambda grow at most as fixed powers of log(1/g). Require
Lambda^2>=A[N+log(1/g)], with A a fixed sufficiently large constant
relative to the tail constants below, as well as 3E0<v_*/4 and
rho<r_N/4. Thus epsilon_bulk,W+tau_g<1/4 for sufficiently small g;
the lower bound on Lambda, not polylogarithmic growth alone, makes
the Gamma window tail small. Additional retained weak-data parameters
must still obey the original TURN42 containment requirements.
The true integral is

 a_W(u;zeta)=int exp[-(g^-2-zeta)f(B,u)-W(B,u;zeta)]
                         J(B,u) psi(f(B,u)/E0)dB.     (1.3)

Thus the source is physical if W contains the actual previously
generated source residual. In the affine case W=W0-zeta P, derivatives
of (1.3) insert f+P. The theorem never identifies a chosen W with that
physical residual without a separate exact integration identity.

The cutoff remains psi(f/E0), not psi((f+g^2 W)/E0). No cutoff, chart,
saddle, or reference datum is moved when zeta varies. This premise must
be checked when applying the result to a generated RG step.

2. THE GENERATED AMPLITUDE AND WHY A LINEAR JET IS ALLOWED

Define

 G(y,u;zeta)=[A(y,u)/A0(u)]
             exp[-Wtilde(y,u;zeta)+W_*(u;zeta)].       (2.1)

Then G(0,u;zeta)=1. Its logarithm has first derivative at most CN+M1
and second derivative at most CN+M2. Taylor's formula along a complex
line yields, for ||y||<=2sqrt(v_*),

 |[G(y)+G(-y)]/2-1| <= C K ||y||^2.                  (2.2)

Indeed the linear term of G cancels between y and -y. The remaining
second derivative is bounded by C[(CN+M1)^2+CN+M2], multiplied by
exp[C(CN+M1)||y||+C(CN+M2)||y||^2]. Both exponents are bounded on
this ball by (1.2), and the displayed coefficient is at most C K.
This proof does not set D_y Wtilde(0) to zero.

For d>0 let

 h_W(v,u;zeta)=average_(theta in S^(d-1))
                       G(sqrt(2v)theta,u;zeta).

Odd angular powers vanish. Hence h_W is holomorphic in v on |v|<2v_*,
jointly with u and zeta, and

 h_W(0,u;zeta)=1,       |h_W(v,u;zeta)-1|<=C K |v|.   (2.3)

Choosing c_v small gives |h_W|<=2 there. The average is normalized
surface measure, so it introduces no sphere-area or dimension factor.
The old saddle need not be the saddle of f+g^2 W: the displacement
caused by the linear W jet is already encoded in (2.1)--(2.3).

3. ACTUAL JOINT HOLOMORPHY, ZERO FREENESS, AND BULK/TAIL SPLIT

Put lambda=g^-2-zeta and use the principal powers in its right-half-
plane disc. The exact radial formula from TURN44 applies with h_W:

 a_W(u;zeta)=exp[-lambda s(u)-W_*(u;zeta)]
                 A0(u)(2pi/lambda)^a R_E,W(u;zeta).   (3.1)

To define R_E,W at complex u split at the fixed real total energy
t0=E0/2, put q=t0-s(u), and use

 R_E,W=lambda^a/Gamma(a) {
   q^a int_0^1 exp(-lambda q z) z^(a-1) h_W(qz,u;zeta) dz
   + int_t0^(2E0) exp[-lambda(t-s)] (t-s)^(a-1)
                           h_W(t-s,u;zeta) psi(t/E0)dt }.
                                                               (3.2)

Every evaluation of psi is at a REAL t/E0. Parameter integration
proves the joint holomorphic extension on (rho, c_s/g^2). For d=0
the ratio is exactly one and all following error terms can be zero.

Define separately, on the larger datum domain r_N,

 R_bulk,W=lambda^a/Gamma(a) int_0^(v_*)
                   exp(-lambda v) v^(a-1)h_W(v,u;zeta)dv.         (3.3)

As in TURN44, change variable w=lambda v and deform the contour to a
positive real Gamma segment followed by a vertical connector. Its
triangle remains within |v|<=v_* and the corresponding amplitude
domain. On the real segment (2.3) gives

 Gamma(a)^-1 int_0^infinity exp(-w)w^(a-1)
                  C K |w/lambda|dw <= C g^2 N K.

Only the endpoint connectors and omitted tails cost exp(CN). Thus

 ||R_bulk,W-1||_(r_N,c_s/g^2)
   <=C g^2 N K+C exp[CN-c/(g^2 K)] =:epsilon_bulk,W,  (3.4)
 ||R_E,W-R_bulk,W||_(rho,c_s/g^2)
   <=C exp(CN-c Lambda^2)=:tau_g.                    (3.5)

For (3.5), the common real Gamma core cancels before estimating. The
remaining paths begin at Re(lambda q)>=c Lambda^2. All their h_W
arguments remain within |v|<2v_*; the true outer interval has
|t-s|<=17E0/8. The endpoint of (3.3) is farther away. These facts
justify the same Gamma-tail estimate as TURN44 without an exp(CN)
factor multiplying the main g^2 N K error.

For small g the ratios are within 1/4 of one. They and a_W are zero
free on their declared domains. There are analytic logarithms

 B_W=log R_bulk,W,       ||B_W||<=C epsilon_bulk,W,
 T_W=log R_E,W-log R_bulk,W,       ||T_W||<=C tau_g.    (3.6)

An extensive W_* does not invalidate zero freeness: exp(-W_*) never
vanishes. A hidden branch of log W is not used; W itself is assumed
as the actual analytic action in (1.1).

3A. A SMALL-DOMAIN ANALYTIC TAIL NEED NOT EXTEND TO r_N

The already produced ACTUAL action does not automatically belong to
the larger-domain W class: its energy-window tail was proved only on
the rho domain. There is a direct extension that respects this fact.

Let the additional action be W+V, where W obeys Section 1 and V is
holomorphic only on

 ||u||_infinity<rho,  ||y||_2<3g Lambda,  |zeta|<c_s/g^2,
                         ||V||<=eta,                (3A.1)

with eta<1/16. Here V is written in the OLD Morse coordinates; its
true field-domain containment is an application premise. It need not
have derivative bounds on the larger r_N chart. For the zero-source
integral to remain a positive conditional law, require V real on real
fields at zeta=0 as well; this extra condition is unnecessary for the
analytic estimates alone. Do not factor V(0)
out. Replace h_W by the angular average of G(y)exp[-V(y)].
On (3A.1), |G(y)|<=C: integrate its bounded logarithmic first
derivative along the radius and use g Lambda(N+M1)=o(1). Therefore
the new angular amplitude differs from h_W by at most C eta.

All contours used for the actual radial formula have
|v|<=17E0/8, hence ||sqrt(2v)theta||<3g Lambda. Rotating the inner
core to the real Gamma segment bounds its ratio difference by C eta;
the other contour pieces give C eta exp(CN-c Lambda^2). Thus

 ||a_(W+V)/a_W-1||_(rho,c_s/g^2)<=C eta.              (3A.2)

For sufficiently small eta this ratio is zero free, its logarithm is
O(eta), and it adds to (4.3) at most

 n! C^n g^(2n) rho^-2 eta
                         ||v||_infinity||w||_infinity.           (3A.3)

In particular its first marked Hessian costs C N Lambda^-2 eta.
An exponentially small inherited analytic tail can consequently be
kept on its smaller domain. The theorem still requires that the
ENTIRE next-step energy support and the indicated complex contours
lie in that inherited domain. No repeated-domain containment is
deduced from the small value eta.

4. EXACT TRANSPORT OF THE MARKED GENERATED ACTION

Let Delta W_*(u;zeta)=W_*(u;zeta)-W_*(u;0). The true source log is

 L_W(u,zeta)=log[a_W(u;zeta)/a_W(u;0)]
 =zeta s(u)-Delta W_*(u;zeta)
       -a log(1-g^2 zeta)+E_W(u,zeta),                (4.1)

 E_W=log R_E,W(u;zeta)-log R_E,W(u;0).

Subtract its value at u=0, using s(0)=0:

 C_W(u,zeta)=zeta s(u)
   -[Delta W_*(u;zeta)-Delta W_*(0;zeta)]
                   +E_W(u,zeta)-E_W(0,zeta).         (4.2)

For n>=1, Cauchy on the TWO datum domains gives, at zero and in any
two datum directions v,w,

 |D_u^2 partial_zeta^n E_W(0,0)[v,w]|
 <= n! C^n {g^(2n+2) N^2 K + g^(2n)rho^-2 tau_g}
                         ||v||_infinity||w||_infinity.           (4.3)

The remote bulk endpoint is absorbed into tau_g under 3E0<v_*/4.
The constants come from r_N^-2=O(N), and all source orders use the
same disc c_s/g^2. Therefore

 D^2 partial_zeta C_W(0,0)
   =D^2s(0)-D^2 partial_zeta W_*(0;0)+Err_1,

 ||Err_1||_(infinity,infinity)
 <=C g^4 N^2 K+C N Lambda^-2 exp(CN-c Lambda^2).       (4.4)

For n>=2 the classical D^2s term is absent and the exact transported
term is -D^2 partial_zeta^n W_*(0;0), with error (4.3).

If W is source independent, the transported term is exactly zero.
If M1,M2<=C N, then K<=C N^2 and (4.4) gives precisely

           O(g^4 N^4)+the stated window tail.        (4.5)

Thus an O(1)-per-cell one-loop action does not destroy the summable
coupling power, even if its old-saddle linear jet is nonzero.

In the affine physical case W=W0-zeta P, (4.4) instead reads

 D^2 partial_zeta C_W=D^2(s+P_*)+Err_1,
            P_*(u)=P(B_*(u),u).                     (4.6)

To invoke (4.5) in this case it suffices that W0 has the stated O(N)
fluctuation derivatives and that P has first/second fluctuation
derivatives O(g^2 N) on the large datum chart. That is what makes
W0-zeta P obey (1.1) on |zeta|<c_s/g^2. Boundedness of W0 alone
does not supply this marked premise.

The generated coarse action, up to explicit vacuum factors, has the
EXACT finite-step form

    lambda s(u)+W_*(u;zeta)-log A0(u)-log R_E,W(u;zeta).           (4.7)

The new complete one-loop term -log A0 is source independent. Its
original Haar/coarea factors remain. The physical marked residual is
the transported old one plus the marked logarithmic ratio; it is not
identified just by declaring a coupling shift.

5. TWO EXACT GAUSSIAN CHECKS

A. Source-independent, nonzero linear jet.
Take one fluctuation y, f(y,u)=s(u)+y^2/2, A=1, and W=a(u)y.
Ignoring the remote energy cutoff for this exact Gaussian check,

 int exp[-lambda f-W]dy
 =exp[-lambda s]sqrt(2pi/lambda)exp[a(u)^2/(2lambda)].

Hence

 partial_zeta L(u,0)=s(u)+g^2/2+g^4 a(u)^2/2.         (5.1)

The nonzero linear W jet creates a g^4 correction, not a g^2 marked
coarse response. It is correctly retained by the angular estimate.
Putting back the same distant energy cutoff gives the previously
quantified exponentially small tail under the theorem's assumptions.

B. A source residual cannot be omitted or inferred from W0.
Take s(u)=u^2/2, f=(u^2+y^2)/2, W0=0, and P(u,y)=epsilon u^2 y^2.
At zeta=0, y is centered Gaussian with variance g^2, so the ACTUAL
observable f+P has conditional mean

       u^2/2+g^2/2+epsilon g^2 u^2.                  (5.2)

Its marked u Hessian differs from s'' by 2 epsilon g^2. Here P_*=0.
Thus a generic O(epsilon) analytic source residual gives a g^2 term;
one cannot assert an O(g^4) remainder from source-free data. On the
large common source disc, D_y^2(-zeta P) grows as epsilon g^-2 u^2,
so (1.1) with O(N) constants indeed fails unless the marked coefficient
has the additional g^2 scale or its marginal structure is extracted.
This finite-dimensional example is a test of the claimed hypotheses,
not a counterexample to Yang--Mills or to a gauge-normalized RG class.

6. FIXING THE MARGINAL EXTRACTION INSTEAD OF USING A FREE SPLIT

Let H=D^2s(0), and choose in advance a bounded linear functional ell
on the relevant symmetric datum Hessians such that ell(H)=1. Such
an ell is an explicit normalization choice; it must be supplied by
the intended scalar marginal frame, not selected after an estimate.
Define for the SAME exact C_W,

 zeta_out(zeta)=ell[D^2 C_W(0,zeta)],
 R_mark(u,zeta)=C_W(u,zeta)-zeta_out(zeta)s(u).        (6.1)

Then R_mark(0,zeta)=0 and ell[D^2 R_mark(0,zeta)]=0 exactly.
This is a fixed finite-step decomposition. Changing zeta_out by an
arbitrary h(zeta) violates the displayed normalization unless h=0.

For the physical affine case,

 zeta_out'(0)=1+ell[D^2P_*(0)]+ell[Err_1].            (6.2)

Thus the source coefficient and its residual are both computed from
the actual integral. The last term is bounded by ||ell|| times (4.4),
in the declared absolute bilinear norm. The same projection applied to
all n-source jets gives their transported term plus (4.3). No relative
bound in a gauge-null direction is asserted.

If an input marked family has a common anchored analytic tree norm,
its evaluation at the local saddle preserves that property, at a
smaller fixed radius: compose its Taylor tensors with the any-leg
saddle tensors of TURN41, apply the tree-contraction lemma, and use
the same convergent scalar majorant. The constant is independent of
box volume. This controls the transported term conditionally on that
input tree norm; it does NOT localize the new R_E,W remainder, for
which (4.3) remains a whole-box bound.

7. NONAMPLIFICATION OF A RELATIVE QUADRATIC SOURCE UNDER TRANSPORT

There is an exact improvement over repeatedly using arbitrary sup-norm
composition constants. Let S(x) have dS(0)=0 and positive semidefinite
H_f=D^2S(0). Let x_*(u) be a classical constrained minimizer with
x_*(0)=0, P_0=D x_*(0), and s(u)=S(x_*(u)). Then

                       H_c=D^2s(0)=P_0^t H_f P_0.   (7.1)

Let Q(x) be an input marked residual with dQ(0)=0 and K_f=D^2Q(0).
The chain rule gives

                     D^2[Q(x_*(u))](0)=P_0^t K_f P_0.            (7.2)

If -epsilon H_f<=K_f<=epsilon H_f as real quadratic forms, then

 -epsilon H_c<=D^2[Q(x_*(u))](0)<=epsilon H_c.         (7.3)

No invertibility or gap is needed for (7.3); gauge-null directions are
handled by the form inequality itself. No factor C>1 is incurred.
The zero-gradient premise is essential: otherwise the additional
dQ(0) D^2x_*(0) appears. For gauge-invariant analytic scalar functions
at the fully flat semisimple gauge configuration, simultaneous color
invariance implies this linear jet vanishes. At nonflat data it need
not vanish and is retained by the full formula (4.2).

Consequently, for a sequence of EXACT classical maps with
H_(j+1)=P_j^t H_j P_j and marked Hessian recurrence

 K_(j+1)=P_j^t K_j P_j+E_j,
                  -epsilon_j H_(j+1)<=E_j<=epsilon_j H_(j+1),

starting with K_0=H_0, induction gives

 (1-sum_(i<j)epsilon_i)H_j <= K_j
                   <=(1+sum_(i<j)epsilon_i)H_j.       (7.4)

This is an abstract transport statement, not an assertion that our
actual successive interacting steps meet all its premises. Applying
(4.4) to (7.4) also requires conversion of the absolute remainder to
a RELATIVE form bound. If H_c>=gamma I on a stated physical subspace,
the absolute ||.||_(infinity,infinity) estimate e gives a relative
constant at most e/gamma there, since ||v||_infinity<=||v||_2.
One must account for the actual gamma and for null directions. The
conversion is not automatic for a massless coarse Hessian.

Even (7.4) does not ensure the large complex source domain survives:
smallness relative to H alone need not imply the O(g_j^2) marked
fluctuation-derivative allowance in Section 4 uniformly along a chosen
trajectory. A fixed scalar extraction and contraction of irrelevant
source terms remain necessary. This prevents using (7.4) to disguise
the missing all-scale analytic source class.

There is nevertheless a sharper conditional UV bookkeeping statement.
Index j TOWARD the ultraviolet and let x_j=g_j^-2 satisfy
x_(j+1)>=x_j+c, with c>0. For a finite bare cutoff J assume the
downward classical maps and marked Hessians obey

 H_j=P_j^t H_(j+1) P_j,
 K_j=P_j^t K_(j+1) P_j+E_j,          K_J=H_J,
 -epsilon_j H_j<=E_j<=epsilon_j H_j,
 epsilon_j<=C x_j^-2(1+log x_j)^q,  q>=0.            (7.5)

Backward induction using (7.3) gives

 -eta_j H_j<=K_j-H_j<=eta_j H_j,
 eta_j=sum_(ell=j)^(J-1)epsilon_ell
      <=C' x_j^-1(1+log x_j)^q,                    (7.6)

uniformly in J, for x_j sufficiently large. For the final inequality
the function x^-2(1+log x)^q is eventually decreasing, use
x_(j+n)>=x_j+cn, and compare the resulting sum with its integral.
Thus when the premises hold the accumulated quadratic mark is of
order g_j^2 times logarithms at the CURRENT scale, rather than a
fixed error inherited from a coarser reference scale. This matches
the natural marked allowance at quadratic order.

The indices in (7.5) distinguish UV growth of inverse coupling from
the direction of coarse integration. Neither these maps nor the
trajectory have been constructed here. The claim is only about
quadratic forms at the stationary flat point; it supplies no higher
Taylor jet, field-domain bound, original-source identity, or control
of irrelevant operators. The derivative norms in (1.1) must still be
proved on their full joint complex domain before this conditional
bookkeeping can be applied to repeated actual integrals.

8. WHAT THIS DOES AND DOES NOT ADVANCE

Under the explicit common-domain assumptions, source-independent
generated actions of one-loop size are now admitted in the actual
finite-step g^4 response estimate. Their linear jets are handled.
Source-dependent generated actions have an exact triangular formula,
their transported marks are kept, and marginal extraction can be
fixed by a declared projection. The relative quadratic transport lemma
removes an artificial composition loss when its metric premises hold.

For x_j=g_j^-2>=X+cj, polylogarithmic N_j,K_j, and M1_j,M2_j<=CN_j,
the NEW unweighted errors sum as sum g_j^4 N_j^4, exactly as TURN44.
This conditional arithmetic is not an iteration theorem. The inherited
marks, complex domains, complete large-field histories, native local
norm of the new remainder, and actual running marginal extraction are
not supplied by it. Charging the entire box still costs its full
exp(kappa C_L N) carrier weight. The separate TURN45 pure-tail
comparison improves payment of that weight for the original action
by retaining its local auxiliary correction; this note does not
automatically supply that local representation for arbitrary W.
The classical s is not silently replaced by the nearest-neighbor
coarse Wilson action.

No nontrivial continuum Yang--Mills construction follows from this note.
The new finite-step theorem specifies, more sharply, what must be
preserved by the genuine source induction and why an unmarked one-loop
bound alone is insufficient.
