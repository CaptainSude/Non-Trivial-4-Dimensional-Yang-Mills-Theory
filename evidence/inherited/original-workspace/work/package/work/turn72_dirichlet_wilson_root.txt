AN ACTUAL NONLINEAR WILSON BLOCK: FROZEN-WINDOW SOURCE CORRECTION
TURN72. Root derivation. 20 October 2026. Pending independent audit.

This note proves a finite-block full-versus-selected source estimate
for the actual compact SU(2) Wilson measure with Dirichlet boundary
links close to identity. It uses global action coercivity on that
fixed block, not an extension of a local analytic effective action.
Constants depend on the block, but not on a larger exterior volume.

It is NOT yet a theorem for arbitrary boundaries, generated effective
densities, Bałaban's full constrained fibers, or all RG histories.
The distinction from those missing hypotheses is part of the result.

1. THE ORIGINAL FINITE CONDITIONAL MEASURE

Let B be a fixed nondegenerate finite rectangular cubical box of
dimension d>=2, with its plaquettes and edges. Fix all edge variables
in its boundary subcomplex to prescribed SU(2) values b_e. Integrate
the remaining edge variables with product normalized Haar measure,
and put

 h(U)=1-(1/2)Tr U,                 S_b(U)=sum_(p in B) h(U_p).

Thus h>=0, with equality exactly at U=I. The normalized conditional
Wilson law is

 dmu_(g,b)(U)=Z_(g,b)^-1 exp[-S_b(U)/g^2] product_e dU_e.

Changing the fixed overall action coefficient only changes constants
and the convention for g. Plaquettes depending only on boundary
variables are allowed; their scalar contribution cancels from this
probability law and from the action differences used below.

Choose a rooted forest containing one edge per interior vertex, each
component anchored at a boundary vertex. It contains no boundary edge.
Fix its edge variables to identity by gauge transformations equal
to identity at boundary vertices. Leaf-to-root Haar changes of
variables show that integrating the forest variables gives mass one;
the remaining link variables x have product Haar measure on

                         M=SU(2)^m.

The gauge-fixed action and every gauge-invariant plaquette observable
are therefore integrated with EXACTLY this quotient probability law.
There is no Faddeev--Popov approximation. If m=0 the law is a point
mass and the covariance assertions below are trivial. Assume m>0.

The box is contractible, and its boundary edge graph is connected.
When all b_e=I, a configuration with S_I=0 is flat: every plaquette
product is I. Path independence on a simply connected cubical box
expresses it as a pure gauge. Boundary edge identities force its
vertex gauges to be equal on the boundary, so that gauge can be
chosen equal to I there. The forest gauge then forces every interior
vertex gauge to be I too. Hence x=I is the UNIQUE zero of S_I on M.

This global uniqueness is what excludes remote equal-depth wells.
It is not inferred merely from a small-field Taylor expansion.

2. UNIFORM GLOBAL COERCIVITY FOR WEAK BOUNDARY DATA

Use fixed exponential coordinates for x near I and for boundary
links b near I. The quadratic part of S_I is a positive multiple of

                         ||d_lat a||^2,

on edge variations vanishing on the boundary and the fixed forest.
If d_lat a=0, simple connectivity makes a=d_lat phi. Boundary edge
zeros make phi constant on the connected boundary; choose it zero
there. Forest edge zeros then make phi zero at every interior
vertex. Thus a=0. The Hessian on these independent coordinates is
strictly positive definite because the vector space is finite.

Smoothness and the implicit-function theorem give a unique critical
point x_b near I for all sufficiently weak boundary tuples b.
The coordinate Hessian is uniformly positive on a fixed small
convex coordinate neighborhood. The point x_b is a strict local
minimum there and depends smoothly on b.

To make this global, choose a smaller fixed neighborhood of I.
On its compact complement, S_I has a strictly positive minimum.
Uniform continuity of S_b in b, and S_b(x_b)->0 as b->I, leave a
uniform positive action gap on that complement. Inside the chosen
coordinate neighborhood, the positive Hessian gives the quadratic
lower bound. Shrinking the boundary neighborhood once if needed,
these two statements give constants c,C>0 such that

 c dist_M(x,x_b)^2 <= S_b(x)-S_b(x_b)
                         <= C dist_M(x,x_b)^2             (2.1)

for EVERY x in M and all admitted b. The upper bound follows from
the vanishing gradient at x_b and the bounded Hessian on the compact
manifold, by a minimizing geodesic. The lower bound outside the
coordinate neighborhood uses its action gap divided by the square
of the finite diameter of M. In particular x_b is the unique global
minimum for these boundary tuples.

All constants here depend on the fixed box, forest and metric.
This proof supplies no box-size-uniform Hessian or coercivity bound.

Let D=3m and r_b(x)=dist_M(x,x_b). Uniform small-ball Haar volume
bounds, (2.1), and a local ball of radius g give, for 0<g<=g0,

 c_Z g^D <= integral exp[-(S_b(x)-S_b(x_b))/g^2]dx
                                                <= C_Z g^D.   (2.2)

Indeed the lower integral is bounded on r_b<=g using the upper
quadratic bound. For the upper integral, finite-dimensional volume
growth on the compact product group gives a radial bound by
C r^(D-1)dr near zero; the complement has an exponentially small
factor and is absorbed into C_Z g^D.

For every fixed integer k>=0, the same radial integration gives

 E_mu r_b^k <= C_k g^k,
 E_mu[r_b^k 1_(r_b>=gT)] <= C_k g^k exp(-c_k T^2),
                                      T>=1.             (2.3)

If gT exceeds the diameter the second expression is zero. In the
other cases polynomial tail factors can be absorbed by reducing
c_k once. The finite list k<=4 will suffice below.

These are full conditional Wilson expectations with their original
normalizer. They are uniform over the stated weak boundary set.

3. BOTH A METRIC WINDOW AND A PROJECTED GAUSSIAN WINDOW

The simpler selection is w=1_(r_b<=gT). Equation(2.3) directly
controls its omitted weighted moments.

A projected product window of the form used in the small-field
program can be treated too, on this BLOCK. In a fixed local chart
centered at x_b, write z_b(x) in R^D, with z_b(x_b)=0 and
|z_b(x)|<=C r_b(x). Let L_b be a whitening matrix with uniformly
bounded operator norm and inverse on the boundary neighborhood.
Let J_b:R^D -> R^N be an isometric embedding, with N fixed by the
block, and P_b=J_b J_b^T. Divide the ambient coordinates into fixed
finite-dimensional color blocks.

Conditionally on x, append a centered Gaussian eta_b of covariance
I-P_b in the orthogonal complement of ran J_b. In the local chart
put

 xi = J_b L_b z_b(x)/g + eta_b,
 w_(g,b,T)(x)=P_eta(max_i |xi_i|<=T).                   (3.1)

Set w=0 outside the chart. This is a literal weight 0<=w<=1
against mu_(g,b); the auxiliary Gaussian has mass one. The chart,
embedding, whitening and window are fixed when sources are
differentiated.

There are constants c0,c1,C1>0 such that if T>=T0, g<=g0 and
gT<=c0, then

 1-w(x) <= 1_(r_b(x)>c1 gT)+C1 exp(-c1 T^2).          (3.2)

To prove it, when r_b<=c1 gT choose c1 small enough that x lies in
the chart and every color block of the deterministic mean in (3.1)
has norm <=T/2. Every Gaussian block of eta has covariance bounded
above by identity. A finite union of its usual Gaussian radial
tails is <=C1 exp(-c1 T^2), whether or not those blocks are
independent. Outside this small ball the bound by one is enough.

It follows from (2.3) and (3.2) that, for k=0,1,2,3,4,

 E_mu[r_b^k(1-w)] <= C_k g^k exp(-c T^2).              (3.3)

The restriction gT<=c0 accounts for the zero weight outside the
coordinate chart. No unbounded Gaussian extension of the physical
interaction has been used.

The projected form (3.1) matches the abstract geometric form of
the earlier window, but it is not asserted to identify a Dirichlet
block with Bałaban's constrained periodic coarse fiber. Such an
identification would require its own geometry and action estimate.

4. A MIXED ORIGINAL-SOURCE ESTIMATE WITH ITS NORMALIZER

Let F,H be real smooth gauge-invariant observables on this fixed
block, evaluated with the fixed boundary b. Define

 a_F=|dF(x_b)|,
 b_F=(1/2) sup_M ||Hess F||,
 A_F(g)=g a_F+g^2 b_F,

and likewise for H. Hessians and gradients use the product metric;
allow uniform upper bounds in b if preferred. Taylor's formula
along a minimizing geodesic gives globally

 |F(x)-F(x_b)| <= a_F r_b(x)+b_F r_b(x)^2.              (4.1)

By (2.3) and (3.3), for f=F-F(x_b), h=H-H(x_b),

 E|f| <= C A_F,                  E f^2 <= C A_F^2,
 E[|f|(1-w)] <= C A_F exp(-cT^2),
 E[f^2(1-w)] <= C A_F^2 exp(-cT^2),                   (4.2)

and the same bounds hold for h. Cauchy--Schwarz for the measure
(1-w)dmu gives

 E[|fh|(1-w)] <= C A_F A_H exp(-cT^2).                (4.3)

Choose T0 large enough that q=E(1-w)<=1/2. Write p=1-q and
dnu=w dmu/p. For any integrable G, the exact difference is

 E_nu G-E_mu G
      = [q E_mu G-E_mu((1-w)G)]/p.                    (4.4)

Apply (4.4) to f, h and fh. Equations(4.2)--(4.3) give

 |E_nu f-E_mu f| <= C A_F exp(-cT^2),
 |E_nu fh-E_mu fh| <= C A_F A_H exp(-cT^2).

Since both means are O(A_F), subtracting their products proves

 |Cov_mu(F,H)-Cov_nu(F,H)|
           <= C A_F(g) A_H(g) exp(-cT^2).             (4.5)

For the ORIGINAL source-tilted selection weight

 p(s,t)=E_mu[w exp(sF+tH)]/E_mu[exp(sF+tH)],

all integrals are finite at this fixed regulator and

 partial_st log p|_0 = Cov_nu(F,H)-Cov_mu(F,H).

Thus (4.5) is an ACTUAL mixed selection-source susceptibility bound
for the nonlinear full conditional Wilson block, with the raw
normalizer retained. Source-independent constants F(x_b),H(x_b)
cancel from covariance; no mixed source vacuum was removed.

This argument does not require a complex zero-free disc uniform
in g, a perturbative expansion, or a Gaussian approximation to
the actual conditional law.

5. ACTION MARKS AND WHAT THE SCALE FACTORS MEAN

For weighted plaquette actions

 F_b(x)=sum_p f_p h(U_p(x,b)),
 H_b(x)=sum_p h_p h(U_p(x,b)),

the fixed-block Hessian norms are bounded by constants times the
respective finite coefficient norms. At identity boundary the
minimizer is flat, and every plaquette action has zero first
derivative. Therefore a_F=a_H=0 and

 |Cov_mu(F,H)-Cov_nu(F,H)|
       <= C_f,h g^4 exp(-cT^2),             b=I.       (5.1)

For the canonically divided block actions F/g^2,H/g^2, the same
bound is C_f,h exp(-cT^2). This is just the stated observable
rescaling of (5.1), not a claim about its continuum calibration.

For small nonflat boundary tuples, smoothness of x_b and of the
plaquettes gives a_F<=C_f |log b| and a_H<=C_h |log b|.
Consequently the general estimate is bounded by a constant times

 [g |log b|+g^2]^2 exp(-cT^2).                         (5.2)

The boundary term cannot be dropped. A boundary small by a fixed
amount independent of g is not automatically a field of size O(g).

The choice T^2=A log(1/g), for g sufficiently small, satisfies
gT<=c0 and also the earlier bounded-window condition gT^3<<1.
The exponential error can then be made smaller than any prescribed
fixed power of g by increasing the fixed A. Constants remain
block-dependent; no whole-volume selected-mass claim follows.

6. WHAT THIS DOES AND DOES NOT CLOSE

The new estimate is for an actual original positive conditional
Wilson measure. It shows that the frozen-window correction can
be proved small in a nonlinear gauge model when GLOBAL coercivity
and a weak boundary have been established. It goes beyond the
untruncated Gaussian-reference calculation.

It can be applied inside an arbitrary larger Wilson volume to a
fixed block whose conditioned exterior supplies the prescribed
weak boundary, provided every action term involving a variable
interior edge is included in the block conditional action. One
can take the fixed boundary collar large enough to ensure that
property. Constants then depend only on this fixed conditional
block/collar, not the larger volume.

It does not prove:

 - that boundaries of all relevant blocks are weak under the full law;
 - uniform versions for growing blocks or arbitrarily fine
   substructures inside an effective block;
 - the same global coercivity for a generated effective density
   specified only by a local analytic germ;
 - compatibility with every coarse constraint and private history
   of the native R operation;
 - summable connected corrections after conditioning on many
   previous selections, including coarse mean fluctuations;
 - the full-law separated continuum bound, a nonzero physical
   witness or a Yang--Mills existence theorem.

In particular, summing (4.5) over unrelated conditionings would not
bound TURN71's global log-selection derivative. Its chronological
conditional laws can have nonweak boundaries and additional
effective interactions; those are precisely the new estimates
still needed.
