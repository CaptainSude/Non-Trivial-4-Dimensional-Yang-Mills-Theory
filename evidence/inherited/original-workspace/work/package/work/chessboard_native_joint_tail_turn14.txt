CHESSBOARD TAILS FOR NATIVE BLOCK CYLINDERS: A FIXED-DEPTH THEOREM
19 October 2026. Positive bare events, constrained partitions, and exact limits.

0. RESULT

Reflection positivity DOES give a normalized, surrounding-volume-uniform
tail for an actual fixed-depth native-average cylinder. This is more
than a bare plaquette estimate: a fixed finite native analytic defect
can be dominated by the Wilson energy in its complete reflection cell.
The proof below has no signed-history normalization and keeps the exact
bare partition function throughout.

It does NOT give the required UV-depth-uniform stopped/history estimate.
The cell contains the full bare read of the fixed composite; its constants
and its fine-volume entropy are retained. Source uniform stability gives
a different statement and does not supply the constrained numerator
needed to replace that entropy by stopped coarse volume. A reflection-
positive countermodel shows why the pointwise stability sandwich alone
cannot imply bad-cylinder rarity.

1. MODEL AND REFLECTION-CELL CONVENTION

Let mu_beta be the normalized positive bare Wilson law on a compatible
four-dimensional periodic lattice:

 mu_beta(dU)=Z_beta^(-1) exp[-beta S_tot(U)] product_e d_H U_e,
 S_tot=sum_p s(U_p),                 s>=0.                    (1.1)

Use a faithful unitary representation for the plaquette term, so
s(U)=0 iff U=1. Choose an ordinary closed coordinate-reflection cell C
whose translates/reflections tile the torus with the usual shared-face
convention. Its side in BARE lattice units is fixed for the theorem.
Let m be the number of cells, and let S_C be the sum of all bare
plaquette terms wholly in C. Write q_* for the maximal multiplicity
with which a plaquette appears in these closed-cell sums. It is a fixed
geometric number; the crude q_*<=2^4 is sufficient. Thus

                   sum_cells S_C <= q_* S_tot.                (1.2)

Use the exact Wilson chessboard inequality for this cell convention.
For a nonnegative cell function f, its disseminated product is obtained
by the actual reflections, not by imposing that the field itself is
reflection symmetric. Every complete read of f must lie in C.

2. A FIXED-CELL DEFECT-TO-ACTION LEMMA

The cell, considered as a free-boundary cubical complex, is simply
connected. The fixed-graph theorem in full_bare_finite_graph_joint_law
turn13 applies to S_C after a based tree gauge: the unique zero is the
identity chord tuple, and its finite Hessian is positive.

Let D be a gauge-invariant defect defined on a fixed near-flat branch,
with complete read in C. In its finite tree coordinates x suppose

                D(1)=0,       D(exp x)<=L_C |x|               (2.1)

on a fixed neighborhood. This holds, for example, for the norm of an
analytic local output difference that vanishes at the identity. No
analyticity in a varying source or growing fine graph is asserted.

Choose a smaller near-flat neighborhood O on which the map is admitted.
Define E_delta to mean D>=delta there, and optionally also declare
failure to belong to O to be bad. This gives a positive measurable
cell event; outside O no undefined analytic branch is evaluated.
For 0<delta<=delta_0, there is c_C>0 such that

                    E_delta implies S_C>=c_C delta^2.        (2.2)

Proof. On O, the tree-slice lower bound S_C>=lambda_C |x|^2 and
(2.1) give (2.2). On the complement of O, compactness and the unique
flat orbit give S_C>=Delta_C>0. Decrease c_C to at most
Delta_C/delta_0^2. All these constants concern this fixed cell.

The cell tree gauge need not agree with any RG gauge: D is a genuine
gauge-invariant function, so the gauge is used only to prove an energy
inequality for the original bare event. A failure branch is included
only when small S_C actually guarantees membership in the selected
native branch. This follows from the same finite chart bound for the
examples in Section4; it is not automatic for an arbitrary algorithm.

3. NORMALIZED REFLECTED ARRAYS: COMPLETE DENOMINATOR PROOF

Let E_array be the intersection of all m reflected copies of E_delta.
Equations(1.2),(2.2) imply on that event

                 S_tot >= m c_C delta^2/q_*.

Therefore the constrained periodic partition satisfies exactly

 Z_beta(E_array)
   <=exp[-beta m c_C delta^2/(2q_*)] Z_(beta/2).               (3.1)

The other half of the ORIGINAL positive Wilson action is retained.
There is no RG history expansion in this inequality.

For completeness, normalize the ratio with a real lower subset.
Restrict each independent link to a group ball of radius c beta^(-1/2).
Its Haar volume is at least c_1 beta^(-dim G/2), and every plaquette
action is at most C/beta. With N_0 the number of bare sites, this gives

              Z_beta>=exp[-C N_0(1+log beta)],
              Z_(beta/2)<=1.                                  (3.2)

Taking the chessboard root only AFTER (3.1)--(3.2) yields

 mu_beta(E_delta)
 <=z_C(beta,delta)
 :=min{1,exp[-c_C' beta delta^2
                       +C_C v_C(1+log beta)]},                (3.3)

where v_C=N_0/m is the number of bare sites per cell, with the fixed
tiling normalization. It is independent of the total torus volume,
but it is NOT omitted from the bound. The usual multilinear chessboard
inequality also gives, for any prescribed distinct reflected event cells,

       mu_beta(intersection_(a in A) E_delta,a)<=z_C^|A|.      (3.4)

One can allow finitely many reflected defect types with a common
energy threshold by applying their respective disseminated norms.
No independence of the cells or their boundary links is assumed.

If delta=beta^(-1/2)(log beta)^p, p>1/2, and C is fixed BEFORE beta
tends to infinity, (3.3) is smaller than every fixed beta power.
The gain beta delta^2 defeats this fixed-cell logarithmic entropy.

4. AN ACTUAL NATIVE-AVERAGE CYLINDER TO WHICH THIS APPLIES

Use the ordinary symmetrized group averaging of CMP109 (0.5)--(0.12),
with a FIXED odd ratio L, and a FIXED finite number k of compositions.
Every coarse bond reads its two endpoint L^k blocks and their original
contours. The complete footprint of a coarse plaquette is consequently
a fixed finite collection of such blocks. Choose C large enough that
this whole footprint lies strictly inside it.

The branch is analytic near the flat orbit, gauge covariant, sends the
identity field to the identity field, and has a finite derivative bound
on a reserved fixed graph. Its coarse plaquette defect

              D(U)=||1-Q^k(U)(boundary p_coarse)||             (4.1)

is gauge invariant, vanishes at the flat orbit and satisfies (2.1).
Thus (3.3)--(3.4) apply. Declaring branch failure bad is legal as in
Section2. Alternatively use the globally Borel native-on-good/fallback
extension constructed in reflection_compatible_block_statistic_turn3;
small S_C puts every intermediate local mean in its native branch.

The prior note proves the required actual path locality and reflection
covariance, including a global straight-path fallback only outside
the unique-mean domain. It also proves that (Q^k)_*mu_beta is RP at
the compatible block-face planes. For the present argument one can
simply apply BARE chessboard to (4.1); this avoids asserting RP of
any resampled law. Reflected event copies carry their reflected full
path families and have the same action constant.

This is an honest finite-level native-average event, not a replacement
by thin path decimation or a condition on a chosen gauge potential.
The new conclusion is normalized rarity of this event under the full
bare Wilson law, uniformly in the surrounding volume. It extends the
old bare-plaquette estimate by an explicit fixed-cell energy lemma.

It does not say that a Q/R flag using a minimizing background is the
same event. Such a flag has additional full read collars and possibly
prior history data. The existing exact free-bond R residual proxy can
be used only when those data have already been identified with a
deterministic bare-local function on a correct ancestry event.

5. MARKED VERSIONS THAT ARE ACTUALLY JUSTIFIED

For a bounded bare mark M,

 |mu_beta(M 1_E)|<=||M||_infinity z_C.                         (5.1)

For a true normalized L^r bound with r>1, ordinary Holder gives

 |mu_beta(M 1_E)|<=||M||_(L^r(mu_beta)) z_C^(1-1/r).           (5.2)

These bounds retain the actual denominator. For a fixed finite product
of compact Wilson-loop functions with a prescribed normalization bounded
by C beta^D, (5.1) suffices: the fixed power is absorbed by (3.3) at a
logarithmic threshold with p>1/2. Such a statement is uniform in total
volume at this fixed bare geometry.

Equation(5.2) cannot be applied to a renormalized continuum scalar or
tensor insertion merely because its separated Schwinger functions are
bounded. The coincident L^r moment in (5.2) is an additional quantity and
can have a UV divergence. The existing scalar event-array slab consumer
in first_contact_scalar_chessboard_turn3 Section1 is an alternative:
an array bound such as (3.4), plus genuine positive kernel domination
of the insertion, gives an OS-core bound without invoking that ordinary
coincident moment. The kernel domination itself must be proved for the
actual bare mark; a signed history-coefficient norm is not enough.

6. WHY THE FIXED-DEPTH THEOREM DOES NOT SOLVE THE STOPPED-SCALE PROBLEM

The constants in Section2 come from a cell with all bare read links.
If k grows, its physical size at the stopped scale may stay fixed while
its BARE volume v_C grows like L^(4k), up to collar factors. Neither
c_C nor v_C was proved uniform in k. Even a hypothetical uniform
nonlinear energy inequality would not remove v_C in the ratio (3.2).
Weakening beta to beta/2 changes all bare ultraviolet Gaussian modes.
Its determinant/free-energy cost is counted in bare volume.

For the intended stopped event one instead needs a constrained positive
partition estimate of the form

 Z(E_array)<=exp[-E_UV] exp[-m P_b+C m v_b],
 Z(1)>=exp[-E_UV] exp[-C' m v_b(1+log gamma_b)],                (6.1)

with the SAME extracted ultraviolet factor and v_b counting stopped
cells in ONE full event cell. Then bare chessboard would indeed give

 mu(E)<=exp[-P_b+C''v_b(1+log gamma_b)].                       (6.2)

The arithmetic is valid, and no RP assertion for a conditioned or
signed-history measure is required. The unsupplied object is the first
line of (6.1) for the literal positive event in question, on the same
finite construction. An unconstrained upper bound for Z does not
provide the event penalty P_b. Re-running an RG on a tilted action can
change E_UV, so its vacuum cannot simply be canceled against the old
one. Geometry-dependent missing vacuum terms are also not one common
scalar.

The Gaussian linearized native average has an exact coarse-rank
determinant pressure, already proved in reflection_compatible_block_
statistic_turn3 Section5. That is a positive benchmark, not a nonlinear
Wilson comparison. The required nonlinear event-source comparison
uniformly in k remains additional.

8. RP PLUS THE STABILITY SANDWICH DOES NOT FORCE RARITY

There is an exact countermodel to that INFERENCE. On a reflection-
compatible finite gauge lattice take the positive density

                      rho_beta(U)=exp[-beta S_tot(U)]+1.      (8.1)

Both the Wilson measure and product Haar are reflection positive;
their positive unnormalized sum is reflection positive, and subsequent
scalar normalization preserves RP. Pointwise,

                exp[-beta S_tot]<=rho_beta<=2.               (8.2)

This has a classical small-field lower bound and an absolute extensive
upper bound of exactly the relevant qualitative type. Nevertheless,
for a fixed local nonzero-energy event E with positive Haar mass m_E,

 mu_(rho_beta)(E)>=m_E/(Z_beta+1)>=m_E/2.                      (8.3)

Thus RP, positivity and that sandwich do not imply any weak-coupling
rarity of E. The example is NOT a claim about the actual Bałaban density;
it proves that a separate constrained/action-sensitive numerator input
cannot be omitted from the proposed deduction.

With a stretched-log coefficient epsilon_beta in place of1, one can
retain rare-sector fixed-order bounds while destroying a fixed positive
energy-tilt bound; that variant is already proved in
stopped_coarse_energy_pressure_turn3 Section4. Even all fixed moments
are not a replacement for the large positive source in (6.1).

9. EVENT-LEVEL BARE ESTIMATES AND RG HISTORY ARE DISTINGUISHABLE

The pure statistic Q^k(U_bare), the current field in the resampled
RT density, and an auxiliary surviving-history label are different
random objects. The source refill integrates current variables in
its interior without fixing their next ordinary average; the literal
constraint check is in reflection_compatible_block_statistic_turn3
Section8. No identity M_*R rho=M_*rho follows from preservation of
the total integral.

A simple probabilistic test makes the distinction exact. Start with
any RP bare law mu and augment it by an independent Bernoulli history
label H of arbitrary parameter p. All bare cylinder probabilities,
partition functions and their RP bounds are unchanged, while P(H=1)=p.
One may equivalently retain a label from an earlier field and resample
the present field independently. Then the label can persist while
every presently inspected field statistic has the same small tail.
This is not a model of the native RG; it proves that a theorem about
the bare/current marginal cannot alone identify or bound a label's
probability without an event domination or a relative-history theorem.

For a genuine positive native kernel the dual function
p_H(U)=P(H|U) lies in [0,1], but need not be a function of a closed
reflection cell. Localized signed T coefficients are not positive
cell-local pieces of p_H. To use the bare event theorem one needs a
positive cell-local majorant/decomposition WITH its carrier error and
the matching disseminated estimate, or a direct normalized surgery.

The earlier 'all prior flags good' shortcut does not provide this:
prior tests crossing a reflection-cell face are not imposed by all
within-cell copies, and first LIVE contact can follow already healed
R operations. The exact failure is documented in first_contact_regular_
array_pressure_turn3 Sections4--5. Nothing in the new fixed-cell energy
lemma removes those additional ancestry and crossing-face reads.

10. PRECISE ADVANCE AND REMAINING TARGET

The proved addition is (2.2)--(3.4) for an actual fixed finite native-
average defect, with normalized marked versions at their stated scope.
It is a full bare-measure event theorem uniform in the surrounding
volume, and it never divides a signed component by an unknown mass.

The needed larger theorem remains concrete: prove (6.1), or its
normalized positive-source equivalent, for the complete deterministic
native coarse cylinder at growing UV depth; then identify the positive
event/mark which dominates the specific RG history contribution. The
pointwise source stability sandwich and finite-graph Gaussian tails do
not already prove either step. A fixed-volume minorization of the RT
law is useful for a different purpose and is not contradicted by this
audit, but it does not supply this upper rarity estimate.
