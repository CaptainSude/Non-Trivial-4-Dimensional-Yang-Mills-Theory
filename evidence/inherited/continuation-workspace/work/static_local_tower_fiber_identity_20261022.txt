1. THE NATIVE AVERAGING FACT THAT IS AVAILABLE

Use ONE averaging convention throughout the old array, the local tower and
all comparisons. B98 printed23--24 (42)--(43) defines the ordinary average
recursively. If M_j denotes that j-fold average, then, literally,

             M_p(U)=M_{p:r}(M_r(U)),       p>=r.             (1)

Here M_{p:r} applies the same one-step operations on the successively coarser
grids. It is not the assertion that a separately defined direct large-block
average equals an iterated average. B98 immediately after (43) states that a
j-row uses only bonds in the two j-blocks associated with its endpoints.
The covariance statement in the following paragraph permits a common gauge
to be used throughout this comparison.

B109 printed252--254 replaces the original contour choice by the symmetric
one-step definition (0.12), and explicitly retains the B98 results for that
choice. The argument here uses its same recursion, block locality and gauge
covariance. It does NOT replace the actual averages by the older nonsymmetric
ones. Nor does it use the corrected relative averages B98(88)--(92) as if they
were ordinary M_j. Those are different objects.

2. COMPLETE TOWER DATA DETERMINE THE GLOBAL TOP AVERAGE

Let D_0 contain the mutable fine field and let

             D_0 superset D_1 ... superset D_r,
             D_(r+1)=empty,

be a native local determining tower as in B119256--257 (2.13), or B109273
before (3.18). Each D_j is a union of the native j-blocks. Its collars obey
the native separation, so an elementary j-edge cannot jump from D_(j+1)
to D_j^c. Use the actual incident-edge convention: an edge is included in
a vertex region when at least one endpoint belongs to it. Prescribe M_j
on the incident rows of D_j\D_(j+1), and fix the exterior fine field and
the native fine Dirichlet boundary rows. This is the complete tower, not
only the interior top-r rows.

Suppose U and W have these same data and the same fixed exterior. Then

                         M_r(U)=M_r(W) globally.           (2)

Here "globally" is rowwise on the common domain of the native recursive
averages. If an unnecessary far-exterior coarse average is not defined,
no value is assigned to it: run the following completion only on the
finite backward reads of the old constraints that are actually used,
together with the local tower. Those reads are admitted for W, and
the induction preserves their values and hence their definition for
the compared local variation. No regularity of arbitrary far-exterior
coarse rows is being added. If all global averages are defined, (2)
holds literally on the whole grid.

Proof by upward completion. At grade0, the prescribed 0-rows and the fixed
exterior determine every fine row with an endpoint outside D_1. Assume
M_(j-1) agrees on all rows with an endpoint outside D_j. Consider a j-row
with an endpoint outside D_(j+1).

* If it has an endpoint in D_j\D_(j+1), it is a prescribed j-row.
* Otherwise both endpoints are outside D_j. The remaining possibility of
  a direct edge from D_(j+1) to D_j^c is excluded by the native collar.
  The two endpoint j-blocks are outside D_j, since D_j is a union of
  those blocks. The complete one-step read in the (j-1)-field therefore
  uses only rows already fixed by the induction hypothesis. Applying
  the SAME one-step average gives equality for this row.

Boundary-crossing incident rows are in the first case; they are not silently
discarded. Bonds at the faces of the two blocks are included in the known
incident rows in the second case. The induction determines M_j on the
complement of D_(j+1), with these incident rows. At j=r the complement is
the whole grid, which proves (2). This is the exact recursive virtual-row
completion underlying the interface convention in CMP102278 (7).

Consequently (1) gives M_p(U)=M_p(W) for every p>=r, including an old
p-row whose averaging read crosses the local box. It is not necessary
to impose such crossing old p-rows separately after (2) is known.

tested. The old criticality gives local constrained criticality.

fixed native fine boundary, because exterior plaquettes are unchanged

CMP102278--279 (6)--(9) now gives equality of the local critical orbit:

             W|D_0 = U(B_r(D_0), M'W)|D_0

This uses admissibility (b). Constraint inclusion alone does not prove

Likewise the stronger assertion that EVERY distant regular point in
