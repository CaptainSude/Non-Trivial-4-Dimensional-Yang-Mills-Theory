extends to every nonwrapping four-dimensional rectangular vertex box.
An explicit, deliberately inefficient filling estimate gives nonlinear
pinning c R^-4 times the largest remaining link displacement squared,

plaquette occurrence multiplicity in the filling.

Keeping the dimension in the contour estimates gives one conservative
zero-free regime: boundary complex radius c R^-60 and beta>=C R^80.
The Gaussian error is RELATIVE to the exact leading Gaussian coefficient,
not an absolute fixed-dimensional error followed by an exponentially
costly division. A raw interior mark forcing energy tau^2/2 consequently
has a rare tube of radius c min(R^-60,tau R^-6), with logarithmic entropy
O(R^4 log beta). These exponents are proved sufficient below and are not
optimized. Independent review, including the relative contour constants

 work/rectangular_cavity_quantitative_pinning_audit_20261024.txt.

1. ORIGINAL CAVITY AND AN EXACT ROOTED GAUGE FOREST

Let D=product_(mu=1)^4 {0,...,n_mu}, n_mu>=2, be a nonwrapping box, and
R=max_mu(n_mu+2). Let K contain all nearest-neighbor links with both
endpoints in D. Let B contain EVERY exterior link in a plaquette meeting
K. Hold B at y and integrate K with normalized SU(2) Haar. Write

 H(U;y)=sum_(q meeting K) h_q(U,y),
 h^hol(W)=1-(Tr W+Tr W^-1)/4,
 Z(y)=integral exp[-beta H(U;y)]dU.                         (1)

For real SU(2), h(W)=||W-I||op^2/2. beta is the ORIGINAL Wilson parameter,
beta=C_W/g0^2, with its fixed trace convention. The original exterior
mass is Z_full^-1 exp[-beta H_out] Z(y)dy. It is never replaced below.

There are

 E=|K|=sum_mu n_mu product_(nu!=mu)(n_nu+1),
 v_int=product_mu(n_mu-1),
 M=E-v_int, d=3M,                                        (2)

and all these counts, and |B|, are at most C R^4. At each strict interior
vertex v select the edge (v-e4,v). These edges form disjoint vertical
line forests, rooted at the boundary x4=0. Each path has length at most R.

Set the gauge to identity at all boundary and outside vertices. Along a
forest path define gamma_v recursively so that

              V_xy=gamma_x U_xy gamma_y^-1

is I on each selected edge. The selected links and the gamma_v variables
are in triangular bijection by group multiplication. Conditional on
them, every remaining link change is a left/right Haar translation.
Consequently the product-Haar change is exact, and each of the v_int
normalized gauge integrals is exactly1. The reduced integral has M
SU(2) variables with selected links set to I. There is no determinant,
gauge-volume constant, or extra beta power from this transformation.

Every interior vertex has all its incident links inside K; hence this
gauge does not alter y. The COMPLETE crossing-plaquette action is
invariant. The same equality holds for complex y near I by algebraic
trace invariance. A source times a fixed interior mark is preserved if
that complete marked insertion is gauge invariant. Arbitrary noninvariant
Borel insertions require their transformed gauge-variable dependence and
are not included in the reduced marked statement below.

2. UNIQUE FLAT MINIMUM AND FILLING WITH MULTIPLICITY RETAINED

At y=I, H>=0. If H=0, all internal plaquettes are flat. Coordinate path
swaps on the filled box give a vertex pure gauge. Every boundary-tangent
edge has an outward plaquette whose other three links are held at I.
That plaquette is in (1), so every boundary-tangent edge is I. The boundary
graph is connected. The vertex gauge is therefore constant on the entire
boundary; the rooted forest then forces the same constant at every
interior vertex. The unique reduced zero-action configuration is V=I.
Constant conjugation fixes this point and gives no nonzero zero mode.

Here is a quantitative version that does not assume a good gauge-fixing
condition number. For a remaining link e, close e by its endpoint forest
paths to the boundary, followed by an exterior path joining their boundary
endpoints. A boundary endpoint itself needs no forest path. The exterior
path can be constructed in the expanded box product[-1,n_mu+1]: step
outward through a boundary normal, keep that coordinate outside while
moving the others to -1, then move it to -1. Join through the common
exterior corner. Every link of this path is outside K and is I at flat y.
The closed loop has length <=C R and holonomy V_e or V_e^-1.
Any additional exterior links needed just for this flat filling, beyond
the original B, are assigned identity for the geometric argument. They
are not extra held or integrated variables in the original cavity law.

An ordered-coordinate contraction fills such a loop with at most C R^2
plaquette OCCURRENCES. For example, replace each edge by its two root
paths and commute coordinate segments; each root path has length <=C R,
and there are <=C R original loop edges. All faces stay in the expanded
rectangular box. A face not meeting K is all exterior and is I; every
other face is included in (1).

Use the nonabelian plaquette product along this filling, with unitary
transport conjugations. Telescoping products and the operator norm give

 ||V_e-I|| <= sum_occ ||U_q-I||
           <= C R^2 max_(q meeting K)||U_q-I||
           <= C R^2 (sum_(q meeting K)||U_q-I||^2)^(1/2).

In particular

       H(V;I)>=c R^-4 max_e ||V_e-I||^2.                 (3)

This uses the number of occurrences, not the number of distinct faces.
Equivalently, Cauchy followed by max multiplicity <=C R^2 gives the same
loss. No bounded-occurrence claim is being used. A sharper filling may
improve powers, but is unnecessary for the present result.

In reduced Pauli coordinates V_e=exp(i A_e), let Q be the flat Hessian,
so H(A;0)=A^T Q A/2+O(A^3). Differentiating (3) on every ray gives

 A^T Q A>=c R^-4 max_e |A_e|^2,
             c R^-8 I <= Q <= C I.                     (4)

For the lower spectral bound sum the first inequality over at most C R^4
remaining links. The upper bound follows from the bounded incidence of
links in plaquettes. The constants absorb the fixed three color entries.
This supplies both linearized positivity and a global nonlinear gap.

3. LOCAL ANALYTIC DERIVATIVE BOUNDS WITH DIMENSION SHOWN

Use y_b=exp(i z_b), and per-link sup norms for z and A. On a fixed small
complex log chart the reduced action is still a sum of elementary Wilson
plaquettes with some links set to I. It is not a new nonlocal action.
Every term reads at most four links and every variable has bounded
incidence, independently of R. It follows directly by differentiating
the group products that

 ||H_AA(A,z)-Q||op <=C (||A||infty+||z||infty),
 ||H_A(0,z)||2 <=C R^2 ||z||infty,
 |H(0,z)| <=C R^4 ||z||infty^2.                         (5)

The second bound conservatively counts all O(R^4) rows. The last bound
uses the vanished joint first jet at flat fields. The third-order Taylor
remainder about a nearby complex stationary point obeys

 |T3(u,z)| <=C sum_e |u_e|^3
           <=C ||u||infty ||u||2^2.                    (6)

Mixed adjacent-link monomials are reduced to the displayed sum by Young's
inequality and bounded incidence. These are pointwise estimates at the
actual nonuniform u, not sup-radius estimates substituted after integration.

The product Haar density is J(A)=product_e j(A_e), analytic on this chart,
with J(0)=(2pi^2)^-M in Pauli coordinates. Its logarithm is a sum of local
even functions with bounded derivatives and zero first jet at0. Globally
on real reduced links and complex boundary parameters in a fixed chart,

 |H(V;z)-H(V;0)|<=C R^4 ||z||infty,
 Re H(V;u+i v)>=H(V;u)-C R^4 ||v||infty^2.              (7)

The second statement follows by reality on the real locus: the first
imaginary variation is purely imaginary. It remains valid for rough V.

4. A SMALL POLYNOMIAL STATIONARY TUBE

Fix small absolute constants and choose

 a=c_a R^-16,       rho=c_rho R^-60,
                         beta>=C_beta R^80.            (8)

The central real logarithm cube has halfwidth a in each scalar color
coordinate. These exponents have substantial spare room.

The contraction based on Q^-1 and (5) gives a unique small holomorphic
stationary branch A_*(z), for ||z||infty<rho, with

 ||A_*(z)||2<=C R^10 ||z||infty,
 e_*(z):=H(A_*,z),       |e_*(z)|<=C R^12 ||z||infty^2.  (9)

Indeed ||Q^-1||<=C R^8, the force at0 is C R^2 rho, and the derivative
of the nonlinear fixed-point correction is <=C R^18 rho. For the action
bound retain b=H_A(0,z). The stationary equation implies

 A_*^T Q A_*=-b.A_*+
       O((||A_*||infty+||z||infty)||A_*||2^2).

Thus the quadratic term is estimated using b.A_*, which costs
C R^12 ||z||infty^2, rather than the crude C R^20 ||z||infty^2.
The nonlinear error costs at most C R^30 ||z||infty^3 and is smaller
on (8). This establishes the pointwise bound in (9), also on every
smaller marked tube in Section6. Also

 D(z)=H_AA(A_*,z),
 ||Q^-1/2(D-Q)Q^-1/2||op<=C R^18 rho.                  (10)

Multiplication by the dimension costs at most C R^22 rho, which is small.
Thus the Gaussian determinants of D, Re D and Q are relatively comparable
by exp(C R^22 rho), an absolute bounded factor. In particular no C^d loss
is introduced by comparing the complex and positive Gaussians. The branch
of det D^(-1/2) is continued from positive Q. Likewise J(A_*)/J(0) is
bounded above and below by absolute constants using the local Haar logs.

5. RELATIVE CONTOUR ESTIMATE; NO EXPONENTIAL LOSS IN THE CENTRAL ERROR

Define the actual leading coefficient

       L_R(z)=(2pi)^(d/2) J(A_*(z)) det D(z)^(-1/2).

At0, and within the tube up to absolute factors,

 exp[-C R^4] <= |L_R(z)| <= exp[C R^4 log R].             (11)

The lower bound uses Q<=C I and the exact normalized Haar constant; the
upper uses (4). These are extensive entropy estimates, not polynomial
absolute prefactors.

Split the ORIGINAL compact reduced integral into its central real cube
and complement. Translate the central contour by A_* one coordinate at
a time. On the translated cube, write A=A_*+u and use

             H=e_*+u^T D u/2+T3(u,z).

The cubic and Haar errors are estimated relative to the normalized
Gaussian, not as absolute integrals. On this cube

 |T3|<=C a ||u||2^2 <=C(a/lambda) u^T Q u,
 lambda=c R^-8,     d a/lambda <=C R^-4.                (12)

After reducing c_a, the exponential remainder is dominated by a Gaussian
whose relative determinant inflation is bounded: (1-Ca/lambda)^(-d/2)
is O(1). Under that normalized Gaussian, after v=sqrt(beta)u,

 sum_e E|v_e|^3 <= C M lambda^-3/2 <=C R^16.

Therefore the RELATIVE cubic error is <=C R^16 beta^-1/2. The local Haar
log gives relative errors bounded, conservatively, by

             C R^12/beta + C R^18 rho/sqrt(beta).

These estimates follow by the same Gaussian domination: sum E|u_e|^2 is
C R^12/beta, and the linear Haar term contains the small A_* factor.
The normalized Gaussian tail outside the cube is bounded by

                 C R^4 exp[-c beta R^-40],              (13)

using the coordinate variance <=C beta^-1 R^8 and a=c_a R^-16.
The determinant comparison (10) makes (13) relative to the complex
Gaussian as well. All central error bounds are thus polynomial in R.

For the compact complement, (3) gives the real flat action gap
c R^-4 a^2=c R^-36. The real boundary perturbation in (7) is at most
C R^4 rho, much smaller. On any connecting side prism, one real scalar
coordinate remains at least a/2 in magnitude, since ||A_*||2<<a. Its
real part has the same gap. The imaginary shift changes the real action
by at most C(R^20 rho^2+R^4 rho^2), again negligible; the first imaginary
variation vanishes in real part. The factor exp(beta e_*) also costs much
less than that gap by (9).

There are at most d side pieces. Their coordinate Haar bounds and volume
can conservatively be bounded by exp(C R^4). The compact complement has
normalized Haar mass at most1. Dividing THESE errors by the leading
beta^-d/2 L_R costs the displayed entropy, giving a relative bound

            exp[C R^4 log(beta R)-c beta R^-36].         (14)

Even the weaker exponent R^-40 would suffice for (8). We do not absorb
the entropy in a purported fixed-dimensional constant.

Combining the contour identity and these estimates gives the EQUALITY

 Z(y(z))=exp[-beta e_*(z)] beta^-d/2 L_R(z)(1+E_R,beta(z)),

 |E_R,beta| <=C[R^16/sqrt(beta)+R^12/beta
                +R^18 rho/sqrt(beta)+R^4 exp(-c beta R^-40)]
              +exp[C R^4 log(beta R)-c beta R^-36].      (15)

Choose the absolute C_beta large and c_a,c_rho small. For every R>=4,
beta>=C_beta R^80 makes the right side <=1/4. For example the central
cubic bound is O(R^-24), and beta R^-40 dominates R^4 log(beta R) at
the floor and thereafter. The ORIGINAL Z is consequently zero-free on
the stated polynomial tube, with the original power beta^-3M/2 and all
exact gauge scalars retained. No event-conditioned partition function
has been declared zero-free.

6. RAW RARE INTERIOR COEFFICIENTS AND THEIR ENTROPY

Let M_bad be a fixed gauge-invariant Borel mark whose complete read is
inside K and which forces

            H(V;y(u))>=tau^2/2                         (16)

for every real boundary u. Specified completely internal plaquette
witnesses give this directly. Let F be bounded, |F|<=A, with the complete
marked insertion gauge invariant and boundary independent. Consider
the ORIGINAL raw coefficient

 b(z)=Z(y(z))^-1 integral F(V)1_Mbad(V)
                                      exp[-beta H(V;y(z))]dV.

The numerator is kept on real variables; its discontinuous mark is NEVER
shifted by the contour argument. Equations(7),(16) bound its modulus by
A exp[-beta tau^2/2+C beta R^4 ||Im z||infty^2]. Together with (9),(11),(15)
this proves, on the smaller tube

 rho_bad=c min(R^-60,tau R^-6),

       |b(z)|<=A exp[C R^4] beta^(3M/2)
                                      exp[-beta tau^2/4]. (17)

The inverse denominator's stationary value costs C beta R^12 rho_bad^2,
which is why tau R^-6 is sufficient. The logarithm of the prefactor is
O(R^4 log beta), with the exact reduced dimension retained. It is not an
absolute polynomial prefactor uniform in the number of links.

The same assertion applies to raw mixed insertions with their actual
amplitude and the SAME mark, hence one rare factor. Original scalar,
raw-mixed and connected subtraction terms are not dropped. In the full
measure the exterior marginal remains the exact Z(y) from(1); (15) is
only an estimate of it, not a substitution into that marginal.

7. WHAT THIS DOES AND DOES NOT PAY IN THE NATIVE SOURCE PROBLEM

For a fixed number of native bad witnesses, tau is a fixed multiple of
epsilon0. With beta=C_W/g0^2 and epsilon0~g0(log g0^-2)^p0, the energy
exponent is of order (log beta)^(2p0). The entropy in(17) can be absorbed
only while, for instance,

              R^4 log beta << (log beta)^(2p0),          (18)

besides the very conservative beta>=C R^80 requirement. This is a possible
polylogarithmic growth range, not an arbitrary-cluster theorem. If n
distinct witnesses force tau^2>=c n epsilon0^2, replace the right side
of(18) by n(log beta)^(2p0); the actual distinct-witness/overlap geometry
must still be proved before using that count.

More decisively, the original native first cut is

              delta0=(A1/A0)epsilon0.

On a source map with per-edge image bound C_map(R) delta0, the sufficient
marked tube in(17) would require

 C_map(R) delta0 <= c epsilon0 R^-6,
 or A0/A1 >= C C_map(R) R^6,                             (19)

and the R^-60 cap can be more restrictive. A fixed hierarchy does not
certify (19) for unbounded R even if C_map were constant. For n<=C R^4
witnesses, tau~sqrt(n)epsilon0 improves this sufficient radius at most
to C epsilon0 R^-4; it still shrinks. Further complete-read and pair-map
costs can only strengthen the requirement unless sharper estimates are
proved.

This is a limitation of the sufficient pinning/contour bounds derived
here, NOT a proof that growing-cavity admission is impossible. It shows
precisely why a large-deviation estimate beating extensive entropy is
not by itself the missing source-domain theorem. No whole-history map,
renormalization of the original calibration, microscopic-root census,
or all-scale closure is asserted. The fixed enlarged-cavity deliverable
remains a separate fixed-geometry result with its own fixed hierarchy.
