1. JOINT RARITY IS VALID WITHOUT INDEPENDENCE

The convex product has a uniform Poincare constant under every
linear tilt because the Hessian is unchanged. Integrating the tilted
variance inequality twice gives the centered multivariate Gaussian
moment bound (3). Averaging it against an independent standard
Gaussian vector on Q yields (4) exactly: the factor is
(1-2s/lambda)^(-m|Q|/2), not a product-independence assumption.
At s=lambda/4, the supported-guard mean bound and K>M+3 make the
simultaneous radial tail at distinct sites exponentially small in
r^2 times their number.

incidence B ensures at least n/B distinct witnesses. The union bound
therefore gives rho^n with rho=v exp(-c r^2/B), after one fixed floor.
No conditional rarity under already failed masks is needed. The
normalizer is the original finite reference probability nu(D);
nu(D)>=1-Nrho follows directly and N sqrt(rho)<=1/4 keeps it >=3/4.

2. THE EVENT STANDARD DEVIATION AND SPATIAL ARM CAN COEXIST

f_T=nu(F|x_T) in Cov(F,1_A). Complementary integration is over a
FIXED product, so differentiating with respect to x_j, j in T,
produces the full normalized score formula (7). There is no indicator
derivative. The conditional weighted covariance theorem applies on
every principal complement, including complements with holes.
The complete mixed Hessian column at j has a bounded exponentially
weighted l2 norm, giving

 |grad_j f_T| <= L_j+C(sum_(i notin T)L_i^2
                                      exp(-2eta d(i,j)))^(1/2).

Squaring and summing in j does not introduce |T|. For eta'<eta,
the lattice shell estimate

 sum_(j in T) exp(-2eta d(i,j))
       <=C_(eta,eta') exp(-2eta' d(i,T))

is uniform in T and volume. The direct L_j terms have the same
bound. Apply the Poincare inequality of the FULL law to f_T(x_T),
then Cauchy--Schwarz with sd(1_A)<=sqrt(nu A). This proves (9).
It avoids an unproved marginal Hessian assertion and retains both
the rare-event square root and the spatial arm. Product boundaries
are handled by the same Lipschitz/Sobolev approximation as in the
underlying convex-product inequality; measurable events require no
additional approximation of their boundaries. The empty conditional
