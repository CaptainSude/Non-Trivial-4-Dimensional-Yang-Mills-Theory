TAGGED WINDING HISTORIES AND THE NATIVE DATED ENERGY LEDGER
20 October 2026. TURN54. history47.

RESULT AND SCOPE

The prospective K_j reserve in B122II cannot simply be spent
again on a winding tag. It is already promised to later steps.
There is, however, a valid stronger version of its induction:
reduce every PRIMITIVE large-field credit by one fixed factor
lambda in (0,1), keep the dated cost constant fixed (or enlarge
it once), and run the actual birth/aging/renewal/merger proof.
The same prospective-cost bound survives at a sufficiently
weak floor. This is proved below using the source recurrence.

For a complete continued history, a fraction of the original
credits can therefore be kept outside this weaker ledger.
It pays a single tag carried by the original history geometry.
The remaining weaker ledger pays every subsequent native
merger and renewal. This is a family of terminal operator/test
bounds, not a pointwise comparison inferred from a small mass.

The geometry statement covers the actual bounded layer and
small-hull rules, together with retained localization/walk
supports. It does not include an uncharged global ancillary
gauge read. In particular the joint G0,T0 choice law and its
normalizing identity remain outside the result.

2. THE SOURCE'S LEDGER, WITH EVERY CREDIT KEPT AT ITS ORIGIN

For a connected prefix Z at date j, the scalar history factor
is written

                   exp[-K_j(Z)-2P_(b(Z))],              (2.1)

where b(Z) is the earliest large-field birth in that component.
The root token 2P_(b(Z)) is not recreated at every merger.
It is the surviving part of an original primitive credit.

The source has three sorts of additions to this account:

 A. A genuine fresh large-field component F at date j
    contributes at least

                  c_b P_j^2 q_j(F)+2P_j,               (2.2)

    with a fixed c_b>0, after the native parameter choices.

 B. A new preparatory rejection on a mature bounded-small
    component contributes P_j. It renews the ability to pay
    future costs; it does not create another copy of an old
    root token.

 C. Joining components returns their already present root
    tokens to the combined account and leaves just one
    root token for the result.

Let B(h) denote the sum of the primitive credits in A and B
over a complete discrete history h. The 2P_j terms in A are
counted once, at that genuine birth. Tokens moved or released
in C are NOT additional terms in B(h).

All positive costs, including Gaussian dimensions, local
normalizers and the admissible-sequence census, are kept
in the dated cost sum. The source converts these to (1.1).
The same cost constant is used in the strong and weaker
scalar ledgers below. Scalar splitting of an already
exposed complete-history credit requires no change of
the physical Gaussian measure.

3. A FIXED FRACTION OF THE PRIMITIVE CREDITS CAN BE RESERVED

Fix lambda_0>0, for example lambda_0=1/2. For every
lambda in [lambda_0,1], define the weaker account by
multiplying (2.2), every renewal P_j, and every original
root token by lambda. The positive dated costs are unchanged.
Denote the resulting reserve by K_j^lambda.

This definition is on the complete scalar history ledger.
In particular it does not mean K_j^lambda=lambda K_j:
the costs have not been scaled. Nor is a fraction being
removed from K_j after its prospective obligations were
assigned.

Let F_j(Z) be the actual future-cost sum in (1.80), up to
the source's first maturation time under repeated S.
The geometric calculation (1.81) gives

 F_j(Z) <= C_F M^d L^(d+1) R_j^(d+2) q_j(Z).            (3.1)

All constants in this section can be chosen uniformly for
lambda>=lambda_0. The following are sufficient weak-floor
conditions:

 lambda_0 c_b P_j^2 >= C_B M^d L^(d+1) R_j^(d+2),
 lambda_0 P_j       >= C_R M^d L^(d+1) R_j^(d+2),
 2 lambda_0 c_beta P_j
                     >= C_M M^d L^(d+1) R_j^(d+2).      (3.2)

The constants cover the original geometric bounds and the
one fixed enlarged cost C_hist. These are genuine quantitative
conditions. For P_j a sufficiently high fixed power of
1+|log g_j| and R_j a lower fixed power, they hold at one
sufficiently weak floor. For example the renewal and merger
conditions follow from P_j/R_j^(d+2) tending to infinity.
In d=4 the displayed power is R_j^6.
Write P(g)=(1+|log g|)^p and R(g) comparable to
(1+|log g|)^r to distinguish the exponent p from the
function P. A sufficient fixed choice is p>(d+2)r.
That exponent choice must precede the weak floor and
remain feasible in the complete native parameter
hierarchy. Raising the floor alone does not repair a
fixed choice p<=(d+2)r. The source's instruction to
take p0 large is used at this stronger quantitative
choice; feasibility of an already frozen different
hierarchy is not asserted.

Proof of the scaled induction K_j^lambda(Z)>=F_j(Z):

Initial fresh births.
The covering/dilation estimate used at (1.82) bounds q_j(Z)
by a fixed multiple of the sum of q_j(F) over the fresh
components determining Z. Thus the surviving fresh
lambda c_b P_j^2 credit pays the current cost and (3.1),
by the first condition of (3.2).

No new field.
The actual recurrence (1.83) becomes

 K_(j+1)^lambda(SZ)
                      =K_j^lambda(Z)-a_(j+1)(SZ).        (3.3)

The same prospective-cost argument as (1.80)--(1.83)
passes the induction to the next date. Nothing new is
debited for a tag in (3.3).

Mature preparatory renewal.
For the bounded-small component the source replaces
the exhausted prospective reserve by

                  lambda P_j-a_(j+1)(SZ).              (3.4)

Its size and maturation horizon are bounded as in p386.
The second condition of (3.2) pays that horizon.
An old reserve already spent at maturation is not used
again in this argument.

Mergers.
The definition (1.85) is unchanged except for the
lambda factors on primitive credits and root tokens.
For a binary decomposition into touching connected
subfamilies X,Y it gives the exact token surplus

 2lambda[P_(b(X))+P_(b(Y))-P_(b(Z))]
                            >=2lambda c_beta P_(j+1).  (3.5)

The index b(Z) equals one of b(X),b(Y), so this is the
remaining old token, compared at the current date by
the native hypothesis. It is not an independently
created credit.

The spatial cost defect in (1.86)--(1.87) is bounded
by C M^d R_(j+1)^(d+1). The extra forecast cost when
the two maturation schedules are combined is bounded
by C M^d L^(d+1)R_(j+1)^(d+2), as in (1.88).
The third condition of (3.2) pays both. A spanning-tree
leaf induction in the source's actual merger graph
proves the result for any number of components.

This proves the scaled induction for every date and every
admitted completed history. Its constants do not depend
on the number of later mergers or renewals. For a terminal
mature component, F=0 and the scaled root token is still
present.

If a residual terminal spatial or placement reserve is
needed, include its fixed geometric/local-cost allowance
in C_hist before defining F_j and applying (3.2).
For example a desired kappa_c d_j(Z) charge is bounded
by a fixed multiple of M^d R_j^(d+1)q_j(Z), with the
native size conversion retained. The source's general-
region version following (1.89) uses this allowance.
The scaled proof then pays it as part of the specified
cost. It is not obtained by halving a K_j whose future
obligations have already been promised.

4. WHAT THE OPERATOR STATEMENT MEANS

It is unnecessary, and generally unjustified, to infer a
pointwise inequality between the original tagged measure
and a new measure from the integrated display (1.79).
Instead fix a complete continuation h through terminal
date k, retaining the tag, every original read, all later
native integrand factors, and every remaining integration.

Apply the native positive-factor extraction underlying
(1.79) to THIS entire continued expression. For a bounded
terminal test F, the operator estimate first uses the
actual native absolute/positive comparison and retains
||F||_infinity. All native future multipliers and
denominators are inside this calculation; they are not
treated as arbitrary extra tests after a small old mass
has been obtained.

Private integrations may be performed before splitting
the scalar credit. At that point retain the indexed
complete-history bound with exponent C(h)-B_all(h),
where C(h) is the full positive dated cost and B_all
the original primitive credit. Restrict to tagged
histories before erasing their geometry by any supremum
or entropy bound. A counting bound for all admissible
sequences also bounds this restricted family; it must
not replace the tagged geometric supremum by an
unrestricted one before the tag has been extracted.
Use exactly the source's summation convention here.
Before counting a subfamily, retain its individual
positive majorants. After its count has been replaced
by a volume cost times a geometric supremum, retain
that restricted supremum, rather than summing those
already-counted majorants again. C_hist includes the
one native entropy cost. The notation A below refers
to the indexed object before its next such counting
step; the same scalar inequality passes to each
weighted supremum used by the source.

Write A_k^lambda(h) for its positive scalar majorant with
the primitive credits multiplied by lambda and the fixed
enlarged cost from Section2. It retains the complete dated
history and read labels. If B_i(h) is the primitive credit
in the ancestral part of h through the tag date i, the
same extraction and the elementary scalar splitting give

 |T_(k,h) F|
       <= exp[-(1-lambda) B_i(h)]
                       A_k^lambda(h) ||F||_infinity.     (4.1)

The exact inequality used is B_all=B_i+B_>i with
nonnegative primitive credits, whence

 exp[-B_all] <= exp[-(1-lambda)B_i] exp[-lambda B_all].

Thus later credits may all be weakened to lambda as
well. Their unused positive fractions only enlarge
the bound, and the same A^lambda works uniformly
over all continuations. A support tag is constant
on the complete discrete summand h.

The prefix factor is extracted ONCE for one designated
tag. A first-tag partition is another permitted use.
Detecting the same ancestral winding again at a later
date does not create another independent factor.

Formula (4.1) imports the collective positive extraction
module actually used for (1.79), including its delegated
preparatory rejection. Its new step is the explicit
scaled-credit induction proving that A^lambda remains
accepted by the subsequent native census.
It does not require a new pointwise density allocation
stronger than the published complete-history estimate.

The tested class is bounded functions of the terminal
integration variables on the admitted native domain.
It includes complex tests by absolute value. It is not
an estimate against arbitrary unbounded later physical
sources, and it is not a comparison of the original
measures on private variables already integrated out.
For every later native continuation one applies (4.1)
to that complete continuation anew. No eliminated
variable or pointwise measure is reconstructed.

5. CHARGING THE GEOMETRY OF A NATIVE R HISTORY

The S operation is a smallest next-grid cover followed
by ten layers. It preserves unions. The TURN53 rounded
physical-grid bound implies

       sum_(r=t)^i ell_r <= C_L ell_i.                  (5.1)

Consequently a pure-S lineage, of arbitrarily many dates,
stays within C ell_i of its ancestral set in common
physical coordinates. This estimate does not multiply
a constant at each date.
One may take C_L=2+L^(-1)/(1-L^(-1/2)) from the
rounded-grid comparison. The minimal next cover plus
ten layers moves points by at most 11 ell_r in the
sup metric at that step, up to a fixed native collar
convention; summing gives 11 C_L ell_i.

A small-hull replacement is used only on a component
passing the native bounded-size test, so its additional
reach is at most C ell_r at its date. If more than one
hull/merge operation occurs at the same date, its
connections are charged to distinct merger edges,
not repeatedly treated as one free collar.

Here is an explicit charging construction. Compress
maximal no-new-field lineages in the actual merger
forest. Retain as event skeletons the genuine fresh
components, with unit count q_t(F), and genuine
preparatory renewals with unit count one. A fresh
event has a connected tree through its native cells
of physical length at most C ell_t q_t(F).
A renewal determining domain is bounded-small.

At each binary merger choose actual touching points
of the enlarged parent domains. Trace these back
along the two collapsed lineages to their event
skeletons. By (5.1) and the bounded hull rule the
additional length is at most C ell_i for each edge.
A forest with m event skeletons needs at most m-1
such joining edges within one connected component.
Released root tokens are not event skeletons.

Equivalently the union-preserving layer rule places
the geometry in the union of the original seed sets
thickened by C ell_i; the actual connected overlap
graph supplies the same joining edges.

It follows that the connected R geometry through date i
has a complete lifted span controlled by

 C ell_i Q_i(h),   Q_i(h)=1+
      sum_(fresh t<=i) q_t(F)+ #(true renewals <=i).      (5.2)

All ancestral geometric sets are retained, including
their deciding collars; (5.2) is not just a statement
about the displayed final carrier. Nonempty histories
have at least one primitive event, so the added 1 is
absorbed by the event count.
An externally supplied retained component must bring
its original ancestral ledger. It is not a free seed.

If H/ell_i exceeds a fixed collar constant and this
complete geometric support is long, use either its
diameter (at least a fixed fraction of H) or a genuine
cycle with nonzero deck displacement. Crossing a
chosen coordinate seam alone is not a winding tag.

For the cycle case retain, besides the chosen joining
tree, one extra overlap edge completing a simple
nonzero-deck cycle. Such a cycle has at most m edges.
The connecting length bound is therefore unchanged
up to a fixed constant. A single seed which already
winds is charged by its own support. In every case
the retained graph has length at least cH, so

                       Q_i(h)>=c H/ell_i.              (5.3)

Every true event in (5.2) has a primitive credit at least
a fixed positive number at the chosen weak floor; a
fresh support has credit at least c q_t(F). Hence

                       B_i(h)>=c' H/ell_i              (5.4)

for this geometric class. No credit is assigned to a
merger merely because a root token is recycled there.

6. WALK AND COEFFICIENT SUPPORTS

The R geometry alone does not bound a walk that leaves
a region and returns, or the read support of an inherited
coefficient. Such supports must remain separately marked
as in the source's all-support localization expansion.

Keep a fixed strong walk/carrier exponent, a fixed
weaker exponent sufficient for the native preparation
and census, and their positive difference. A portion
of this difference pays the complete added walk/read
blocks of a SINGLE tag. These are original supports,
not only their endpoints or their compressed final
carrier. At a past date t<=i their physical block unit
is at most a fixed multiple of ell_i.

Adding these blocks and the actual connectors to the
construction in Section5 gives

  B_i(h)+c_w W_i(h) >= c H/ell_i                       (6.1)

whenever this combined charged support winds. Here W_i
counts the retained walk/read support in its actual
native expansion convention, and c_w is the available
fixed exponent reserve. The remaining walk weights
use the SAME weaker exponent at all later steps.
The tagged factor is extracted once; it is not
extracted again whenever the marked object is moved.

This applies to the original full support of a regular
carrier and to relative boundary connectors paired
with their retained R geometry. It does not convert
a relative boundary norm into a full boundary norm
without those interiors.

Unspecified gauge-choice deciding data are not among
the blocks counted by W_i. A global G0/T0 choice or
its weight could read farther than the supplied
geometric/localization construction. No bound on such
a read is inferred from (6.1). This is the precise
family still lacking a charged-support attachment.

7. THE SINGLE-TAG CONTINUATION BOUND

Take lambda=lambda_0 once. For a tag whose complete
support is covered by Sections5--6, split the unused
energy/walk reserve once and combine (4.1), (6.1).
For every admitted continuation through any date k>=i,

 |T_(k,h)(1_(tag at i) F)|
    <= C exp[-c N_i/(M R_i)]
                      A_(k,weak)(h) ||F||_infinity.     (7.1)

A_(k,weak) retains all dated labels and the weaker
positive history ledger. Section3 proves that its
future no-new-field, renewal and merger costs are
paid with the same fixed constants. The remaining
walk and placement sums use their fixed native
weaker exponents and census reserves.

The tag restriction can be dropped when summing the
nonnegative right side. Thus the original native
future census bounds the sum over continuations
without losing the scalar exponential in (7.1).
Where the published proof uses an entropy-weighted
supremum in place of a literal sum, apply the same
argument to that positive operation. There is no
second sum over a subfamily whose count has already
been included in the ledger cost.

In particular, for a fixed terminal native component X,
with the residual full-size allowance specified in
Section3, the resulting operator bound has the form

 |T_(k,X;tag_i) F|
    <= C exp[-c N_i/(MR_i)]
         exp[-2lambda_0 c_beta P_k-kappa_c d_k(X)]
                                               ||F||_infinity. (7.2)

Here T_(k,X;tag_i) means the original complete continued
component history restricted to the one designated
charged tag, with the fixed admitted ancillary data.
The terminal mass and general-region size factors
are the scaled versions of the conclusions after
(1.89). Formula (7.2) is a bound on that raw operator,
not on a final normalized boundary logarithm or on
an arbitrary reweighting of an earlier small mass.

If there is more than one possible location or date
for a single tag, keep that label and perform the
corresponding explicit rooted/volume sum. A bound
for an arbitrary product of tags would need its own
credit allocation; it is not asserted here.

This closes the future-merger obstruction for the
charged geometric/walk class at the stated native
positive extraction and admitted-continuation modules.
In particular no fractional spatial exponent is spent
at every future date, no small mass is treated as an
action perturbation, and no recycled root token is
counted as new energy.

8. WHAT IS STILL OUTSIDE THE RESULT

The joint G0,T0 choice law and its full choicewise
identity remain uninspected. If one of its deciding
reads is long but has no native charged walk/support,
Sections5--7 do not control that tag. Ordinary local
pivots, bounded gauge contours and explicit block
Haar factors have their previously checked supports;
the unspecified joint law is a separate case.

The complete original-Wilson normalization is also
separate. The test estimate does not replace an
uninspected source-dependent denominator by a fixed
one. Its native continued integrand and all its
actual normalizers are part of the imported module.

Finally, matching the short lifted coefficients
between two volumes and tuning common bare data
remain logically distinct from the bound on long
tagged histories. The result supplies a missing
propagation estimate; it is not a complete two-cover
pressure theorem or a Yang--Mills existence proof.
