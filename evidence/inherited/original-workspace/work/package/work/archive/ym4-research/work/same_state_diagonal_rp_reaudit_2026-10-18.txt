1. WHAT THE OPERATOR ARGUMENT ACTUALLY NEEDS

The adapted diagonal torus already supplies diagonal RP. New coordinate
planes are used only to obtain reflection INVARIANCE of a comparison law,
and then invariance under even translations. It is unnecessary to realize
every coordinate reflected quadratic as a positive operator X. The proof
does not claim that the expectation-preserving local reduction is positive.

For a finite selected spatial edge set S, C_S is convolution with
K_S(v,u)=product_e exp[(beta/2)Tr(v_e u_e^-1)]. Its Peter--Weyl eigenvalues
c_j=2 I_(2j+1)(beta)/beta are strictly positive. The insertion is

 G_X(u,v)=[C_S^(1/2) X C_S^(1/2)](v,u)/K_S(v,u).

The identity is allowed separately from finite-rank kernels, and gives
G_I=1 exactly. Finite representation kernels and I+itA have bounded
continuous G_X. An X commuting with simultaneous endpoint gauge actions
embeds as X tensor I into the full physical transfer space, with the same
operator-norm upper bound. Diagonal/coordinate reflections give unitary
or antiunitary relabelings (and adjoints), preserving that norm.

The amplified bound |lambda(G_X)|<=||X||, including I+itA for selfadjoint
A, gives Im lambda(G_A)=0 by expansion of

 |1+it lambda(G_A)|^2<=1+t^2||A||^2.

Thus the functional is Hermitian. The crossing-kernel argument swap is
G_X composed theta=conjugate(G_(X*)); conjugation of probability
expectations then gives lambda(G_X composed theta)=lambda(G_X).

For an arbitrary smooth gauge-invariant two-layer observable Y, first
temporally transport the bottom variables to the top. Enlarge S to include
all spatial links it reads on both slices. Then Y=Y(u_S,v_S) and is
invariant under simultaneous upper endpoint gauges. K_Y=K_S Y has the
same equivariance. Product Peter--Weyl projections P_m commute with those
gauges and converge in smooth kernels. Consequently

 X_m=C_S^-1/2 P_m K_Y P_m C_S^-1/2

is bounded finite-rank and equivariant. G_(X_m) tends uniformly to Y,
because K_S has a positive lower bound on this finite compact space.
The norms ||X_m|| may diverge: tilt removal is done for each fixed m
before m tends to infinity. This is sufficient and introduces no hidden
uniform approximation requirement. Uniform approximation extends to
continuous two-layer gauge-invariant observables.

2. THE PAIRED LOCAL REDUCTION IS AN EXACT EXPECTATION DEVICE

At the bottom slice j, gauge invariance of P gives the pointwise identity
P(U)=Ptilde(u,U_>=j+1), u_e=a_x^-1 b_e a_y. This does not gauge fix the law.
Conditional integration of the selected upper partners v_e has exactly
K_S(v,u)W(v,eta), where all factors W read only slices j+1,j+2 and their

For finite Peter--Weyl f, T_f=C_S^-1 M_f C_S maps C-infinity to itself
with finite derivative loss. Multiplication changes spin by a bounded
amount, while c_j/c_k is polynomial in j for bounded |j-k|. The tensor
product estimate on fixed S has no ambient-volume factor. Therefore

 Q=W^-1 sum_i T_(f_i)(W P_i)

is a smooth local upper-slice observable with exactly the same expectation
as P=sum_i f_i(u)P_i(v,eta). Endpoint equivariance of convolution makes

The exact Q_t can retain them, but ||Q_t-Q||_infinity<=C_P t uniformly
in volume, since only finitely many smooth source copies meet S and the
derivative loss is finite. That is all the tilted reduction needs.

Successive upper/lower removals, with gauge-invariant polynomial

They do not multiply earlier sup-norm errors by later inverse-operator
