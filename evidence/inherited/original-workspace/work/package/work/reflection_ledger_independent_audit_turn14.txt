INDEPENDENT AUDIT OF THE REFLECTED POSITIVE-CHANNEL LEDGER
19 October 2026. Admission/proof agent.

Audited source: work/reflection_channel_covariance_ledger_turn14.txt,
196-line version containing the pure-gauge lemma in Section 5.

VERDICT

The displayed identities, covariance constants, and stated sufficient
OS-isometry criterion are correct. The reflection ledger genuinely can
require less than the ordinary innovation ledger: independent noise in
opposite reflected halves can contribute ordinary variance and contribute
zero reflected covariance. No native Balaban attachment follows merely
from this finite-probability statement; the source note says so explicitly.

Two qualifications should remain explicit in subsequent use:

 (i) The signed reflection budget controls an INTEGRATED finite Gram
 matrix. Unlike the ordinary positive covariance ledger, it does not
 imply an L1 bound for the conditional product defect.

 (ii) A noise's geometric block must contain every output coordinate
 affected by that noise, including reconstruction, gauge/frame, and
 auxiliary-label reads. Independence of the random seeds alone does not
 make their effects spatially confined.

1. EXACT TELESCOPE AND THE POSITION OF THE CONJUGATION

For the actual probability joint law mu_j(dx) K_j(x,dy), Jensen gives
F_j=K_j F_(j+1) in L2(mu_j). Reflection invariance and a change of variable
x -> theta_j x give

 conjugate(G_j[b,a])
  = integral F_j^b(theta_j x) conjugate(F_j^a(x)) dmu_j(x)
  = G_j[a,b].

Thus G_j is Hermitian even without reflection positivity. For
f_j=sum_a c_a F_j^a, the quadratic form of the one-step difference is

 c* E_j c
  = integral Cov_(K_j(x,.))(f_(j+1) o theta_(j+1), f_(j+1)) dmu_j(x),

where Cov(u,v)=E[conjugate(u)v]-conjugate(Eu) Ev. Reflection
intertwining is precisely what identifies K_j(f o theta) with the
reflected backward mean. The integrated expression is real, although
the conditional expression need not be real for a fixed input x.

The two terms integrate to G_(j+1) and G_j. Consequently the telescope
and the operator-norm implication of (1.4) are exact. The order of the
supremum and the date sum in (1.4) is sufficient: for each unit c,

 |c*(sum_j E_j)c| <= sum_j |c*E_j c| <= epsilon.

No positive-semidefinite statement about E_j was used. The centering
statement is also correct, since the ordinary means of F_j are constant
along the chain.

The initial-state warning at source lines 50--53 is essential. If an
auxiliary variable A is already randomized in X_0=(U,A), its elimination
to the bare field U is another conditional channel and has its own
reflected product defect. An arbitrary enlargement of the initial
sigma-field cannot be used to make that defect disappear.

2. SIGNED DEFECTS DO NOT HAVE THE ORDINARY L1 CONSEQUENCE

Here is an exact finite counterexample. Let the input x be a fair sign,
with input reflection the identity. Given x, sample a fair sign Y_+ and
put Y_-=x Y_+. Output reflection exchanges Y_+ and Y_-. Every conditional
law is exchange-invariant, so the kernel intertwines reflections. Put
f(Y_+,Y_-)=Y_+. Then

 Kf=0,
 K[(Theta f)f]-(Theta Kf)(Kf)=x.

The integrated defect is zero, while its L1 norm is one. The output
unconditional law is the independent product of two fair signs and is
reflection positive. Thus this issue is not caused by a failure of RP.

Accordingly, (1.4) transports the finite reflected Gram floor exactly as
claimed; it does not imply approximate multiplicativity in conditional
L1, let alone pointwise or as an operator algebra. A bound obtained by
integrating the ABSOLUTE oscillation majorant is stronger and does give
the corresponding L1 estimate, but that is an additional bound.

3. SHARED-NOISE COVARIANCE ESTIMATE

Fix all input and geometry data, and let the noises be a genuine product
law. Exposing them in a fixed order gives orthogonal martingale
differences d_i f and d_i h. Conditional on the preceding noises, the
averaged function of the i-th noise has diameter no greater than osc_i f.
For real functions, conditional variance is at most (osc_i f)^2/4.
For complex functions, the independent-copy identity gives the valid
bound (osc_i f)^2/2. Conditional Cauchy--Schwarz and summation therefore
give exactly source (2.1) and (2.2). No sign restriction is needed.

The resulting reflected estimate involves

 osc_i(f o theta) osc_i(f),

not either of the two squared oscillations separately. If one factor
vanishes, the noise is free in this estimate even when it creates large
variance in the other half.

The geometric interpretation at source lines 85--95 is valid provided
changing noise Y_i changes only coordinates in its COMPLETE output
block. For example, independent seeds followed by a global constrained
reconstruction are not a block-local output law merely because the
seeds were assigned small initial regions. Likewise, a random gauge
choice or geometry label which enters a backward source is part of its
read. The source's explicit backward-function warning is necessary.

The total-covariance identity (3.1) is exact, including for label-dependent
functions interpreted on the complete output state. Conditional
independence after fixing a shared selection label does not remove the
covariance of the conditional means. Treating that selection as its own
transition is a valid alternative only if it is included in the ledger.

4. HALF-ALGEBRA ISOMETRY

Properties (a)--(c) of Section 4 imply, for every pair in the actual
output positive-half algebra,

 <f,h>_(OS,out) = <Kf,Kh>_(OS,in).

The input positive-half membership in (b) is not dispensable. With it,
input RP proves output RP, null vectors map to null vectors, and the
linear map extends isometrically from the output OS completion into the
input OS completion. Surjectivity is not asserted and does not follow.

Conditional independence of the two output half sigma-fields given the
complete input implies (c), since K Theta = Theta K. Shared random
reflection-plane variables prevent that simple factorization unless
they are fixed in the conditioning or handled by a separate boundary
Hilbert-space formulation. The source correctly retains this clause.

A finite-matrix approximate version cannot establish RP on the whole
algebra. Conversely, original bare RP can be used as the ambient form
without proving output RP if the particular backward sources belong to
the original half algebra and their reflected Gram is controlled. These
are the two distinct uses already separated in the source.

5. PURE GAUGE SUBSTEPS

For an actual kernel supported in the input field's gauge orbit, and
functions which are functions of that orbit, f(y)=f(x) for every output
y in the kernel support. Therefore Kf=f and K(fh)=fh pointwise. The
same is true of Theta f when reflection conjugates the gauge action
into the reflected gauge action. Both ordinary and reflected defects
are zero, independently of how correlated the gauge randomness is.

This proof requires equality as observables on the complete input and
output states. Gauge invariance in the field variable alone does not
remove a dependence on an auxiliary label which the substep also
changes. One should either work on the actual field quotient or retain
such labels in the observable and test orbit constancy there.

Preservation of gauge invariance by every later channel ensures that
the backward field functions stay in this zero-defect class. An
integration-only equality of gauge-slice formulas is not, by itself, a
joint transition supported on gauge orbits. The source explicitly makes
this distinction and does not misclassify a physical refill as a gauge
transformation.

6. TERMINAL WITNESS AND NATIVE SCOPE

The independent-Haar example in Section 6 is correct: strictly
half-supported centered functions have positive ordinary variance and
zero OS norm. A positive density lower bound, or an ordinary covariance
floor, cannot substitute for a reflected Gram floor. Positivity of the
measure mu-alpha nu is also different from reflection positivity of
that remainder.

The revised target in Section 7 is therefore a valid narrower target:
an actual terminal reflected witness, backward half-algebra attachment,
and the complete shared-noise defect budget. The present audit proves
no such native budget, no terminal YM witness, and no locality of the
actual R backward functions. In particular it does not repair the
unverified normalized positive-channel dictionary for all gauge-slice
and large-field operations. It verifies the finite-probability consumer
which can be used once those inputs are established.
