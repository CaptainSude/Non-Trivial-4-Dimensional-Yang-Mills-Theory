SPATIALLY SMOOTHED MAGNETIC POINT PROBE: FREE LATTICE LIMIT -- TURN63
20 October 2026

This note proves the free-reference lattice-to-continuum calculation.
The actual compact gauge-invariant observable is defined separately
in spatial_flow_probe_turn63.txt. No nonperturbative comparison of
the original Yang--Mills law with this Gaussian reference is assumed
proved. In particular, finite positive spatial flow does not by
itself construct a continuum nonlinear gauge connection.

1. CANONICAL FREE REFERENCE AND THE MAGNETIC CHOICE

Use three independent canonical Maxwell colors in Euclidean R^4.
One may calculate gauge-invariant linear curvature covariances in
Feynman gauge, where each potential component has Fourier covariance
1/(p0^2+|k|^2). The gauge-dependent potential is only a calculation
device; the following quantities use F_12.

Apply the spatial heat operator exp(s Delta_space), s>0, to the
magnetic curvature:

 X_s(t,x)=exp(s Delta_space) F_12(t,x).

For one color,

 Cov(X_s(t,x),X_s(u,y))
  = integral_R3 d^3k/(2pi)^3
      exp(ik.(x-y)) (k1^2+k2^2)/(2|k|)
      exp[-2s|k|^2-|t-u||k|].                           (1.1)

This follows from
 integral_R dp0/(2pi) exp(ip0 v)/(p0^2+|k|^2)
                         = exp(-|k||v|)/(2|k|).

The equal-point variance is finite:

 v_s = (1/(6pi^2)) integral_0^infinity
                       r^3 exp(-2sr^2) dr
     = 1/(48pi^2 s^2).                                  (1.2)

The angular average of k1^2+k2^2 is 2|k|^2/3. No temporal
smear and no color transport are needed for this variance.

This is specific to a spatial magnetic component. Spatial smoothing
does not remove the high-p0 contribution from F_0i. For example the
p0^2 term in its Fourier covariance tends to1 as |p0| tends to
infinity at fixed spatial momentum. Point evaluation of that
electric component therefore retains a temporal contact divergence.

2. A NONZERO REFLECTION MARGIN AT FIXED FLOW TIME

Take a physical time T>0 and the same spatial point x on both sides
of reflection. Write X_+=X_s(T,x), X_-=X_s(-T,x), each a three-color
vector. Then the per-color covariance is

 c_s(T)=(1/(6pi^2)) integral_0^infinity
                  r^3 exp[-2sr^2-2Tr] dr,              (2.1)
 0<c_s(T)<v_s.

For h>0 define B_+=exp[-h|X_+|^2], B_-=exp[-h|X_-|^2].
Their Gaussian moments are

 m_s(h)=(1+2hv_s)^(-3/2),
 q_s(h)=[(1+2hv_s)^2-4h^2 c_s(T)^2]^(-3/2).             (2.2)

Choose h=h_s=1/(2v_s)=24pi^2s^2 and r_s=c_s(T)/v_s.
The centered reflected form is

 D_s(T)=q_s(h_s)-m_s(h_s)^2
       =(4-r_s^2)^(-3/2)-1/8
       >=3r_s^2/64>0.                                  (2.3)

The lower bound follows by integrating the derivative of
(4-u)^(-3/2) on 0<=u<=r_s^2; that derivative is at least3/64.

There is an explicit scale-invariant lower bound. In the
probability density proportional to r^3 exp(-2sr^2)dr,

 E r = 3sqrt(pi)/(4sqrt(2s)).

Convexity of exp(-2Tr) gives

 r_s >=exp[-3sqrt(pi)T/(2sqrt(2s))],
 D_s(T)>= (3/64) exp[-3sqrt(pi)T/sqrt(2s)].              (2.4)

For example T=sqrt(s)/2 gives one fixed positive numerical margin,
independent of a lattice cutoff. This is a FREE-reference margin.

3. EXACT FREE LATTICE COVARIANCE

Let a>0 be the lattice spacing. On the infinite lattice use the
canonical massless Gaussian covariance in Feynman gauge. For
k in B_a=[-pi/a,pi/a]^3 define

 hat{k}_i=2sin(ak_i/2)/a,
 omega_a(k)^2=sum_i hat{k}_i^2,
 E_a(k)=(2/a)asinh(a omega_a(k)/2).

Phases from forward link differences cancel in the same-point
magnetic covariance. Linear spatial flow multiplies it by
exp[-s omega_a(k)^2].

For t=n a, temporal Fourier integration is exact:

 integral_(-pi/a)^(pi/a) dp0/(2pi)
     exp(ip0 t)/(4a^-2 sin^2(ap0/2)+omega_a^2)

   = exp[-E_a |t|] /
          [2omega_a sqrt(1+a^2 omega_a^2/4)].            (3.1)

Proof: set theta=a p0 and cosh(aE_a)=1+a^2omega_a^2/2.
The left side is

 (a/2) integral_(-pi)^pi dtheta/(2pi)
              exp(in theta)/(cosh(aE_a)-cos theta)
 = a exp(-|n|aE_a)/(2sinh(aE_a)),

which is (3.1). The zero momentum limit is interpreted in the
integral; the magnetic numerator removes the infrared singularity.

Consequently, if T_a>0 is a lattice time, the equal-point and
reflected covariances are

 v_(a,s)=integral_Ba d^3k/(2pi)^3
     (hat{k}_1^2+hat{k}_2^2) exp[-2s omega_a^2] /
     [2omega_a sqrt(1+a^2omega_a^2/4)],                 (3.2)

 c_(a,s,T_a)=the same integral with exp[-2T_a E_a].      (3.3)

The two reflected point vectors are a genuine Gaussian pair
with these covariances; the radial determinant formulas (2.2)
apply exactly to this reference at every a.

4. UNIFORM DOMINATION AND CONTINUUM LIMIT

On B_a,

 2|k|/pi <=omega_a(k)<=|k|.

The nonnegative density in (3.2) is therefore bounded by

 C |k| exp[-8s|k|^2/pi^2].                              (4.1)

Extend the integrand by0 outside B_a. For fixed s>0, (4.1)
is integrable on R^3 and independent of a. Pointwise the
symbols converge to their continuum values. If T_a->T>=0,
dominated convergence gives

 v_(a,s)->v_s,       c_(a,s,T_a)->c_s(T),                (4.2)
 sup_a v_(a,s)<=C s^-2.

Thus the radial reference moments and centered reflected form
converge to (2.2)--(2.3). With T>0 and h_s fixed, for sufficiently
small a the reflected form is at least D_s(T)/2.

A quantitative estimate is also available when a^2<=s:

 |v_(a,s)-v_s| <=C a^2 s^-3,
 |c_(a,s,T_a)-c_s(T)|
       <=C[a^2 s^-3+|T_a-T|s^-5/2].                    (4.3)

Here C can be chosen independently of T,T_a>=0. To see this,
on B_a use

 |omega_a^2-|k|^2|<=C a^2 |k|^4,
 |omega_a-|k||<=C a^2 |k|^3,
 |E_a-|k||<=C a^2 |k|^3,
 E_a>=c|k|.

The numerator/denominator replacement costs
C a^2 |k|^3 exp(-cs|k|^2). The heat multiplier replacement
costs C a^2 s |k|^5 exp(-cs|k|^2). Both integrate to
C a^2 s^-3 in dimension3. For the temporal multiplier,

 |exp(-2TE_a)-exp(-2T|k|)|
   <=C a^2 T |k|^3 exp(-cT|k|)
   <=C a^2 |k|^2,

using sup_(u>=0) u exp(-cu)<infinity. This again costs
C a^2 s^-3 after multiplication by the spatial density.
Changing T to T_a costs
C|T_a-T| integral |k|^2 exp(-cs|k|^2)d^3k
=C|T_a-T|s^-5/2. The continuum tail outside B_a is bounded
by C a^2 s^-3 for a^2<=s using its Gaussian decay.
These estimates prove (4.3).

5. WHY THE FLOW TIME CANNOT JUST BE REMOVED

At fixed T>0, dominated convergence in (2.1) as s decreases to0
gives

 c_s(T)->(1/(6pi^2)) integral_0^infinity r^3 exp(-2Tr)dr
        =1/(16pi^2 T^4).

Together with (1.2),

 r_s~3s^2/T^4,
 D_s(T)~27s^4/(64T^8).                                 (5.1)

Thus the chosen normalized bounded radial witness loses its
reflected margin if its spatial smoothing is removed at fixed
separation. This is not a proof that all local observables fail;
it is an exact restriction on this particular bounded probe.

Keeping positive physical s supplies a possible nontriviality
witness for a theory already equipped with the corresponding
observables. It does not replace the work of constructing the
continuum local observable algebra and its Euclidean symmetries.

6. PHYSICAL COUPLING NORMALIZATION

For the compact SU(2) proposal, let the flowed plaquette be
P_(a,s)=p0 I+i p.sigma and use

 B_(a,s,T)=exp[-h_s |2p/(g_* a^2)|^2],                  (6.1)

where g_*>0 is a fixed reference coupling at a fixed physical
scale. This expression is literally bounded and gauge invariant.
Its compact nonlinear definition and time-slice support are
handled in spatial_flow_probe_turn63.txt.

The Gaussian calculation here uses CANONICAL curvature.
It matches (6.1) only if the physical flowed connection has
leading curvature g_*F_can in this convention. Establishing
that comparison along an actual tuned trajectory is part of
the unresolved renormalization problem.

In particular, replacing the interacting theory by a bare free
law with connection g_a A_can and g_a->0 gives variance
(g_a/g_*)^2 v_s in (6.1), and B tends to1. Its centered
reflection norm tends to0. Bare weak coupling alone therefore
does not supply the fixed-physical-coupling comparison.

Equally, dividing a flowed connection observable by g_a instead
of g_* without tracking the running normalization is unjustified.
The finite free lattice calculation settles its reference limit,
not this interacting normalization.

7. EXACT COMPARISON TARGET

For the actual reflected original-law observables from (6.1),
suppose one can PROVE, with the same tuned trajectory and
fixed s,T,g_*,

 |E B_(a,s,T)-m_s(h_s)|<=delta_1(a),
 |E (theta B_(a,s,T)) B_(a,s,T)-q_s(h_s)|<=delta_2(a).

Since both actual and reference means lie in[0,1],

 ||B_(a,s,T)-E B_(a,s,T)||_OS^2
       >=D_s(T)-delta_2(a)-2delta_1(a).                 (7.1)

Thus limsup[delta_2+2delta_1]<D_s(T) would give a nonzero
cutoff-uniform centered reflection margin. If comparison is
made first to the free lattice reference, (4.2) supplies its
vanishing discretization error.

The inequalities defining delta_1 and delta_2 are NOT proved
in this note. The history count from TURN62 alone does not
prove them, and the exact Gaussian limit must not be described
as their interacting analogue.
