REFLECTION POSITIVITY, CONDITIONAL VARIANCE, AND THE ENDPOINT SIGN
18 October 2026. Strategic independent audit. No new full-theory claim.

1. A REGULAR GAUSSIAN COUNTEREXAMPLE WITH THE EXACT F^2 WEIGHTS

Let 0<a<1. Let U and V be independent centered two-component Gaussians,
with covariance matrices

        Cov(U) = [[1,a],[a,1]],
        Cov(V) = [[1,-a],[-a,1]].

Put X=U+V and reflect by interchanging components 1 and 2. The two X
components are independent with variance 2. Their law is reflection
positive: for any positive-half function F(X1), its reflected square
has expectation |E F(X1)|^2. All centered OS norms are zero.

The simplest linear example is

 E Cov(X1,X2|V)=a,
 Cov(E[X1|V],E[X2|V])=-a,

so the positive conditional covariance is canceled exactly by the
endpoint term. Positivity of the exact auxiliary probability law and
reflection positivity of the original X law do not prevent this.

For the relevant ENERGY-type marks put F_i=X_i^2-2. Conditional on V,

       E(F_i|V)=V_i^2-1,
       Cov(F1,F2|V)=2a^2+4a V1 V2.                    (1.1)

The conditional Gaussian at V=0 gives the positive scalar 2a^2.
The remaining mixed nonvacuum mark has expectation -4a^2, while
the endpoint first-mark covariance is +2a^2. Hence

       Cov(F1,F2)=2a^2-4a^2+2a^2=0.                  (1.2)

In particular, even NONNEGATIVITY of the endpoint first-mark
covariance alone does not save the proposed lower bound: the mixed
conditional mark can cancel it and the positive Gaussian vacuum.
After replacing U,V by gU,gV, the scalar is 2a^2 g^4 and the mixed
mark is 4a g^2 V1 V2 in physical V coordinates. These are exactly
the g^4 scalar and g^2 b^2 nonvacuum weights in the current two-mark
ledger. No large field, cutoff failure, or nonlinear interaction is
needed for the cancellation. This does not contradict the controlled
regular endpoint estimate; it shows why a remaining uncontrolled
endpoint/history contribution cannot be assigned a favorable sign.

2. THE PRECISE CONDITIONAL-PROJECTION SUFFICIENT CONDITION

Let mu be a reflection-invariant probability measure with reflection
theta, and let A_+ be its positive-half observable algebra. Suppose
mu is reflection positive on A_+. Let G be a theta-stable sigma-field
and P=E_mu[.|G], the ORDINARY conditional expectation in L2(mu).
Require the additional locality property

                         P A_+ subset A_+.              (2.1)

Then P commutes with theta, is self-adjoint and idempotent in L2(mu),
and is also self-adjoint for the OS sesquilinear form

          (F,H)_OS = E_mu[(theta conjugate F) H].

Indeed, use L2 self-adjointness and P theta=theta P to move P from
one factor to the other. Therefore P F and (1-P)F are OS orthogonal.
Both belong to A_+ by (2.1), so reflection positivity gives

 ||F||_OS^2 = ||PF||_OS^2 + ||(1-P)F||_OS^2,
                         both summands >=0.             (2.2)

The centered statement follows because P1=1. Either summand can give
a rigorous lower bound if it is quantitatively identified with the
computed regular Gaussian contribution.

Theta-stability of G, positivity of the conditional probability, or
reflection covariance of the blocking map is NOT enough for (2.1).
Conditional expectation onto the full global coarse configuration
generally depends on coarse variables on both sides of the plane,
even when the original observable lies on just one side. The ordinary
minimizing reconstruction already has this nonlocal dependence at
linear Gaussian level. Exponential decay of that dependence is not
strict half-space locality, and the relevant packets meet their scale
of separation precisely where the omitted dependence can matter.

The current raw source recursion has not been identified with P
satisfying (2.1). Its later R-operation is a density-dependent
resampling/reorganization, not an established OS orthogonal projection.
Even a resampling preserving reflection positivity of its own output
would not by itself identify the source derivatives with PF or
(1-P)F. The derivative of a sourced resampling includes its additional
mixed coefficients, exactly the terms at issue in (1.1).

3. WHAT BARE WILSON SITE-PLANE FACTORIZATION DOES GIVE

On a compatible even periodic bare lattice, choose a coordinate SITE
reflection. There are two fixed site planes. Let sigma comprise the
spatial links lying in those planes, and let x,y comprise the remaining
links in the two reflected open halves. Every plaquette is contained
in a closed half or in a fixed plane. Haar measure factorizes. Thus,
with the usual assignment of fixed-plane plaquettes,

 dmu = Z^-1 exp[-S_0(sigma)]
       exp[-S_+(x,sigma)] exp[-S_+(theta y,sigma)]
       d sigma dx dy.                                 (3.1)

The two halves are conditionally independent given sigma, and their
conditional laws are reflected copies. Define

 T F(sigma)=E_mu[F(x,sigma)|sigma],
 d pi(sigma)=Z^-1 exp[-S_0(sigma)] Z_+(sigma)^2 d sigma.

For centered positive-half F one obtains the exact identity

                   ||F||_OS^2 = Var_pi(TF).             (3.2)

This is a genuine use of variance positivity for a reflected square;
it follows from (3.1), not from a generic cross-covariance inequality.
Gauge invariance is not needed for this algebraic factorization.

If G is generated by any measurable coarse variables of sigma alone,
the usual variance decomposition of TF gives two nonnegative terms.
Equivalently the projection in Section2 maps into the fixed-plane
algebra. This supplies a possible reflection-compatible construction:
coarsen the reflection-plane data and retain the exact half-space
conditional amplitudes.

However the Gaussian vacuum extracted by the current isotropic bulk
RG is not identified with either term of this boundary variance
decomposition. Establishing that identification would require control
of TF and of the induced boundary measure pi, with its half-space
normalizers Z_+(sigma). Those are not the normalized inner kernels
already estimated in the current proof. This alternative can remove
sign cancellation, but it does not automatically supply a UV-uniform
lower bound. A positive lower bound on a good boundary sector still
needs that sector to have a quantitatively nonvanishing pi mass.

4. A SECOND SUFFICIENT INTERFACE DIRECTLY IN THE SOURCE LEDGER

Suppose an exact two-source representation is

 log Z(z_+,z_-)-log Z(0)
   = c(z_+,z_-)
     +log E_nu exp[z_+ A_+ + z_- A_- +z_+ z_- M+...].

Then its mixed derivative is exactly

        c_+- + E_nu M + Cov_nu(A_+,A_-).               (4.1)

A sufficient sign mechanism is:
 (a) nu is reflection positive;
 (b) A_+ belongs to its positive-half algebra and A_-=theta conjugate A_+;
 (c) M belongs to its OS positive cone, for example
     M=sum_l c_l (theta conjugate B_l) B_l,
     c_l>=0, B_l in that positive-half algebra,
     with a convergent expectation/sum.

Then the centered covariance and E_nu M are nonnegative, so (4.1)
is at least c_+-. A direct lower bound E_nu M>=-epsilon is a weaker
sufficient replacement for (c). Conditions (a),(b) alone are not
enough, as the exact mixed term in (1.1) illustrates why it must be
tracked. Pointwise positivity of M would also suffice, but is much
stronger than the Gaussian mixed-mark structure generally provides.

The present source decomposition supplies neither (a),(b),(c) for
the complete surviving-history endpoint. Original Wilson RP does
not pass automatically to its joint R-history law; the effective
first marks can read both halves through the reconstruction and
history; localized connected coefficients have signs; and the
mixed mark is a genuine signed response. Source partition-function
preservation for each z retains these derivatives algebraically,
but confers no individual sign on them.

5. STRATEGIC CONCLUSION

There is no valid deduction from existing bare RP plus positive
auxiliary kernels to a nonnegative unestimated endpoint remainder.
The Gaussian example (1.1)--(1.2) rules out that deduction already
for quadratic energy marks, including their exact mixed field term.

An OS-orthogonal conditional projection satisfying (2.1), or the
explicit coarse positive-cone factorization (4.1)(a)--(c), would be
a concrete sufficient substitute for an absolute surviving-history
estimate. Neither is presently matched to the raw RG/R construction.
Bare site-plane factorization offers an exact alternative framework,
but its boundary amplitudes and normalizers are new analytic objects.
For the current proof, a normalized marked endpoint bound remains
the direct unresolved interface. It may be weaker than controlling
all bad histories in total variation, but its sign cannot simply be
discarded by invoking reflection positivity.
