2. THE ORIGINAL GAUGE INSERTION SUPPLIES A SECOND, INDEPENDENT WITNESS

For every noncenter x in a fixed L block B(y), let
Y_(y,x)=U^u(y,x), the actual averaged contour in B119(1.5).
Contour endpoint covariance and u(y)=I imply that its dependence on
u(x) is left or right Haar multiplication by the fixed U(y,x). Internal
gauges along every path cancel. Therefore, conditional on the original
U, these Y variables are independent and have the SAME normalized law

 d nu_G(Y)=z_G^(-1) 1_{||Y-I||<epsilon0}
                        exp[-beta_G h(Y)] dHaar(Y),
                    beta_G=c_G/g0^2.                       (4)

The scalar z_G in (4) is the original scalar in (1.5), with its trace
normalization converted to h; no new normalizer is introduced.
Original Wilson energies are unchanged by the inserted gauge. The
coarse average is unchanged because all coarse-center gauges are I.
Hence (4) is independent of all gauge-invariant Wilson energies and
of the held coarse value, before imposing any event on Y.

Only the original P0-good domain is needed to define the native group
average. Outside that domain an arbitrary normalized auxiliary kernel
may be used to complete the probability space; no formula there is
used. Conditional independence and (4) on the P0-good event suffice
for every estimate: after integrating Y, drop that event and apply (3).
This avoids an assertion about undefined group averages on bad fields.

There is an elementary uniform moment for (4), including its cutoff:

       nu_G exp[(beta_G/2) h(Y)] <= 2^(3/2).                (5)

Indeed u=h(Y) has density proportional to
u^(1/2)e^(-beta_G u)(2-u)^(1/2)1_[0,epsilon0^2/2](u).
It is a Gamma(shape 3/2, rate beta_G) density tilted by a decreasing
nonnegative function. For increasing phi its expectation is at most
the untilted Gamma expectation, by the two-copy covariance inequality.
Taking phi=e^(beta_G u/2) proves (5). Thus no volume-dependent lower
bound for a gauge partition function is needed.

Set lambda=min(beta_W/4,beta_G/2)=c_*/g0^2 and C_*=2^(3/2).
For finite plaquette and gauge-contour witness sets E and F,

 E_aug[1_{P0 good} exp(lambda[sum_E h(U_p)+sum_F h(Y_v)])]

This is the original augmented normalized law. Additional pre-record,

3. A LOCAL Q1 FAILURE HAS ONE OF THESE TWO WITNESSES

        v_Q <= C_L (1+M2 R1)^4,   B_Q <= C_L.              (7)

The second constant is independent of R1: native seeds are translated

v_Q and B_Q if a finite geometry has exceptional short periods.

4. ORIGINAL-NORMALIZER BAD-SEED PRESSURE AND LOCAL GUARD MASS

For any deterministic finite set J of distinct original Q1 seeds,

 P_aug(H_delta and every D in J is Q1 bad) <= rho_Q^|J|,
 rho_Q=min{1, C_* v_Q exp[-lambda tau^2/(2 B_Q)]}.
                                                               (9)

Proof. Choose a witness from (8) for each seed. There are at most
v_Q^|J| choices. A witness occurs at most B_Q times, so the union of
distinct chosen witnesses has at least |J|/B_Q members, each with
h>=tau^2/2, and at most |J| members. Apply exponential Markov and
(6) to that union. Summing these choices proves (9).

In particular rho_Q <= C v_Q exp[-c delta0^2/g0^2]. The square of
the native logarithmic fluctuation radius occurs; no source factor
or current/bare-coupling identification has been suppressed.

For a deterministic seed set S define
N_S^H=1_H_delta sum_(D in S)1_{D Q1 bad}. Expansion of the finite
product, using (9) on every nonempty subset, gives for t>=0

 E_aug exp(t N_S^H)
       <= [1+rho_Q(e^t-1)]^|S|
       <= exp[|S|rho_Q(e^t-1)].                            (10)

Let G_C be the exact local guard of local_pre_q1_completion_native:

       P_aug(H_delta and G_C^c) <= p_C^bad,

Thus a polynomial-size buffered guard costs only its actual seed
