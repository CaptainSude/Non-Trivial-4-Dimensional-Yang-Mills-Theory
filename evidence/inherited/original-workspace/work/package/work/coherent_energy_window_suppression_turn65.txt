WILSON SUPPRESSION OF THE HOMOGENEOUS ENERGY WINDOW -- TURN65
20 October 2026. Root derivation; independent audit required.

STATUS

The deterministic Hessian counterexample in radial_flow_hessian_turn65
does not establish a divergent Wilson average. This companion result
shows more: a fixed, sufficiently thin gauge-invariant energy window
around that homogeneous example has exponentially small ORIGINAL
Wilson mass in the spatial volume. Even a crude global nonlinear-flow
derivative bound then makes its entire Hessian contribution vanish
at fixed physical spatial volume.

The result controls this particular window, not every configuration
with a large flow derivative. It is not the missing full-measure
derivative estimate. It does identify a genuine way in which the
Wilson conditional law supplies information absent from the bare
plaquette moment inequality.

1. A UNIFORM SINGLE-EDGE SMALL-WINDOW ESTIMATE

Use the original finite nondegenerate periodic four-dimensional SU(2)
Wilson law with homogeneous beta>=1. Given all links except e, its
quaternion q has density proportional to

                 exp(z.q),  |z|<=6beta, q in S^3,

relative to normalized Haar dω. Any incident plaquette energy is
h_p=1-a.q for an exterior-measurable unit vector a.

There is a numerical C, independent of the volume, beta, and exterior,
such that

 P(|beta h_p-1|<=epsilon | all other links)
                     <= C epsilon,  0<epsilon<=1/2.     (1.1)

Proof. Let kappa=|z|. A spherical cap of radius
c/sqrt(1+kappa) about z/kappa has Haar mass at least
c'(1+kappa)^(-3/2) and satisfies z.q>=kappa-C'.
The kappa=0 case follows directly from Haar normalization. Therefore

 Z(z)=int exp(z.q)dω(q)>=c exp(kappa)(1+kappa)^(-3/2),
 sup_q exp(z.q)/Z(z)<=C(1+kappa)^(3/2)<=C' beta^(3/2).
                                                               (1.2)

Under Haar, h=1-a.q has density (2/pi)sqrt(h(2-h)) on [0,2],
independently of a. The interval
[(1-epsilon)/beta,(1+epsilon)/beta] has length2epsilon/beta,
and its density is at most C beta^(-1/2). Its Haar probability
is at most C epsilon beta^(-3/2). Multiplying by (1.2) proves (1.1).
No favorable staple, action convexity, or independence is needed.

The center1 could be replaced by any fixed positive center with a
modified constant on a fixed compact range. Only center1 is used here.
This is an upper anti-concentration bound, not a lower fluctuation
bound and not a tail estimate for the entire low-energy region.

2. MANY SPECIFIED PLAQUETTES

Let p_1,...,p_m be plaquettes with designated distinct edges e_i such
that e_i does not occur in p_j for j!=i. The Wilson action may still
couple the designated variables through other plaquettes. Put
A_i={|beta h_(p_i)-1|<=epsilon}. Conditioning on every link but e_i,
all the other A_j are measurable. Equation (1.1) gives

 P(intersection_(i=1)^m A_i)<=(C epsilon)^m.              (2.1)

To see that no conditional-independence premise is hidden here,
write E[1_(intersection_{j!=i} A_j) E(1_Ai | outside e_i)]
and use (1.1), then repeat. The outside includes all temporal links
and the full remainder of the four-dimensional lattice.

In a fixed spatial slice with n sites there are n plaquettes of
orientation12. Choose a maximal edge-disjoint subset greedily.
On a nondegenerate periodic lattice every such plaquette shares
an edge with at most four other plaquettes of that SAME orientation.
Thus a subset of size m>=n/5 can be selected (rounding downward,
or replacing1/5 by1/10 for all sufficiently large n, is harmless).
Any edge in each selected plaquette is a valid designated edge.

Define the gauge-invariant full-slice event

 E_epsilon={|beta h_(12,x)-1|<=epsilon for every spatial site x}.

It contains the homogeneous field used in radial_flow_hessian_turn65
and all its gauge transforms. It also contains an open neighborhood
of that field at each fixed regulator. Applying (2.1) to the selected
subset yields

                     P_W(E_epsilon)<=(C epsilon)^m.     (2.2)

Fix epsilon0 in (0,1/2] small enough that C epsilon0<=exp(-1).
For all sufficiently large spatial tori,

                     P_W(E_epsilon0)<=exp(-c n).         (2.3)

This is uniform for beta>=1 and in the time period. No comparison
with a gauge-fixed measure or covering of a gauge orbit is used.
The entire event is gauge invariant from its definition.

3. A CRUDE GLOBAL FLOW HESSIAN BOUND

For completeness, the actual bounded magnetic probe of the preceding
note has, for fixed alpha,g_*>0 and a<=1,

 sup_U ||Hess F_s,a(U)||op <= C a^-8 exp(C' s/a^2).        (3.1)

The constants can be taken independent of spatial volume and beta;
they depend on alpha,g_* and the fixed lattice incidence degree.
This estimate is deliberately crude and is not a continuum bound.

Here is a finite-dimensional justification that keeps dimension
dependence explicit. Embed every SU(2) link as a unit quaternion
in R^4. Plaquette energies are fixed-degree polynomials in neighboring
quaternions, including their linear conjugates. The round tangential
gradient is the ambient gradient projected by I-q q^T. Thus the
spatial flow has a polynomial ambient extension

                   q_s=a^-2 V(q)

whose component for a link depends on a bounded number of neighboring
links. Along the product of unit spheres all coefficients of V,
DV and D^2 V are bounded by incidence-dependent constants. The first
and second derivative OPERATOR norms are bounded independently of
the number of links:

                  ||DV||op<=C, ||D^2V||bilinear<=C.

For the first derivative, apply Cauchy--Schwarz on each bounded
neighborhood and then sum; each input link occurs a bounded number
of times. For the second, bound one local l2 factor by its global
l2 norm, then sum the squares of the other local factor using the
same bounded overlap. This proves a bilinear l2 bound with no factor n.

The variational equations and Gronwall therefore give

 ||D Phi_s||op<=exp(C s/a^2),
 ||D^2 Phi_s||bilinear<=C exp(C' s/a^2).                  (3.2)

For the second inequality, differentiate the first variational
equation once more: the inhomogeneous term is bounded by
C a^-2 exp(2C s/a^2), and its time integral is bounded by an exponential
with a larger fixed rate. Ambient derivatives at the compact base
flow suffice; no assertion about global ambient solutions is needed.

A single final plaquette has first and second ambient derivatives
bounded independently of volume. The function
exp[-4alpha |p|^2/(g_*^2 a^4)] has, on that compact plaquette range,
first derivative bounded by C a^-4 and second derivative bounded
by C a^-8. The chain rule and (3.2) give the ambient version of (3.1).

Finally, the second derivative along a unit product-sphere geodesic
includes its ambient acceleration. In quaternion coordinates, the
Euclidean norm of that acceleration is bounded by a constant times
the square of the product-metric speed: sum_e |q_e'|^4 is at most
(sum_e |q_e'|^2)^2. Its pairing with the first derivative has the same
or smaller bound. Hence (3.1) holds for the actual Riemannian Hessian.
The Pauli/2 metric differs from the ambient round metric only by
the fixed factor4 and changes no dimension or cutoff exponents.

4. THE EXPLICIT WINDOW HAS VANISHING HESSIAN CONTRIBUTION

Take a sequence of cubic spatial tori of fixed side L>0, with
n=(L/a)^3, and fix s>0, alpha>0, g_*>0. Equations (2.3),(3.1) give
the actual original-law estimate

 E_W[1_(E_epsilon0) ||Hess F_s,a||op]
       <=C a^-8 exp[C' s/a^2-c(L/a)^3] ->0.             (4.1)

This holds uniformly for beta>=1, in particular on the sequence
used for the deterministic Hessian divergence. The same conclusion
holds for any fixed positive power of the norm, by changing the
prefactor and the C' coefficient; the negative a^-3 exponent wins.

There is no contradiction with the homogeneous example. A pointwise
large derivative can lie in a window that is sufficiently rare in
the actual Gibbs measure. The moment-only counterlaw of the preceding
note places all its mass in this window and violates (1.1). That is
the extra information furnished by the exact Wilson conditional law.

The event E_epsilon0 does NOT exhaust fields with large derivatives.
Its complement includes almost all configurations, including ordinary
spatially fluctuating ones, and no Hessian bound there has been proved
by this argument. We have not decomposed the complete bad derivative
set into such volume-sized coherent windows. Taking a union over
unspecified backgrounds without controlling its size would invalidate
the estimate.

The conclusion is therefore a positive but limited actual-law
consumer: the specific homogeneous obstruction and this entire
gauge-invariant window around it do not force an averaged Hessian
failure. The uniform physical variance, actual nonlinear temporal
increment, and complete YM4 construction remain open.
