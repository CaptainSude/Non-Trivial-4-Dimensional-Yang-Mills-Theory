1. A TRANSVERSE ESTIMATE FOR THE ACTUAL AVERAGE

This is a direct reproof of the archived alias inequality, with the
actual native A in(1). Let x be divergence free, D^T x=0, including
harmonic one-forms. In a coarse Bloch fiber p in [-pi,pi]^4, use the
K^4 fine aliases k_r=(p+2pi r)/K in the fine Brillouin zone. Write
u_r=K d(k_r), P_r=|u_r|^2. The unitary alias coordinates x_r obey
x_r perpendicular to u_r, and their Maxwell energy is
K^-2 sum_r P_r |x_r|^2.

For the scalar centered mean put
a_r=K^-4 sum_(z in B_K) exp(i k_r.z). The normalized straight
multiplier is diagonal in the bond direction alpha, with entry

 omega_(r,alpha)=a_r K^-1 sum_(s=0)^(K-1) exp(i s k_(r,alpha)).

In unitary counting Fourier coordinates,
 K (A x)(p)=sum_r Omega_r x_r. This K factor is essential.
The elementary multiplier facts are

 P_r>=4 for r!=0,
 sum_r |omega_(r,alpha)|^2<=1,
 min_alpha |omega_(0,alpha)|>=c_omega=(2/pi)^5,
 Omega_0 u_0=a_0 d_c(p).                                   (3)

The first follows since a noncentral alias has a component of
absolute physical momentum at least pi, and
K sin(pi/(2K))>=1. The second is the fiber form of ||A||<=K^-1,
already proved by row/column counting. For the third, each centered
block sinc and the additional straight-string sinc on the central
alias is at least 2/pi in modulus. The last is the exact telescope
of the straight difference, equivalently(1). Phases are kept in
Omega_0; only their absolute values enter the estimates.

Let P_c be orthogonal projection away from d_c(p), with P_c=I at
p=0. Set y=P_c sum_r Omega_r x_r and y_0=P_c Omega_0 x_0.
For nonzero p,

 x_0=P_(u_0)^perp Omega_0^-1 y_0,
 |x_0|<=c_omega^-1 |y_0|.

Indeed the removed coarse longitudinal vector pulls back to a
multiple of u_0, by(3). At p=0, Omega_0=I and the identity is direct.
The high-alias contribution to y has norm at most ||x_high|| by
(3), and its energy sum is at least 4||x_high||^2. Therefore

 ||x||^2 <= K^2[A_0 ||P_c A x||^2+B_0 ||d_1 x||^2],
 A_0=2 c_omega^-2,   B_0=(1+2 c_omega^-2)/4.                 (4)

This proof uses only the literal flat average and Fourier/Hodge
geometry. It is uniform on compatible tori (including p=0) and on
the infinite lattice. It uses neither a point-pinned inverse nor
positivity of a classical residual. For K=1 the same conclusion
holds immediately and the conservative constants remain valid.

2. SCALAR MEAN LIFT AND THE FULL Q_B GAP

Block Neumann Poincare gives ||psi||<=C_P^1/2 K||D psi|| whenever
Q_0 psi=0. For a coarse scalar b, let H_b minimize ||D phi||^2
subject to Q_0 phi=b. At finite volume the mean constraint fixes
the constant mode and the minimizer exists uniquely. Its equation
is P_0 Delta_0 H_b=0. A central-alias feasible lift gives

 ||D H_b||^2<=C_lift K^2 ||D_c b||^2.                       (5)

To check the scale, the central scalar multiplier satisfies
|a_0(p)|>= (2/pi)^4 and the fine frequency is p/K. Divide the
central fine plane wave by a_0(p); its average is the prescribed
coarse wave. Counting Parseval costs K^4, while
|d(p/K)|<=pi |d_c(p)|/(2K). This proves(5) with a fixed constant.
The zero coarse mode is lifted constantly with zero energy.
At a coarse Nyquist mode taking the real part of a complex feasible
lift preserves the real constraint and cannot increase energy.

For arbitrary phi put b=Q_0 phi and psi=phi-H_b. Then
Q_0 psi=0, P_0 Delta_0 psi=P_0 Delta_0 phi=g, and
||D psi||^2=<psi,g>. Poincare and(5) imply

 ||D phi||^2<=2 C_P K^2 ||P_0 Delta_0 phi||^2
                +2 C_lift K^2 ||D_c Q_0 phi||^2.            (6)

Use the orthogonal Hodge decomposition x=x_T+D phi. Then
P_0 Delta_0 phi=P_0 D^T x, d_1 x_T=d_1 x, and
P_c A x_T=P_c A x by(1). Also
||D_c Q_0 phi||<=||A x||+mu||x_T||.
Substitution into(6), followed by(4), gives

 ||x||^2<=C_gap K^2[||d_1 x||^2+||P_0 D^T x||^2+||A x||^2]. (7)

For example take C_gap to be the maximum of
(1+4 C_lift)B_0, 2 C_P, and (1+4 C_lift)A_0+4 C_lift.
The classical and coarse inputs in(2) imply
<x,Q_B x> >= ||d_1 x||^2+||P_0 D^T x||^2+||A x||^2.
Thus Q_B>=C_gap^-1 mu^2 I. All mean and Hodge zero modes have
been kept. On the infinite lattice the inequalities follow by
Fourier/finite-support limits; no uniform global Hodge inverse is
being asserted on arbitrary unweighted bounded data.

3. TENT LIFT, FULL ENERGY COMPARISON, AND BOTH COARSE GAPS

A block-local smooth lift controls the missing mean divergence.

 Pi_0 E e=e,     ||E||<=C_0,     ||D E||<=C_1 mu.             (8)

Internal edges use the O(K^-1) differences of f. Crossing edges
use its O(K^-1) endpoint values and
sum_y|e_(y+alpha)-e_y|^2<=4 sum_y|e_y|^2. Dividing by the
block counting norm K^4 sum_y|e_y|^2 proves(8), uniformly also
when periodic interfaces coincide. For K=1 use E=I.

Since E e-e is in ran P_0,
<Pi_0 D^T x,e>=<x,D E e>-<P_0 D^T x,E e-e>. Hence

 ||Pi_0 D^T x||<=C_1 mu||x||+(C_0+1)||P_0 D^T x||.          (9)

Combine(9) with(7) to obtain Delta_1<=C_Delta Q_B. Together with

 c R_v<=Q_B<=C R_v,       R_v=Delta_1+mu^2 I.                (10)

In particular D Pi_0 D^T<=Delta_1<=C_Delta Q_B. Woodbury in(2)

                 c_S I<=S_0<=I.                            (11)

For the scalar coarse factor use the SAME lift: E J_0 is a right

Let H_s be the minimum of ||D phi||^2 with J_0^T phi=b.
Then 0<=H_s<=C_1^2 mu^2 I. The energy Schur complement of T is
H_s+mu^2 I, so

This is a genuine lower bound for both factors. Neither follows

4. WEIGHTED COARSE INVERSES

Let final blocks be the spatial units, with fixed color fiber.
H1 supplies exponentially weighted fine resolvents and energy legs.
For the scalar these give

 ||T_0^-1||_weighted<=C mu^-2,
 ||D T_0^-1||_weighted+||T_0^-1 D^T||_weighted<=C mu^-1,
 ||D T_0^-1 D^T||_weighted<=C.                              (13)

The analogous V_0^-1 bounds use the vector energy R_v. The J_0
columns have norm one and support in their own final block.
Consequently M_0 and S_0 have uniform exponentially decaying
final-block kernels. For S_0 use I-J_0^T R_f J_0 with
R_f=D^T V_0^-1 D, whose weighted norm is bounded by H1.

Because the coarse fibers have fixed color dimension, summing the
exponential kernels shows that conjugation by a small coarse
Lipschitz exponential weight changes M_0 or S_0 by O(|theta|).
Equations(11)--(12) and a Neumann inverse therefore imply

 ||(M_0+tI)^-1||_weighted+||(S_0+tI)^-1||_weighted
                                         <=C/(1+t), t>=0. (14)

The same conclusion follows first for linear weights and then by
kernel Schur summation. It introduces no fine-coordinate dimension.
This proves uniform coarse inverses and logarithm resolvents, not
just entrywise decay of the un-inverted factors.

The scalar also has a uniform physical complex ball. For bounded
physical A, D(A)-D_0=O(mu||A||) and J(A)-J_0=O(||A||) by the
literal T/M theorem. Expansion of T(A) relative to T_0 is therefore
O(||A||+||A||^2). Its inverse and M(A)-M_0 inherit uniform weighted
bounds on a smaller ball, using(13) and the own-block support of J.
The coarse gap persists there by a complex Neumann argument. This
is not a positivity claim for complex transpose products.

5. ROOTED FIRST AND SECOND SOURCE JETS

Directions a_u have complete smooth physical collars of fixed

All collar and recursive reads are retained.

H2 supplies rooted energy-sandwiched T_u,T_uv,V_u,V_uv. The T/M
theorem supplies own-block derivatives of J, with sizes C b_u and
C b_u b_v. D_u has size C mu b_u and D_uv has size C mu^2 b_u b_v
on their actual local reads.

Differentiate R=T^-1 by
 R_u=-R T_u R,
 R_uv=R T_u R T_v R+R T_v R T_u R-R T_uv R.                  (16)
Insert(16) into mu^2 J^T R J, including every endpoint J derivative.
All powers balance. Equivalently use the bounded energy legs
mu R_s^-1/2 J, the bounded sandwiched inverse, and the H2 vertices.
For an explicit generator term,
mu^2(J^T R D^T) D_u(R J) costs

At two insertions the intervening segments are R,DR,RD^T,DRD^T
with their exact powers from(13). A projection vertex contributes

For the vector keep the factorization

       R_f(A)=D(A)^T V(A)^-1 D(A),
       S_tilde(A)=I-J(A)^T R_f(A)J(A).                      (17)

Its first derivative is
 (R_f)_u=D_u^T V^-1 D+D^T V^-1 D_u
                         -D^T V^-1 V_u V^-1 D.
The second derivative contains the two ordered V_u,V_v insertions,
one V_uv insertion, all D_uv endpoint terms and the mixed D_u terms.
This is simply the full product rule and(16) with T replaced by V.
The legs R_v^-1/2 D and R_v^-1/2 D_u are bounded by C and C b_u;
R_v^-1/2 D_uv is bounded by C b_u b_v. The flat sandwiched inverse
and H2 vertices bound every term. Finally attach J,J_u,J_uv outside
R_f. In particular D J_u is never estimated as a raw interface

would enter if one differentiated the sharp column too early.

Insert physical-block restrictions at each differentiated factor.

vertex joins both matrix endpoints to its complete source roots.
Own-block J derivatives are local endpoint insertions. A fixed
exponent reserve pays the intermediate block sums. Consequently,

 ||D_(u_1,...,u_r)F(i,j)||
 <=C_r product b_(u_l) exp[-c tree(i,j,u_1,...,u_r)].         (18)

The estimates concern Hilbert operators restricted to physical blocks,

6. LOGARITHMIC DENSITIES WITHOUT A PARTIAL-TRACE CYCLE

For F_0=M_0 or S_0, let R_t=(F_0+tI)^-1. Choose the logarithm germ
continued from the positive flat matrix. The exact first derivative
of log F is integral_0^infty R_t F_u R_t dt. Its mixed second
derivative is the integral of

 R_t F_uv R_t
 -R_t F_u R_t F_v R_t-R_t F_v R_t F_u R_t.                  (19)

The density at a fixed coarse root o is the color trace of the
(o,o) block of(19). DO NOT cycle this partial trace. Bounds(14),(18)
join every displayed source back to o. The t integral is finite
because the first term has (1+t)^-2 and the latter terms have
(1+t)^-3. Thus

 |D_(u,v) tr_color(log F)_(o,o)|
       <=C b_u b_v exp[-c' tree(o,u,v)].                    (20)

First derivatives have the corresponding one-root bound. The only
trace factor is the fixed color dimension. Every fixed polynomial
moment of the source distances is summable uniformly in m,K.
Flat subtraction N0 changes none of these derivatives.
