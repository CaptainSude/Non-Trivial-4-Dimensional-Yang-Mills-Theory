The narrowest substantive import in one_run_source_gradient_closure
Sections12--16 is the generalized marginal layer estimate in
boundary_history_gradient_independent_audit Sections6,8. Its proof
imports L1--L3. It does not derive those inputs from the mixed-map

Write i for the birth scale, n for its actual determining layer, j for
the localization endpoint, h=ell^(i-n), a_n=alpha0,n and
m_in=min(1,a_i/a_n). For a vacuum-subtracted own-field-invariant

 |W_i(Phi_i,n,j(U,J;s,t))-W_i(1,0)|
 <= C nu_i h^4 m_in^-2 (1+diam_i X)^2 exp[-kappa' d_i(X)] . (A)

an enlarged amplitude contour t carrying the FIRST nonempty mixed
response. Cauchy in t must retain a connection factor exp[-sigma D].
A fixed g_n^-C_b and a fixed polynomial of j-i are harmless here.
A loss h^-epsilon, g_n^-C|Y| or C^(j-i) is not harmless in this use.

For near X, a sufficient precise map statement is that the pair on
its complete read neighborhood, INCLUDING the independent companion
current, is in an n-scale C1/current ball of radius C a_n on those
contours. Its first response in the same complete graph norm may be
bounded by C g_n^-C P(j-i) exp[-sigma D]. A t radius proportional to
a_n g_n^C exp[sigma' D]/P(j-i), with sigma'<sigma, then keeps a fixed
fraction of the n-scale domain. Applying the already proved neutral
estimate on THAT contour and then Cauchy gives the product of h^4
and the connection factor. This is a genuine implication, not the
minimum of two unrelated bounds. Far X uses the old dated-domain
contour and an independent spatial tail to supply h^4.

       |W_i(X)| <= c g_i^kappa_R exp[-kappa_s d_i(X)].   (4.1)
