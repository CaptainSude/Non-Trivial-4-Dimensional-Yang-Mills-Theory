PROOF: UNIFORM SEPARATED GAUSSIAN RESPONSE
========================================================================

TURN105 -- SEPARATED GAUSSIAN ENERGY RESPONSES HAVE A UNIFORM ANALYTIC DOMAIN
21 October 2026. Root derivation; independently audited by literature102.
Admitted exclusively for the Gaussian reference model stated below.

SCOPE

This is a theorem for the three-color exact-curvature Gaussian reference
model, not for the original interacting Wilson measure. It strengthens
TURN103's separated second-moment benchmark to all mixed source jets
on a cutoff-independent complex domain. Its proof retains the complete
quadratic source kernel and resums each source region exactly before
estimating the interaction between regions. It does not assume a bound
on either region's diagonal energy variance.

1. FINITE-DIMENSIONAL STATEMENT

Let C be a real symmetric positive contraction on a finite real Hilbert
space H. Allow C to be singular. Let Y^1,Y^2,Y^3 be independent centered
Gaussian vectors with covariance C. Decompose H=H_A+H_B+H_O orthogonally.
Let W_f and W_g be real symmetric operators supported respectively in
H_A and H_B, with F=||W_f|| and G=||W_g||. Thus W_f W_g=0. In the lattice
application they are multiplication by real, possibly signed profiles.
Define centered quadratic variables

 X_f = (1/2) sum_(c=1)^3 [<Y^c,W_f Y^c>-Tr(C W_f)],
 X_g = (1/2) sum_(c=1)^3 [<Y^c,W_g Y^c>-Tr(C W_g)].

Let Z(s,t)=E exp(sX_f+tX_g). For |s|F+|t|G<1, the real part of
the Gaussian quadratic form is strictly positive on the underlying
standard Gaussian variables, so the integral and its analytic logarithm
are well defined. Put

 W(s,t)=log Z(s,t)-log Z(s,0)-log Z(0,t),

with the branches zero at the origin. Then, writing C_AB=P_A C P_B,

 |W(s,t)| <= (3/2) |st| F G
                 /(1-|s|F-|t|G) ||C_AB||_HS^2.             (1.1)

This bound contains no Tr(C_AA^2), no Tr(C_BB^2), and no dimension of H.
If either profile is zero, all statements reduce to W=0.

For p,q>=1, Cauchy's formula on |s|=1/(4F), |t|=1/(4G) gives

 |partial_s^p partial_t^q W(0,0)|
 <=(3/16) p! q! (4F)^p (4G)^q ||C_AB||_HS^2.              (1.2)

In particular the exact second jet is

 partial_s partial_t W(0,0)
    =(3/2) Tr(W_f C_AB W_g C_BA).                         (1.3)

The trace in (1.3) is on H_A with the evident restrictions of W_f,W_g.
For multiplication operators this is (3/2)sum_(p in A,q in B)
f_p g_q C(p,q)^2. It can have either sign for signed f,g.

2. EXACT RESUMMATION OF THE QUADRATIC KERNEL

First work with uncentered quadratic variables; the scalar linear
centering cancels identically from W. Gaussian integration gives

 E exp[(1/2)sum_c <Y^c,(sW_f+tW_g)Y^c>]
   =det(I-C^(1/2)(sW_f+tW_g)C^(1/2))^(-3/2).

For singular C this follows by writing Y=C^(1/2)z for a standard
Gaussian z. The identity det(I-AB)=det(I-BA), followed by eliminating
H_O, reduces its determinant to

 det [[I-s C_AA W_f,       -t C_AB W_g],
      [-s C_BA W_f,       I-t C_BB W_g]].

Let A_s=I-s C_AA W_f and B_t=I-t C_BB W_g. Their inverses have norms
at most (1-|s|F)^(-1) and (1-|t|G)^(-1), by their Neumann series.
The exact Schur determinant identity yields

 exp W(s,t)=det(I-T(s,t))^(-3/2),
 T(s,t)=st B_t^(-1) C_BA W_f A_s^(-1) C_AB W_g.            (2.1)

This determinant is on H_B. Both within-region source kernels have
been kept in their full inverses. They have not been replaced by
their values at zero source or estimated by a divergent trace.

Write alpha=|s|F, gamma=|t|G. Since ||C_AB||<=1,

 ||T|| <= alpha gamma/[(1-alpha)(1-gamma)] =:q<1,
 ||T||_1 <= alpha gamma/[(1-alpha)(1-gamma)] ||C_AB||_HS^2.

The trace norm estimate uses ||RST||_1<=||R||_HS||S|| ||T||_HS
after grouping the two off-diagonal covariance factors; all other
factors are bounded operators. Consequently the absolutely convergent
series

 W=(3/2) sum_(n>=1) Tr(T^n)/n

has absolute value at most (3/2)||T||_1/(1-q). Substituting q and
using (1-alpha)(1-gamma)-alpha gamma=1-alpha-gamma proves (1.1).
On the stated Cauchy circles its bound is (3/16)||C_AB||_HS^2,
which proves (1.2). Expanding (2.1) to order st proves (1.3).
These are exact finite-dimensional identities for every C in the class.

3. APPLICATION TO FOUR-DIMENSIONAL LATTICE CURVATURE

Use the unit periodic four-torus, spacing a=1/N, with six plaquette
directions and three independent colors. Let Delta_N have symbol
lambda_N(k)=4 sum_mu sin^2(k_mu/2). Let

 P_N(k)=d_1(k)d_1(k)^*/lambda_N(k), k!=0; P_N(0)=0.

This is the orthogonal projection onto exact plaquette two-forms.
For any s_N>=0 put C_N=exp(-s_N Delta_N)P_N. It is a real symmetric
positive contraction, including all orientation components. If original
Gaussian curvature F has covariance beta^(-1)P_N, the variables in
section 1 are the centered energies of sqrt(beta) exp[-s_N Delta_N/2]F.
At s_N=N^2/log(N)^2 they coincide with the TURN103 filtered energy
variables P^0_(t_a/2) X_(a,f), t_a=1/[beta a^2 log(1/a)^2].

Let f,g be bounded real profiles whose physical supports have periodic
distance at least delta>0. The actual four-link/plaquette-center offsets
are bounded in lattice units and do not affect fixed positive physical
separation for sufficiently large N. Let A_N,B_N contain the respective
plaquette roots; |A_N|,|B_N|<=6N^4.

For the filter s_N=N^2/log(N)^2, TURN103's differentiated periodic
heat-kernel estimate proves

 |C_N(p,q)|<=C_delta N^(-4), p in A_N, q in B_N.            (3.1)

All direction entries obey this bound. Thus

 ||(C_N)_(A_N B_N)||_HS^2<=C_delta.                        (3.2)

The full N^8 pair count is paid by the square N^(-8) kernel bound.
Sections 1--2 therefore prove a source domain independent of N and
beta for the normalized mixed response, together with uniform bounds
on every fixed mixed derivative. The one-region variances can diverge:
for continuous physical f at the stated filter, TURN103 gives their
leading growth (9/(128pi^2))log(N)^4 integral f^2.
There is no contradiction because that variance is not in (1.1).

For completeness (3.1) also holds for unfiltered P_N and uniformly
for all s_N>=0. The representation is

 C_N=d_1 d_1^* integral_(s_N)^infinity
                              [exp(-u Delta_N)-Pi_0] du.

For 1<=u<=N^2, each second difference of the scalar heat kernel is
bounded by C u^(-3) exp[-c r/sqrt(u)], with periodic distance r.
For u>=N^2 it is bounded by C N^(-6) exp[-c u/N^2]. These are proved
in TURN103 by contour shift and the nonzero Fourier sum respectively.
Integrating their absolute values from 1 to infinity at r>=c_delta N
gives C_delta N^(-4). For 0<=u<=1 a fixed imaginary Fourier shift
gives C exp(-c r) for second differences at separated indices; its
periodic image sum has the same form, with uniform constants. Its
integral is exponentially small in N. Integrating over any subinterval
[s_N,infinity) is bounded by the complete absolute integral. This
proves the stated uniform extension, with zero modes explicitly removed.

4. WHAT THE ESTIMATE DOES AND DOES NOT REQUIRE

The source domain in (1.1) controls the normalized TWO-REGION response.
The unnormalized generating functions can grow exponentially with the
number of sites. Their logarithms and their diagonal energy variances
need not have limits. No probabilistic law for all unrenormalized
composite energies is inferred from a finite mixed ratio.

The structural input doing the work is the exact Gaussian determinant,
the positive-contraction covariance, and the Hilbert--Schmidt estimate
between separated supports. A pointwise small-curvature expansion of
the Wilson law supplies none of these identities for its interacting
source response. In particular (1.1) cannot be transferred merely by
matching the quadratic Taylor coefficient of the Wilson action.

The complete inverses A_s and B_t show why deleting only a scalar
one-source vacuum term is not a replacement for transporting the full
quadratic source kernel. All repeated source insertions within each
region are resummed before the cross-region trace is bounded. This is
a concrete reference calculation for the actual source transport task.

The first two-source Wilson estimate must still retain its generated
quadratic/marginal kernels and bound the signed connected nonlinear
defect. The lattice Gaussian benchmark proves that a uniform separated
mixed-response domain is compatible with diverging diagonal contact
fluctuations; it does not prove the required interacting domain, the
continuum hierarchy, or nonabelian identification.

1. COMPLEX INTEGRABILITY, INCLUDING SINGULAR C

Set M=sW_f+tW_g and Y=C^(1/2)z with z standard real Gaussian. Since
W_f,W_g are real symmetric, Re(C^(1/2)MC^(1/2)) is real symmetric and
has norm at most alpha+gamma, alpha=|s|F, gamma=|t|G. Thus I minus this
real part is positive definite for alpha+gamma<1. Gaussian integration
is locally dominated on this domain. Moreover ||C^(1/2)MC^(1/2)||<1,
so its determinant has the analytic logarithm fixed by the convergent
trace-log series at zero. No inverse of C is required. This validates
the singular-covariance case as well as signed profiles and complex
source parameters. A zero profile gives W=0 as stated.

Each of the three colors contributes a determinant exponent -1/2;
the total exponent is -3/2. The centering is the linear scalar
-(3/2)Tr(CM) in log Z and cancels exactly from the mixed response.

2. SCHUR FACTOR ORDER AND SIGNS

After det(I-AB)=det(I-BA), I-CM has third block column (0,0,I) in
H_A+H_B+H_O. Its determinant is therefore the stated 2x2 determinant.
The lower Schur complement is

 B_t - st C_BA W_f A_s^(-1) C_AB W_g
 = B_t[I-st B_t^(-1)C_BA W_f A_s^(-1)C_AB W_g].

This gives exactly the T in (2.1); no commuting of W_f with C_AA or
of W_g with C_BB is used. The signs give +st in T and consequently
-(3/2)log det(I-T) in W. The identities agree at zero and continue
analytically throughout alpha+gamma<1, where ||T||<1. Thus the
fractional determinant power has an unambiguous specified branch.

The two off-diagonal factors can be bounded in Hilbert--Schmidt norm:

 ||B_t^(-1) C_BA [W_f A_s^(-1)] C_AB W_g||_1
 <= F G ||C_AB||_HS^2 /[(1-alpha)(1-gamma)].

The operator norm bound q=alpha gamma/[(1-alpha)(1-gamma)]<1 follows
from ||C_AB||<=1. The inequality |Tr(T^n)|<=||T||_1||T||^(n-1)
and 1/n<=1 yield (1.1) with denominator 1-alpha-gamma. This estimate
does not contain any diagonal Hilbert--Schmidt norms.

3. CAUCHY CONSTANT AND SECOND JET

At alpha=gamma=1/4, the right side of (1.1) is
 (3/2)(1/16)/(1/2) ||C_AB||_HS^2=(3/16)||C_AB||_HS^2.
The closed product of the two Cauchy circles lies strictly within the
analytic domain. Multiplication by p!q!(4F)^p(4G)^q gives (1.2).
For the coefficient st, A_s^(-1),B_t^(-1) may be replaced by I and
all powers T^n with n>=2 have total order at least four. Hence
 partial_s partial_t W(0,0)=(3/2)Tr(W_f C_AB W_g C_BA).
This independently agrees with the Wick covariance of three centered
quadratic forms, each carrying the factor 1/2. Signed profiles can
give a signed trace; the proof does not introduce association.

4. CURVATURE SYMBOL AND SEMIGROUP NORMALIZATION

For nonzero Fourier mode, d_1d_1^*/lambda_N is the rank-three
orthogonal projection onto exact two-forms. P_N(0)=0 removes the
harmonic modes. Thus C_N=e^(-s_N Delta_N)P_N is a real symmetric
positive contraction for all s_N>=0; Fourier complex phases merely
encode the real cochain operator.

If Cov(F)=beta^(-1)P_N, then sqrt(beta)e^(-s_N Delta_N/2)F has
covariance C_N. The Gaussian OU conditional mean at time r is
e^(-beta r Delta_N)F. Setting r=t_a/2 gives s_N=beta t_a=N^2/log(N)^2,
which is exactly the TURN103 normalization. Centering remains the
centering for this smoothed covariance. No extra beta or factor two
is missing.

5. OFF-DIAGONAL ESTIMATE, INCLUDING s_N=0

The zero-mode-subtracted integral is well-defined componentwise on
two-forms, and d_1d_1^* annihilates the componentwise constant mode.
Each matrix entry of its differentiated heat kernel is a fixed finite
sum of translated second differences of the scalar heat kernel.

The TURN103 proof of the middle and large time bounds is valid: for
1<=u<=N^2, shift an infinite-lattice Fourier coordinate by i eta with
eta of order u^(-1/2), gaining spatial exponential decay while the two
difference factors give u^(-1). The four-dimensional integral gives
u^(-2). Periodizing has a uniformly bounded image sum since
N/sqrt(u)>=1. For u>=N^2 the nonzero Fourier sum is bounded by
C N^(-6)exp(-cu/N^2).

For the added interval 0<=u<=1, take instead a fixed nonzero imaginary
shift. The difference factors and exp[-u lambda(k+i eta)] are uniformly
bounded on the compact contour. This gives C exp(-c|x|) on Z^4.
The periodic image sum retains C exp(-c' r), uniformly for N>=4.
This is a legitimate extension even at u=0; second differences of
the delta kernel have finite support and are zero at the separated
indices once N is sufficiently large.

At r>=c_delta N, integrate the three intervals. The first contributes
C exp(-c_delta N). In the middle interval v=r/sqrt(u) gives at most
C r^(-4) integral_0^infinity v^3 e^(-cv)dv. The last interval gives
C N^(-4). Therefore every entry is bounded by C_delta N^(-4)
uniformly for the integral over ANY [s_N,infinity), s_N>=0. The finite
cochain translations only decrease r by a constant and are absorbed
for sufficiently large N at fixed positive physical separation.

There are at most 6N^4 roots in each support. Squaring the entry
bound and summing pays the entire N^8 pair count, proving (3.2).
The constants may depend on physical separation, as required.

file correctly keeps interacting attachment, contact-compatible
