2. WHAT IS ALREADY EXACT FOR OUR POSITIVE SELECTED OPERATION

Let rho be a finite positive compact-lattice density, M its actual
coarse map, and W(U) the TURN48 window, including its zero extension
outside the admitted chart. Define, with the same Haar/delta factors,

 (T rho)(V)   = integral delta(M(U)V^-1) rho(U) dU,
 (T_W rho)(V) = integral delta(M(U)V^-1) W(U)rho(U) dU.

Then, exactly and before logarithms,

                  T rho = T_W rho + T_(1-W) rho.              (2.1)

For every coarse datum where the selected density is positive, set

 D(V)=log[(T rho)(V)/(T_W rho)(V)] >=0.                         (2.2)

The full effective action differs from the selected one by -D(V).
After extracting the vacuum, the required field correction is
D(V)-D(1), not D(V) and not a discarded scalar constant. A valid
attachment must estimate this actual quantity, or replace both
operations by an exactly identified third operation.

Integrating (2.1) shows that T_W is generally a strict submass
operation. This is compatible with the exact TURN48 label completion:
the other labels and cemetery restore T. No claim that W approaches
one uniformly in total volume is needed or available.

The same-step weak-chart domination already proved in TURN50 bounds
the total defect relative to the selected numerator INSIDE that chart.
It does not supply the D of (2.2), because the complement contains the
chart cemetery. Even a bound of the form 0<=D<=epsilon times the number
of fine cells would not, alone, control the rooted field variation or
the difference of D at two covering volumes.

3. MASS-PRESERVING REPLACEMENT: THE NORMALIZATION THAT MUST BE MATCHED

Here is the elementary identity underlying any prospective native
replacement attachment. It is proved independently of the unread
Large-I display; its identification with that display is an explicit
remaining source check.

Split variables into an exterior y and replaced variables x. For
nonnegative integrable f(y,x), h(y,x), with

             0<m_h(y)=integral h(y,x) dx<infinity,

put m_f(y)=integral f(y,x)dx and

       (R_h f)(y,x)=h(y,x) m_f(y)/m_h(y).                     (3.1)

Tonelli gives

 integral (R_h f)(y,x)dx=m_f(y),
 integral R_h f=integral f.                                 (3.2)

The replacement retains the actual numerator mass m_f. Replacing f
by h/m_h instead would erase it. A changed vacuum factor also has to
be retained explicitly in (3.1).

This identity does not preserve arbitrary observables pointwise.
Their exact exterior representative is

  A_f(y)=integral A(y,x)f(y,x)dx/m_f(y)

where m_f>0. Then integral R_h f times A_f=integral f A.
For an exponential source the correct representative is the full
conditional source transform, not exp of a conditional mean.
Thus a successful unsourced partition attachment would still leave
the independent local-source problem unless source transport is
proved on the actual replacement domains.

4. WHY A NORMALIZED KERNEL ESTIMATE CANNOT SUPPLY FULL PRESSURE

For any positive integral operator L_X write

                  m_X=L_X 1,       P_X=L_X/m_X.              (4.1)

At real admitted data P_X is a probability kernel, so

                  |P_X F|<=sup_actual_domain |F|.            (4.2)

If L_X is replaced by c_X L_X for any positive c_X, P_X is unchanged
while log m_X changes by log c_X. Consequently no estimate which sees
only P_X can determine a pressure contribution, a vacuum
normalization, or its covering defect. This is an exact invariance
argument, not an issue of a slightly worse constant.

The complex comparison in B122II (1.75) adds its controlled analytic
factor to (4.2); that does not remove this scalar invariance.
Also the supremum in (4.2) is over the actual retained integration
domain. A local weak-background germ is insufficient unless its
pullback is admitted on that entire domain.

For the final retained-domain expansion one must distinguish:

 (i) a connected logarithm with Y_1,...,Y_s held fixed;
 (ii) a connected logarithm after summing all possible Y_i histories.

Relative decay in the complement of the Y_i can prove convergence
in (i). It does not pay a winding Y_i whose collapsed relative
length is zero. To prove (ii), the geometric size of Y_i has to be
paid by m_(Y_i), its original energy/census, or a separate proven
majorant before the sum. Treating each normalized P_(Y_i) as a small
activity would discard exactly that payment.

This pinpoints the nonattachment in the TURN35 renewal argument:
its complete native row/census is an input, not a consequence of
normalization. It also pinpoints why the final B122II boundary
logarithm cannot simply be relabeled LC1--LC3 of TURN50.

5. A CHANGED WINDOW CHANGES THE FIELD MAP BEYOND THE LEADING GAUSSIAN

The following finite-dimensional calculation disproves a tempting
formal implication. It does not purport to calculate the actual
Yang--Mills discrepancy.

Let Z be a standard q-dimensional real Gaussian, q>=1. Fix finite
T>0, c_T=Pr(|Z|<=T), and write E_T for conditioning on that ball.
Let lambda(u)>=0 be bounded and nonconstant on real admitted data.
Consider the same saddle and the same Hessian in

 I_full(u,g)=E exp[-g^2 lambda(u)|Z|^4],
 I_T(u,g)=c_T E_T exp[-g^2 lambda(u)|Z|^4].                    (5.1)

Uniformly for lambda in a bounded nonnegative interval, as real
g tends to zero,

 log I_T-log I_full
   =log c_T+g^2 lambda(u)[q(q+2)-E_T|Z|^4]+O(g^4).           (5.2)

Proof. For x>=0, |exp(-x)-1+x|<=x^2/2. Apply it to
x=g^2 lambda |Z|^4; both eighth moments are finite. The two
integrals are 1+O(g^2) after dividing I_T by c_T, so the real
logarithm has the same uniform second-order expansion.
E|Z|^4=q(q+2). Strict monotonicity of r^4 and positive Gaussian
mass both below and above T imply E_T|Z|^4<E|Z|^4.

After the exact vacuum log c_T is removed, (5.2) is field-dependent.
The conclusion persists if lambda is an allowed local invariant
function. Matching the classical saddle, constrained Hessian and
coarea determinant therefore does NOT identify the nonlinear map.
It matches their leading Gaussian contribution.

The example only uses a real asymptotic expansion. No holomorphic
extension of the untruncated quartic integral through complex g=0
is asserted; such an extension would be false on a full disc.

6. SAME LEADING DRIFT DOES NOT GIVE THE SAME WEAK BARE TRAJECTORY

An even stronger diagnostic addresses the exact weak-coupling premise
of the native theorem. Fix B,c,Gamma>0. Compare the scalar maps

 selected: s_(j+1)=s_j-B,
 alternate: gamma_(j+1)=gamma_j-B-c/gamma_j.                 (6.1)

Both have leading drift B as gamma=g^-2 tends to infinity. Their
difference is merely O(g^2), the size compatible with a bounded
even-g fluctuation correction.

Tune the selected map for N steps by

       s_0=gamma_0=Gamma+BN,   s_j=Gamma+B(N-j).

As long as the alternate iterates are positive, induction yields
gamma_j<=s_j. If all N iterates were positive, telescoping would give

 gamma_N=Gamma-c sum_(j=0)^(N-1)1/gamma_j
        <=Gamma-c sum_(m=1)^N 1/(Gamma+Bm).                  (6.2)

The last harmonic sum diverges with N. For sufficiently large N,
(6.2) contradicts gamma_N>0. Hence identical one-loop drift plus
an O(g^2) map discrepancy does not preserve the SAME selected bare
prescription through arbitrary depth.

This does not preclude tuning the alternate map separately.
It proves that such tuning, or a comparison strong enough to
transfer the endpoint, is a substantive theorem. Our TURN49
finite selected shooting theorem cannot be substituted into the
native weak-coupling premise just because its determinant drift
agrees to leading order.

7. A SHORTER PRESSURE TARGET: NATIVE VACUUM COVER DEFECT

The following algebra isolates a potentially useful alternative to
constructing a complete full-law rooted winding catalogue.

Fix compatible reference volumes V and V_j=j^4 V, one bare
prescription, and one terminal physical scale. Suppose the following
two identities/bounds have actually been verified for the native
operation at both volumes:

  Z_a(V)=exp[E_a(V)] I_a(V),                                (7.1)
  |log I_a(V)|<=C_V, |log I_a(V_j)|<=C_(V_j),                (7.2)

where the constants are independent of the ultraviolet cutoff a.
Here E_a is the COMPLETE extracted vacuum, with its stated sign.
Equation (7.1) must include any mass-preserving R replacements and
all Haar, Gaussian, constraint and cutoff normalizations.

Then the raw common-cover pressure defect is exactly

 j^4 log Z_a(V)-log Z_a(V_j)
 =j^4 E_a(V)-E_a(V_j)
       +j^4 log I_a(V)-log I_a(V_j).                         (7.3)

Therefore

 |j^4 log Z_a(V)-log Z_a(V_j)|
 <=|j^4 E_a(V)-E_a(V_j)|+j^4 C_V+C_(V_j).                    (7.4)

A UV-uniform bound for the native vacuum cover defect suffices.
There is no need to derive spatial decay for the terminal residual
history measure solely for this fixed-reference pressure purpose.
This reduction does NOT by itself provide normalized local-source
products, reflection positivity of the transformed measure, or
observable convergence.

B119 Corollary3 is the natural candidate for (7.2), at the actual

Even granting (7.1)--(7.2), the bound on
j^4 E_a(V)-E_a(V_j) is not proved by ultraviolet stability alone.

For example, |log(e^-E Z)|<=C at each volume permits an arbitrary

8. HOW THE NATIVE VACUUM TARGET RELATES TO THE TURN50 CRITERION

If the exact native vacuum can be decomposed into same-volume-
independent per-cell constants plus localized connected vacuum
coefficients with common lifts, the TURN50 covering argument can
be applied to those vacuum coefficients alone. All determinant,
cutoff and gauge constants remain in the per-cell part or in the
declared connected part. A field quotient is insufficient.

The whole-infinite-lattice prescription in B119 (3.56)--(3.57) is
promising for a common marginal convention. It is stronger than
translation invariance inside one torus. It still does not prove
that our selected finite-volume marginal equals that prescription.
Nor does it prove that every native vacuum coefficient, including
mature large-field feedback and winding paths, agrees on covers.

There is a benign geometric variation of TURN50 worth retaining.
Suppose, at ultraviolet distance k from the terminal scale,
n_k=x ell^k, and a verified native vacuum cover-error bound is

 |epsilon_k(n_k)| <= C(1+k)^m n_k^d (1+n_k)^p
                                  exp[-c n_k/R_k],           (8.1)
 R_k <= C_R(1+k)^r,                                          (8.2)

with fixed constants, x>0, ell>1. Then

                      sum_(k>=0)|epsilon_k(n_k)|<infinity,   (8.3)

uniformly in the ultraviolet truncation.

Proof. The logarithm of the polynomial prefactor is O(k+log(1+k)).
The negative exponent is at least c' ell^k/(1+k)^r. For all
sufficiently large k it exceeds twice the prefactor logarithm
and grows faster than, for example, ell^(k/2). The resulting
tail is summable; the finitely many remaining k contribute a
fixed constant. This proof also works for a fixed polylogarithmic
R_k, and for periods bounded below by a fixed positive x.

Thus an increasing native localization-block size is not by itself
an obstruction to ultraviolet pressure summability. The unproved
part is the actual common-cover vacuum coefficient estimate (8.1),
with all path/read geometry and feedback included. It cannot be
obtained from a distance bound on a finished matrix element alone.

10. POSITIVE ALTERNATIVE: TUNE THE NATIVE FLOW IN ITS OWN SCHEME

Sections5--6 do not force us to prove equality with the selected map.
A better route may be to tune the native RT flow separately. The
finite clipping/shooting METHOD of physical_determinant_drift_turn49,
Section10, is applicable to a different flow if its own input class,
fixed-block drift and finite continuity are supplied. No summability
of a difference between the two schemes is needed in that route.

10A. What the small native feedback can pay.

Suppose a native birth activity has rooted amplitude at most C g^K,
and a specified finite-order marginal extraction/pullback costs at
most C g^-s [log(g^-2)]^t, for fixed s,t. Then its contribution is

                  C g^(K-s)[log(g^-2)]^t.                   (10.1)

For any target power p one may choose K>s+p, fix all parameters
needed for that K, and then take the weak floor sufficiently high.
The contribution is uniformly O(g^p). This elementary implication
uses no uniform complex-g disc and no sign of the correction.

The native arbitrary-power feedback estimate is therefore capable
of absorbing a FIXED number of Cauchy-radius, geometry and maturity
losses. It cannot by itself pay a number of losses growing with
history length or an unproved source/domain pullback. The exact
operator norm and finite exponent s of the actual marginal
extraction still have to be recorded. This is a much more specific
task than demanding complete selected/native scheme equivalence.

10B. Running-radius value bounds do not control the regular drift.

There is a separate difficulty for the regular generated activity,
whose available value bound need not have an arbitrary g power.
A simple exact diagnostic shows why applying Cauchy blindly to a
shrinking background ball is insufficient.

Let alpha=g[log(g^-2)]^q, with q>0, and let Z have a fixed symmetric
bounded probability law, nonzero variance, supported in [-1,1].
For g small put

 E_g(u)=epsilon u^4/alpha^4,
 F_g(u)=log E exp{E_g(u+gZ)-E_g(u)},   0<epsilon<=1/100.      (10.2)

E_g has zero quadratic jet and sup_(|u|<=alpha)|E_g|<=epsilon.
On |u|<=alpha/2 the increment in (10.2) is uniformly bounded,
because g/alpha tends to zero. Hence this is an admissible small
bounded-potential example with a convergent analytic logarithm.

Writing delta=g/alpha and using symmetry gives the exact second
Taylor coefficient

 [u^2]F_g(u)
  =6epsilon g^2 alpha^-4 E_weighted Z^2
    +8epsilon^2 g^6 alpha^-8 E_weighted Z^6,                (10.3)

where E_weighted uses the positive normalized weight
exp(epsilon delta^4 Z^4). In particular the first term is
asymptotic to

       6epsilon E Z^2 g^-2[log(g^-2)]^(-4q),

which diverges. Vacuum subtraction does not change it.

This is not asserted to be a native gauge-theory activity. It proves
that a small value bound on alpha(g), even with a vanishing incoming
quadratic jet and bounded fluctuation variables, does not logically
bound the generated marginal coefficient. Gauge/Ward and dated
irrelevant structure must be used quantitatively, not just named.

To apply our determinant sign result to native beta one needs,
for its ACTUAL regular correction, either a fixed-background-domain
core with controlled derivatives or a directly differentiated
cluster/diagram estimate proving

 |Pi(native correction minus its matched leading determinant)|
                         <=eta(g),  eta(g)->0,              (10.4)

uniformly over the admitted native input histories, with the
inherited irrelevant response also controlled in the same class.
It is sufficient to establish a fixed-block version of (10.4)
whose error is smaller than the verified positive block drift.
The value-only running-radius estimate does not establish (10.4).

10C. Finite continuity and the auxiliary histories.

For a FIXED finite native hierarchy and fixed regular branch,
positive finite-dimensional integrals with continuously moving
thresholds are ordinarily continuous when their boundary sets
have measure zero and there is a common integrable domination.
To use this observation here one must check those properties for
the actual constraints, minimizers, normalization denominators and
domain cuts. Holomorphy in background fields is not that check.

There is an additional discrete issue: the prescribed R_j is an
L-power selected from a coupling-dependent threshold. Changing
g can change R_j, the partition, and the filing/maturity of terms.
Fixed-hierarchy continuity says nothing about that change.
Possible resolutions are a proved hierarchy-independent marginal
coefficient, matching across switches, or a deterministic hierarchy
envelope admitted uniformly during the whole finite shooting
argument. None is inferred here from the field analytic bounds.

Finally the selected shooting proof clips the evaluation coupling
at a weak floor while keeping the scalar decrement UNCLIPPED.
Its class bound is available for every such auxiliary evaluation
history, before proving that clipping is inactive on the endpoint
solution. The native theorem about an actual weak recursive flow
does not automatically state this stronger auxiliary-history
property. Native dated radii, maturation, and inter-age comparisons
must be preserved for those auxiliary maps, or a different shooting
argument must be supplied.

CONCLUSION OF THIS EXTENSION. Separate native tuning is a viable
reduction of the attachment problem. Arbitrary-power feedback can
absorb fixed extraction losses. The remaining quantitative issues
are the actual regular marginal bound (10.4), admission/continuity
through the native hierarchy and the auxiliary weak-history class.
No computation here proves or refutes those missing native inputs.

No claim of a complete YM4 construction is made.
