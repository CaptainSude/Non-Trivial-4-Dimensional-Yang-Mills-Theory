1. GEOMETRY AND PLAQUETTE ASSIGNMENT

The aligned nested block partitions have integer side lengths, each
dividing the next, with compatible periodic sides. A link strictly
interior to a fine block is strictly interior to its unique containing
coarse block. Hence I_j is a subset of I_(j+1), giving the stated
nested skeletons S_(j+1) subset S_j. The requirement of at least two
top-level blocks per periodic direction permits ordinary local lifts
of every block and avoids a self-wrapping block convention.

An elementary plaquette containing an interior link of B lies in the
closed cube B. It cannot contain an interior link of a different
same-level block: its unit coordinate intervals are adjacent to a grid
hyperplane or inside a single grid strip, and any links reaching the
hyperplane have a boundary endpoint. This remains valid at intersections
of grid hyperplanes. At m_0=1 there are no interior links. At m=2 the
strict definition also gives no interior links; an identity step there
is harmless. No positivity or nonemptiness of each interior was used.

Thus the sets P_j(B) and P_j^S form a disjoint exhaustive partition of
the ORIGINAL plaquettes. The boundary read includes spokes as well as
tangential links. A closed m-cube has 4m(m+1)^3 links and its strict
interior has 4(m-2)(m-1)^3 links for m>=2, so the stated O((m+1)^3)
bound is correct. It is only a read-count bound, not a uniform norm.
