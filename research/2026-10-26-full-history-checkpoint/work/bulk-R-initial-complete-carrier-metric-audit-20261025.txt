2. TREE SIZE PAYS M-CELL VOLUME

Write h(X) for the number of M-cells. There are dimension-dependent
constants c_4,C_4 such that every connected M-cell animal satisfies

  h(X) <= C_4(1+d(X)),          d(X) <= h(X)-1.                (2)

For the upper bound, connect cell centers by a spanning tree of the
face-adjacency graph. It stays in X, has h-1 edges of length M and meets
all cells. This uses the ACTUAL native convention: B109 p257 expressly
defines a connected family by chains whose consecutive cubes share a
wall, namely a (d-1)-dimensional cube (text lines 426-430). If another
consumer allows corner/edge contact instead, use center-to-contact-to-
center paths; then d(X)<=C_dim(h(X)-1), and replace a by C_dim a in q
below. The lower comparison and every conclusion are unchanged.
For the lower bound, scale M to 1. Traverse a finite tree of
length d twice, in total length at most 2d. Divide this traversal into
arcs of length <=1. Each arc lies in a unit ball about its initial point,
and that ball meets at most a dimension-dependent number of unit cells.
Every cell met by the tree is therefore among at most C_4(1+d) cells.
Take an approximating minimizing sequence if necessary. This also covers
trees meeting several closed cells at a common boundary point.

One MR_i cube has exactly R_i^4 M-cells (with the aligned native
partitions). Hence its tree size is at least c_4 R_i^4-1. It is incorrect
to charge it tree size 1 in the native estimate (1).

3. COMPLETE-CARRIER ENLARGEMENT AND THE INITIAL STRONG VALUE NORM

Let H_X be the complete current carrier used by the chosen source
consumer. Required geometric registration is

  X subset H_X, H_X connected, h(H_X) <= C_H h(X),             (3)

with C_H independent of i,R_i and remaining fine depth. A fixed number
of M-cell stencil/cushion layers satisfies (3). Even a fixed number of
MR_i layers satisfies (3) for these initial sources: X is already a
union of MR_i cubes, and each such cube adds only a fixed number of
neighboring macro-cubes. Thus this particular initial enlargement has
no new factor R_i^4 in the ratio of complete to original size. A new
unbounded number of such layers or an additional unconstrained fine
read is NOT covered by (3).

Suppose the initial coefficient envelope, including explicitly
registered roles, is bounded by

  sum_roles ||F_(X,role)|| <= C R_i^s h(X)^m eta_i e^(-kappa d(X)), (4)

where m,s are fixed. For the actual unmarked bulk R coefficient, m=s=0
and C=1 in (1). For its vacuum-subtracted coefficient, C=2 suffices.
The norms here are VALUE norms on the actual admitted running pair
domain; no field/current/noise Cauchy derivative is taken.

Allow the target complete norm to charge exp[a d(H_X)] and the
protected seed to charge exp[c_seed h(H_X)]. Define

  N_i = sup_v sum_(X:H_X contains v)
          exp[a d(H_X)+c_seed h(H_X)] sum_roles ||F_(X,role)||. (5)

There is a fixed kappa_req(a,c_seed,C_H,m,dimension) such that

  kappa > kappa_req  ==>  N_i <= C' R_i^s eta_i.              (6)

Proof. By (2)-(3), d(X) >= h(H_X)/(C_4 C_H)-1,
d(H_X)<=h(H_X), and h(X)^m<=h(H_X)^m. Therefore the summand in (5) is
bounded by C R_i^s eta_i exp(kappa) h(H_X)^m exp[-q h(H_X)], with

  q = kappa/(C_4 C_H)-a-c_seed.

For a fixed connected complete carrier H there are at most 2^h(H)
subsets X contained in H, so this bounds all multiplicities of the
map X -> H_X without an unrecorded rerooting cost. The number of
connected M-cell animals of size h containing v is at most C_an^h.
Consequently the remaining sum is bounded by

  sum_(h>=1) h^m exp[-(q-log 2-log C_an)h],

finite whenever q>log 2+log C_an. This proves (6), including cases
where the complete carrier contains v but the original X does not.
All constants may depend on the FIXED chosen spatial exponent kappa.
No dependence on R_i or fine depth is introduced.

The native R_i is a rounded L-power of a fixed logarithmic power:
R_i <= C_L (log x_i)^r, x_i=g_i^(-2). Thus a separately registered
factor R_i^s in (4) yields at most a declared polylogarithmic factor in
(6). In particular the actual unmarked bulk catalogue has N_i<=C eta_i.

This is the promised initial complete norm, not an assertion that an
unweighted MR_i source has norm O(1). A seed exp[c R_i^4] is genuinely
present and is paid by the spatial reserve in exp[-kappa d_i(X)].
No sacrifice of the rarity exponent p_0 is needed for this payment.

4. ROOTS, CALIBRATION, AND THE LIMIT OF THE PRIMARY STATEMENT

B119(2.27) gives E^(j)(X,U,z) with an external root z. The R^(j)(X,U)
appearing in (2.30)-(2.31) has no such observable root. This difference
must remain explicit.

For the exact UNMARKED total sum_X R_X one may choose nonnegative
numerical weights w_(X,z), z in the current cells of X, with
sum_z w_(X,z)=1, and set F_(X,z)=w_(X,z)R_X. Then

  sum_z F_(X,z)=R_X,     sum_z ||F_(X,z)||=||R_X||.

This costs 1 in the strong sum of root envelopes. Uniform cell weights
preserve lattice symmetry. Rooting at all current unit sites instead
costs only the fixed number M^4 of sites per M-cell if normalized in
the same way. These are current-scale counts, with no fine-lattice
ultraviolet multiplicity.

However sum_z f(z) w_(X,z) R_X for arbitrary f is a newly prescribed
profiled insertion. It is not automatically the original microscopic
source/profile, its calibrated derivative, or a marked large-field
coefficient. For an ORIGINAL marked source family the identities and
envelope estimate (4), with its real roots/orientations/cavity roles,
must be obtained from that family's actual definition. The unmarked
bound (1) cannot prove such a derivative or label census by itself.
If the original finite roles satisfy a polynomial bound as in (4),
the proof above absorbs the h(X)^m factor in spatial reserve and
retains only the stated polylogarithmic R_i^s factor. If instead a
microscopic label sum is asserted, its native mesh/calibration measure
must be supplied before invoking this argument.
