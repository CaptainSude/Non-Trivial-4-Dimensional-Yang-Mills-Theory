RESULT

The cubic COMPOSITE current identified in
static_split_actual_current_identification_20261022.txt is useful to the
pair Ward estimate. It does not, merely by being small, give an elliptic
equation or a Holder estimate for the first component on all independent-
pair/localizer contours. B109272 explicitly explains this distinction.

This does NOT require rejecting the direct static route. A proper-scale
regular base plus a fixed finite number of compatible, covariantly
C1,alpha responses suffices. CMP102(190) and B116(1.11)--(1.21) provide
precisely useful response norms when their actual background, tower,
frame and scale have been matched. The remaining native task is that
matching on the complete actual split read, not a new elliptic theorem
for every pair in LI(1.64)--(1.69).

1. WHAT THE DOMAIN DOES AND DOES NOT SAY

B109261--263 (1.8)--(1.16) separates the physical current

       J_phys(U)=D_U^* xi^(-2) pi Im partial U

from a new independent variable J. The U clauses bound curvature and
first derivatives in a local gauge; the J clause bounds this independent
variable. The additional U_p,J_p tests refer to separately constructed
local minima. They do not identify J with J_phys(U).

The paragraph on printed263 gives a useful check: sufficiently smaller
constants in the first three conditions imply the derived tests as well.
Consequently those tests are not a hidden Holder condition on raw U.
The same distinction survives the mixed-grade LI spaces. LII(1.65)
certifies membership of its COMPOSITE OLD PAIR in those spaces. That
membership controls the current component, but it is not a field PDE.

A compact domain countertest illustrates the exact missing implication.
In a homogeneous r-scale box choose a fixed Cartan matrix T and, on a
successively finer lattice, take

       A_2(x)=c delta sin(x_1/delta) T,  A_mu=0 otherwise,
       U=exp(i eta A),                 J=0.                (1)

Use wavelengths resolved by the lattice and take c smaller than the
domain constants. Uniformly in delta, |A|<=c delta and |nabla A|<=C c;
the curvature is O(c). Thus the small-constant criterion above admits
the pair, including its derived-minimum tests. But curvature oscillates
by order c across distance O(delta); its alpha-Holder seminorm grows
like c delta^(-alpha). No gauge can remove this Cartan curvature
oscillation. The same calculation with finite differences gives the
lattice example. In born-i coordinates, epsilon=L^{-(r-i)}, it reads

       A_2(x)=c epsilon delta sin(epsilon x_1/delta) T,
       |nabla A|=O(c epsilon^2),
       [nabla A]_alpha ~ c epsilon^(2+alpha) delta^(-alpha).

This disproves a uniform C1,alpha inference from the domain inequalities.
It is NOT a counterexample to the actual Phi image, which has additional
minimum/response structure. Its physical current is not the independent
J=0 in (1), so it also does not contradict regularity for a physical
minimum with a correctly matched current equation.

2. THE PRIMARY CURRENT ALGEBRA EXPLAINS WHY AN ELLIPTIC SHORTCUT FAILS

B109272 (3.11) has, in its own normalized units,

 J_phys(exp(i xi A) U)=J_phys(U)+D_U^* D_U A+F(U,A).       (2)

For the original response H, the Landau condition R D_U^* H=0 allows
replacement of D^*D by D^*D+D R D^*. The paper rewrites this as
Delta_U-P_1 plus a lower local operator. Its canonical independent-pair
substitution (3.13) is therefore

 J_can,out=s^3 J+Delta_U A-P_1 A+F_1(U,A).               (3)

With the same operators and lower terms, subtraction of (2) from (3)
exhibits the missing quantity schematically but exactly at this one
substitution:

 J_can,out-J_phys,out
    =s^3 (J-J_phys,in)+D_U R D_U^* A.                   (4)

Here J_phys,in is in the input current units, and s^3 is exactly the
unit conversion in B109(3.12); all other terms are in output units.
Formula (4) is simply the operator replacement stated immediately
before (3.13), not a newly asserted identity between independent inputs.

Most decisively, B109272 says that later localized terms have the
required covariant-Laplacian bounds, while the corresponding D^*D
bounds need not hold. It gives this as the reason for introducing J.
Thus one cannot use the small canonical J alone to control the physical
Euler current of the localized field. Nor may one assume every H(s)
satisfies the original Landau equation on the complex localization
circles. The same issue can occur at successive nested substitutions.

CMP102297--300 derives its regularity through criticality, the Landau
condition, and the equations (127)--(143). In particular the discussion
after (143) says the regularity equation does not itself imply the
Landau condition used in deriving it. Section F then applies these
facts to actual critical configurations with matching determining data.
This is more information than arbitrary independent-pair admission.

3. A SUFFICIENT FINITE-COMPOSITION LEMMA

Fix 0<alpha<1, a branch of a complete read, and the born-i units. Let
epsilon=L^{-(r-i)}<=1. Work on a common branch frame, including a fixed
read collar, on every parameter value of the actual complex contours.
Suppose a base field admits a potential A_0 with

 |A_0|<=C epsilon,
 |nabla A_0|<=C epsilon^2,
 [nabla A_0]_alpha<=C epsilon^(2+alpha).                 (5)

Suppose the actual field is obtained by m relative exponential factors,
with m bounded independently of scales, each expressed in this same
compatible frame. Assume for every relative potential H_l

 |H_l|<=C_l epsilon,
 |nabla H_l|<=C_l epsilon^2,
 [nabla H_l]_alpha<=C_l epsilon^(2+alpha).               (6)

The logarithms are taken in a single small analytic neighborhood. Then
the final product has a potential A_fin satisfying (5), with a constant
depending on the finite m, alpha, and the stated C_l, but not on r-i or
the fine lattice mesh.

Proof. The matrix product and its small logarithm are analytic functions
of the finitely many potentials. Their first two derivatives are
bounded on that neighborhood. The chain rule gives the first-gradient
bound. Interpolation of the zeroth and first bounds gives
[A_0]_alpha+[H_l]_alpha<=C epsilon^(1+alpha). In the Holder difference
of the first derivative of the analytic product, the direct gradient
differences have size epsilon^(2+alpha); variation of the product's
Jacobian contributes at most epsilon^(1+alpha) epsilon^2. Since
epsilon<=1, this is no larger. The same estimates hold for finite
differences, with their bounded shifted product factors. This proves
the claim. Small xi factors in exp(i xi H_l) only improve the bounds.

An equivalent practical hypothesis replaces (6) by covariant C1,alpha
bounds relative to the ACTUAL intermediate background. Once earlier
factors have (5), conversion to the common frame changes a covariant
gradient by products of a connection of order epsilon and H_l of order
epsilon. Their Holder differences are order epsilon^(2+alpha), by the
same interpolation. This gives (6) inductively. This equivalence requires
the covariant norms to use compatible transports and the same branch
read; it is not an inference from unrelated gauges.

Gauge returns must be handled by their actual covariance identities:
pull every response back to the chosen base frame, retaining its native
bounds there, or prove the required bounds for the composed return
gauge. A gauge-invariant coefficient allows evaluation in a common
representative. It does not imply that separately chosen good gauges
for several factors are compatible or that an arbitrary complex gauge
has bounded conjugation norm. No derivative of an uncontrolled gauge
return is silently discarded in this lemma.

Piecewise application is legitimate on each branch with its complete
collar. The interface assumptions of the separately audited
piecewise_neutral_Ward_interface_20261022.txt still have to be supplied;
this lemma does not turn a branch estimate into a global one.

4. WHAT THE NATIVE RESPONSE RESULTS ALREADY PROVIDE

CMP102308 (190) explicitly includes the functional derivative of H,
its covariant gradient, a Holder difference of that gradient, and its
second-order bounds, with actual local grade factors. Proposition9 on
309 states the analytic extension and the same estimates for admitted
external configurations.

B1163 permits replacement of the propagators by operators having the
same regularity bounds. B1165 (1.11) retains all the cited operator
norms on the complex s circles. B1166 (1.15)--(1.18) carries all
admissible norms through the nonlinear fixed point; (1.21) and the
following paragraph on7 use them to retain the near-response norms
(B1093.31). All these assertions are uniform over the stated input
pair domain. This is a constructive route to (6) and does not require
equating canonical and physical currents of the localized response.

The unresolved matching is specific. On each actual split branch one
must identify the base minimum/tower, its regularity scale on the whole
read, and the backgrounds relative to which the finite nested responses
have the norms just cited. One must also identify the common gauge and
check that its return identities hold on the occupied complex contours.
A generic current-grade domain inclusion does not change an old
determining stratum into a homogeneous proper-r minimum. Conversely,
once this actual base/response match is provided, the composition lemma
above supplies the field estimate without any new physical-current
identity for H(s).

5. SCOPE OF THE CURRENT CONCLUSION

The v19 cubic-current conclusion remains intact. The deficient
inference is ONLY: cubic independent composite J plus pair-domain
membership implies field Holder regularity. No actual Phi counterexample
has been constructed, and no impossibility of the direct static proof
is asserted. A direct native image proof using (5)--(6), or a separately
justified static near re-extension, remains available in principle.

smoothed-core lemma were read as corroborating source pointers. The
