FIXED-q DOMAIN GAIN WITH STRICT LOCALIZATION BY THREE CIRCLES
18 October 2026. Separate research derivation; not part of the reviewed
Notebook43 PDF. One use of a fixed number q of homogeneous charts.

RESULT AND SCOPE

The fixed-q field-domain gain of Notebook42 can be combined with the
strict localization of Notebook43 even when the gauge used to exhibit
the gain has no proved supported expansion. The scalar observable is
bounded on the larger domain using the gauge-adjusted field. On a
smaller concentric domain it equals the ordinary, unadjusted chart
composition, whose strict locality is known. Hadamard's three-circles
theorem interpolates the shell bounds between these two domains.

The result is a strict supported scalar family on an enlarged
background-field ball, with positive exponential diameter decay and
every fixed derivative cost C_{q,k} r^{-k}. Constants are uniform in
preceding UV refinement and compatible volume, for q fixed. This is
a theorem for a composed observable before integration. It does not
bound a normalized conditional, its weights, its background scores,
or an iterated family of quantum descendants.

1. PRECISE INPUTS AND UNITS

Fix the primitive ratio ell, and choose q ONCE so that

  S=ell^q,                 theta=C_0/S<1/4.                  (1)

M_ref denotes preceding homogeneous UV refinement. Distances below
are measured in the FINAL, coarsest unit lattice after these q steps.
The finer retained variables at all q levels are grouped by their
final-scale cells. The number of scalar coordinates in a grouped
cell can depend on q, but not on M_ref or total volume.

Let F_M(w,t) be the exact unrooted retained chart mrg-chart. Compose
the q maps with the correct successive refinement depths and write

  B_q(w;t_0,...,t_{q-1})

for the resulting old-scale field. The already proved mrg-block-gain
gives a background-only holomorphic gauge gamma_q(w), and

  Bhat_q(w;t)=log[(exp(i B_q(w;t)))^{gamma_q(w)}]/i,
  ||Bhat_q(w;t)|| <= theta ||w|| + C_q max_a ||t_a||.       (2)

These maps are holomorphic on fixed q-dependent product balls, with
constants uniform in M_ref and volume. The bound and gauge are those
of Notebook42; no additional locality property of gamma_q is used.

Let R_X(U,J) be an actual gauge-invariant homogeneous source birth,
with |R_X|<=A_X=epsilon exp(-kappa_s d_i(X)) on its independent source
domain. Define f_X(b)=R_X(T(b)), where T is the complete ordinary
physical pair (potential,current) of Notebook43. Choose r comparable
to the source radius so that f_X is holomorphic and bounded by A_X
on ||b||<r. This includes the strict fractional margin needed for
independent source-domain Cauchy bounds. Further require r<=r_q,
where r_q>0 is fixed and ensures every domain below lies inside the
fixed q-chart domains. No constants below deteriorate as r shrinks.

Gauge invariance is used in its precise usual sense: if b and b^v
are sufficiently small ordinary coarse representatives related by
a compatible gauge v, the equivariance/uniqueness of the homogeneous
minimum gives gauge-equivalent complete physical pairs. Therefore
f_X(b^v)=f_X(b) whenever the compared representatives lie in the
source domain. This identity is also holomorphic for the small
complex gauges at issue here, by the ordinary-minimum comparison and
analytic continuation from real fields/gauges. Only this overlap
identity, not an extension of f_X to arbitrary large raw fields, is
used below.

The raw finite composition has the following locality consequence
of the supported maps already used in Notebook43. For q fixed there
are K_q<infinity and kappa_q>0 such that the complete pair

  (w,t) -> T(B_q(w;t))                                  (3)

has strict supported families on a fixed product ball, with complete
physical output norm and exponential spatial weight in FINAL units.
In particular, if two joint inputs agree on the n-neighborhood of
the projected source collar, and their norms are at most a, then
the difference of (3) on that collar is at most

  K_q a exp(-kappa_q n).                                (4)

Here and below an enlarged K_q covers finitely many collar cells.
To verify (3)--(4), compose the supported stationary prefix maps,
the actual finite-range retained pivot charts, and the finite-range
rooting/unrooting maps, then the supported complete physical pair T.
There are only q compositions. Use the complete-output Banach norms
before summing supports; the fixed-q regrouping and the strict support
gluing inequality give some positive kappa_q and finite K_q. Taking
the maximum over outputs in the source collar gives (4), without a
factor counting its fine coordinates. All scale factors in this
argument are fixed at q. No claim is made that kappa_q equals S
times a single-step exponent.

2. A LARGE BOUNDED DOMAIN AND A SMALL LOCAL DOMAIN

Use the balanced joint product domain

  Q_r={||w||<2r,  max_a ||t_a||<r/(4 C_q)}.               (5)

If necessary enlarge C_q in (2), and decrease r_q. Equation (2)
then gives ||Bhat_q||<3r/4 on Q_r. Thus the scalar

  Phi_X(W)=f_X(Bhat_q(W))-f_X(0),   W=(w,t),              (6)

is holomorphic on Q_r and bounded by 2A_X there.

The raw composition and its complete pair vanish at W=0 and have
uniform fixed-q analytic Lipschitz bounds on a fixed ball. Consequently
there is eta_q in (0,1/2), independent of r,M_ref,X and volume, such
that on eta_q Q_r both B_q and Bhat_q, as well as the relevant source
physical pairs, lie in a strict fraction of their source balls.
For clarity eta_q is chosen after all fixed-q chart constants and
source margins, and is held fixed as r decreases. On eta_q Q_r,
gauge invariance gives the exact identity

  Phi_X(W)=R_X(T(B_q(W)))-R_X(0).                        (7)

The right side supplies locality ONLY on this smaller domain. On
Q_r itself the definition is (6); the raw field need not be in the
birth domain.

Let x_X be a deterministic birth anchor. Project the source collar
to the final lattice, and choose a radius D'_X enclosing it about
the projected anchor, with

  D'_X <= C D_X/S + C_q,    D_X<=C mathfrak M(1+d_i(X)). (8)

Let H_{X,n} be the final graph ball of radius D'_X+n. P_H sets ALL
joint coordinate species outside H to zero, including each retained
noise level. It preserves every scaled domain sigma Q_r. Define

  phi_{X,0}(W)=Phi_X(P_{H_{X,0}}W),
  phi_{X,n}(W)=Phi_X(P_{H_{X,n}}W)
                -Phi_X(P_{H_{X,n-1}}W), n>=1.           (9)

Each piece is strictly supported in H_{X,n}, holomorphic on Q_r,
and bounded there by 2A_X. (The vacuum constant cancels for n>=1.)
On a compatible finite torus the series telescopes exactly once
the balls cover the torus.

On eta_q Q_r, insert (7) in both terms of (9). The source independent
product ball gives ||DR_X||<=C A_X/r. Equations (3)--(4) give a
complete-pair difference at most K_q r exp(-kappa_q n), and the
straight segment between the pairs lies in the same smaller convex
product ball. Hence, including n=0 by increasing a constant,

  ||phi_{X,n}||_{eta_q Q_r}
                <= C_q A_X exp(-kappa_q n).             (10)

No support estimate for the comparison gauge was invoked.

3. THREE-CIRCLES TRANSFER AND FIXED JETS

LEMMA. If h is holomorphic on a balanced product domain Q, with
sup_Q|h|<=A and sup_{eta Q}|h|<=B, then for eta<sigma<1,

  sup_{sigma Q}|h| <= B^lambda A^{1-lambda},
  lambda=log(1/sigma)/log(1/eta).                         (11)

Proof. For V in Q, apply Hadamard's three-circles inequality to the
one-variable function z -> h(zV) at radii eta,sigma,1, first using
strictly interior radii if necessary. Then take the supremum over
V and the increasing limit to the boundary radii. This proof works
for every finite regulator with constants independent of dimension.

Apply (11) with sigma=7/8 and eta=eta_q. Equations (9)--(10) give

  ||phi_{X,n}||_{(7/8)Q_r}
      <= C'_q A_X exp(-lambda_q kappa_q n),
  lambda_q=log(8/7)/log(1/eta_q)>0.                      (12)

Take the final domain (3/4)Q_r. Its background radius is 3r/2,
strictly larger than the original radius r. Every coordinate has a
margin at least c_q r between this domain and (7/8)Q_r. Scalar Cauchy
estimates therefore give, for every fixed ordered coordinate tuple
alpha of length k, repetitions allowed,

  ||partial_alpha phi_{X,n}||_{(3/4)Q_r}
     <= C_{q,k} A_X r^{-k} exp(-lambda_q kappa_q n).       (13)

The derivative vanishes unless all its coordinate slots belong to
H_{X,n}. There is no all-degree multivariable Taylor sum. These
bounds prove absolute convergence of the shell family, uniformly
in compatible volume and preceding refinement.

4. ROOTED DIAMETER NORM AND THE ACTUAL RESCALING COST

Assume the same sufficiently strong source exponent kappa_s as in
Notebook43, and sum over actual source births X with their amplitudes
A_X. For every fixed p,k there is mu_q>0 such that

 sup_z sum_{X,n:H_{X,n} contains z}
  (1+|H_{X,n}|)^p exp(mu_q diam H_{X,n})
  sum_{alpha in H_{X,n}^k}
       ||partial_alpha phi_{X,n}||_{(3/4)Q_r}
                       <= C_{q,p,k} epsilon r^{-k}.      (14)

Proof. Repeat the rooted shell summation of mlc-conversion, now with
(12)--(13). Each grouped cell has a fixed q-dependent coordinate
count. The support and derivative sums cost only fixed powers of
1+D'_X+n. Choose mu_q and delta_q>0 sufficiently small compared to
lambda_q kappa_q and to the source exponent in (8). Insert
1_{z in H}<=exp[-delta_q dist(z,x'_X)]
             exp[delta_q(D'_X+n)],
and spend a second small exponential to pay the fixed polynomials.
The n-sum is geometric. Equation (8) and the original source tree
bound pay the D'_X and source-shape sums. Finally sum the anchors.

There are O(S^4) OLD birth anchors above one final cell in four
dimensions. This multiplicity is INCLUDED in C_{q,p,k}; it is not
set equal to one. At q fixed it is a fixed finite cost. Uniformity
in M_ref concerns earlier UV refinement, and does not remove this
age factor. No canonical h^4 cancellation has been used. This
proves (14).

The centered scalar Phi_X(w;t)-Phi_X(w;0) has the same construction,
with a further fixed factor. All norms remain joint-field sup norms
before any retained-variable integration. One may apply finite-order
Taylor identities in t on a smaller domain, but actual retained
supports must lie in it and their moment/score bounds are separate.

5. WHAT SPATIAL RESCALING DOES AND DOES NOT GIVE

Lengths in an inherited old-scale support shrink by S when expressed
in final units. Thus an inherited decay exp(-kappa_in d_old), by
itself, corresponds to exp(-S kappa_in d_final), up to fixed collars.
But the newest classical minimizer and constraint lift generate tails
on the final physical scale. Their locality exponent is controlled
by (3), here denoted kappa_q. It is not multiplied by S just because
the original source support was old.

Accordingly, if a composable weighted analytic norm has separately
established an inherited rate kappa_in and a raw chart rate kappa_q,
the candidate rate after this interpolation is bounded by

       lambda_q min{S kappa_in, kappa_q},                (15)

with any additional fixed reserve loss from that composition proof.
Equation (15) is a diagnostic ceiling, not a theorem of repeated
norm propagation. The proven result here is (14) for one source
birth and one fixed-q composition. The newest-map locality ceiling
prevents concluding exponent replenishment from S lambda_q alone.

For repeated startup, one must prove a composable weighted analytic
norm estimate, retain the multiplicity of old anchors in every
current cell, and establish quantitative constants for the relevant
family of compositions. The shell pieces are generally not separately
gauge invariant, so their gauge adjustment cannot simply be repeated
piece by piece. Applying this argument anew to a complete scalar
requires its larger-domain boundedness and small-domain locality;
those are not consequences of a finite set of real jets alone.

Even a successful repeated observable estimate would leave the actual
normalized conditional weights, their two background responses, and
the stability of the full generated state to be controlled. Thus no
exp(C log^2 gamma) startup cost, no full marked-history bound, and no
four-dimensional existence result is inferred here.

ROOT REVIEW
The one-variable three-circles interpolation, fixed derivative margin,
shell telescope and fixed-q anchor counting were checked against the
displayed inputs. The conclusion is accepted only in the stated one-use,
composed-observable scope. The newest-lift locality ceiling, non-invariant
individual shell pieces and lack of normalized-weight estimates prevent
promoting it to repeated startup or actual descendant propagation.
