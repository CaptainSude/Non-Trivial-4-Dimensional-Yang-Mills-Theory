INPUT. S_n are translation-invariant, symmetric, real continuous
functionals on the Schwartz subspace flat on ALL full four-dimensional
collision diagonals, with a finite-order Schwartz bound for each n.
They arise as limits of the actual full centered Wilson moments on
compact tests off those diagonals. For each stated mirror e, finite
lattice RP passes to those tests. Diagonal RP is an input that must
have been established at the lattice mirrors; it is not inferred here
from coordinate RP. Put S_0=1.

1. USE THE FULL POSITIVE-HALFSPACE VECTOR DOMAIN

Let D_e be the finite sequences F=(F_0,...,F_N), with each F_n smooth
compactly supported where all e.x_i>0 and x_i!=x_j. No ordering of
e.x_i and no separation of the two-dimensional planar projections
is imposed. Define

 (F,G)_e=sum_nm S_{n+m}(theta_e conjugate/reverse(F_n) tensor G_m).

Each reflected tensor is off full collisions: internally by its own
support, and across the two groups by the strict halfspace margin.
The finite-lattice polynomial RP limit therefore gives (F,F)_e>=0.
The same local tensor approximation used for separated tests applies
on these compact off-collision supports. Complete D_e modulo its
null space to obtain H_e. Vacuum Omega=(1,0,...) has norm1. Thus H_e
is nonzero, but a nonvacuum state is NOT guaranteed by these inputs.

D_e is a vector domain, not an ordinary polynomial algebra. Multiplying
two internally collision-flat test tensors can create cross-collisions
within the resulting positive-halfspace word. Even F_1 tensor F_1 is
not collision-flat if F_1 is nonzero. Products are available when their
complete supports are disjoint, giving a partial test algebra. No
Euclidean E[|F|^2] for a composite F is used in this construction.

Cutoffs at infinity, at the halfspace boundary and at FULL collision
diagonals approximate the corresponding flat Schwartz positive test
space. This does NOT authorize cutting out equal-time or equal-planar-
projection hypersurfaces. They contain many points off full collisions;
a function nonzero there cannot be approximated in Schwartz topology
by functions vanishing on neighborhoods of those hypersurfaces.

2. TRANSLATIONS AND CONTRACTIVITY FROM POLYNOMIAL GROWTH

For a perpendicular to e, common translation U_e(a) of every argument
preserves D_e. Translation invariance of S gives an isometry, with
inverse U_e(-a), hence a strongly continuous unitary group on H_e.
For t>=0, common translation T_e(t) by t e preserves D_e and obeys

 (T_e(t)F,G)_e=(F,T_e(t)G)_e,
 ||T_e(t)F||_e^2=(F,T_e(2t)F)_e.                       (2.1)

Cauchy--Schwarz for the positive form gives

 ||T_e(t)F||_e <= ||F||_e^(1-2^-m)
                    ||T_e(2^m t)F||_e^(2^-m).       (2.2)

For fixed F the defining reflected tensors remain collision-flat at
all t>=0. Their Schwartz seminorms grow at most polynomially in t,
so ||T_e(s)F||_e<=C_F(1+s)^M_F. Taking m->infinity in(2.2) proves
||T_e(t)F||_e<=||F||_e. If F is null, already the first inequality
in(2.1) shows T_e(t)F is null. Thus T_e descends to a contraction.
Continuity of translated tests proves strong continuity at zero.
Its symmetry gives a nonnegative selfadjoint generator H_e:
T_e(t)=exp(-tH_e). This elementary construction does not invoke the
uncorrected OS-I reconstruction theorem or full Euclidean rotations.

Spatial translations commute with T_e on the test domain and hence
on H_e. Their selfadjoint generators P_e strongly commute with H_e,
so the joint spectral measure of (H_e,P_e) exists, with H_e>=0.
No spectral inequality relating H_e and P_e follows yet.

3. AN ACUTE-ANGLE COMMON DOMAIN IS DENSE, WITHOUT EXTRA CUTOFFS

Take normals e,n with e.n>0 and set D_en=D_e intersect D_n as literal
test sequences. For every F in D_e there exists t_F such that
T_e(t)F is in D_en for all t>=t_F: all supports are compact and
n.(x+t e) increases strictly with t. Suppose v in H_e is orthogonal
to the image of D_en. Then

 z -> (v,exp(-z H_e)[F])_e

is holomorphic on Re z>0 and vanishes on the real interval t>t_F.
The identity theorem makes it zero everywhere in that half-plane;
strong continuity at t=0 gives (v,[F])_e=0. Since D_e defines H_e,
v=0. Hence D_en is dense in H_e. Interchanging e,n proves density
in H_n as well. This is a Hilbert-norm argument, not a claimed
Schwartz density of a geometrically restricted subspace.

More generally, if e.n_l>0 for a FINITE set of normals, their common
positive-halfspace domain is dense in H_e by the same proof. Thus
for e=e_0 and n_+=(e_0+e_1)/sqrt2, n_-=(e_0-e_1)/sqrt2, one common
domain is dense in H_e0 and lies in both diagonal positive domains.
Pairwise D_e0,n+ and D_e0,n- are each dense also in the corresponding
diagonal Hilbert space. Density of the triple intersection in a
diagonal Hilbert space is NOT asserted: n+.n-=0, so the stated proof
does not apply. Nor does it apply to arbitrary orthogonal normals.

4. OPERATOR-CORE STRENGTHENING IN THE COORDINATE HILBERT SPACE

Test translation derivatives keep D_e inside itself, so D_e consists
of smooth vectors for H_e and all spatial generators. It is a joint
core for A_mk=(1+H_e)^m(1+|P_e|^2)^k. One direct proof uses their
commuting resolvent powers. These are integrals of T_e(t) and spatial
unitaries with integrable scalar kernels. Applied to a vector of D_e,
truncate the integrals and approximate them by finite sums of such
translates, which remain in D_e. Differentiating up to the indicated
fixed orders gives convergence in the A_mk graph norm. The inverse
A_mk^-1 maps the dense set D_e densely onto Dom(A_mk) in graph norm,
which proves the core assertion. Equivalently, make the truncation
first for a fixed smooth F so all required derivatives are bounded.

The acute common domain is a core in the SAME graph norm. If v is
orthogonal to it in Dom(A_mk) with inner product
(F,G)_graph=(F,G)+(A_mk F,A_mk G), then for F in D_e the function
(v,T_e(z)F)_graph is holomorphic on Re z>0, because A_mk commutes
with T_e and T_e is holomorphic in that graph space. It vanishes
for all sufficiently large real z, hence for all positive z. Taking
z down to zero and using the preceding D_e core property gives v=0.
Thus common positive-halfspace tests form a common core for every
fixed polynomial in the coordinate generators H_e,P_e. This also
applies to the finite diagonal-normal intersection in H_e0.

The literal common test domain is not invariant under EVERY spatial
translation: such a translation may leave a diagonal positive halfspace.
The coordinate representation itself is translation invariant. If an
invariant operator core is wanted, the joint compact-spectral-support
vectors give one, but their representation as literal finite test
polynomials is not claimed. To transfer an inequality proved on the
literal common core to a closed coordinate operator, graph density is
the relevant property, not a fictitious invariant test algebra.

5. WHAT STILL MUST BE SHOWN FOR A SPECTRAL CONE

The identity on D_en does not define an isometry between H_e and H_n.
Their reflection forms differ, even in a fully Euclidean theory.
Their null spaces need not agree from the inputs stated here. Thus
one cannot identify H_n with a linear combination of H_e and P_e
merely because the test-function domain is shared and dense.

The proven core is sufficient for a separately derived identity or
closed quadratic-form inequality involving the COORDINATE operators,
for example H_e+/-P_1>=0 on D_e0,n+/-. Once such an actual identity
or inequality is proved, the core result extends it by closure and
the joint spectral theorem gives the corresponding cone. Diagonal
reflection positivity by itself has not been converted into that
coordinate-generator inequality by the present argument. That
analytic/inter-frame comparison is the remaining step assigned to
the spectral-cone route, not a hidden common-core hypothesis.

Finally, nontriviality of the excited Hilbert space is separate.
The sequence S_0=1, S_n=0 for n>=1 obeys every estimate and positivity
used here but produces only the vacuum. A lower bound for an actual
reflection norm or equivalent nonzero limiting correlation remains
necessary; no amount of dense-core bookkeeping supplies it.

6. THE DIRECT TWO-SPECTRAL-MEASURE COMPARISON CLOSES THE PLANAR CONE

This section supplies the comparison left open in Section5. It is a
literal adaptation of YM_Rotation_Invariance_simplified, Section3.3,
PDF pages5--6, to the full-collision test-vector spaces above. The
ordinary Euclidean L2 bound is unnecessary: finite diagonal RP norms
of two specific test polynomials suffice. No identification of the two
Hilbert spaces, no common Hilbert core, and no point-field product is
used. The inputs remain those stated at the beginning, including RP
and invariance at the diagonal mirror t+x=0.

Let e=e0 and n=(e0+e1)/sqrt2. Take any finite polynomial test sequence
A in D_e. For a sufficiently large L all arguments in every component
of A lie in B={0<t<L, |x|<L}; the other coordinates remain in a compact
set. Let R_0,R_d be the LINEAR pullbacks of the coordinate and diagonal
reflections, and J_0,J_d their compositions with complex conjugation.
Reversal of word order can be included as usual and is immaterial by
permutation symmetry. Put b=L(e0+e1)/2 and

 A1=R_d tau_-b R_0 A,       A2=tau_(2Le0-b) A.

Before the final R_d, the t+x coordinate in the first expression is
-t+x-L<0; in A2 it is t+x+L>0. Hence A1,A2 lie in D_n. Reflection and
translation preserve all internal full-collision exclusions. Their
diagonal reflection norms are finite, with no uniform bound in A
required.

For a,c>=0 define the SAME distributional pairing

 G(a,c)=S[(J_0 A) tau_(2Le0+a(e0+e1)+c(e0-e1)) A].    (6.1)

Every tensor in this finite polynomial pairing is admissible: the
two groups are strictly separated in coordinate time, while each
group is already off its own full collisions. The coordinate joint
spectral measure in [A], denoted rho>=0, gives

 G(a,c)=integral exp(-2L omega) exp(-a z-c conjugate(z)) d rho,
                    z=omega-i p, omega>=0.           (6.2)

Translate the complete test tensor in (6.1) by -b. Since
J_d A1=tau_-b J_0 A, this is precisely the diagonal pairing of A1 with
tau_(sqrt2 a n+sqrt2 c n_perp) A2, where n_perp=(e0-e1)/sqrt2.
The diagonal spectral representation therefore gives

 G(a,c)=integral exp(-a omega'+i c p') d rho', omega'>=0. (6.3)

The factors sqrt2 are absorbed into omega',p'. The mixed spectral
measure satisfies

             ||rho'||TV <= ||A1||_n ||A2||_n=:C_A<infinity. (6.4)

Indeed, sum the absolute matrix elements of the mutually orthogonal
joint spectral projections over a finite partition and apply Cauchy--
Schwarz. This is the only bound on the second Hilbert space needed.

For h in L1(0,infinity), let F(z)=integral_0^infinity h(a)e^-az da.
Integrate (6.2)--(6.3) against h(a)conjugate(h(c)). Finite measure and
L1 norms justify Fubini. Positivity of rho and (6.4) yield

 integral exp(-2L omega)|F(omega-i p)|^2 d rho
       <= C_A sup_(r>=0)|F(r)| sup_(s real)|F(i s)|.   (6.5)

For epsilon=+1 or -1 and j>=1 use the exact original probes

 F_j(z)=exp(-j) z^(1-4i epsilon j/pi)(1+z)^-3,
 F_j(0)=0, principal powers on Re z>0.                (6.6)

The first factor exp(-j) is a scalar, NOT exp(-j z). To check these
are L1 Laplace transforms, write nu=1-4i epsilon j/pi and
z^nu(1+z)^-3=[1-(1+z)^-1]^nu (1+z)^-(3-nu).
The first factor is the transform of the finite complex measure

 delta_0+sum_(k>=1)(-1)^k binom(nu,k)
                         a^(k-1)e^-a/(k-1)! da;

its coefficients are O_nu(k^-2). The second is the transform of
a^(2-nu)e^-a/Gamma(3-nu), an L1 function. Their convolution, times
exp(-j), is h_j. Integrability is needed for each j separately, not
uniformly. Continuity extends the transform identity to Re z=0.

For Re z>=0, |z||1+z|^-3<=1. Consequently the two suprema on the
right of (6.5) are at most e^-j and e^j, respectively. Their product
is at most1. On epsilon arg z>=pi/4+eta, eta>0,

 |F_j(z)|^2 >= exp(8 eta j/pi)|z|^2|1+z|^-6.

Equation(6.5) implies that the rho integral over this sector of the
strictly positive weight e^-2Lomega |z|^2|1+z|^-6 is at most
C_A exp(-8 eta j/pi). Sending j to infinity, then taking both signs
and a countable eta down to0, excludes exactly |p|>omega. Thus the
coordinate spectral projection of {omega<|p|} annihilates [A]. Every
A in the defining compact D_e admits such an L, and their images are
dense by construction. The projection therefore vanishes identically:

                         H_e0 >= |P_1|.             (6.7)

This is the PLANAR spectral cone needed for the (e0,e1) rotation
argument. Repeating in other available coordinate planes gives
H>=|P_i| separately. These separate inequalities alone do NOT imply
H>=sqrt(P_1^2+P_2^2+P_3^2); the full Euclidean momentum cone needs the
corresponding additional directions or an established spatial rotation
symmetry. The planar operator extension is already valid:
exp(-z_t H+i z_x P_1) is contractive when Re z_t>=|Im z_x| and is
operator-norm holomorphic on the strict inequality. This follows
directly from the joint spectral theorem and (6.7).
