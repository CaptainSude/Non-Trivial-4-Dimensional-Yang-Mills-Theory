7. WHY TWO-JET PROFILES ALONE DO NOT GIVE SMALL TWO-JET ANNULI

There is a second, distinct warning about a proposed rough annular
localization. Put a near coordinate x at the source root and a far
coordinate y at distance D. Let psi be a fixed bounded smooth function
with psi(0)=0, psi(1)=1 and bounded first two derivatives. Define

 epsilon_D=exp(-kappa D),
 F_D(x,y)=epsilon_D^2 cos(x/epsilon_D) psi(y).             (11)

Its far first derivative and far/far second derivative are O(epsilon_D^2).
Its near/far second derivative is O(epsilon_D). Its near first and
near/near second derivatives are O(epsilon_D) and O(1), respectively.
Thus it satisfies the usual source-rooted first/two-jet spatial
profiles with constants independent of D. Its value is even smaller,
O(epsilon_D^2). But replacing the held far coordinate y=1 by y=0
changes the near/near Hessian by -cos(x/epsilon_D), of order1.
Consequently an exponentially small annular difference in VALUE or
first derivative need not have an exponentially small C^{1,1} norm.

Replacing cos by 1-cos also gives |F_D|<=C x^2 while keeping this
failure. This is a finite coordinate diagnostic of the claimed norm
implication, not a native YM counterexample. The Hessian's change
under a distant field perturbation would require a third derivative
or a separate Hessian-modulus/locality estimate; it is not supplied
by a two-root bound at each fixed field.

This explains the usefulness of the stronger analytic-projection
route. The new local_full_projection_spatial_profile theorem gives
