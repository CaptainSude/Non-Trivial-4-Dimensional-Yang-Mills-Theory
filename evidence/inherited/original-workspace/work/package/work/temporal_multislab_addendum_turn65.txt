RESULT

The single-slab restriction in temporal_quadratic_increment_turn65.txt
can be removed for its real exponential sources by a different
operator argument. The frozen single-slab producer is not edited.

In the SAME original finite periodic SU2 Wilson model and quaternion
normalization, let j=0,...,N-1 label all temporal slabs. Let

 Delta_(j,e)=u_(j,e)-(u_(j+1,e))^(h_j)

be the quaternion difference after alignment by the ACTUAL temporal
links h_j on that slab. The last time index is periodic. For any
deterministic real vectors b_(j,e) in R^4,

 E_W exp[sum_(j,e) b_(j,e).Delta_(j,e)]
       <= exp[(1/2)sum_(j,e)|b_(j,e)|^2/beta_e].       (0.1)

The complete original partition function is in the denominator.
There is no factor depending additionally on N or on the volume.
The spatial action and temporal Wilson coefficients must be
time independent, as in the preceding theorem. Sources may vary
arbitrarily with j and e, and may vanish on any subset.

A finite-family corollary in Section5 allows a field-dependent
selection from a proved finite deterministic source family, at the
explicit logarithmic cardinality cost. This does not identify the
actual nonlinear flow Jacobian with such a family.

1. SETTING AND THE EXACT GAUSSIAN FEATURE MAP

Use precisely Section1 of temporal_quadratic_increment_turn65.txt:
normalized SU2 and site Haar measures, finite ordinary periodic
spatial graph, N>=4, real bounded gauge-invariant V_sp independent
of time, M=exp[-V_sp/2], and beta_e>0 independent of time.
All the statements below include the ordinary orientation-dependent
Wilson action.

Write X=(S^3)^E in its quaternion embedding in R^(4|E|), and let
dmu be the product of normalized slice Haar measures. The electric
kernel is

 Q(u,v)=product_e c(beta_e)^-1
                exp[-beta_e|u_e-v_e|^2/2],
 c(beta)=2 exp[-beta]I_1(beta)/beta.                   (1.1)

On the ambient Fourier space define the positive finite measure

 dnu(xi)=product_e [
   c(beta_e)^-1 (2 pi beta_e)^-2
   exp[-|xi_e|^2/(2 beta_e)] d^4xi_e ].

Let F:L2(X,dmu)->L2(dnu) be the integral operator

 (F f)(xi)=int_X exp[-i sum_e xi_e.u_e]f(u)dmu(u).

It is Hilbert--Schmidt because nu has finite total mass and the
kernel has modulus1. Fourier inversion gives the EXACT identity

 F* F=Q.                                             (1.2)

In particular all c(beta_e) factors in the normalized Wilson
kernel are retained in nu. There is no approximation by an ambient
Gaussian probability law on the link variables.

For b_j=(b_(j,e)), put

 B_j=sum_e |b_(j,e)|^2/beta_e,
 (U_j f)(xi)=exp[-i sum_e b_(j,e).xi_e/beta_e] f(xi).

U_j is unitary. Completing the square in the ambient Gaussian
Fourier integral proves

 Q_(exp,j)(u,v)
   :=Q(u,v)exp[sum_e b_(j,e).(u_e-v_e)]
    =exp[B_j/2](F* U_j F)(u,v).                       (1.3)

The marked operator Q_(exp,j) generally is neither self-adjoint
nor gauge invariant. Its kernel is positive for real b_j. Only
the unitary factorization(1.3), not a Loewner order for this
nonsymmetric operator, will be used.

2. ALL THE GAUGE PROJECTIONS IN THE ACTUAL SOURCED TRACE

Let P be the orthogonal spatial gauge projection. M and Q commute
with P, but Q_(exp,j) need not. The unmarked full-space transfer
operator is

 T=M Q P M=P T P.

It restricts to the actual physical transfer operator. The
original partition function is

 Z_W=[product_e c(beta_e)]^N Tr_phys(T^N).             (2.1)

Integrating the temporal links on slab j gives the exact marked
operator

 K_j=M Q_(exp,j) P M=C_j P,
 C_j=M Q_(exp,j) M.                                  (2.2)

This follows by writing its kernel as the integral over h_j of
Q_(exp,j)(U_j,U_(j+1)^(h_j)), with the same half spatial factors
as in the unmarked Wilson trace. In particular the source uses
the actual aligned difference on that slab, not an independently
chosen pair of current gauge representatives.

The sourced partition numerator is

 [product_e c(beta_e)]^N Tr(K_0...K_(N-1)).

Here multiple noninvariant insertions cause no lost projection:
K_j P=K_j. In the decomposition H=P H plus (I-P)H, each K_j has
block form

 [ a_j  0 ]
 [ c_j  0 ].

Consequently

 Tr(K_0...K_(N-1))
     =Tr_phys[(P C_0 P)...(P C_(N-1) P)].              (2.3)

All products are trace class at finite volume. The block identity
can equivalently be obtained by cyclicity and insertion of the
already present P's. It does not assert source commutation.
No extra gauge-volume factor is introduced by these normalized
orthogonal projections.

3. SCHATTEN HOLDER AND EXACT DENOMINATOR CANCELLATION

Set A=F M P, as an operator from the full slice space into the
Fourier space, or restrict its domain to the physical subspace.
Then

 A* A=P M Q M P=T,
 P C_j P=exp[B_j/2] A* U_j A.                        (3.1)

For the Schatten norm with exponent N, Holder gives

 ||A* U_j A||_N
   <=||A*||_(2N) ||U_j A||_(2N)
   = ||A||_(2N)^2
   =(Tr_phys T^N)^(1/N).                             (3.2)

The equality uses unitarity of U_j. A is Hilbert--Schmidt, so
all these norms are finite. No positivity of A*U_jA, no
commutation between marked factors, and no monotonicity of
operator powers is being assumed.

Applying the N-factor Schatten trace inequality to(2.3) yields

 |Tr(K_0...K_(N-1))|
   <=exp[(1/2)sum_j B_j] Tr_phys(T^N).                (3.3)

For real b_j the left trace is the nonnegative actual source
integral. Divide by(2.1), cancelling the same factors c(beta_e)
and the exact physical trace. This proves(0.1).

The proof uses a common M and common unmarked Q for all slabs.
It does not give the same normalized ratio for arbitrary
time-dependent spatial actions by simply applying Holder to
different unmarked operators.

The single-slab characteristic-function LOWER bound in the frozen
producer does not follow on multiple slabs from this norm
argument. No such multi-slab lower bound is claimed.

4. CONSEQUENCES AND SOURCE SCOPE

For

 Z_b=sum_(j,e) b_(j,e).Delta_(j,e),
 sigma_b^2=sum_(j,e)|b_(j,e)|^2/beta_e,

the bound holds with b replaced by t b for every real t. Since
all variables are bounded at finite volume, comparison at0
gives

 E Z_b=0,
 E Z_b^2<=sigma_b^2,
 Pr(|Z_b|>=r)<=2 exp[-r^2/(2 sigma_b^2)]               (4.1)

for sigma_b>0. When sigma_b=0, Z_b=0. The second derivative
comparison is valid because the constant and linear terms agree;
no higher mixed moment coefficient is inferred from the
moment-generating inequality.

For deterministic matrices A_(j,e):R^4->R^r, applying(4.1) to
their rows gives

 E |sum_(j,e) A_(j,e)Delta_(j,e)|^2
       <=sum_(j,e)||A_(j,e)||_HS^2/beta_e.             (4.2)

The sources may be chosen as deterministic functions of lattice
spacing, coupling, or the finite geometry. They must be fixed
before drawing the gauge field. In particular they are not the
random derivative of a nonlinear spatially flowed field.

For any fixed finite source support, these estimates pass to a
fixed-spacing local weak limit of the periodic Wilson laws:
the source is a bounded continuous function of finitely many
spatial and temporal original links. This does not supply a

The ambient increments and sources are generally gauge variant;

5. A FINITE DETERMINISTIC FAMILY CAN HANDLE A RANDOM SELECTION

Let b^(r), r=1,...,L, be a specified finite family of deterministic
source arrays, each with sigma_(b^(r))^2<=sigma^2. Their random
variables Z_r may be arbitrarily correlated. Equation(4.1) and
the union bound imply

 Pr(max_(1<=r<=L)|Z_r|^2>u)
   <=min[1,2L exp[-u/(2 sigma^2)]].

Integrating this bound at the splitting point
u0=2 sigma^2 log(2L) gives

 E max_r |Z_r|^2
        <=2 sigma^2[log(2L)+1].                      (5.1)

It follows for ANY measurable field-dependent selection r(omega)
that

 E |Z_(r(omega))|^2
        <=2 sigma^2[log(2L)+1].                      (5.2)

No independence between the selection and the increments is
required. The same conclusion applies to a random linear
combination sum_r alpha_r(omega)Z_r if
sum_r |alpha_r(omega)|<=1 pointwise.

Suppose a real bounded physical slice observable satisfies the
ACTUAL pathwise estimate

 |F_1-F_0|<=max_r |Z_r|+|R|                           (5.3)

under the original Wilson law, with an explicitly controlled
remainder R. Then (a+b)^2<=2a^2+2b^2 proves

 d_N(F):=(1/2)E(F_1-F_0)^2
       <=2 sigma^2[log(2L)+1]+E|R|^2.                (5.4)

This is a concrete consumer of the original-law source theorem,
not an assumed conditional Gaussian law for random derivatives.

For example, IF a magnetic observable admitted(5.3) with
sigma^2<=C a/beta, log(2L)<=C' beta, and E|R|^2<=C'' a,
then(5.4) would prove d_N(F)=O(a), with physical parameters fixed.
Such a finite deterministic description of the actual nonlinear
