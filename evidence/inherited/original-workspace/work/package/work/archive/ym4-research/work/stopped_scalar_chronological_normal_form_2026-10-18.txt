On a finite torus these are literal finite sums, or absolutely

 exp[sum_(alpha in E_l) A_(l,alpha)]

exp[-D_l(H_l)] is a genuinely history-dependent boundary factor,

Thus induction over a finite sequence of operations gives

       full sourced density = exp[sum_l V_l] times

Equation(4) is valid before taking the terminal integral, and for the
complete nilpotent density germ. Products of scalar coefficients

It is not merely an identity for one derivative or a logarithm.

3. WHY RETROACTIVE HISTORICAL MASKS PRODUCE A FALSE COST

Suppose a later R operation at date p changes Lambda_l for l<p.
One may rewrite the endpoint action using this new domain, but its
normalization/boundary correction must then reproduce the difference
between the old and new scalar masks. It is incorrect to replace
D_l(H_l) in (3) by a fresh deficit made from Lambda_l at date p
while omitting that compensator. Such a replacement can introduce

The chronological form avoids this rewriting altogether. Opened
histories already retain their old integration/cut information.

This does NOT erase a scalar which was branch-dependent when born.
Such a scalar is precisely D_l or an already present boundary term,
and must be estimated with that birth's actual component census.

4. ADVERSARIAL TWO-BRANCH TESTS

Let a,b be two unmarked branch weights and eta^2=0.

(a) A scalar v born BEFORE the split gives
       a(1+eta v)+b(1+eta v)=(a+b)(1+eta v).
Its later assignment only to the regular branch would incorrectly
give a(1+eta v)+b. The missing eta b v is exactly the omitted
retroactive compensation. There is no legitimate new deficit here.

(b) A scalar v born ONLY on the regular branch gives
       a(1+eta v)+b
          =(1+eta v)[a+b(1-eta v)].                  (5)
The -v on the bad branch is real and cannot be dropped. It is dated
at this birth, not at a future merger. An arbitrary linear map applied
to the two-component vector preserves (5), even when it mixes branches.

(c) With two copy labels use v=a1 eta1+a2 eta2+a12 eta1eta2.
Then exp(v) has mixed coefficient a12+a1a2. Formula(3) retains this
product and its inverse counterpart automatically. Replacing exp(v)
by 1+v before complete-copy regrouping would be false.

These tests distinguish global and branch constants and show why
linearity is sufficient for chronology, but not for discarding D_l.
