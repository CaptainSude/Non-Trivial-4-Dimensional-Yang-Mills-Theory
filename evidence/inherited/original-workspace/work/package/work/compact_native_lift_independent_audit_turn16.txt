1. The auxiliary corrected map is legitimate

Use the convention V^g(c)=g(c_-)V(c)g(c_+)^{-1}. Let a(V;y) be the
exponential of the arithmetic mean of the small logarithms of the
source's averaged contour variables U_V(y,x), for x in B(y). Put

 C(V)(c)=a(V;c_-)^{-1} M(V)(c) a(V;c_+).

This is an AUXILIARY map. It need not equal the corrected Q used in
the variational theorem, and no such identification is needed.

Differentiating primary (0.12) at the identity gives the average of
the straight translated strings and two endpoint-contour terms.
Primary (0.8) says the derivative of the contour group mean is the
arithmetic mean. Thus the two factors a in C remove exactly the two
endpoint terms. With input physical lattice spacing h,

 F_h(A)=(i Lh)^{-1} log C(exp(i hA))

satisfies DF_h(0)=Q_st, where Q_st is a positive normalized average
of input physical components, of sup operator norm one.

At fixed primitive L every output read contains a fixed finite number
of links. Holomorphy on a fixed dimensionless angle ball therefore gives

 ||DF_h(A)|| <= 1+C_L h ||A||,
 ||F_h(A)|| <= ||A||+C_L h||A||^2.                       (1.1)

The h factor is indispensable and is present: degree n in the input
angles contributes h^(n-1) after division by Lh. The constants do not
depend on the number of earlier averaging steps or on total volume.

2. The ordinary iterate has a bounded holomorphic physical radius

Let eta=L^{-k}, h_t=L^t eta, C_0=U, C_{t+1}=C(C_t), and write
C_t=exp(i h_t A_t). Since sum_{t<k}h_t<1/(L-1), (1.1) gives
||A_t||<=2||A_0|| on one sufficiently small complex sup ball, uniformly
in k,t. This is a summable-scale bootstrap, not a C^k estimate.

The ordinary map M is gauge covariant, including for the small complex
gauge transformations used here. This follows directly from (0.12),
similarity covariance of its logarithm, and the left/right covariance
of the contour mean. Define, at surviving coarse vertices,

 g_0=1,                  g_{t+1}=g_t a(C_t).

Then M^t(U)=C_t^{g_t} exactly. The order of this product matters.
Moreover ||log a(C_t)||<=C_L h_t ||A_0||, so both g_t and its inverse
differ from 1 by at most C_L h_t||A_0||. The sum through t is of order
h_t, rather than of order one. Consequently

 ||(i h_t)^{-1} log M^t(exp(i eta A))|| <= K_L||A||.       (2.1)

One fixed reduction of the input radius keeps every logarithm on its
original branch. The proof is valid for complex A. It does not use
current estimates, stationarity, or a derivative of A.

Banach-valued Cauchy applied to (2.1), at h_k=1, bounds the first two
derivatives of the complete physical ordinary-average logarithm on a
strictly smaller sup ball, uniformly in k. This remains dimension
independent: the input norm is the link sup norm and each Cauchy
restriction is along finitely many prescribed directions.

For U_*=exp(i eta A_*), the relative perturbation exp(i eta a)U_*
is represented by the BCH input coordinate

 (i eta)^{-1} log[exp(i eta a) exp(i eta A_*)].

Its derivatives are uniform in eta. The output relative logarithm is
another bounded BCH change at M^kU_*. Thus, on any fixed uniformly

 ||D(M^k)_{U_*}E-D(M^k)_1E|| <= C||A_*||,
 ||D^2(M^k)_{U_*}[E,E]|| <= C.                          (2.2)

Both expressions mean the specified relative logarithmic coordinates.
No dimension-dependent or refinement-dependent inverse is hidden here.

3. The flat complete-star moment calculation

Set N=L^k=2m+1, and use physical current spacing one. On a component
mu, the k-fold straight-string average is EXACTLY

 (Q_st,N a)_mu(y)
   =N^{-(d+1)} sum_{u in [-m,m]^d} sum_{s=0}^{N-1}
                  a_mu(Ny+u+s e_mu).                   (3.1)

To verify the semigroup identity, combine the base-L digits of the
successive start offsets into the unique centered offset u, and the
successive path indices into s in {0,...,N-1}. Both combinations are
bijective. This proves (3.1); an approximate continuum kernel is not
being substituted.

Restrict a to fine bonds strictly inside the owner block at y=0.
For a longitudinal fine tail coordinate j=-m,...,m, the outgoing
coarse row has multiplicity j+m+1, and the incoming row has multiplicity
m-j. Hence, relative to physical fine counting N^{-d}, the two weights
are

        w_+(j)=(j+m+1)/N,       w_-(j)=(m-j)/N.          (3.2)

They sum to one. Other directions read no a_mu at this straight level.

Choose smooth nonnegative longitudinal bumps on (1/8,3/8) and
(-3/8,-1/8), and a smooth transverse bump strictly inside the block.
Use bumps positive throughout those open intervals, or explicitly
check the finitely many shallow meshes. Their physical discrete masses
are bounded above and below uniformly for N=L^k, k>=1. One proof is
Riemann convergence for large N plus the finitely many positive shallow
mass values. Normalizing the masses has uniformly bounded C^2 cost.

The normalized w_+ means differ by at least 1/4. Since the w_- means
are their complements, the 2 by 2 moment determinant is bounded away
from zero. Repeating per direction and Lie-algebra coordinate gives
a right inverse for ALL 2d incident oriented coarse rows, with uniformly
bounded physical C^2 norm. It is not merely an inverse of the single
target row.

For k=0, averaging is the identity. Use the target link coordinate and
its complete protected Wilson stencil. This separate fixed-mesh case
does not have a strictly interior continuum bubble, and should not be
described as having one. Its constants are fixed.

4. Ordinary endpoint terms and exact support

The final flat ordinary average has the form

        Q_ord,k a=Q_st,N a+d_c chi_k(a).

The ordered gauge construction proves this identity and bounds chi_k
uniformly. Its value at a coarse vertex reads only that vertex's final
fine block. This support statement follows from the literal contour
paths: they stay in the owner block, and nested aligned blocks compose
without leaving that final block.

For a bubble in one owner block, chi_k is therefore supported at its
one coarse vertex. Take a smooth compact fine gauge parameter lambda
equal to -chi_k at that vertex and zero at all other coarse vertices.
Its physical C^3 norm is uniformly bounded. Exact ordinary gauge
covariance implies

                   Q_ord,k d_eta lambda=d_c lambda.

Adding this fine gauge gradient to the straight-average bubble cancels
the endpoint term. It produces an ordinary right inverse E_ord with
uniform C^2 norm and the same compact owner-block support. In particular
one does not assume the ordinary and straight averages coincide.

An ordinary output row depends on the two fine endpoint blocks. The
same statement holds for the complete iterate by aligned composition.
Thus a compact perturbation inside one owner block can affect only the
incident star. This is why controlling the whole finite star suffices
to fix every other coarse link exactly, including at nonlinear order.
