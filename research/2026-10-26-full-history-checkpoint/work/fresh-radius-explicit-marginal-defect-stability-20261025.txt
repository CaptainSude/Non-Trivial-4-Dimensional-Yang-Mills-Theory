1. WHY THE ADDITIONAL CHANNEL IS NECESSARY

Let h_j be the declared covariant unmarked ACTUAL-MINUS-CORE logarithmic
birth from the companion comparison. Write b_aff,j+1 for its affine
scalar and b_phys,j+1=beta_actual,j+1-beta_C,j+1 for its actual numerical
coupling filing. Let delta_j=b_aff,j+1-b_phys,j+1. With its vacuum v_j
retained,

 h_j-v_j-b_phys,j+1 W_(j+1)
 = [h_j-v_j-b_aff,j+1 W_(j+1)]+delta_j W_(j+1).                  (1)

Only the bracket is an exactly neutral birth. W_(j+1) is the SAME
complete fine Wilson reference, with its source roots and original
measure. It must not be replaced by a fresh coarse Wilson action.
Equation (1) is an identity, not a new counterterm convention.

If a finite/universal comparison is stated instead for complete actual
and core births, subtract BOTH defects to obtain this delta_j. A defect
for the complete actual birth alone is not the defect of h_j unless the
core's corresponding defect has been proved zero. Retain any remaining
core defect as another contribution to the same marginal ledger.

The direct static theorem gives a uniformly bounded normalized value
for a retained fine Wilson marginal cohort. It gives no positive age
decay for that cohort. Therefore keep a marginal ledger Z_j separately
from the neutral regular difference E_j:

 Z_(j+1)=S_j Z_j+delta_j W_(j+1),  Z_0=0.                       (2)

Positive summation of the actual dated reference copies gives

 ||Z_j||_Bj <=C_W sum_(i<j)|delta_i|.                           (3)

This uses the exact fine-reference static identity and common-fresh
radius-normalized bounds, not an unrelated beta-extractor commutation.

3. UNIFORM INVARIANT BOUND

There exist fixed A,K,mu_*>0 and a sufficiently weak floor Y, independent
of N, such that mu<=mu_* implies

                 d_j<=A x_j^-s+K m_j<=A x_j^-s+K mu.            (6)

Here is the choice of margins. Let w_j=x_j^-s and
eta_Y=(1+B/Y)^s, so w_j/w_(j+1)<=eta_Y. Fix q0<qbar<1 with q_*<=q0

 [(A+R_*)w_j+(K+1)m_j]^2
 <=2(A+R_*)^2 w_j^2+2(K+1)^2 m_j^2.                            (7)

Choose A and Y so the w-dependent forcing, including the first term

 C epsilon_* <=(1-q0)K/2.

Finally choose mu_* so

 2C sup_(x>=Y)a(x)^2 (K+1)^2 mu_* <=(1-q0)K/2.                 (8)

The marginal part of (5) is then at most K m_j<=K m_(j+1), since
m_j is nondecreasing, and its w-dependent part is at most A w_(j+1).
Induction from d_0=0 proves (6).

 |beta_actual,j+1-beta_C,j+1|=|b_phys,j+1|

 sum_(j<N)|beta_actual,j+1-beta_C,j+1|

 <=C Y^(1-s)+C(N+1)mu.                                       (11)

5. USE FOR A SIMULTANEOUS CUTOFF FAMILY: EXPLICIT CONDITIONAL PREMISE

Suppose a finite family has horizon H, stopping date n_H<=rho H+C0 with
rho<1, and current periods at dates j<=n_H at least

                  ell_H=T L^((1-rho)H-C0).                    (12)

If a separately established finite-to-universal comparison gives a
causal bound, uniformly for all the relevant admitted states,

 |delta_j|<=P(H) exp[-c ell_H/P(H)],                           (13)

with a fixed polynomial P and c>0, then n_H=O(H) gives

 mu_H<=C H P(H) exp[-c ell_H/P(H)],
 (n_H+1)mu_H ->0.                                             (14)

No unspecified growing factor is absorbed into (13). A different rate
must be checked against (12) directly before concluding (14).

Consequently (6),(9),(11) retain the original comparison and summability
up to an error tending to zero. This proves HOW to propagate a genuine
wrapping/extractor defect. It does not prove (13).

For a CAUSAL first-passage application use the deterministic trial
horizon J_H=floor((sigma H+C0)/lambda)+1, with 0<sigma<lambda, before
the stopping date is known. The period at every j<=J_H is at least
T L^(H-J_H). Require the actual defect comparison uniformly on each
already-admitted prefix j<J_H, with this deterministic lower period
in place of ell_H. It gives an a priori small bound for m_j and the
next delta_j on that trial prefix. Equations (5)--(10), the causal
drift argument and the first-passage contradiction can then be applied
in that order. Deriving a period bound only after assuming the desired
stopping estimate would not justify this causal use.

In particular, exponential decay of each finite-volume coefficient and
existence of an infinite-volume subsequential limit do not imply a
quantitative comparison between the two. A local kernel could have an
arbitrary volume-dependent scalar multiplier while satisfying both
properties. Formula (13) requires actual finite/universal compatibility
of the coefficients or a proved boundary-sensitivity estimate for their
defining operations. The companion endpoint audit must supply that
premise before (14) is applied to the original laws.
