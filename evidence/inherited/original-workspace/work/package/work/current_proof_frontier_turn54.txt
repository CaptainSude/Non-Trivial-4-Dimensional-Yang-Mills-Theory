2. THE NEW FIXED-FRACTION NATIVE LEDGER

Use P_j=p0(g_j), R_j the rounded native block parameter,
and q_j(Z)=1+d'_j(Z), where d'_j counts native MR_j cells.
The source retains a component factor

                  exp[-K_j(Z)-2P_birth].

K_j is a prospective account paying future no-new-field
steps until maturation. Its present value is not all spare
credit. A valid proof cannot simply replace K_j by K_j/2.

The new induction instead multiplies every genuine
primitive birth/renewal credit and its original root
token by one fixed lambda in (0,1). All positive dated
costs are retained, with at most one fixed enlargement
of their constant. The weaker account is K_j^lambda.
It is generally not lambda K_j.

The source's full future forecast is bounded by

 C M^d L^(d+1) R_j^(d+2) q_j(Z).

The scaled proof checks four actual cases:

 * Fresh births: lambda c_b P_j^2 q_j supplies the
   current and prospective costs.
 * Pure enlargement: subtract the next actual cost
   from the already funded weaker forecast.
 * Mature preparatory rejection: the new lambda P_j
   supplies the bounded component's next horizon.
 * Merger: returned parent tokens leave one surviving
   root. Their surplus is at least a fixed multiple
   of lambda P_current and pays the extra merger
   forecast.

The sufficient comparisons include

 lambda P_j >= C M^d L^(d+1) R_j^(d+2),
 lambda c_b P_j^2 >= C' M^d L^(d+1) R_j^(d+2).

In dimension four the displayed R power is six.
The logarithmic powers must be chosen to dominate this
power before increasing the weak-coupling floor. Merely
decreasing g cannot repair an unfavorable exponent.
The native parameter hierarchy and trajectory comparisons
are retained as stated inputs.

This induction has constants independent of the number
of later dates and mergers. It uses the actual complete
history census, not just its final mass consequence.

3. NO CREDIT IS CREATED BY MERGING

Writing the complete account as A=K+T, with T the root
token, the merger identity is

 A_new = sum A_parent + fresh_credit + renewal_credit
                                      - current_cost.

Over a finite merger forest, intermediate tokens cancel.
The final accounts equal the sum of primitive supplies
minus actual costs. A released token can pay a merger
cost but is not a new primitive credit for a second sum.

Let B_i(h) be the primitive credit in the prefix through
a tag date i, and B(h) the total credit of a complete
continued history. Then

 exp[-B(h)] <= exp[-(1-lambda)B_i(h)] exp[-lambda B(h)].

If that prefix is long, its spare first factor can pay
one winding penalty. The weaker full ledger pays later
costs. The tag retains its original date and period
even if its displayed component later becomes small.

Repeated detection of the same ancestral winding does
not yield repeated independent factors. Use one
distinguished tag, a first-bad-date decomposition, or
an explicit union bound. Multiple extracted penalties
need a separate disjoint credit allocation.

4. COMPLETE SUPPORT, INCLUDING OLD COLLARS

In common physical coordinates let ell_j=M L^j R_j.
The TURN53 rounded-grid estimate implies

                  sum_(t<=i) ell_t <= C ell_i.

Consequently arbitrarily many pure enlargements add
only O(ell_i) to a lineage's reach at date i. They do
not create an age factor in its physical collar width.

For union-preserving covers/layers this follows directly
by repeated dilation. Bounded small-hull operations at
additional merger events are counted using the actual
merger forest. Their joining edges are charged to its
genuine seed/renewal events; recycled root tokens are
not additional seeds.

The resulting connected span is bounded by

 C ell_i [sum_fresh (1+d'_birth(F)) + number_of_renewals].

Add the full separately charged walk/read supports,
including ancestral supports that have since coarsened.
A genuine winding loop, or a support rejected for its
macroscopic span, then requires at least c H/ell_i
total charged size, where H is the physical period.
Crossing a coordinate seam with a small contractible
support is not winding.

Thus spare primitive plus walk credit supplies
exp[-c N_i/(M R_i)], with N_i=H/L^i, for this class.
A global ancillary choice with uncharged deciding data
is expressly outside the geometry statement.

Keep the tag as a predicate of the complete discrete
history h. Apply the native positive bounds to its
entire continuation through date k, including all
intermediate native interactions and normalizers.
Before taking the final history supremum, retain its

 |T_(k,h)(1_tag F)|
   <= C exp[-c N_i/(M R_i)]
                      A_(k,weak)(h) ||F||_infinity.

A_(k,weak) uses the one fixed weaker ledger and spatial

survival, renewal and merger costs.

 C exp[-c N_i/(M R_i)]
   exp[-2 lambda c_beta P_k-kappa_c d_k(X)] ||F||_infinity.

This retains fixed admitted ancillary data and one designated
charged tag. It is not a normalized boundary logarithm bound.

This is a bound on COMPLETE continued histories.
The field integrations may precede the scalar credit
split: the tag is constant on a fixed discrete history.
Restricting a positive history sum before its final
supremum is legitimate. The published collective
extraction is imported as a whole, including its
delegated preliminary rejection estimate.

No inequality between measures is inferred merely from
a small integrated mass. No arbitrary later unbounded

The complete native integrand and its permitted external

This addresses the future-merger obstruction for the
charged geometric/walk class. It is not a complete
matching theorem for every native choice.

7. THREE NORMALIZATION/CHOICE ASSERTIONS ARE SEPARATE

A. The full R,T composition preserves the original
   integrated partition function, with every vacuum
   scalar included.
B. Inserted original observables are transported by
   that composition with the correct numerator.
C. Local kernels, or their fully labelled weighted
   expansions, match between short lifted geometries.

The inspected primary sources establish the ordinary
normalized T pushforward identity. They also establish
the terminal first-class reverse-gauge-fixing identity,
including insertions invariant under that gauge group.

They do not yet identify the whole basic R input/output
normalization needed for A and B. The relevant endpoint
is deferred to Large-I; a readable matching numerator,
reference denominator, domain and Jacobian identity has
not been recovered in this audit.

If that reference insertion is normalized choicewise
for arbitrary incoming densities, A and the correctly
transported version of B would follow under normalized
averaging. The exact cover-local law C is not logically
necessary for that implication. If weights depend on an
input variable, their normalization must hold before
that variable is integrated.

The Wilson-only reference denominator in Part II is
consistent with this mechanism but does not identify
the missing matching numerator. The terminal normalized
retained operation also cannot establish the original
whole-R identity by itself.

Conversely, mass preservation alone does not establish
observable preservation or a cover-local choice law.
A verified integrated identity from the source could
settle A without reconstructing its entire proof; no
claim is made that Bałaban's construction lacks it.
