RESULT AND STATUS

This note proves a terminal-filter consumer and supplies an actual finite
coarse Wilson benchmark. It also attaches the existing forward-pullback
identity to ONE admitted, suitably located literal basic-R replacement.
It does not attach that identity to a complete native RG trajectory.

The useful new reduction is this. A positive terminal restriction need
not exclude every earlier large-field history. If an actual half-local
forward map exists, and the normalized BOTH-half terminal restriction
has a nonzero centered reflected channel, then an exactly centered,
bounded observable of the ORIGINAL Wilson field has that channel,
multiplied by only the terminal restriction's probability. No small
probability of ANY bad event through the whole UV history is required.

The source gives a concrete reason to consider this restriction: healed
components enter the regular R action in Large-II(1.98)--(1.101), whereas
the surviving current components remain in(1.102)--(1.104). These are
different from the all-small selected histories of TURN70.

There are nevertheless two actual missing attachments, not just a
normalizing scalar: native replacement components may cross a reflection
plane, and a global no-current-raw filter reads arbitrarily near that
plane. Shared fresh boundary variables do not repair the first problem.
Consequently the terminal filtered native density has not been identified
with the output of the required strict-positive-time forward kernel.
The nonzero lower bound below is a target, not an established YM bound.

2. WHAT THE LITERAL BASIC-R JOINT LAW DOES TO A NEW PROBE

Freeze one actually admitted component, graph choice, and old branch.
Write e for its COMPLETE retained data, x for its old integrated field,
and xi for older/private records still read. Large-I(1.100) has the
normalized real reference

 q_(h,e)(dy) = w_(h,e)(y) dy / Z_(h,e),
 Z_(h,e) = integral w_(h,e)(y)dy > 0,                    (2.1)

where w contains the actual axial delta/slice measure, relative-link
cutoff and localized Wilson reference action. The exterior admission
gate is outside this quotient. The numerator and denominator use the
SAME reference cutoff and current-to-relative change of coordinates.
The passive joint lift is the literal expression

                  d tau_h(e,x,xi) q_(h,e)(dy).          (2.2)

It follows from the displayed old integration, not from an arbitrary
coupling invented using equality of partition functions. Gauge changes
retain their inverse old-variable records as in TURN60(6.2); marginal
gauge invariance alone cannot simplify the complete joint weight.

For a bounded function f of the new current field in this component,
including its actual relative-to-current map, its forward mean is

 K_h f(e,x,xi) = integral f(new(y,e)) q_(h,e)(dy)
               =: m_h(e).                              (2.3)

Thus it does not depend on the eliminated x,xi at this fixed branch.
In particular the conditional variance of a fresh reference fluctuation
is auxiliary variance; it is not by itself a reflected channel in the
old field. For two independent reflected reference draws, the product
expectation conditional on the complete old data is the product of
their means m_h. All original reflected signal then resides in those
means, including branch selection and retained/exterior data.

The full real partition-of-unity branching can be realized probabilistically
only with its literal nonnegative weights summing to one at the complete
current record. Graph choices retain their normalized positive law.
First/second-class tests made on y are evaluated AFTER (2.1); preselecting
a desired successful outcome and still calling q normalized is invalid.
Signed connected expansions are coefficient representations, not extra
random branches. The printed choice-normalization reciprocal ambiguity
recorded in TURN60 is not used to change the source's normalized law.

There is a genuine elementary locality consequence. If the COMPLETE
fixed-branch footprint, reference datum, gauge anchors, and all decisions
for this operation lie in a strict positive half, then (2.1) defines a
normalized forward kernel in that half. Its reflected copy can be drawn
independently in the negative half. Applying TURN63 Section10 to this
actual elementary pair is valid. This is a one-component native
attachment; it does not prove that the source's entire geometry-selection
algorithm has these footprints or that all its components avoid the cut.

3. A NONZERO ACTUAL FINITE COARSE WILSON BENCHMARK

Fix a finite, nondegenerate periodic spatial graph and an even temporal
period N>=4. All spacings and numbers of sites are fixed in this section.
Take the actual SU2 Wilson law with time-independent positive temporal
couplings beta_e and real bounded gauge-invariant spatial action V. For the usual Wilson
benchmark V is the time-independent spatial plaquette action.

On H_kin=L2(SU2^E,Haar), let P be normalized site-gauge averaging,
M=exp(-V/2), and C product convolution with

 q_beta(W)=exp[-beta(1-Tr W/2)]/c(beta),
 c(beta)=2 exp(-beta) I_1(beta)/beta.

C and M commute with P. On H_phys=P H_kin,

 T=M C M,
 Z_W=[product_e c(beta_e)]^N Tr_phys(T^N).              (3.1)

The convolution eigenvalue in spin j is I_(2j+1)(beta)/I_1(beta)>0.
C is positive trace class; M is bounded and invertible on this fixed
compact graph. Hence T is positive trace class and has no zero eigenvector
on H_phys. Its eigenvalues lambda_i are strictly positive, though no
positive uniform spectral floor is asserted. Gauge projection, temporal
link Haar integrals, and the scalar in (3.1) are all retained.

Let f be a real bounded nonconstant gauge-invariant slice function.
For example, Tr of one nondegenerate spatial plaquette divided by2
qualifies. Set A=f-E_W f as a multiplication operator. For1<=m<N,

 C_W(m) = Cov_W(f_0,f_m)
  = [Tr T^N]^-1 sum_(i,j) lambda_i^(N-m) lambda_j^m
                                      |A_ij|^2 > 0.   (3.2)

Every term is nonnegative and every eigenvalue factor is positive. If
the sum vanished, all matrix elements of A would vanish, so A=0 on
H_phys. Applying A to the constant gauge-invariant vector gives f
constant Haar-almost everywhere, contrary to the assumption. This
argument proves strictness, not merely nonnegative reflection positivity.
For a reflected slice at time r,1<=r<N/2, m=2r and the complete slice
read has positive distance r*a_t and (N/2-r)*a_t from BOTH fixed planes.
Finite spatial smoothing within that slice is allowed if f remains a
nonconstant bounded gauge-invariant function of its original links.

If the ordinary Wilson coupling parameters vary in a fixed compact
subset of strictly positive finite values, (3.2) is continuous: use the
literal finite compact-group integral and its positive denominator.
Consequently, for this same graph, f and m,

                    inf_parameters C_W(m)=c_*>0.        (3.3)

This is an actual source-free, nonperturbative finite-model benchmark.
It replaces the need to identify a Gaussian benchmark in the reduction
below. It supplies no numeric lower margin at arbitrarily weak coupling,
no spatial-cutoff uniformity and no bound on c_* as N or the graph grows.
Compactness alone does NOT show that native approximation errors are
smaller than c_*. It also does not turn an unsmeared fine Wilson loop
into a nontrivial continuum observable.

4. THE TERMINAL REFLECTED-FILTER IDENTITY

Let mu be the ORIGINAL reflection-positive law. Assume a verified forward
kernel K with reflection-related half kernels, conditionally independent
given the original field, as in TURN63 Section10. Let nu=mu K. For a
bounded positive-half output function G, write F=K_+G. Then

 nu G=mu F,             nu[(Theta G)G]=mu[(Theta F)F].  (4.1)

All deterministic and random records used by G must belong to that same
half. In particular shared fresh randomness has not been silently added.
Gauge equivariance of K and gauge invariance of G imply that F is an
original gauge-invariant observable. Equation(4.1) also implies RP of nu.

Take0<=A<=1 a positive-half output filter, real |f|<=1 a positive-half
output probe, and assume

 Z=nu[(Theta A)A]>0,
 nu_A(dV)=Z^-1 (Theta A)(V) A(V) nu(dV).

The both-half restricted law nu_A is reflection invariant and RP. Put

 c=nu(A f)/nu(A),          G=A(f-c)/2.                  (4.2)

Z>0 implies nu(A)>0; |c|<=1, |G|<=1 and nu G=0. Direct expansion gives

 C_mu(K_+G)=nu[(Theta G)G]
 =Z/4 { C_(nu_A)(f) + [nu_A(f)-c]^2 }
 >= Z C_(nu_A)(f)/4.                                  (4.3)

No relation between the one-half mean c and the two-half mean nu_A(f)
was assumed. The leftover square is favorable. Moreover RP applied to
A-nu(A) proves

                         Z >= nu(A)^2.                (4.4)

This is equivalent to using the two-dimensional OS Gram matrix of
A and Af and choosing a coefficient vector perpendicular to their
actual unfiltered mean vector. Formula(4.3) provides its exact centering
and a bounded normalization without requiring f odd or guessing its mean.

If the joint law of the two f reads under nu_A differs by half-total-
variation distance at most delta from a fixed coarse Wilson benchmark
in Section3, then

          C_(nu_A)(f) >= c_* - 6 delta.                (4.5)

Indeed bounded product expectations differ by at most2delta, while
the two squared means differ by at most4delta. Thus delta<=c_*/12 and
Z>=z_*>0 give C_mu(K_+G)>=z_* c_*/8. A full output-law comparison is
unnecessary; comparison of these two means and one reflected product
already suffices, with the corresponding sum of their errors.

For an unnormalized complete terminal density Q, the filter mass is
Z=Q(A Theta A)/Q(1), in the SAME vacuum convention. A complete upper
bound Q(1)<=e^(CV) and a lower bound Q(A Theta A)>=i_*(V)>0 would give
z_*=i_*e^(-CV) at fixed stopping volume. Neither lower bound for the
filtered numerator nor (4.5) is inferred merely from an integrated
unmarked stability bound. A tiny raw component mass is never divided out.

5. WHY TERMINAL-ONLY SELECTION IS A REAL DIFFERENT POSSIBILITY

The source distinction is explicit. In Large-II, first-class components
are summed and exponentiated into R' in(1.98). After boundary pieces are
separated, (1.100) gives the bulk bound

 |R'^(k)(X,(U,J))| <= exp[-p0(g_k)] exp[-kappa d_k(X)].  (5.1)

They enter the new action in(1.101). The surviving second-class Y
components remain in(1.102), or as the positive T'_k(Y)1 multiplication
factors of(1.103)--(1.104). Thus a terminal region with no CURRENT raw
component can still include contributions from histories that were bad
earlier and have already healed. It is not the law obtained by retaining
only all-small Gaussian/product-ball kernels at every date.

This matters because the latter global all-good mass can vanish in the
UV limit even when each individual defect is rare. The candidate filter
in (4.2) should concern only a present terminal property, with the old
histories already summed in the actual density. One must not reintroduce
a factor for absence of every previously healed event.

What the current source reading actually supplies is a representation
and coefficient bounds, not the desired filtered reflected comparison.
B119(2.43)--(2.49) first controls action terms with their DATED volumes;
the positive census and integration then give(2.50). TURN56 explains
why those dated volumes cannot first be replaced by terminal V in an
arbitrary naked prefactor. Large-II(1.103) normalizes operations only
with retained Y fixed; it does not remove those raw masses from the
full outer law. No pointwise two-sided density comparison for our
two-filter channel is obtained by these operations alone.

Even if a globally current-regular sector is explicitly homogeneous,
it is not yet the sector selected by a strict-positive-time half-local
filter. That geometry/chronology match must be made before estimating
its action. Section6 identifies the obstruction.

6. CONCRETE NATIVE OBSTRUCTIONS TO USING(4.3) IMMEDIATELY

6A. A crossing reference is not two independent half references.

Native components may extend across a reflection plane. For a component
Lambda of this kind, (2.1) is ONE normalized reference on Lambda. It
contains the actual localized action, constraints and gauge slice on
the whole component. If that action couples variables across the plane,
the conditional reference kernel at fixed old data is not a product of
two half kernels. Reflection covariance of q does not give such a product.
It therefore does not meet the premise of(4.1). Conditional variance
created by this one fresh reference cannot be credited as an original
physical reflection channel using (2.3).

Conditioning on a fresh boundary variable Bc is not a cure. Suppose the
two output halves become independent given old U and fresh Bc, with means
m_+(U,Bc),m_-(U,Bc). Their full product and the product of separately
averaged pullbacks differ by

 E_mu Cov_(Bc|U)(m_-(U,Bc),m_+(U,Bc)).                 (6.1)

For a reflection-symmetric fixed input and equal means on both halves,
this is an ordinary nonnegative variance and can be the entire signal.
One cannot regard Bc as an original boundary variable. The obstruction
vanishes if Bc is deterministic from the appropriate original boundary,
or if this covariance loss is separately controlled, not just because
the full updated law is reflection invariant or RP.

Freezing a buffer around the planes or refusing crossing R updates is
a legitimate possible modified positive algorithm. Its terminal action
and unmarked estimates would need a new attachment; it is not the
unchanged published full native map. Restricting to a favorable local
branch also requires its actual probability/read structure.

6B. The filter itself must stay at positive physical time.

For the intended physical witness every original input read of K_+G
must stay a fixed distance delta_0>0 from BOTH fixed planes as a->0.
This includes A, branch and eligibility decisions, the reference
minimum, constraints, gauge anchors and every backward averaging read.
It is insufficient that only the displayed coarse f has that property.

In particular, A=indicator{the ENTIRE positive half has no current raw
component} reads up to the planes. Its multiplication into f does not
produce the required strict-positive-time observable. One may instead
put A on a buffered positive-time region containing all the complete
backward reads. But then A Theta A leaves current raw components near
the fixed planes, so its output density is not automatically the fully
homogeneous current-regular density. Their boundary/outer contribution
remains in the very reflection channel under investigation.

Ordinary T averaging must also obey a reflection-compatible placement
and complete-read bound. The native weak-region determining fields and
reference minima, not just the displayed link sites, enter those reads.
Exponential localization of coefficient expansions does not establish
an EXACT bounded-read stochastic kernel; signed localization terms are
not independently selectable probabilities.

6C. Integrated equivalence is not equality for arbitrary filters.

Large-II printed378 explicitly states that first-class reverse-FP
removal is valid when the density is integrated over the corresponding
X variables. To carry A or Af through this step, retain the actual
inverse gauge records, or prove that the complete inserted function is
invariant under that local gauge change. A current label or a filter
depending on unrecorded gauge choices cannot simply be multiplied into
the already integrated equivalence. The final first/second-class split
also occurs after the fresh reference, as specified in Section2.

6D. Radius growth by itself is not the decisive obstruction.

For clarity, if a future exact half-local construction proves a complete
read-radius bound C M a_j R_j at each date, with a_j=ell L^(-r),
R_j<=C'[log(2+Gamma+r)]^s and r=n-j>=0, then

 sum_j C M a_j R_j
 <= C_(L,s) M ell [log(2+Gamma)]^s.                    (6.2)

Use log(2+Gamma+r)<=log(2+Gamma)+log(1+r) and the convergent geometrically
weighted sum of [1+log(1+r)]^s. This is uniform in initial UV depth.
Thus logarithmically growing native block radii are compatible with a
fixed physical buffer. The missing issue is the COMPLETE read bound,
including arbitrarily large connected regions and deciding reads,
not the elementary series in(6.2). The source's existing long-history
bounds cannot be treated as probabilities of all such read failures
without the positive-event/complete-history attachment they require.

7. WHAT THIS CHANGES IN THE NEXT ATTACK

The next narrowly useful source-free target is an unmarked, reflected,
two-filter terminal representation with all earlier healed histories
retained, at fixed stopping graph and fixed weak coupling range. Prove
either its two-read comparison with the actual coarse Wilson benchmark,
or directly a strictly positive centered two-read Gram eigenvalue.
At the same time prove the elementary native moves that realize it
have strict-half complete original reads, or quantitatively pay the
crossing/fresh-boundary covariance loss in (6.1).

This route would not need an original action-density source trajectory.
It would construct a new bounded gauge-invariant original observable
by forward conditional averaging. It nevertheless needs common
observable/support data across cutoffs, and its positive value alone
does not give continuum Schwinger functions, Euclidean symmetry or OS
reconstruction. A zero-energy limit remains a further issue if the
support/separated-time family is not controlled.

The previously proved thin-loop collapse remains applicable to bounded
functions of the actual sparse private-edge thin-loop family, including
bounded deterministic temporal filtering. It does not apply merely
because an observable is gauge invariant, and does not rule out a
thick new forward-pullback probe. Conversely, no known fresh-reference
Gaussian variance or selected covariance supplies its original channel.

The concrete advance here is(4.3), with its actual finite Wilson
benchmark(3.2)--(3.3), and the one-component native attachment(2.3).
The source inspection identifies exactly why these do not yet compose
