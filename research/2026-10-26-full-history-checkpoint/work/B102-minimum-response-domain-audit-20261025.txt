1. WHAT B102 ACTUALLY PROVES

Theorem1, printed279, is a theorem about a real compact-group variational
problem on a prescribed multiscale determining geometry. The domains Omega_j,
Lambda_j and determining bond set mathfrak B_k are fixed as on printed277--278.
The datum V satisfies the small mixed-scale plaquette condition(7). There is
a unique critical gauge orbit in the stated regular class; its minimum obeys
the curvature/current tests(2) and local gauge bounds(9),(10). The constants
B3 and the smallness constants depend on fixed d,L. Therefore an L^{-2} factor
cannot be compared with B3 by merely declaring that L is large.

SectionG fixes a base minimum U0=U_k(V0), V0 satisfying(7), and writes

  V=V' V0,  V'=exp(iB),  |V'-1|<C1 epsilon1,
  |B|<2 C1 epsilon1 on mathfrak B_k.                 [B102(172), p305]

The relative Landau response H(B) is determined by

  H = A1 + H1 B - Hlin D(A1+H1 B),                  [174]
  A1 + G (delta Vnonlin/delta A')(A1+H1 B)=0,        [175]

or by its second, better second-derivative representation

  H = A0 + H0 B - Hlin D(A0+H0 B),                  [179]
  A0 - Gtilde Delta^(2) H0 B
       + Gtilde (delta Vnonlin/delta A')(A0+H0 B)=0. [180]

Here Hlin is the linear propagator denoted H in the paper, distinguished from
the nonlinear response by typography. These are native constrained equations,
not a generic Banach-space implicit-function ansatz.

Proposition9, printed309, states analyticity in complex B on the stated small
chart, analyticity of the returned axial minimum U_k(V'V0), and analyticity in
the external field U subject to the cited complete regularity conditions
(3.35)--(3.38) of the propagator paper [5], or(1.7)--(1.9) of the gauge paper [6].
It also states the regularity conditions(19)--(21) with epsilon2=B5 epsilon1,
where(173) gives B5=6 B1 B3 C1 as a sufficient bound, and the response kernel
estimate(190).

Those external-field hypotheses must not be replaced by a curvature bound
alone, nor by a pointwise bound on a gauge return. The paper does not state
Proposition9 as a theorem on arbitrary independent pairs (U,J). The J in the
SectionG stationarity equations(170),(171),(178) is the physical current of
the base minimum. B109 later explicitly prescribes the independent-current
extension; see Section3 below.

The exact response bounds(19), printed281, are, on Omega_j and s_j=L^j eta,

  |H| < epsilon2 s_j^{-1},
  |nabla_U^eta H| < epsilon2 s_j^{-2},
  |D_U^{eta*}D_U^eta H|, |Delta_U^eta H|
       < epsilon2 s_j^{-3},                         [19]
  Q_j(U,eta H)=B on Lambda_j,                       [20]
  R(U)D_U^{eta*}H=0.                               [21]

P1 H is not literally one of the displayed rows in(19).

2. RESPONSE KERNELS, NORMALIZATION, AND THE FOURTH SOURCE SLOT

At printed308, Eq(190), for y in Lambda_j, y' in Lambda_j', and
x in Delta(y), the displayed rows are

  delta H(x)/delta B(y'),
  nabla_x delta H(x)/delta B(y'),
  the localized Holder norm of nabla delta H/delta B,
  D_U^*D_U delta H(x)/delta B(y'),
  Delta_U delta H(x)/delta B(y').

Their respective bounds are a common C times

  [s_j^{-1}, s_j^{-2},
   (||zeta||_beta+|zeta|) s_j^{-2-beta},
   s_j^{-3}, s_j^{-3}]
        s_j'^{-d} exp[-delta0 d(y,y')/8].           [190]

The functional derivative uses the native cell-volume measure. Conversion
to an ordinary derivative with respect to one coarse bond coordinate
multiplies this kernel by s_j'^d. Omitting that cancellation produces a
spurious scale loss. A sup-norm operator estimate also needs the weighted
row sum of the exponential kernel on the actual determining geometry.
In a homogeneous, single-coarse-grade chart this row sum is bounded by the
usual lattice exponential sum, uniformly in volume; this observation alone
is not a proof of every multiscale/localized row sum.

If a star-shaped B chart, H(0)=0, and a native weighted kernel row-sum bound
K are fixed, integrating(190) on the segment tB gives

  |H(B)| <= CK s_j^{-1} ||B||,
  |nabla H(B)| <= CK s_j^{-2} ||B||,
  |D*D H(B)|+|Delta H(B)| <= CK s_j^{-3} ||B||.

This is a rigorous one-response consequence, with its segment and row-sum
hypotheses explicit. It applies equally to a difference H(B1)-H(B0) when
the segment between the two inputs stays in the admitted chart.

There is a native route to the P1 slot, rather than a new elliptic estimate.
B109 printed272, between(3.11) and(3.12), uses

  D*D+DRD* = Delta_U - P1 + L_U,                    [A]

where L_U is a lower-order local operator. Since RD*H=0,

  P1 H = Delta_U H - D*D H + L_U H.                [B]

Consequently the two second-order rows in(190) also control P1 H, provided
the stated native local-coefficient bound for L_U is retained on the same
complete chart. If B varies while U is fixed, differentiating[B] gives
the same identity for delta H/delta B, so the decay estimate transfers to
P1 delta H/delta B with only the fixed finite-range/local coefficient loss.
The lower-order coefficient estimate is not invented here: B102 printed298,
(134)--(136), and printed299,(140), explicitly derive the corresponding
covariant Hodge identity and invoke the bounded DPD* operator and curvature
coefficients from the propagator paper. Its exact constants are an imported
published input, not recomputed in this note.

Thus B102 plus the native Hodge identity supplies the four source slots
H,nabla H,P1 H,Delta H for an admitted unlocalized physical response. It
does not say that an arbitrary localization preserves D*D control; B109
introduces the selected current precisely because that claim is false.

For evidentiary accuracy: B102 printed308 explicitly omits the detailed
kernel calculation leading to(189), calling it lengthy, then states the
bound and deduces(190). This note uses that published theorem; it has not
independently reconstructed the omitted kernel calculation.

3. THE SELECTED CURRENT AND ITS PUBLISHED ONE-STEP DOMAIN

B109 printed272,(3.10)--(3.14), starts from the physical current expansion

  Jphys(exp(i xi A)U)=Jphys(U)+D_U^*D_U A+F(U,A).

Using[A] on a Landau response, it defines an independent-pair continuation
by the selected companion

  ( F_A, K_A ) =
  ( exp(i xi A)U,
    theta^3 J + Delta_U^xi A - P1 A + F1(U,A) ),     [3.13]
  theta=L^{j-1} eta.

F1 is the native lower-order local remainder; it must stay that literal
function. In particular K_0=theta^3 J. Replacing K_A by a recomputed
physical current after weakening changes this chosen continuation.

On the full old domain

  U_j^c(X, alpha0/2, alpha1/2, alpha0),

the source evaluated at[3.13] is analytic if

  |A|, |P1 A|, |nabla_U^xi A|, |Delta_U^xi A|<alpha2
  on the complete X read.                          [3.14]

Printed273 explicitly instructs replacing U_(k+1),J_(k+1) in the operators
of B102(179),(180) by independent U,J. It does not infer that independence
from the physical minimum theorem. Its displayed domain is

  U_j^c(X,alpha0/2,alpha1/2,alpha0)
    intersect {direct conditions(i)--(iii) at grade k+1,
       with constants (1+beta)alpha0,(1+beta)alpha1,alpha0}. [3.16]

It assumes epsilon1 sufficiently small that H_j(B(t)) satisfies all four
conditions(3.14) with alpha2/3, and states that the subsequent native
localizations retain the bounds(3.14),(3.9). This is a genuine published
one-step independent-current input. To apply it to a prefix one still must
put the entire actual input image in(3.16) at every descendant and on every
source contour. The action-only t_square Cauchy circle in(3.15) is not an
additional source-noise argument.

4. DETERMINING TOWERS: TWO DIFFERENT SUFFICIENT MECHANISMS

B109 printed262--263 defines U_j^c as a union of simultaneous complex
field/current gauge orbits. A representative must satisfy all(i)--(iv):
compact real reference and its local gauge cubes; relative complex C1
field; complex curvature and the actual independent current allowance;
and every specified reconstructed minimum/current on the complete
determining tower with its collars/insets. These reconstructed tests are
not erased by gauge invariance.

First mechanism: the smaller complete direct domain at printed263 gives
the reconstructed estimates

  |partial U_n-1| <= 2B3 alpha0' xi^2,
  |J_n| <= 2B3 alpha0' (L^n xi)^2,                  [1.17]

with its corresponding relative-field conditions. One can in principle
choose fixed strict thresholds and invoke this criterion ONCE for each
actual input field. It is not necessary to multiply B3 at every previous
date if direct norm bounds have independently been proved for that field.
But every stated direct condition, every read and the actual current cap
must be verified first. A recurrence for curvature/current alone is not
this verification.

Second mechanism: B109 printed277--280 gives a special minimum-image
admission theorem, culminating in Lemma4,(3.53). For a full determining
cube square0, it rebuilds U_(k+1)(square0,M'(U)), chooses a local generalized
axial gauge and Landau gauge, forms

  calA=(1/(i eta))log[ exp(i eta A)
                      exp(i L^-1 eta H_(k+1)) ],
  B=Q(eta calA),                                   [3.30]

and assumes the C1/Holder bounds(3.31) on A. The admitted expression is
the analytic minimum U_j(square0,exp(i(tau B+B'))) and its J_j, for a
small independent coarse perturbation B'. The explicit reserve includes

  B3 alpha3 < alpha1/2,
  2 B3 alpha3 <= beta L^-2 alpha0,                  [p280,3.51]

as well as the earlier fixed smallness assumptions.

The special identity(3.38), printed278, says that reconstructing a lower
minimum from the average of this same minimum yields that same minimum
up to the blockwise lift of its return gauge. This converts condition(iv)
to condition(iii) with a little reserve. It is an exact on-minimum identity,
not U_n(M^n U)=U for arbitrary independent input U.

IMPORTANT SCOPE: immediately after(3.38) the pair is displayed with the
PHYSICAL current D*_(exp i xi H) xi^-2 pi Im partial(exp i xi H). The proof
of(3.42) likewise uses physical minimum currents. Printed277 says the
background expansion(3.34) involves configurations U,A only. Therefore
Lemma4 certainly supplies a full domain theorem for its analytic-minimum
pair, but it does not itself identify an arbitrary selected companion
K_A from[3.13] after source weakening with that physical current. An actual
prefix invoking this lemma must prove the selected-pair interface, or
declare a new continuation and verify all original scalar identities and
normalizers in it. The suppressed notation after(3.24) is not permission
to silently change a source current.

5. COHERENT FRAMES AND THE RELATIVE AXIAL OBSTACLE

B102(181), printed307, is precise: for a coarse gauge v,

  U_k(V^v)=U_k(V)^(vtilde),

where vtilde is constant on the native blocks B^j(y) and equals v(y) at
their centers. This formula extends the axial minimum to complex orbits
containing an admitted local chart. It is not arbitrary-fine-gauge
equivariance of the chosen axial representative.

As verified separately from B98/B109, a native physical one-step minimum
F_orig first goes by a relative axial h, h=1 on T^(j), to F_ax, and then
by u^-1 to F_L. With V^g(b)=g(b_-)V(b)g(b_+)^-1,

  F_orig=F_L^(h^-1 u).

The B98 product bound controls u on a common physical complex chart; it
does not automatically control h. The equation h=1 on T^(j) eliminates
its action on current-grade coarse B only. It does not eliminate its
restriction to T^(j-1), the finer lattice exposed by the next descent.
As a logical test, an SL(2,C) gauge can equal1 on T^(j) while taking
diag(exp r,exp(-r)) at a newly exposed vertex. Its adjoint norm there is
at least exp(2r). This is not a counterexample to the actual native h;
it proves only that the coarse normalization alone supplies no such bound.

One may evaluate an already admitted invariant scalar in F_L and omit h
from that scalar calculation. To continue the actual original-noise
history, however, all subsequent noise/coarse increments must be transformed
in the corresponding frame. The original real noise integration variable
is retained; its transformed value is a deterministic complex argument.
Before writing x -> Ad(g)x on FREE noise coordinates, one must check the
actual embedding and solved pivots. The required covariance has the form

  B'_j(U^g, Ad(g)J, T_g x)
       =Ad(r_j g) B'_j(U,J,x),
  B'_j=g_j C_j x-h_j Dtilde_j(g_j C_j x),

with the actual coordinate transport T_g proved, not guessed. Here h_j in
the second line is the native pivot map, not the relative axial gauge h.

There is a directly relevant local repair already in B109 printed275--276:
for the rebuilt determining-cube minimum, Eq(3.26) controls its generalized
axial gauge v and Eq(3.27) its Landau return u_(k+1). The text then explicitly
transforms BOTH J and B by R(u_(k+1)^-1)R(v^-1), and uses their sup bounds to
preserve the estimates. Eq(3.29) is the simultaneous covariance statement.
The construction is local on square0 and assumes its full domain first.
It is not a proof that a previously chosen arbitrary fine h has the same
bound, nor that these local frames glue into one full-prefix frame.

6. WHAT HAS BEEN GAINED, AND WHAT REMAINS

Established published inputs and exact deductions:
- Native complex minimum response, both implicit representations, complete
  fixed-grade hypotheses, and the correct derivative density normalization.
- One-response bounds for H,nabla H,D*D H,Delta H; P1 H follows from the
  native Hodge identity with its bounded local coefficients retained.
- B109's explicit independent-current extension, selected theta^3J slot,
  four response norms, and precise intersection domain(3.16).
- Two useful tower mechanisms: one fixed direct-to-full application per
  actual input, or the special analytic-minimum tower identity(3.38).
- A native local frame construction that explicitly rotates both J and B.

Not established by these statements:
- Common complex chart and complete direct norm reserves for every moving
  prefix background after all source s-localizations.
- Covariance and bounds of the full original free-noise map including
  every solved pivot, near/far term and actual determining read.
- A bound for the total physical frame h^-1u, or a proved representative
  choice that removes h throughout the literal prefix while preserving
  its noise/scalar identities.
- Identification of a weakened selected current with the physical-current
  pair in the special Lemma4 tower theorem.
- Simultaneous admission and normalized catalogue estimates through a
  logarithmic prefix. G1b and G1c therefore remain open.
