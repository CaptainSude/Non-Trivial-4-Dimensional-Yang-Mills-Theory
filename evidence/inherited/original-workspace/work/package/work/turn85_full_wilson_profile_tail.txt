20 October 2026. tail47. Exact finite-regulator results.

RESULT

There is an actual full-Wilson cancellation which improves an absolute
source displacement estimate. For a Haar-divergence-free change of an
original link, a constant local action response is exactly a derivative
of the remaining test. If that test reads no changed link, the derivative
is zero. A varying action profile then enters through differences across
the incident plaquette star. This is an exact integration identity under
the original normalized law, not stationarity only at a classical minimum.

A finite one-link conditional calculation sharpens this: the conditional
drift of a constant-star action under an exterior-measurable rotation is
exactly quadratic in its angle. The full separated covariance change is
the OUTER covariance of this explicitly known drift. It is retained, not
declared zero.

A native-style field-dependent cutoff rotation is different. Its Haar
divergence adds a definite source row. A concrete original Wilson one-link
conditional measure, with a smooth switch supported only in a rare outer
shell, has a STRICTLY POSITIVE first constant-action response from this
term. Complete action stationarity does not cancel it. The term is small
by a genuine conditional tail estimate in this elementary model, but its
native full-record analogue and physical root census remain to be proved.

This complements the exact noisy-square obstruction in
work/turn85_noisy_plaquette_response_gauge.txt, independently audited in
work/turn85_noisy_plaquette_audit_tail.txt. It does not duplicate that
square or assert it is a trajectory of the full native constrained map.

1. ORIGINAL NORMALIZATION AND THE EXACT HAAR IDENTITY

Take the nondegenerate finite periodic SU(2) Wilson model, periods at
least4, normalized link Haar measure dU and homogeneous ORIGINAL beta>0:

 mu(dU)=Z^(-1) exp[-beta S(U)]dU,
 S=sum_p h_p, h_p=1-Tr(U_p)/2.

All statements also make sense with fixed exterior links where specified.
No selected weak law, altered partition denominator or native effective
action is substituted for this density.

Let D_e be a real smooth vector field acting only on link e. Its coefficient
may depend on other links. Write d_e=div_Haar D_e, with the divergence
taken in the acted-on link coordinate. For every smooth test G, integration
of a total divergence on the compact group gives

 beta E_mu[(D_e S)G] = E_mu[D_e G]+E_mu[d_e G].              (1.1)

Bounded tests with the required weak derivatives follow by approximation.
At finite volume all smooth source exponentials used below are integrable.
There are no discarded boundary terms on the complete original group.

For example D_e U_e=X_e(U_others)U_e with X_e anti-Hermitian traceless
and independent of U_e has d_e=0. Conditional on all other links this is
a left-invariant Haar vector field. Dependence on exterior links does not
create a divergence in the e coordinate. A sum of such fields remains
divergence-free, even if its different exterior reads overlap. Smoothness
and the actual reads still matter for its finite flow and locality.

Gauge invariance of the comparison can be retained by requiring
X_e(U^g)=g_(tail e) X_e(U) g_(tail e)^(-1). Without that property the
integration identity remains true, but the new observable need not be
gauge invariant. Reflection support likewise includes the full read of X_e.
There are concrete nonconstant choices: take X_e=(W-W^dagger)/2 for an
exterior closed Wilson loop W based at the tail and not using e. This is
traceless anti-Hermitian in SU(2), has the stated covariance, and remains
independent of U_e. Its entire loop is part of the declared read.

2. EXACT SOURCE-GRADIENT AND TWO-JET FORMULAS

Let F_f=beta sum_p f_p h_p be the canonical original action source,
with deterministic real profile f. For each changed link choose a real
anchor value f_e, for example its physical interpolant at the link center.
For D=sum_e D_e define

 R_f=beta sum_e sum_(p incident e) (f_p-f_e) D_e h_p,
 D^f=sum_e f_e D_e,                 d^f=sum_e f_e d_e.       (2.1)

Each h_p is differentiated with its physical orientation; the identity
D_e S=sum_(p incident e)D_e h_p is literal. Summing(1.1) gives

 E[(D F_f)G] = E[R_f G]+E[D^f G]+E[d^f G].                 (2.2)

This holds against the COMPLETE remaining test G. Thus the four raw rows,
source-dependent scalar means and mixed contacts are preserved. One may
take G=G0 exp(s F_f+t H_h) at finite regulator, retaining D^f G0 as well as
s D^f F_f+t D^f H_h. There is no silent source-free substitution for G0.

In particular,

 E D F_f = E R_f+E d^f,
 Cov(D F_f,H_h)
   =Cov(R_f,H_h)+E D^f H_h+Cov(d^f,H_h).                   (2.3)

The middle term is the exact same-read contact term. For a family of
source functions F_t,H_t with derivatives DF,DH at0, the derivative of
their centered covariance is the sum of(2.3) and its F,H interchange.
The analogous formula with a bounded outside test is immediate. These
are first variations of the actual centered covariance, not just its
unnormalized numerator.

If every d_e=0 and H reads none of the links in D^f, the last two terms
of(2.3) vanish. The first response is then Cov(R_f,H), with the actual
profile differences f_p-f_e. If f is constant on each changed star,
R_f=0 and this response is EXACTLY zero. No independence of the Wilson
links or small-field hypothesis is needed.

This gain is not an automatic product of two profile differences. The
remaining Cov(R_f,H) is a full original-law covariance. Nor can disjoint
supports of two nominal labels replace disjointness of their complete
original link reads. When d^f is nonzero, even disjoint H retains its
outer covariance in(2.3).

For reference, the full original energy bound from TURN64 yields a
quantitative finite first-variation estimate. If ||X_e||<=v_e for a
single-link left generator, then

 |D_e h_p|<=v_e sqrt(2h_p),
 ||R_f||_2<=4 sqrt(beta) sum_e v_e omega_e(f),
 omega_e(f)=sum_(p incident e)|f_p-f_e|.                    (2.4)

Here E beta h_p<=8 is a deliberately loose consequence of
E exp(beta h_p/4)<=2^(3/4). If H=beta sum hprof_p h_p and W_h=sum|hprof_p|,
then ||H-EH||_2<=16 W_h. In the divergence-free disjoint-read case,

 |Cov(D F_f,H)|<=64 sqrt(beta) W_h sum_e v_e omega_e(f).     (2.5)

For a smooth physical f, omega_e(f)<=C a [f]_Lip on the original mesh a.
This is one genuine profile-gradient gain. It does not by itself pay the
number of changed links in a fixed four-dimensional physical volume.
For a different calibration c0 sum f h, the two derivative/divergence
terms in(2.2) acquire c0/beta and R_f uses c0. Canonical c0=beta here
always means the ORIGINAL coupling, not a running native coupling.

3. EXACT FINITE CONDITIONAL DRIFT: A CONSTANT STAR IS QUADRATIC

Fix a link e and condition on all other original links, denoted omega.
Each incident plaquette has the form

 h_p(U_e,omega)=1-u.v_p(omega),

where u in S^3 is the quaternion of U_e and v_p in S^3 is the correctly
oriented exterior staple. Put

 v=sum_(p incident e) v_p,  r=beta|v|<=6beta,
 n=v/|v| if v!=0,       m(r)=I_2(r)/I_1(r), m(0)=0.

The ACTUAL conditional law is

 q_omega(du)=exp(r n.u)du / [2 I_1(r)/r],
                    E_q u=m(r)n.                         (3.1)

The r=0 interpretation is Haar and the mean is zero. The normalizer and
Bessel ratio follow by Haar rotation from the standard single-link
Wilson integral, already proved in TURN64. The exterior marginal is
still the exact original one and contains its conditional partition
factor; it is not replaced by an independent boundary law.

Let k(omega)=exp(i theta(omega) a(omega).sigma), |a|=1, and change only
the source read U_e to k U_e. The angle and axis must be independent of
U_e given omega. Let L_k be its real orthogonal action on quaternions.
It satisfies

 (L_k+L_k^t)/2=cos(theta)I,
 ||I-L_k||=2|sin(theta/2)|.                                (3.2)

Define a_f=beta sum_(p incident e) f_p v_p and
delta a_f=beta sum_(p incident e)(f_p-f_e)v_p. The complete source F_f
also has terms independent of e; they cancel in its difference. Direct
use of(3.1)--(3.2) gives the exact conditional drift

 b_f(omega)=E_q[F_f(kU_e,omega)-F_f(U_e,omega)]
 = f_e r m(r)(1-cos theta)
       +m(r) delta a_f.(I-L_k)n.                          (3.3)

For r=0 the entire expression is0, independent of the arbitrary n.
In particular, for a constant profile on the six incident plaquettes,

              b_f=f_e r m(r)(1-cos theta).                 (3.4)

This is nonnegative for f_e>=0 and genuinely quadratic, rather than just
a zero first derivative. A useful uniform estimate is

 |b_f|<=3 beta |f_e| theta^2+beta |theta| omega_e(f),        (3.5)

using0<=m(r)<=1, r<=6beta and the elementary trigonometric inequalities.
No exterior weak-field assumption was made. A flat or balanced-staple
exterior is included without a division by its staple norm.

If H is ANY integrable original observable independent of U_e, conditioning
first gives an exact full-law comparison:

 Cov_mu(F_f(kU_e),H)-Cov_mu(F_f(U_e),H)
                          =Cov_(mu_out)(b_f,H).            (3.6)

Thus the fiber contribution cancels and the remaining outer channel is
explicit. It is not zero merely because b_f is small, positive or quadratic.
For canonical H, a crude bound is16 W_h times the supremum in(3.5), if
that supremum is available. More refined outer estimates remain possible.

The formula extends by summation to a set of free links when no plaquette
reads two of them and every rotation depends only on their common exterior.
Those hypotheses give the actual conditional product and an additive
decomposition of F. It must not be applied link-by-link to simultaneous
changes sharing plaquettes or reading one another's new variables.

4. AN ACTUAL SMOOTH RARE-TAIL SWITCH HAS AN EXTRA FIRST RESPONSE

The preceding cancellation is sensitive to volume preservation, not just
small displacement. Let D0 U=i sigma3 U and put

 D=chi(h(U)) D0,
 0<=chi<=1, chi=0 on[0,a], chi=1 on[b,2],
                  0<a<b<2, chi' nonnegative and not identically zero.

This has the same inner-zero/outer-one switch direction as the native
smooth cutoff. Its Haar divergence is

 d=D0 chi(h(U))=chi'(1-u0) u3.                              (4.1)

Indeed D0 u0=-u3. The map exp[i t chi(h(U))sigma3]U has this vector field
and Jacobian derivative at t=0; for sufficiently small t it is a smooth
invertible map. It is not a Haar-preserving constant rotation.

Take the genuine one-link conditional Wilson law(3.1) with
n=(cos phi,0,0,sin phi), 0<phi<pi, r>0. Six identical exterior staples
V=exp(i phi sigma3) realize r=6beta: set the transverse exterior links
to I and the six opposite parallel links to the required V, with the
appropriate orientation. They are distinct for the stated periods.
Other plaquettes depend only on fixed exterior variables.

Pairing u3 with -u3 in the Haar integral gives

                       E_q d>0.                           (4.2)

In fact on u3>0 the paired integrand is proportional to
2 chi'(1-u0)u3 exp(r cos(phi)u0) sinh(r sin(phi)u3), positive on a
set of positive Haar measure. Let A_e=beta sum_(p incident e)h_p be the
complete conditional canonical action. Equation(1.1) now says

       d/dt E_q A_e(exp[i t chi(h(U))sigma3]U)|_(t=0)
                           = E_q d>0.                     (4.3)

The conditional classical action is stationary at its minimum U=V.
Choose phi small enough that h(V)<a, so the switch even vanishes near
that minimum. Neither fact nulls(4.3). This is an exact compact original
Wilson conditional calculation, not an abstract change of Gaussian
moments. It does not assert this coordinate is the full native B_j map.

There is a legitimate rare-tail bound in this model, with the COMPLETE
conditional denominator. Put Delta=(sqrt(a)-sqrt(h(V)))^2>0. On the
support of chi', triangle inequality gives h(V^(-1)U)>=Delta. Under q,
W=V^(-1)U has the central one-link Wilson law at coupling r. Its radial
Gamma-tilt bound gives E exp[r h(W)/2]<=2^(3/2). Hence

 0<E_q d<=2^(3/2)||chi'||_infinity exp(-r Delta/2).          (4.4)

This demonstrates exactly what must be retained: a switch derivative,
its actual tail support, and its conditional normalizer. A sharper source
statement cannot simply erase the divergence because the switch equals
one on an outer plateau. The derivative lives at its transition.

5. COMPLETE REMAINING TESTS AND THE NATIVE ATTACHMENT

For a non-volume-preserving original vector field, the decisive separated
formula is already(2.3): even if D^f H=0, the original covariance response
contains Cov(d^f,H). For a single changed link and outside H the exact
raw identity is

 E[(D F_f)H]=E[R_f H]+f_e E[d_e H].                        (5.1)

The comparison cannot omit this term, or replace it by its mean, while
later tests read the exterior. A full source package exp(sF+tH) retains
both normalization derivatives and all repeated/contact rows through(2.2).
It needs no analytic continuation of an inverse original record for this
REAL identity, but does need the actual original vector field and its
divergence (or an exact finite change-of-variables equivalent).

For a native free-coordinate integral, Haar in(1.1) is replaced by the
actual Gaussian/coarea density and domain. A moving hard cut produces
the corresponding boundary measure; a smooth cut produces its derivative;
constraints require a tangent vector field or their own response. The
identity with only beta D S is then false unless all these factors are
included. Likewise, the actual effective action has old interactions:
its stationarity does not identify an original bare source with its
current action. No running beta_j replaces beta0 in this calculation.

The same caution applies before changing coordinates if auxiliary records
are retained. For a smooth positive joint density mu(dU)kappa(dxi|U),
holding xi fixed adds D_e log kappa to the right-hand score in(1.1).
Normalization of kappa makes its unweighted mean score zero, not its
pairing with a source/remaining test depending on xi. An independent-seed
representation avoids that density score only by retaining the complete
U-dependence of the reconstructed ancillary reads in D_e G and in D_e.
Thus the original-link conditional calculation(3.3) cannot be applied
after conditioning on an arbitrary native ancillary record as though the
remaining conditional link law were still the same tilted Haar law.

This pinpoints a source-specific next calculation. If the original switch
response can be expressed as an exterior-measurable, divergence-free
rotation of the affected fine links, Sections2--3 give a real profile
gain and a quadratic local drift. If its coefficient reads the acted-on
field, the exact extra row is its divergence/score, illustrated by(4.3).
One must bound that row under the full continued law, with the full
remaining-test algebra and actual primitive reserve. Its required
ownership and source-to-plane read are not supplied by ||H_j|| alone.

6. SCALE CONSEQUENCE AND LIMITS

The deterministic noisy square shows why a curvature-only fixed-noise
L^(-2j) estimate can be too strong. The exact identities here show one
way that integration may recover an additional order, but under stricter
conditions than a small background or a classical saddle equation.

For a smooth physical profile at mesh a, the first term in(2.5) gains one
power a through omega_e(f). A constant-star action instead has the exact
finite O(beta theta^2) drift(3.4). Neither is a general curvature-fourth-
power estimate. Summing over order a^(-4) original roots and retaining
their coherent outer covariance can still defeat the local gain. At a
bare shift theta~g0 polylog exp(-R0), beta0 theta^2 is small; that alone
does not pay the physical root count identified in TURN84.

No theorem in this note supplies the original field response of an
arbitrary stopped/restarted native record, the native cut/coarea score,
or a nonzero reflected continuum channel. The exact positive finite-law
profile and conditional-drift identities are the new constructive
consumers; the actual switch-divergence example identifies the remaining
term which a valid native application has to estimate.
