1. THE COMMON THRESHOLD

At each of a countable set of spatial regulators there are countably
many spacetime action cells. Each one-dimensional law has at most
countably many atoms. Their union is countable, so an R in the
nonempty open interval (R0,R0+1) avoids every atom. This argument
does not require stationarity or any unproved density theorem.

Taking R0>c+log D makes q=exp(c-R) satisfy Dq<1 with a uniform
strict margin. A Boolean event on finitely many comparisons
X_c>=R has its discontinuity set contained in the finite union
of threshold hyperplanes. The limiting ground vector assigns
that set zero mass. No uniform thin-shell estimate is needed
because the temporal approximation is selected separately at
each fixed spatial regulator.

2. THE TWO FINITE-SLAB EXPECTATIONS

The observation slab contains all spatial tiles and both source
supports. It has only two temporal boundary layers. A local
touching-graph step changes the time-cell index by at most one.

For source-cell marks c in A_+, d in A_-, the supports are disjoint,
so the distinct-mark estimate applies. Each actual centered source
cell is bounded by 4M_+(X_c+c) or 4M_-(X_d+c).

For the inside event one sums over:
 - N_+ N_- source-mark pairs;
 - N_+ N_- possible endpoints of a spanning path.

This gives N_+^2 N_-^2 in (4.1).
For exit, replace the second pair count by N_+ N_B, giving
N_+^2 N_- N_B in (4.2). The marks need not lie on that path.
The constants, the leading factor16, the geometric series
q/(1-Dq), and the distance exponents are unchanged.

Both source means are those of the same actual Hamiltonian
ground law. The source is not tilted, independently sampled,
or centered in a conditional reference measure.

At fixed physical spatial volume, N_+,N_-=O(a^-4) and N_B=O(a^-3).
Hence the two prefactors are a^-16 and a^-15 respectively.
For arbitrary finite spatial volume the actual N_B remains in
the formula until the adaptive-buffer argument is used.

3. FIXED-REGULATOR CONVERGENCE OF THE FINITE VECTOR

Fix a once and for all. Restrict delta=a/m with m tending to
infinity. Every coarse time cell then contains exactly m
temporal intervals. The left-sampled X_c and Q_+,Q_- are literal
gauge-invariant original Wilson multiplication observables.
Their temporal transfer parameters are exactly

 beta_time=4a/(g_a^2 delta),  beta_space=4delta/(g_a^2 a).

The final text explicitly assumes continuous or bounded piecewise
continuous time profiles, so the stated quadrature converges.
This precision is necessary: arbitrary bounded profiles sampled
at mesh points would not suffice.

At fixed a, all these coordinates have common compact bounds:
h_p<=2, all relevant time intervals are finite, and each source
reads finitely many plaquettes. The bound may depend arbitrarily
badly on a, g_a and the chosen slab; this causes no logical
problem for the fixed-a temporal limit.

For each m the list of monomials of total degree at most m in
this fixed finite vector is finite. The positive transfer
ground limit permits selection of an arbitrarily large original
period after delta, matching that finite list to the corresponding
ground-chain moments. Requiring this period to be a multiple
of2m, to contain the observation slab without wraparound, and
to have diverging physical length only restricts it to a cofinal
set of large periods. The transfer ground limit remains valid.

For each fixed monomial, its degree eventually appears in the
list; fixed-spatial source convergence then identifies its
continuous-time ground expectation. Polynomials are dense on the
common compact box, hence these finite-period vectors converge
in law to the actual ground vector.

This uses the exact original Wilson normalizer supplied by the
source theorem. It does not identify finite-temperature full
Hamiltonian path laws with finite-temperature Gauss-projected
Wilson path laws. Only their gauge-invariant ground limits
are identified, as required.

4. WEIGHTED CONTINUITY SETS AND CHANGING CENTERS

The finite events E_in and E_exit have zero-probability boundaries
under the limiting vector. The weight

 |(Q_+-E Q_+)(Q_--E Q_-)|

is continuous on its compact coordinate box. First-moment
convergence makes the finite-period ORIGINAL means converge
to the ground means. Replacing those centers changes the weight
uniformly on the fixed-a box.

Weak convergence therefore proves both assertions (5.1).
There is no hidden cutoff-uniform integrability assertion:
boundedness is used only with a fixed spatial regulator.
The finite mesh or finite-period laws may have atoms at R;
only the limiting continuity-set condition is needed.

5. THE ENTIRE TIME CIRCLE

The full bridge event is defined on the literal complete
periodic time board. Any finite self-avoiding path from A_+
to A_- either stays in the observation slab or has a first
exit. In the second case its initial segment lies in the slab,
is entirely bad, and ends on one of its boundary time layers.
Local adjacency cannot skip that layer. The chosen period
keeps the two layers and their outside neighbors distinct.

Thus E_full is pointwise contained in E_in union E_exit.
This is a deterministic graph argument. It does not exchange
an infinite-time event limit with convergence of finite
observables, and does not assume anything about the law outside
the observation slab.

Multiplying by the nonnegative centered source weight and
using the two convergent finite-window expectations gives
exactly (6.2), with any specified error epsilon_a after
choosing delta and then the finite period. The shrinking
temporal mesh can satisfy delta<=a^2 simultaneously.

6. ADAPTIVE BUFFER: CHECK OF THE NEW VOLUME-INDEPENDENT DIAGONAL

Let eta=Dq<1. The boundary count N_B is twice the number of
spatial tiles, independent of the time duration of the slab.
Choose its integer buffer distance k_a so that

 k_a >= d0/a + log(max(1,N_B))/|log eta|.

Because eta is less than one, exponentiation gives

 N_B eta^k_a <= eta^(d0/a).

The exit bound then has prefactor N_+^2 N_-=O(a^-12),
independent of the total spatial volume. The inside bound
has the already established O(a^-16) prefactor. With fixed
compact source geometry satisfying (3.1), both are bounded
by K a^-16 exp(-k/a).

At each a the enlarged observation slab is still finite.
All of its action cells are covered by the original countable
threshold choice. Its larger finite vector may require a much
smaller delta and much larger period, but those choices occur
after the slab is fixed. The proof is therefore valid on any
countable sequence of finite spatial volumes satisfying the
source geometry assumptions.

This does not establish a rate uniform in a prescribed temporal
period, nor an infinite-volume local limit. The final text
preserves this distinction explicitly.
