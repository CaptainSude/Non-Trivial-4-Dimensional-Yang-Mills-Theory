RESULT AND SCOPE

The first-step auxiliary generator of TURN46 has a carrierwise locally
gauge-invariant decomposition with a positive exponential SIZE norm,
uniform in the enclosing volume. Sections1--8 first construct it after
pullback to the coherent weak fine background U, with Y=M(U) and every
retained exterior link equal to its value in U. Section9 gives a separate
direct construction in the independent coarse data Y,e, using bounded
paths in the already retained mixed coarse/exterior datum graph. It is
an exact decomposition of the same auxiliary integral, not a new
approximation to it. Its coefficients are independent of the independent
current J. The complete leading determinant is localized in Section8;
the actual comparison tail remains its controlled invariant whole-box
carrier. The direct coarse-data extension is independently reviewed
separately from the initial fine-background construction.

The construction avoids Taylor expansion in the background link angles.
Instead it expands the relative KKT equation about the feasible background
U itself. A finite polynomial, covariant preconditioner gives a walk
kernel contracting in absolute row AND column norms. Each walk coefficient
reads only a fixed-size neighborhood, and is covariant before summation.
Nonlinear trees and scalar loops then preserve covariance carrier by
carrier. This supplies the missing localization of saddle/background reads
on ONE COHERENT WEAK-BACKGROUND DOMAIN and its gauge saturation.

The theorem does not extend that domain to the entire native domains
whose representatives have only local curvature/reconstruction bounds.
It does not prove that composing a carrier with the nonlocal B116 response
preserves its carrier norm; that requires a compatible localization of the
response. It is not arbitrary-depth RG closure or a continuum theorem.

All constants below may depend on fixed L, group, and the finitely many
bulk/boundary read types, but not on the number N of active blocks.

2. THE FINITE POLYNOMIAL THAT PRODUCES AN ABSOLUTELY CONVERGENT WALK

This is the essential additional step beyond simply saying that a
covariant operator has an exponentially decaying inverse.

At U=I, M_0 is a real symmetric, finite-range KKT matrix with

       ||M_0||_2<=b,   ||M_0^-1||_2<=a^-1,    a>0.    (2.1)

Choose a fixed tau>0 for which B_0=I-tau M_0^t M_0 has spectral norm
q<1. For example tau=b^-2 gives q<=1-a^2/b^2. Let R_0 bound its range.
At fixed L, a row of B_0^p reads at most C(1+p)^4 color/location blocks.
Cauchy--Schwarz and the spectral bound therefore give

 max(row absolute sum, column absolute sum of B_0^p)
                         <= C(1+p)^2 q^p.             (2.2)

Block operator norms may be used throughout; the fixed color factor is
included in C. Choose the integer p ONCE so that (2.2)<1/16. Define the
covariant finite-range matrices

 B_V=I-tau M_V^t M_V,
 K_V=B_V^p,
 A_V=tau sum_(j=0)^(p-1) B_V^j M_V^t.                 (2.3)

They obey A_V M_V=I-K_V exactly. All entries of K_V and A_V are local
analytic functions of V in a fixed neighborhood of their operator legs.
Their read sets have cardinality at most D_p, where p and D_p are FIXED
independently of N. Summing internal matrix indices before estimating an
entry is allowed and essential: it preserves covariance and its fixed
read set while retaining the cancellation in (2.2).

For complex weak V, the finite polynomial perturbation estimate gives

 ||K_V-K_0||_row,col <= C_p r.

The same estimate with entrywise suprema over the weak domain holds:
there are only a fixed number of possible nonzero columns in each row,
and each entry has a uniform O(r) bound. Reduce r so that the resulting
absolute row/column sums are <1/8. Assign each matrix entry to the full
fixed neighborhood containing its two legs and all its background reads.
Choose kappa_0>0 with exp(kappa_0 D_p)<2. Then

     ||K||_(matrix carrier,kappa_0)<1/4.               (2.4)

The norm sums exp(kappa_0|X|) times the supremum of each matrix coefficient
over its local weak background domain, with either operator leg fixed.
Thus it is a STRICT SUPPORT SIZE norm, not merely a distance-weighted
kernel estimate. Each entry is covariant between its two color legs,
because every polynomial factor in (2.3) is. The entries of A have a
finite, possibly large norm in the same algebra.

Products are assigned to unions of their connected read sets. The shared
operator index makes those unions connected and exp(kappa|union|) is at
most the product of the weights. Row and column sums consequently give
the usual submultiplicative matrix-carrier norm. Therefore

            M_V^-1=sum_(n>=0) K_V^n A_V               (2.5)

converges absolutely in this carrier algebra. Each walk is separately
covariant, has its literal background support, and carries no flat
noncovariant inverse. Equation (2.5) is an exact inverse by (2.3).

3. NONLINEAR SADDLE TREES WITH LOCAL BACKGROUND COEFFICIENTS

Expand (1.2) in the RELATIVE VARIABLES z, holding V independent:

       G_V(z)=b_V+M_V z+N_V(z),     N_V(z)=sum_(n>=2) N_(V,n)[z^n].

No Taylor expansion in V is made. All tensor color indices at a given
site are kept together, so covariance is not broken by choosing separate
color monomials as carriers.

Using (2.3), the stationarity equation is exactly

        z=K_V z-A_V b_V-A_V N_V(z),
        z=-R_V b_V-R_V N_V(z),   R_V=sum K_V^n A_V.     (3.1)

Expand (3.1) in rooted trees, with seeds -R b and nonlinear vertices
-R N_n. Every R is its convergent walk expansion (2.5). Each diagram is
covariant, reads the union of its finite background vertex/read sets,
and its support is connected after including its output position.

Here are sufficient quantitative majorants, including the support roots
that are absent from an ordinary output-only norm. Use both external-leg
rooted bounds for all tensors, just as in analytic_tree_norm_turn41.
The finite local tensors have these bounds because their read sizes and
transpose incidences are bounded. For a scalar majorant, first take
kappa_1<kappa_0 and leave a fixed positive difference unused. Summing

       w(t)=a t+b sum_(n>=2) C^n w(t)^n,               (3.2)

where t bounds r, and a,b,C are fixed carrier constants. It has w(0)=0
and an analytic small solution w(t)=O(t) on a common t-disc. Choose r
inside that disc.

To root a summed scalar graph at ANY background cell in its read set,
mark a vertex or walk segment whose fixed read set contains that cell.
The finite read incidence bounds the choices at a mark. Cut the diagram
at that mark and use the any-leg tensor bounds on its branches. A marked
walk sum is bounded by sum_(n>=0)(n+1) k^n=(1-k)^-2. A marked nonlinear
tree sum is bounded by the derivative of (3.2), whose denominator
1-b sum_(n>=2)n C^n w^(n-1) stays positive. A spare fraction of
kappa_0-kappa_1 also pays the finite multiplicity of possible sites in

unmarked l2 bound would not suffice.

Consequently z_*(V) has a common-radius covariant, connected carrier

Every scalar local action vertex, physical plaquette insertion, or their

6. WHICH GAUGE DOMAIN IS ACTUALLY OBTAINED

Each elementary coefficient is defined on its strictly local weak U
read set. Its covariance is an algebraic covariance identity of (1.2),
local derivatives, polynomial preconditioners and invariant contractions.
For real compact gauges the color-ball integral has an exact real
change-of-variables identity. For a complex gauge taking two weak
representatives into each other, extend that identity as follows.

Take a fixed joint Taylor order in z, the physical scalar sources, and
the bounded cluster amplitudes. At that fixed order the integrand has
finite maximal degree in xi: (5.1) pairs relative degree n with positive
z degree n-2 or n, and each fixed source order also has a finite xi degree
at fixed z order. The resulting integrated coefficients are contractions
of invariant Gaussian-ball moment tensors. This is a coefficient-level
argument; no value of V at a possibly huge complex-transformed xi is used.
The q-dimensional truncated Gaussian ball has O(q)-invariant moments;
each even moment is a scalar multiple of the sum of pairings of the
invariant bilinear form and odd moments vanish. These tensors are also
invariant under the complexified orthogonal adjoint action. Every
coefficient is therefore an algebraic invariant for ANY such complex
gauge with both local weak representatives defined. The convergent
amplitude series proves the identity for the activities, and the
convergent cluster series proves it for F_X. One does not deform a real
Gaussian ball by a nonunitary complex gauge.

It follows that F_X defines a consistent invariant analytic function on
the gauge saturation of its local weak-background tube: choose a weak
representative, evaluate there, and use the just-proved identity on
overlaps. Its bound is the same representative supremum. Pulling back
through the bounded forest section promotes the residual-root identity
to the full fine gauge identity, with the complete forest paths included
in the carrier. Independent J can be adjoined with arbitrary admitted
radius since this pullback does not read J.

THIS IS A RESTRICTED DOMAIN THEOREM. It does not show that every field in
the full B109/B116/B119 regular orbit domains has a weak representative
on every large carrier with a radius independent of its size. It also
does not localize the composite U_s=exp(i chi_s(U,J,B))U: admissibility
of that endpoint, proved in TURN46, is a value/domain statement and
does not make chi_s a finite-read substitution. The new catalogue gives
an actual gauge-invariant input family to investigate there; it is not
an automatic import of the native theorem.

7. CONSEQUENCES AND LIMITS

This closes a specific finite-step defect: the actual weak-background
auxiliary generator now has individual local gauge identities, exact sum
identity, and a uniform positive exponential carrier-size norm. The
previous global-color-only averaging can be omitted on this domain.
The carriers here are functions of the pulled-back FINE background U.
Their sum depends on U only through Y=M(U) and the retained exterior,
but individual carriers need not be constant on a fiber of this map.
Thus this is not yet a strict-support decomposition solely in independent
coarse link coordinates Y. Descending individual carriers requires a
specified local covariant coarse-to-fine anchor, or another argument.
For a semisimple group, each invariant carrier's linear datum jet at
identity vanishes, since simultaneous constant conjugation has no
invariant one-color covector. Its value at identity need not vanish;
source/vacuum constants are retained explicitly.

The cycle obstruction in TURN46 is not contradicted: that obstruction
concerned a uniformly bounded support-preserving projector on arbitrary
decompositions. This construction rebuilds the actual saddle and
auxiliary potential from local covariant equations, allows bounded
neighborhood support enlargement, and uses a possibly small positive
localization exponent. It is specialized to the gapped first-step KKT
geometry and a coherent weak representative.

It does not prove the same uniform KKT gap, polynomial degree, carrier
exponent, weak radius or cutoff transport at arbitrarily many scales.
No bad-history branch, original-measure summation, nonzero separated
continuum witness, or Yang--Mills existence theorem follows from this
one-step localization alone.

8. THE COMPLETE LEADING PREFACTOR ALSO HAS INVARIANT LOCAL CARRIERS

This addition addresses the unmarked generated action, not only the
normalized source logarithm. It was suggested in the root review and
uses the SAME covariant blocked walk, so requires no new domain.

Let m_out be the number of real coarse output coordinates, let D_KKT
be the real dimension of the full fine-chord/multiplier KKT space, and
let M_*=[L_* Q_*^t;Q_* 0] be the actual Lagrangian KKT matrix in LEFT
RELATIVE SADDLE COORDINATES. Its constrained Hessian is H_T. Relative
left tangent frames are invariant orthonormal frames at b=0, and the
relative coarse log derivative at Y is the identity in the coarse Haar
tangent frames. Therefore the exact TURN46 coarea calculation gives

      A0(U)=J_*(U)/sqrt(det_T H_T)
            =c_geo/[det(Q_*Q_*^t) det_T H_T]^(1/2).    (8.1)

There is no exponential-coordinate Haar factor at this origin: the
derivative of exp at zero is identity and left multiplication is an
isometry preserving Haar. c_geo retains the normalized Haar-volume
conventions and the already integrated G0 factor. This is the reason
that a determinant formula in other, absolute angle coordinates cannot
simply be copied without its metric/coordinate factors.

The elementary KKT determinant identity in these frames is

       (-1)^m_out det M_* = det(Q_*Q_*^t) det_T H_T.   (8.2)

For proof, choose a real orthonormal decomposition X=T direct-sum N.
In the order T,N,Y the first block is H_T. Eliminate its off-diagonal
blocks; the remaining N,Y determinant has the form
det[(C,Q_N^t;Q_N,0)]=(-1)^m_out(det Q_N)^2, independently of C.

and analytically continues to the chosen weak complex branch. Hence

     -log A0=-log c_geo+.5 log[(-1)^m_out det M_*],    (8.3)

with the branch positive inside the logarithm at the real reference.

The anchor M_V has the same determinant sign. Using B_V from (2.3),

 log[(-1)^m_out det M_V]
       =-(D_KKT/2)log tau-.5 sum_(n>=1) Tr B_V^n/n.  (8.4)

Indeed I-B_V=tau M_V^t M_V=tau M_V^2; the real-flat equality follows
by its positive spectral logarithm. Both sides analytically continue
on the connected weak domain where K_V=B_V^p has norm <1/4. That bound
places the spectrum of B_V in the open unit disc and fixes the analytic
log branch. Formula (8.4) does not assume ||B_V||_row<1.

To obtain a locally summable scalar catalogue, group n=kp+r with
1<=r<=p and k>=0 before estimating. Then

            B_V^n=K_V^k B_V^r.                       (8.5)

Every B_V^r with r<=p is a fixed finite-range, finite-read covariant
matrix with a finite carrier norm C_p. K_V has carrier norm theta<1/4.

the trace. Each term is an invariant loop with its connected read union.
The scalar rooted-support bound is controlled by

       C_p sum_(r=1)^p sum_(k>=0) (k+1) theta^k/(kp+r),

which is finite. The factor k+1 marks a segment whose bounded read
contains the prescribed support root. Thus (8.4) is an absolutely
convergent INVARIANT SIZE-CARRIER expansion, uniform in N.

Finally put Delta=M_*-M_V, in the shared relative color index spaces.
The saddle tree construction gives ||Delta||_carrier<=Cr. After reducing
r once, ||M_V^-1 Delta||_carrier<1/2. Consequently

 log[(-1)^m_out det M_*]
   =log[(-1)^m_out det M_V]
       +sum_(n>=1) (-1)^(n+1) Tr[(M_V^-1 Delta)^n]/n.  (8.6)

The identity is the exact determinant identity and its branch is fixed

loop marking proves the uniform scalar carrier bound.

All constants in (8.3)--(8.6) are retained. The D_KKT log tau constant
can be assigned to the corresponding single coordinate blocks;

dependence on the artificial tau cancels in the exact total (8.4).

Combining this invariant prefactor with s, F and D gives a carrierwise

generated action on the stated fine-background domain, including its

descend separately through U->(M(U),e(U)), as explained in Section7.
The prefactor family has an O(1) rooted value bound and is multiplied

9. DIRECT COARSE-DATUM CARRIERS BY A BOUNDED PATH ANCHOR

This separately labeled extension removes the descent issue stated in
Section7 for the PRECISE first-step box/collar geometry already used.
It does so by rerunning the covariant construction with a different
anchor; it does not assert that the individual fine-U carriers of
Sections1--8 happen to be constant on ordinary-average fibers.

9.1. Exact local datum graph and its connectivity.

The input is now the independent framed ordinary coarse links Y on ALL
coarse bonds with at least one active block endpoint, together with the
retained fine exterior links e on the COMPLETE two-block read collar.
Make the mixed datum graph with those coarse bonds as edges joining
coarse centers and the retained exterior fine bonds as fine edges. A
fine vertex that is also a coarse center is one and the same vertex.
Every coarse-root frame and exterior fine-vertex frame is retained.

For each free fine chord b=(x,y), its block-forest paths end at roots
r_x,r_y within distance C_L. The following bounded path statement is
already established in gaussian_coarse_kernel_turn40 Section2 (the
proof of (2.1)--(2.2)), using exactly this complete collar:

 * If r_x=r_y, the empty path suffices.
 * If both roots are active centers, they are in the same or adjacent
   owner blocks, so retained ordinary coarse rows connect them by a
   path with at most a fixed number of coarse edges.
 * If one is a geometric fine boundary root, join the active center
   to an exterior coarse center by at most two retained coarse rows,
   then use the fixed exterior strip to the boundary root.
 * If both are geometric boundary roots, the same fixed exterior
   strip connects them.

All paths have length <=C_L and lie within distance C_L of b. In the
last two cases a boundary vertex first moves out through its retained
crossing edge, travels in the exterior layer/collar, and returns if
necessary. The fixed odd L, cubical union of complete owner blocks,
and retained two-block footprint ensure that the exterior center and
this detour are available. This is also the exact path connectivity
used by the preceding Gaussian-gap proof; it introduces no new collar.
Small boxes have only finitely many extra local types and are included
by the same fixed C_L. Choose one such path gamma_b for each local
geometry/orientation type; reverse paths for reversed chords.

Each path is a path in the MIXED DATUM GRAPH. An ordinary Y edge is
used as a datum group element, not replaced by the fine straight-path
product that happens to join its endpoints. This does not alter the
ordinary averaging map M appearing in the constraint.

9.2. A covariant anchor that need not be feasible.

Define the section field V(Y,e) by

  V_b=ordered product of Y and e along gamma_b on free chords,
  V_b=I on G0 forest edges,       V_b=e_b on fixed edges.      (9.1)

All values are in the compact group for real data and in its complex

        ||log V||<=C_L r_d.                          (9.2)

The constants are independent of the enclosing box. Endpoint gauge

 V(Y^h,e^h)_b=h(r_x)V(Y,e)_b h(r_y)^-1,              (9.3)

while the forest links remain I under the usual root-constant lift.
The gauges at exterior coarse centers agree with their fine-vertex

In general M(V(Y,e)) is NOT Y. This is harmless and is retained. Set

 F_(Y,e)(c)=log[M(exp(c)V(Y,e))Y^-1],
 G_(Y,e)(c,lambda)=(d_c S_e(exp(c)V)+DF(c)^t lambda,F(c)).   (9.4)

     G(0)=(d_c S_e(V), log[M(V)Y^-1])=O(r_d).        (9.5)

These components are local analytic covariant functions. M_d=D_zG(0)

The coarse constraint logarithm transforms by conjugation at the initial
coarse endpoint, including when its value at c=0 is nonzero.
