A CONTROLLED TEMPORAL LIMIT AND THE NONLINEAR GRADIENT FORM -- TURN66
20 October 2026. Root derivation.

STATUS AND PURPOSE

The exact logarithm of a Wilson transfer matrix at finite isotropic
spacing is not a differential Hamiltonian. This note does not replace
it by one. Instead it proves, at a FIXED finite spatial lattice, the
continuous-time limit of an actual anisotropic Wilson family, including
its ground-state observable variance and adjacent-increment form.

For every fixed smooth gauge-invariant spatial F the result is

 lim_(delta->0) d_delta(F)/delta
       =kappa int Omega(U)^2 |grad F(U)|^2 dU.            (0.1)

Here d_delta is the original Wilson ground-state adjacent increment,
delta is the actual temporal spacing, and Omega is the ground state
of the rigorously obtained finite-spatial Hamiltonian kappa K+V.
The coefficients are not a finite deterministic template net. They
are the actual nonlinear derivatives of F.

This permits an anisotropic diagonal construction IF the remaining
spatial physical estimates can be proved. Those estimates, the
physical variance floor, applicability of the existing isotropic
Bałaban inputs to this sequence, and continuous Euclidean symmetry
are not supplied. This is not a YM4 existence theorem.

Primary context checked this turn:
M. Creutz, Gauge fixing, the transfer matrix, and confinement on a
lattice, Phys. Rev. D15 (1977),1128,
 https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.15.1128
The primary abstract explicitly identifies the Hamiltonian through
the temporal-spacing limit. The finite-spatial spectral and
observable-form argument below is provided directly, not imported
from that abstract as an unverified uniform continuum theorem.

1. FIXED SPATIAL MODEL AND EXACT WILSON NORMALIZATION

Fix a finite nondegenerate spatial periodic graph with E positive
edges. Let X=SU(2)^E with normalized Haar measure and Pauli/2 product
metric. Write

 K=-Delta_X=sum_e K_e >=0,

so spin j on one link has K_e eigenvalue j(j+1).
Let P be the normalized site-gauge projection. Work on H_phys=P L2(X).

Fix kappa>0 and a smooth real gauge-invariant V>=0. V is bounded
because X is compact. Its value and all derivative constants are
FIXED during delta->0. The intended V is a finite spatial Wilson
plaquette potential. Adding a constant to make a real V nonnegative
would not change normalized ground-state expectations.

For delta>0 define

 beta_delta=2/(kappa delta),
 M_delta=exp(-delta V/2),
 Q_delta=product_e q_(beta_delta)*,
 T_delta=M_delta Q_delta P M_delta.                     (1.1)

The one-link normalized kernel is
q_beta(U)=exp[-beta(1-Tr U/2)]/c(beta),
c(beta)=2 exp(-beta) I_1(beta)/beta.

On H_phys, T_delta=M_delta Q_delta M_delta. It is a positive,
trace-class contraction with a strictly positive real kernel.
Its leading eigenvalue lambda_delta>0 is simple, with positive
normalized eigenvector Omega_delta. Gauge invariance follows from
the projection (or uniqueness).

For a temporal period N the ORIGINAL anisotropic Wilson integral
has partition function

 Z_(delta,N)=c(beta_delta)^(N|E|) Tr_phys(T_delta^N).     (1.2)

Thus taking N->infinity at fixed delta gives the literal Wilson
ground-state slice law Omega_delta^2 dU and its transition law.
For a real gauge-invariant smooth F, with w_delta=M_delta Omega_delta,

 d_delta(F)
 = (1/2)E_delta[(F_1-F_0)^2]
 = (1/(2lambda_delta)) int_X int_X
       Q_delta(U,V)(F(V)-F(U))^2 w_delta(U)w_delta(V)dU dV.
                                                               (1.3)

The gauge projection may be removed from this integral because
F and w_delta are gauge invariant. The electric kernel in (1.3)
is the unprojected product Q_delta, not an altered gauge-fixed
probability law. Its scalar normalizers are already exact.

2. THE ELECTRIC GENERATOR LIMIT, WITH A DOMINATING BOUND

On one-link spin j the normalized electric eigenvalue is

       r_j(beta)=I_(2j+1)(beta)/I_1(beta) in(0,1].

For each fixed j,

 r_j(beta)=1-2j(j+1)/beta+O_j(beta^-2), beta->infinity.   (2.1)

One direct verification uses U=cos(theta)I+i sin(theta)n.sigma.
The q_beta radial density is proportional to
exp[-beta(1-cos(theta))]sin^2(theta), 0<=theta<=pi.
Rescaling theta by beta^-1/2 and applying Laplace expansion gives
E theta^2=3/beta+O(beta^-2), E theta^4=O(beta^-2).
The fixed-spin normalized character has expansion
1-(2/3)j(j+1)theta^2+O_j(theta^4), proving (2.1).
The complement of a fixed small angular neighborhood is exponentially
small, so the remainders are controlled.

The previously proved exact estimate, independently obtained by
1-cos(k theta)<=k^2(1-cos theta), is

              0<=1-r_j(beta)<=2j(j+1)/beta.             (2.2)

For a product representation, 1-product r_j<=sum(1-r_j).
Consequently, with B_delta=(I-Q_delta)/delta,

                    0<=B_delta<=kappa K                (2.3)

in their simultaneous Peter--Weyl basis, and

       B_delta f ->kappa K f in L2 for every f in D(K). (2.4)

Dominated convergence in that basis proves (2.4), since the absolute
eigenvalue multiplier is bounded by kappa times the K eigenvalue.
The same argument in every fixed Sobolev norm gives, for smooth f,

       (Q_delta f-f)/delta ->kappa Delta_X f in C^0.     (2.5)

Indeed choose a Sobolev exponent above dim(X)/2 and use the spectral
domination there before applying Sobolev embedding. The dimension
and embedding constants are fixed in this temporal limit.

We shall also use that r_j(beta) decreases with j. Adjacent Bessel
ratios I_(nu+1)/I_nu are strictly between0 and1 for nu>=1, as follows
from their representation as the mean of x in(-1,1) under density
proportional to exp(beta x)(1-x^2)^(nu-1/2), together with positivity.
Their products give the required monotonicity. This is an order-in-
spin statement, not an unproved uniform large-spin asymptotic.

3. GROUND-STATE CONVERGENCE

Set

 A_delta=(I-T_delta)/delta,
 e_delta=(1-lambda_delta)/delta,
 H=kappa K+V on H_phys.                                (3.1)

H is self-adjoint on D(K) intersect H_phys, with compact resolvent.
Its heat kernel on the full compact product is strictly positive;
its unique positive ground state Omega is gauge invariant and smooth.
Denote its ground energy by E0.

For every smooth physical phi,

                        A_delta phi ->H phi in L2.     (3.2)

To see this, use the exact decomposition

 A_delta phi
   =[(1-M_delta^2)/delta]phi
                         +M_delta B_delta M_delta phi.

The first term converges to V phi. The second converges to kappa K phi
by (2.4), because M_delta phi->phi in D(K) and
||B_delta f||2<=kappa||K f||2.

The variational principle, using any fixed smooth trial vector,
shows e_delta is bounded as delta->0. More precisely, using the
smooth ground vector Omega of H,

                         limsup e_delta<=E0.            (3.3)

For compactness, the exact quadratic-form identity gives

 e_delta
  =int [(1-M_delta^2)/delta]Omega_delta^2
                         +<w_delta,B_delta w_delta>
  >=<w_delta,B_delta w_delta>.                          (3.4)

Let Pi_R be the finite-rank spectral projection of K onto [0,R].
Outside it, some edge Casimir is larger than R/|E|. By spin
monotonicity, B_delta there is at least
(1-r_(j_R)(beta_delta))/delta, where j_R is the smallest half-integer
with j_R(j_R+1)>R/|E|. By (2.1), this lower bound tends to
kappa j_R(j_R+1), which tends to infinity with R.

Equation (3.4) therefore implies

 lim_(R->infinity) limsup_(delta->0)
                   ||(I-Pi_R)w_delta||2=0.              (3.5)

Also ||w_delta-Omega_delta||2<=||M_delta-I||infty->0.
The normalized Omega_delta are consequently precompact in L2.

For any convergent subsequence with e_delta->e and Omega_delta->Psi,
self-adjointness and (3.2) give
<H phi,Psi>=e<phi,Psi> for every smooth physical phi.
This core identity implies Psi in D(H), H Psi=e Psi.
Its norm is1. Thus e>=E0, while (3.3) gives e<=E0.
Uniqueness and positivity of the ground state imply Psi=Omega.
Every subsequence has that limit, so

 Omega_delta ->Omega in L2,
 w_delta ->Omega in L2,
 e_delta ->E0,
 -delta^-1 log(lambda_delta) ->E0.                      (3.6)

No uniform-in-spatial-cutoff compactness is being asserted. The
finite-rank and Sobolev constants used here may depend on the
fixed graph, V, and kappa.

For every bounded F, (3.6) also implies convergence of its slice
mean and variance to those in the measure Omega^2 dU.

4. THE NONLINEAR ADJACENT FORM: BOTH SIDES OF THE LIMIT

Define the electric squared-increment function

 C_delta F(U)
   =delta^-1 int Q_delta(U,V)(F(V)-F(U))^2 dV
   =[Q_delta F^2-2F Q_delta F+F^2](U)/delta.

For a smooth real F, (2.5) and the product rule give

             C_delta F ->2kappa |grad F|^2 uniformly.   (4.1)

Arithmetic--geometric mean and symmetry of Q_delta in (1.3) give

 d_delta(F)/delta
  <= (1/(2lambda_delta))int w_delta(U)^2 C_delta F(U)dU
  =:J_delta(F).                                        (4.2)

By (3.6),(4.1),

              J_delta(F)->kappa int Omega^2|grad F|^2. (4.3)

This proves the upper bound. The difference in (4.2) is EXACTLY

 J_delta(F)-d_delta(F)/delta
 = (1/(4lambda_delta delta))int int
      Q_delta(U,V)(F(V)-F(U))^2
                         (w_delta(U)-w_delta(V))^2 dU dV.
                                                               (4.4)

It is nonnegative, and tends to zero without assuming uniform
pointwise bounds on derivatives of Omega_delta.

Here are the estimates. Smooth F is Lipschitz on the fixed compact
product, so

 |F(V)-F(U)|^2<=C_F sum_e h(U_e V_e^-1).                (4.5)

Indeed Pauli/2 geodesic distance squared on SU2 is at most a fixed
constant times h, and product squared distance is the sum.
For one link the scalar inequality
h exp(-beta h/2)<=2/(e beta), together with the normalizer ratio
c(beta/2)/c(beta)<=2^(3/2), gives

        h(W) q_beta(W)<=C beta^-1 q_(beta/2)(W).         (4.6)

The normalizer-ratio bound is the already proved decreasing
Gamma(3/2) tilt estimate and holds for every beta>0.

Let Q_delta,e^half be the product convolution with beta_delta/2
on edge e and beta_delta on the others. It is a positive self-adjoint
Markov contraction, and tends strongly to I as delta->0.
Equations (4.4)--(4.6), with beta_delta delta=2/kappa, yield

 0<=J_delta(F)-d_delta(F)/delta
   <=C_(F,kappa) sum_e
            <w_delta,(I-Q_delta,e^half)w_delta>.         (4.7)

Each summand tends to zero: w_delta->Omega strongly, the operators
I-Q_delta,e^half are uniformly bounded, and
Q_delta,e^half Omega->Omega. There are finitely many edges.
This proves (0.1) as an equality.

Unlike an unjustified finite-step weighted-gradient bound, this proof
retains the exact nonlinear square, proves the limiting generator,
and bounds the AM--GM defect in the actual ground-state pair law.

5. THE TIME-CONTINUOUS CORRELATIONS ALSO CONVERGE

For completeness the generator convergence above also establishes
the actual temporal evolution. Since A_delta>=0 and (3.2) holds on
a core of H, the resolvents converge strongly. One direct proof is
to take f=(H+1)phi for a core vector phi:

 (A_delta+1)^-1 f-phi
       =(A_delta+1)^-1(H-A_delta)phi ->0.

Such f form a dense set and the resolvent norms are at most1.
The spectral functional calculus then yields
exp(-t A_delta)->exp(-t H) strongly for each t>0.

The spectrum of delta A_delta is contained in [0,1].
For n->infinity, the elementary scalar estimate
sup_(0<=x<=1)|(1-x)^n-exp(-nx)|<=C/n
shows

          T_delta^floor(t/delta) ->exp(-t H) strongly.  (5.1)

Replacing floor(t/delta)delta by t in the exponential is harmless
uniformly away from t=0. Ground-state normalization using (3.6) gives

 (T_delta/lambda_delta)^floor(t/delta)
                         ->exp[-t(H-E0)] strongly.      (5.2)

Together with Omega_delta->Omega and bounded multiplication
insertions, this gives convergence of every fixed finite
ground-state correlation word at fixed nonnegative time gaps.
Zero gaps are interpreted by multiplication, and (5.2) is not
needed there. At fixed delta, finite temporal periods approximate
these ground-state correlations by the simple leading-eigenvalue
limit of the exact trace. The period may be chosen as large as
needed after choosing delta.

The resulting fixed-spatial theory has its positive Hamiltonian,
time-continuous correlations and reflection-positive temporal
forms. This is still a spatial lattice theory.

6. WHAT THE DIAGONAL ARGUMENT LEGITIMATELY PERMITS

Let a label a sequence of finite spatial lattices, with smooth
bounded probes F_a, coefficients kappa_a>0 and potentials V_a>=0.
The finite-dimensional constants may grow arbitrarily as a->0.
For each fixed a, the preceding theorem applies.

For any prescribed positive error eta_a and any specified finite
set of bounded smooth probes/correlation words, one can choose
delta_a small enough that their Wilson ground-state means,
variances and fixed-time correlations approximate the Hamiltonian
ones within eta_a, and their d_delta_a/delta_a values approximate
the corresponding gradient forms within eta_a.
One can then choose finite temporal periods N_a large enough to
approximate these finitely many ground-state quantities, including
the adjacent quotient, to the desired tolerance.
Taking delta_a<=a^2, and N_a delta_a tending to infinity if desired,
is compatible with these choices.

This is a diagonal existence statement for the regulated temporal
approximation. It does not assert a numerical convergence rate
uniform in a, or justify exchanging the two limits.

For the usual SU2 normalization set

 kappa_a=g_a^2/(2a),
 V_a=[4/(g_a^2 a)]sum_(spatial p) h_p.

Then the actual anisotropic Wilson coefficients in this argument are

 beta_time=4a/(g_a^2 delta),
 beta_space=4delta/(g_a^2 a).                            (6.1)

At delta=a both equal4/g_a^2, but our temporal argument fixes a
and sends delta->0. It does not prove that the finite isotropic
Hamiltonian already equals kappa_a K+V_a.

In particular the isotropic Bałaban induction, its probability
estimates and its running-coupling dictionary cannot be copied
to (6.1) without checking their anisotropic hypotheses. The earlier
orientation-dependent energy inequality remains true, but its
spatial bound E beta_space h<=3/2 becomes weak as delta->0.
No uniform spatial magnetic energy bound for Omega_a is smuggled
in through that degenerating inequality.

7. THE RESULTING PHYSICAL ESTIMATES STILL TO PROVE

The finite-spatial ground-state identity is now legitimate:

 <(F-mean F)Omega,(H-E0)(F-mean F)Omega>
                       =kappa int Omega^2|grad F|^2.    (7.1)

It follows by integration by parts and H Omega=E0 Omega; the
potential and ground-energy terms cancel exactly. It also agrees
with (0.1). For v=Var_(Omega^2)(F)>0, spectral Jensen gives

 Cov(F(0),F(t))
   >=v exp[-t kappa E_(Omega^2)|grad F|^2/v].            (7.2)

Thus a physical family with

 Var_(Omega_a^2)(F_a)>=v_*>0,
 kappa_a E_(Omega_a^2)|grad F_a|^2<=C                  (7.3)

would have a positive fixed-time reflected norm and could be
approximated by actual anisotropic Wilson regulators as above.
For kappa_a=g_a^2/(2a), the second target is
E|grad F_a|^2<=C'a/g_a^2, in the unscaled Pauli/2 link metric.

Neither estimate in (7.3) has been proved here for the physically
calibrated nonlinear magnetic probe. The finite-template gauge
entropy obstruction does not apply to this local gradient identity,
but an actual Wilson/Hamiltonian averaged derivative theorem is
still needed. The deterministic large-Hessian example does not
by itself disprove (7.3).

Even (7.3) would only supply a nonzero temporal reflection witness
in a compatible spatial limit. It would not by itself establish
positive excitation energy, uniqueness of vacuum, continuous
spatial rotations, full Euclidean symmetry, a local continuum
observable algebra, or the required Yang--Mills identification.

This note closes a controlled finite-spatial temporal bridge.
It does not replace the full goal by constructing only a
Hamiltonian spatial lattice theory. Nontrivial YM4 existence,
with all its remaining physical and reconstruction obligations,
is still unproved.
