18 October 2026. Short audit; no new normalized component claim.

1. WHAT WOULD SUFFICE

Let C be a closed reflection cell in the original bare Wilson lattice,
and let p_C(U) be a nonnegative bounded function of links in C (with
the usual shared-face convention). Suppose the bad-history contribution
in that cell is dominated by p_C and its reflected disseminated product
has the source estimate

  Z(prod_reflections p_C) <= exp[-m p_bad+C_+ V_stop],
  Z(1) >= exp[-C_- V_stop],   V_stop=m v_C.

The bare reflection-positive chessboard inequality then gives

  <p_C> <= exp[-p_bad+(C_++C_-)v_C].

Thus an extensive stability bound is not itself fatal: its volume
factor disappears after the chessboard root. The source suppression
must dominate the actual cell volume v_C, including any logarithmic
collar. This argument also needs a positive majorant for the marked
numerator, not just an unmarked event probability.

2. THE HISTORY INDICATOR IS NOT YET SUCH A BARE LOCAL FUNCTION

A surviving component of the R-resampled description is an event in
the auxiliary history. It need not be a deterministic event of the
original Wilson field. Reflection positivity of the bare measure does
not imply reflection positivity of an arbitrary positive stochastic
resampling law or of its joint auxiliary law.

Even if a history is triggered by some initial bad plaquette, the
proxy 'at least one initial bad plaquette in the ancestor cell' is
too weak. A fixed physical cell contains exponentially many bare
plaquettes as the ultraviolet inverse coupling grows, whereas a
single initial bad-field gain is exp[-c(log gamma_0)^p]. A naive
union over that initial mesh can exceed one. The lifetime renewal
implemented by R is precisely what this proxy throws away.

3. A PRECISE POSSIBLE DUAL-KERNEL ROUTE

If the fixed source-free positive mass-preserving R construction is
written as a Markov kernel on the finite-regulator original measure,
its adjoint defines

  p_bad(U_bare)=P(auxiliary bad history | U_bare),  0<=p_bad<=1.

Its expectation is the desired unmarked bad probability. To use bare
chessboard one must additionally prove BOTH:

 (a) A positive closed-cell local domination or approximation for this
     dual function, with a summably small error for histories whose
     complete carrier leaves the cell.
 (b) The reflected copies' product is bounded by the same disseminated
     source/lifetime majorant, with independent local reference factors
     or an exact compatible factorization.

These are not consequences of mass preservation. In particular the
localized connected coefficients of the unnormalized T expansion can
have signs. They are not probabilities of localized histories.
Localizing an actual conditional probability may involve its component
reference denominator, whereas our nilpotent history estimate purposely
never divides by T(X)1. The source's absolute opened-history bound does
not alone bound that new ratio or prove (a),(b).

Primary check: CMP122-II, pp377--380, (1.68)--(1.75), at

At p378 the components are split according to the current small/bad
characteristic functions. The good class permits reversing the gauge

The surviving class retains the bad current characteristic function.

Equations (1.73)--(1.75) compare the same T_k(X) at a complex and a
real background. Its real density is positive. Normalizing that SAME
operator gives a bounded expectation, although T_k(X,(U,0))1 can be

In particular the current coarse/background U is fixed in this

change a current bad U into a current good fill.

Here is a precise sufficient surgery inequality. Let xi denote the
unchanged exterior/current collar plus the retained outside history.
Let D_ref(xi) be the actual positive reference filling normalization,
and let F_out(xi) be the outside factor kept exactly. The already
proved bad-field estimate has to be matched to the form

  W_bad(X|xi) <= exp[-p_X+C nu_X] D_ref(xi) F_out(xi).

One then needs an ADMITTED healed class of the ACTUAL density, with
the same xi and a controlled inverse multiplicity, such that

  W_fill(X|xi) >= exp[-C' nu_X] D_ref(xi) F_out(xi).    (RF)

Here nu_X is the original sum of own-scale volumes/action debits,
not a fine-volume count of a later component. Dividing these two
inequalities and summing a surgery map with inverse multiplicity
at most exp(C'' nu_X) would cancel the global Z and give the desired
normalized Peierls bound.

To derive (RF) from the reference measure it suffices to exhibit an
actual healed-field set G_X(xi) whose reference probability is at
least exp[-C nu_X], and on which the actual/reference density ratio
is at least exp[-C nu_X], with all constraints, gauge volumes, vacuum
terms, and historical normalizers included. The reference kernel's
normalization alone says its TOTAL mass is one; it says neither that
G_X is admissible nor that it has this lower probability.

The source action completion (1.68)--(1.70) is useful for the local
density-ratio cost once the SAME fill and external constraints have

Conclusion of this source audit: (1.73)--(1.75) do not prove (RF).

6. A SHARP NEGATIVE TEST: A GOOD COLLAR WITHOUT A FRACTIONAL MARGIN

Consider a fixed finite cubical Abelian lattice box, with Dirichlet
potential on a full exterior collar and interior gauge fixed. Its
quadratic Wilson measure is exp[-||dA||^2/(2g^2)]dA. Let A_bar be an
affine potential whose only nonzero curvature component is the constant
t_g on every (1,2) plaquette. It is stationary for the interior action,
because d^*d A_bar=0. The conditional field is exactly

  A=A_bar+g X,

where X is a centered Dirichlet Maxwell Gaussian independent of g.
Choose an interior plaquette p for which the curvature functional
Y=(dX)_{12}(p) has variance sigma^2>0; such a plaquette exists in any
box with an interior cycle. Put epsilon_g=g(log g^-2)^p, p>0, and
t_g=epsilon_g-g^2. The entire fixed exterior collar is strictly good:
its curvature magnitude is less than epsilon_g. Nevertheless

 P((dA)_{12}(p)>epsilon_g | exterior=A_bar)
       =P(Y>g) -> 1/2.

Consequently the conditional weight of a bad interior cube cannot be
bounded by exp[-c(log g^-2)^nu] times its good-filling weight uniformly
over all exterior collars satisfying ONLY |F|<epsilon_g. This already
fails in the flat quadratic gauge theory, before questions about R
normalizers, source counterterms or changing determining sets arise.
The background tends to zero and is inside every fixed small-field
analytic ceiling. Large absolute action of the background does not
repair the ratio: its common classical action cancels exactly.

This is a counterexample to that proposed UNIFORM COLLAR INFERENCE,
not yet a counterexample to the source's actual component geometry.
If its literal prepared collar supplies a strict bound theta epsilon
with theta<1/C for the relevant filling estimate, the example is
excluded. Alternatively a surgery can enlarge the component to absorb
near-threshold collars, but it must prove termination, multiplicities
and compatibility with the original determining constraints.

There is also an important alternative threshold. The endpoint analytic
tube a=g(log gamma)^q is much wider than epsilon=g(log gamma)^p when
q>p. A collar O(polylog·epsilon) may be a vanishing fraction of a,
so the example does not obstruct an estimate for genuine violations
of the analytic tube. The original R reference is concentrated near
its regular minimizing fill and explicitly cuts its displacement.
But an R-history mark can retain old core data even when its CURRENT
field is analytic; classifying a current field as good is not by itself
an identification of that history-dependent mark with the universal
regular coefficient. That identification or a relative history bound
would still be necessary for the normalized witness calculation.
