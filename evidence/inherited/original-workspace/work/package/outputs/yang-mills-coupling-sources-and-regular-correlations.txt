1. ACTUAL HAMILTONIAN AND THE LOCAL CURRENT

Work on a finite nondegenerate periodic cubic spatial lattice.
The link manifold is M=SU2^E with the product Pauli/2 metric and
normalized product Haar measure. Let

 H=kappa K+vS,   K=-Delta,   kappa=g^2/(2a),
 v=4/(g^2 a),    S=sum_p h_p,   h_p=1-Tr U_p/2.

Its normalized ground state Omega is smooth and strictly positive.
Write E0 for its energy, A=H-E0 and mu=Omega^2 dU. Uniqueness
implies that Omega is gauge invariant. Multiplication by Omega is
a unitary map from L2(mu) to L2(dU), and

 L=Omega^-1 A Omega=kappa d_mu^* d,                    (1.1)
 D(F)=<F,L F>_mu=kappa ||dF||_mu^2.                    (1.2)

Here d is the exterior derivative on scalar functions and d_mu^*
its adjoint for the weighted Riemannian volume mu. All statements
are first made at fixed graph, a and g. A fixed-graph spectral gap
above constants exists by compact ellipticity; no uniform gap is
asserted.

For real plaquette weights f_p define

 eta_e=(1/4) sum_(p incident e) f_p,
 Q_f=v sum_p f_p h_p,
 H_f=kappa sum_e eta_e K_e+Q_f,
 q_f=Q_f-<Q_f>,
 r_f=Omega^-1(H_f-<H_f>)Omega.                         (1.3)

Every edge has four incident spatial plaquettes. Both q_f and r_f
are real, centered, smooth and gauge invariant. The function r_f
can read the ground state globally; local differential support
of H_f is not finite support for this quotient.

Define the real local one-form j_f by its edge components

 (j_f)_e=sum_(p incident e)(f_p-eta_e) d_e h_p.          (1.4)

The actual ground equation and Haar integration by parts give

 <dF,dr_f>_mu=v<dF,j_f>_mu                            (1.5)

for every smooth real F. For completeness,

 [H,H_f]=kappa v sum_(e,p)(f_p-eta_e)[K_e,h_p],
 A(r_f Omega)=[H,H_f]Omega,
 <F Omega,[K_e,h_p]Omega>=<d_eF,d_e h_p>_mu.

In the last identity the two terms containing d_e Omega cancel
exactly. Thus (1.5) has no omitted score term.

Let P_ex be the orthogonal projection in L2(mu;T*M) onto the
closed space of exact one-forms. This space is closed at a fixed
compact graph by the scalar Poincare inequality. Equation (1.5)
is precisely

                         dr_f=v P_ex j_f.             (1.6)

The current vanishes for a constant profile. This is the exact
conservation cancellation retained in all subsequent formulas.

2. THE WEIGHTED ONE-FORM OPERATOR AND THE COVARIANCE IDENTITY

Let

                 L1=kappa(d d_mu^*+d_mu^* d)           (2.1)

on one-forms, with the usual closed quadratic-form realization.
The two occurrences of d in (2.1) act between the appropriate
form degrees. It is a nonnegative elliptic operator. Its exact
subspace is reducing, and

                  dL=L1d,   P_ex L1=L1 P_ex.          (2.2)

On the exact subspace there is no zero mode. Inverse powers below
always mean inverses on that subspace. No estimate on the full
one-form harmonic subspace is required.

For a real centered smooth F put

 C_F(t)=<F,e^(-tL)F>_mu,   t>=0.

At fixed graph, spectral calculus and (2.2) give

 C_F(t)=kappa<dF,L1^-1 e^(-tL1)dF>_mu.                 (2.3)

One direct proof differentiates C_F(s):

 -C_F'(s)=kappa<dF,e^(-sL1)dF>_mu,

then integrates from s=t to infinity. Centering removes the
constant mode, and the fixed-graph gap justifies the integral.
There is no passage to a continuum limit in this proof.

For the local energy quotient, (1.6) therefore gives the exact
actual-law formula

 C_(r_f)(t)=kappa v^2
   <P_ex j_f,L1^-1 e^(-tL1)P_ex j_f>_mu.              (2.4)

The positive-time window norm of a centered F is

 R_F(t,d)=<F,e^(-2tL)
                   [(1-e^(-dL))/L]^2 F>_mu,
                      t>0, d>0.                      (2.5)

It equals the reflected covariance of the centered time integral
over [t,t+d] in the stationary ground process. Applying (2.3) to
the spectral multiplier in (2.5) yields

 R_(r_f)(t,d)=kappa v^2
   <P_ex j_f,
      L1^-3 e^(-2tL1)(1-e^(-dL1))^2 P_ex j_f>_mu.     (2.6)

At low energy the multiplier in (2.6) behaves as d^2/E. Thus
replacing the factor (1-e^(-dE)) by its trivial upper bound1
would obscure the correct low-energy inverse power. Formula
(2.6), not an unweighted norm of j_f, is the exact consumer.

These are configuration-space weighted Hodge formulas. Calling
L1^-1 a spatial inverse Laplacian, or replacing its spectral
variable by spatial momentum, would require a further theorem.

3. WHICH GROUND-STATE TERM A SPATIAL COMPARISON MUST CONTROL

The local Weitzenbock expression for (2.1) is

 L1=kappa[weighted rough Laplacian
                    +Ric-2 Hess(log Omega)].         (3.1)

For the Pauli/2 metric on each SU2 factor, Ric=(1/2) times the
metric. The weighted rough Laplacian contains the drift from
mu; (3.1) is not an assertion that its displayed zeroth-order
part is nonnegative pointwise. The complete operator is
nonnegative by (2.1).

The precise coefficient is the Hessian of the ACTUAL ground
logarithm. It is not the Hessian of the bare Wilson action.
The exact ground equation only gives the scalar trace relation

 kappa[Delta log Omega+|d log Omega|^2]=vS-E0.          (3.2)

Neither (3.2) nor the integrated Fisher estimate controls the
matrix Hess(log Omega) as a quadratic form on the projected
current. No such matrix estimate is imported here.

There is a simple normalization check in the harmonic physical
sector. Write U_e=exp(i theta_e.sigma/2) and remove the flat
gauge modes. To quadratic order S=|d_sp theta|^2/8. If
L_sp=d_sp^*d_sp on that sector, the harmonic Hamiltonian is

 H_harm=(g^2/(2a))(-Delta_theta)
                    +(1/(2g^2a))<theta,L_sp theta>.

Its ground amplitude is proportional to

 exp[-<theta,sqrt(L_sp)theta>/(2g^2)].

Consequently -2 Hess log Omega_harm=2sqrt(L_sp)/g^2,
and its multiplication by kappa is sqrt(L_sp)/a. The
ground-state frequency is the square root of the spatial
quadratic kernel. Substituting the bare kernel into (3.1)
already gives the wrong operator in this elementary limit.
This is an operator-dictionary check, not a nonlinear
approximation theorem.

A useful actual target can now be stated without an abstract
measure change: estimate the exact-subspace kernel in (2.6),
with the actual mu and its actual projection P_ex, on currents
(1.4). Such a bound must account for the ground-state Hessian
or otherwise control this same resolvent/semigroup. Pointwise
Wilson curvature estimates alone do not perform that step.

4. AN ACTUAL SEPARATED-TIME HIGH-ENERGY ESTIMATE

The previous current calculation and the actual ground action
bound give, on the cubic lattice,

 B_r:=D(r_f)
       <=(12/a^3) sum_(e,p incident)|f_p-eta_e|^2,

       <=(48/a^3) sum_p f_p^2.                        (4.1)

This uses E_mu h_p<=3g^2/4 and
|d_e h_p|^2=(2h_p-h_p^2)/4<=h_p/2. Thus (4.1) is a

small-field conditional estimate.

For any centered F with D(F)<=B_F let rho_F be its positive
spectral measure for L. Then int E rho_F(dE)<=B_F. For Lambda>0,

 C_F^(>Lambda)(t)
 :=int_(E>Lambda) e^(-tE)rho_F(dE)
 <=B_F e^(-tLambda)/Lambda,                          (4.2)

 R_F^(>Lambda)(t,d)
 :=int_(E>Lambda)e^(-2tE)
                 [(1-e^(-dE))/E]^2rho_F(dE)
 <=B_F e^(-2tLambda)/Lambda^3.                        (4.3)

Both inequalities follow by factoring E rho_F and taking the
supremum of the remaining decreasing positive multiplier.
They require neither a spectral gap nor a Gaussian comparison.

support, (4.1) gives B_q=O(a^-6) and B_r=O(a^-4).

 Lambda_a=(p/(2t)) log(a0/a),  0<a<a0.                (4.4)

Then (4.3) tends to zero for BOTH q_f and r_f. The magnetic
bound is O(a^(p-6)/log(a0/a)^3), up to constants with the

This is a genuine removal of the large-energy contribution

 kappa v^2

remains uncontrolled by (4.1). No uniform upper bound, lower
bound, or smallness of (4.5) is claimed.

For a local log-coupling source, the derivative vector is
b_f=2q_f-r_f. By the Hilbert norm represented by (2.5),

 |sqrt(R_(b_f))-2sqrt(R_(q_f))|<=sqrt(R_(r_f)).        (4.6)

The ultraviolet tail of the error in (4.6) is now controlled.

5. AN EXACT FORCE WARD ESTIMATE AND ITS PHYSICAL PROJECTION

There is another actual dynamical identity worth testing
before interpreting a force estimate as a scalar witness.
Let w_e be deterministic Lie-algebra vectors and set

 D_w=sum_e w_e.d_e^L.

This is a skew-adjoint derivation for product Haar. Since each
link Laplacian is a bi-invariant Casimir,

 [K,D_w]=0,
 A D_w Omega=[H,D_w]Omega=-(D_w V)Omega,
 V=vS.                                               (5.1)

Both D_w Omega and (D_w V)Omega are orthogonal to Omega.
The existing actual local kinetic budget and independent
site-gauge averaging give

 ||D_w Omega||^2 <=18 g^-2 sum_e |w_e|^2.              (5.2)

For clarity, the score is -2D_w Omega/Omega; its square
expectation is at most72g^-2 sum|w|^2. Cross terms at
different initial vertices vanish by adjoint averaging.
The three outgoing links at one vertex are handled by
Cauchy--Schwarz. These are the same exact estimates as
TURN67, now used in the ground equation.

Using max_(E>=0) E^2 exp(-tE)=4/(exp(2)t^2), (5.1)
and (5.2) prove, for every t>0,

 <(D_w V)Omega,e^(-tA)(D_w V)Omega>
 <=[72/(exp(2)t^2 g^2)] sum_e |w_e|^2.               (5.3)

There is also the exact force sum rule

 <D_w Omega,A D_w Omega>
                 =(1/2)E_mu D_w^2 V>=0.              (5.4)

Indeed Haar integration by parts gives
E D_w^2V=-2<D_w Omega,(D_wV)Omega>, and (5.1)
then proves (5.4). For a nondegenerate plaquette,
|D_w^2h_p|<=(sum_(e in p)|w_e|)^2/4
          <=sum_(e in p)|w_e|^2.
Four plaquettes meet a spatial edge, so (5.4) is also at most
2v sum|w_e|^2=8(g^2a)^-1 sum|w_e|^2. The favorable
t-dependent bound (5.3) uses the score norm, not this
more divergent first-moment bound.

However, if P_G is normalized local gauge averaging,

                    P_G D_w Omega=0.                 (5.5)

Each left derivative of Omega transforms in the adjoint
representation at the initial vertex; the fixed numerical
coefficient w_e supplies no compensating transforming
factor. Haar adjoint averaging is zero. As A commutes with
P_G, (5.1) implies

                   P_G (D_wV)Omega=0.                (5.6)

Thus (5.3) is a charged-sector estimate. Its physical
Gauss-projected correlation is identically zero. Summing
color indices in an unprojected two-point function does
not turn the single-time insertion into a physical
operator. A compensating transport or an equivariant
field-dependent coefficient would change the insertion.

For a smooth field-dependent equivariant vector field W,
the exact replacement for (5.1) is

 A W Omega=kappa[K,W]Omega-(WV)Omega.                 (5.7)

In general [K,W] is nonzero; it contains derivatives of W
and terms acting on Omega. It cannot be removed by gauge
invariance. The physical current in Sections1--4 is an
example where actual integration by parts handles such
structure, but leaves the projected inverse-energy norm.
We have not bounded the corresponding term in (5.7)
uniformly for a physical coarse displacement.
