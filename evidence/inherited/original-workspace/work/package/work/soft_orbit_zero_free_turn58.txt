POLYNOMIAL ZERO-FREE DOMAIN FOR THE SOFT ORBIT INTEGRAL -- TURN58
20 October 2026. gauge47.

RESULT

The soft SU(2) orbit integral has a zero-free complex neighborhood
whose radii have polynomial, rather than exponential, dependence on
the finite graph. The proof uses its anchored real energy distribution,
not a crude absolute bound divided by an exponentially small baseline.

The precise radii are

 ||A||_2 < 1/(16 C_* sqrt(lambda K)),
 ||T||_infinity < 1/(32 C_* C_F K),                     (0.1)

where C_* is absolute, K=O(1+m(1+log C_F)), with K>=1, and C_F is
an explicit polynomial forest constant. On this domain,

   |Psi_lambda(A,exp(T)t0)/Psi_lambda(0,t0)-1| < 1/4.   (0.2)

The logarithm and reciprocal therefore exist holomorphically there.
Fixed-order directional jet costs are polynomial in sqrt(lambda K)
and C_F K. This is a result about the ORBIT PROFILE ONLY. It does
not prove zero-freeness of the full fine-action reference denominator.

1. PRECISE FINITE-GRAPH SETUP

Use the graph, anchored group, and conventions of
work/soft_orbit_complex_bound_turn58.txt, Sections1--5.
All fixed exterior-incident vertices are anchored. E contains the
integrated variable links, not fixed exterior links. Every connected
component meets the anchor set B. Let

   m=|V\B|,
   d_v=distance of v from its root in an anchored spanning forest F,
   C_F=max(1,2 sum_(e=(u,v) in E)(d_u+d_v)).

There is exactly one anchor in each forest component, and |F|=m.
For real t0_e in SU(2), complex traceless A_e,T_e, set

 t_e=exp(T_e)t0_e,
 Q(A,t;g)=sum_(e=(u,v))
             q(g_u exp(A_e)t_e g_v^-1 t_e^-1),
 q(U)=1-Tr(U)/2,
 Q0(g)=Q(0,t0;g),
 Psi_lambda(A,t)=int_(SU(2)^(V\B)) exp[-lambda Q(A,t;g)] dg.
                                                               (1.1)

Here lambda>=1 is real; gauge Haar measure is normalized.
This is the twisted relative action appropriate to physical
links exp(A_e)t_e. The Hilbert--Schmidt and operator norms are

 ||A||_2^2=sum_e |A_e|_HS^2,
 ||T||_infinity=max_e ||T_e||op.

The finite compact integral is entire in these complex A,T
coordinates. For real t0 its value

                       Psi0=Psi_lambda(0,t0)

is strictly positive. Psi0 is fixed throughout the comparison:
it is not replaced by Psi_lambda(0,t) when T changes.

Let

 w_s=int_SU(2) exp[-s q(g)]dg,
 phi_lambda(A,t)=Psi_lambda(A,t)/w_lambda^m.

The profile retains exactly this fixed normalization.

2. THE TWO REAL ESTIMATES AND THEIR QUANTITATIVE CONSTANTS

The forest calculation gives

              Q_F <= Q0 <= C_F Q_F,
              int exp[-s Q_F]dg=w_s^m.                  (2.1)

For completeness, |g_v-I| is bounded by the sum of the covariant
edge differences along its rooted forest path. Squaring gives
|g_v-I|^2<=4d_v Q_F. Applying this to both endpoints of every
edge gives Q0<=2 sum_e(d_u+d_v) Q_F. The forest variables have
product Haar law by recursive inversion and translation.

Consequently

          w_(C_F lambda)^m <= Psi0 <= w_lambda^m.        (2.2)

The elementary radial Haar integral supplies constants

   c_H=8 exp(-1/2)/(3 pi^3),
   C_H=pi^(5/2)/2^(5/2),

such that w_s>=c_H s^-3/2 for s>=1 and
w_s<=C_H s^-3/2 for EVERY s>0.
The latter all-positive-s scope matters when 1<=lambda<2.

Define

 L_F=log[(C_H/c_H)(2 C_F)^(3/2)],
 K=max(1,m L_F).                                        (2.3)

Since C_F>=1 and C_H/c_H>1, L_F is positive and
K=O(1+m(1+log C_F)). No unknown partition function enters K.

3. ENERGY EXPONENTIAL MOMENT WITHOUT AN EXPONENTIAL RADIUS LOSS

Consider the real probability measure

      dmu0=Psi0^-1 exp[-lambda Q0(g)]dg,
      X=lambda Q0(g)>=0.

Its moment at one half is

 M(1/2)=E_mu0 exp(X/2)
       =Psi_(lambda/2)(0,t0)/Psi_lambda(0,t0)
       <=[w_(lambda/2)/w_(C_F lambda)]^m
       <=[(C_H/c_H)(2 C_F)^(3/2)]^m
       <=exp(K).                                       (3.1)

The denominator uses C_F lambda>=1; the numerator uses the
all-s upper bound. Thus no exception or hidden restriction
lambda>=2 is needed.

For 0<=s<=1/2, Holder's inequality, or convexity of log M,
gives the sharper consequence

                     M(s)<=exp(2Ks).                   (3.2)

Indeed log M(0)=0 and the graph of log M lies below the chord
joining its values at0 and1/2. The large factor exp(K) in(3.1)
is therefore paid only with exponent2s, which will be O(1/K).
This is the step that avoids an exponentially small radius.

4. THE ACTUAL COMPLEX INCREMENT BOUND

The following is the sharpened trace calculation of
soft_orbit_complex_bound_turn58, Sections3--4, with r_A=1
and its fixed parameter delta=1/2.

Put C0=2 exp(2) and C_R=sqrt(2)exp(3)/4. For

     ||T||_infinity<=1/(4 C0 C_F),   max_e |A_e|_HS<=1,

one has

 |Q(0,t)-Q0| <= (C0/2) C_F ||T||_infinity Q0,

 |Q(A,t)-Q(0,t)| <= (3/2)||A||_2 sqrt(Q0)
                                      +C_R ||A||_2^2.  (4.1)

These estimates are uniform in real t0. The background term
has no additive volume error: writing
B=t0_e g_v^-1 t0_e^-1, its conjugation change has trace zero,
so Tr[g_u(exp(T)Bexp(-T)-B)] contains the additional factor
g_u-I. The anchored path estimate controls both factors.

The A-linear term uses Tr A_e=0 and the l2 bound on
exp(T)Bexp(-T)g_u-I; the Taylor remainder is quadratic in A.
It is important to retain the resulting sqrt(Q0) in(4.1),
rather than replacing it by a worst-case graph volume.

Set

 C_*=max(1,C0/2,3/2,C_R),
 tau_F=C_F ||T||_infinity,
 a=sqrt(lambda)||A||_2.

Multiplying(4.1) by lambda gives

 |lambda[Q(A,t)-Q0]|
              <=C_* (tau_F X+a sqrt(X)+a^2).            (4.2)

The proposed radii(0.1) imply all hypotheses of(4.1):
C_*>=C0/2, K>=1 give
||T||_infinity<=1/(16 C0 C_F)<1/(4C0 C_F), and
max_e |A_e|<=||A||_2<=1/(16C_*)<1.

5. EXPLICIT ZERO-FREE ESTIMATE

Assume the closed inequalities corresponding to(0.1), and use

                         epsilon=1/(32K).

Young's inequality gives

 C_* a sqrt(X)<=epsilon X+C_*^2 a^2/(4epsilon).

Therefore, with Y=|lambda[Q(A,t)-Q0]|,

 Y<=theta X+b,
 theta=C_* tau_F+1/(32K)<=1/(16K),
 b=C_* a^2+8 C_*^2 K a^2<=1/256+1/32=9/256.            (5.1)

In particular theta<=1/2. Combining(3.2) and(5.1),

 E_mu0 exp(Y)
       <=exp[b+2K theta]
       <=exp(9/256+1/8)
       =exp(41/256)<5/4.                               (5.2)

Since |exp(-z)-1|<=exp(|z|)-1,

 |Psi_lambda(A,t)/Psi0-1|
   =|E_mu0 exp[-lambda(Q(A,t)-Q0)]-1|
   <=E_mu0(exp(Y)-1)
   <=exp(41/256)-1
   <1/4.                                               (5.3)

This proves the claimed zero-free neighborhood. It also proves

       (3/4)Psi0 < |Psi_lambda(A,t)| < (5/4)Psi0,
       |Psi0/Psi_lambda(A,t)|<4/3.                     (5.4)

The holomorphic branch

      L(A,T)=log[Psi_lambda(A,exp(T)t0)/Psi0],
      L(0,0)=0,

is given throughout this neighborhood by the convergent log(1+z)
series. Uniformly there,

                         |L|<log(4/3).                 (5.5)

No change of the actual reference measure is involved in this
comparison by the fixed real number Psi0.

There is also a useful bound that tends to zero with the perturbation.
Put b_T=(C0/2)C_F||T||_infinity. The elementary inequality
sqrt(X)<=(X+K)/(2sqrt(K)), applied directly to(4.1), gives

 |lambda[Q(A,t)-Q0]|
   <=sX+3a sqrt(K)/4+C_Ra^2,
       s=b_T+3a/(4sqrt(K)).

Whenever the hypotheses of(4.1) and s<=1/2 hold, (3.2) yields

 |Psi_lambda(A,t)/Psi0-1|
   <=exp[2K b_T+(9/4)a sqrt(K)+C_Ra^2]-1.              (5.6)

Thus the small parameters are explicitly C_F||T||_infinity K
and a sqrt(K). This refinement, suggested independently by
tail47, is compatible with the fixed constants in(0.1).

6. THE BASELINE SCALAR AND THE RECIPROCAL ARE RETAINED

The same relative estimates hold for phi/phi(0,t0). From(2.2),

      c_F^m<=phi_lambda(0,t0)<=1,
      c_F=(c_H/C_H) C_F^-3/2.                            (6.1)

Thus the actual logarithm is

 log phi_lambda(A,t)=log phi_lambda(0,t0)+L(A,T),
             m log c_F<=log phi_lambda(0,t0)<=0,         (6.2)

and

              |phi_lambda(A,t)^-1|< (4/3)c_F^-m.         (6.3)

The radii are polynomial although the absolute reciprocal can
carry this exponential baseline cost. These are different claims.

The baseline is a scalar for each FIXED comparison background t0.
If t0 itself varies among physical backgrounds, it need not be a
universal vacuum constant. Its actual dependence must be retained.
Neither(6.2) nor the proof resets the profile to one at all backgrounds.

When m=0 there are no gauge variables, and Q0=0 exactly. Then

   Psi_lambda(A,t)=exp[-lambda sum_e q(exp(A_e))]

is independent of t and is nonzero for ALL finite complex A.
Its explicit exponent is an entire logarithm. The smaller domain
above remains valid with K=1 but is unnecessary in this case.

7. DIRECTIONAL CAUCHY COROLLARY

Let R_A=1/(16C_*sqrt(lambda K)) and
R_T=1/(32C_*C_F K). At a point satisfying

                    ||A||_2<=R_A/2,
                    ||T||_infinity<=R_T/2,

take fixed complex traceless direction arrays H,J with
||H||_2<=1 and ||J||_infinity<=1. The two-variable function

                    (z,w)->L(A+zH,T+wJ)

is bounded by B0=log(4/3) on |z|<R_A/2, |w|<R_T/2.
For all nonnegative integers p,q, Cauchy's estimate gives

 |partial_z^p partial_w^q L(A+zH,T+wJ)|_(z=w=0)
   <=p!q! B0
        (32 C_* sqrt(lambda K))^p
        (64 C_* C_F K)^q.                              (7.1)

These are repeated derivatives along the stated fixed directions.
General multilinear derivatives require polarization; one may
include the usual factors p^p/p! and q^q/q! in addition, or absorb
them into exponential-in-order constants. No exact p!q! bound for
arbitrary independent directions is asserted without that step.

The same argument applies to the relative reciprocal Psi0/Psi,
with the uniform bound4/3 in place of B0. For the absolute
reciprocal, the scalar factor Psi0^-1 is also retained.

8. SCALE AND MODEL SCOPE

For lambda=(gW)^-2>=1, the A radius is

                        R_A=gW/(16 C_*sqrt(K)).

It is a total l2 radius. A simultaneous per-edge bound
|A_e|<=r is guaranteed to fit only when sqrt(|E|)r<=R_A.
It is not a fixed per-edge radius independent of the graph.
In particular, |A_e|<=C_A g h(g) is admitted by this result
if

           C_A h(g) sqrt(|E|K)/W <=1/(16C_*).           (8.1)

For a fixed bounded block, this costs only fixed constants.
For growing blocks it is an explicit additional width/domain
condition, rather than an automatic native source-radius claim.

At each fixed derivative order, the losses in(7.1) are polynomial
in sqrt(lambda K) and C_F K. If the graph data and W,W^-1 have
at most fixed powers of g^-1 and log(1/g), then a genuinely
available factor exp[-c(log(1/g))^kappa], kappa>1, absorbs
these fixed-order derivative losses. This statement concerns
the polynomial jet factors. An absolute baseline reciprocal
cost such as c_F^-m requires its own comparison with the rare
reserve if m grows; it is not automatically a polynomial.

The actual rare-branch factor, its placement, the native source
domain, and the action cancellation must still be proved for any
proposed replacement. A full denominator weighted by the fine
action has a DIFFERENT real baseline measure; its exponential
moment need not satisfy(3.1). Thus this zero-free orbit theorem
does not certify the full fine-action denominator, a normalized
native replacement, original observable transport, or YM4 existence.
