WINDING ERRORS UNDER DATED POLYNOMIAL RESPONSE
20 October 2026. TURN53. Root.

STATUS AND PURPOSE

This note proves a sequence estimate relevant to the native cover
comparison. It permits polynomial per-step amplification and long
memory. It does NOT prove the missing native full-state comparison
map, matching of reconstruction maps, or matching of hard cuts.
Those are separate premises. In particular, a source estimate on
resummed live boundary terms relative to retained large regions
must not be silently substituted for a full-support difference norm.

The new conclusion is that contraction is unnecessary for this
particular finite-volume error. A quantitative minimum period is
necessary for the uniform polynomial prefactor claimed here. That
condition can be arranged once at the physical reference scale.

1. AN EXACT FORWARD RESOLVENT ESTIMATE

Let j=0,...,n denote successive steps from fine to coarse. Assume
gamma_j>=2 and

 gamma_i <= C_g (gamma_j + j-i + 1),    0<=i<=j<=n.       (1.1)

The fixed constant C_g may incorporate bounded signed primitive
steps and positive sums over a fixed number of steps. No positive
primitive drift is assumed.

Let e_j,b_j be nonnegative numbers satisfying

 e_j <= b_j + sum_(i<j) K_(j,i)e_i,                       (1.2)

 K_(j,i) <= C_0 L^[D(j-i)] (1+j-i)^q
                                  (2+gamma_i+gamma_j)^A, (1.3)

where L>1, C_0>0 and A,D,q>=0 are fixed. Bounds with extra fixed
powers of either endpoint coupling or gamma_i/gamma_j are included
by increasing A; both couplings are at least two. A finite vector
of dated classes is included by
taking its nonnegative matrix row-sum norm and increasing C_0.

There is C_1 depending only on these constants such that

 e_j <= sum_(s=0)^j b_s
          exp[C_1(j-s) log(2+gamma_j+j-s)].                (1.4)

For s=j the exponential is one.

Proof. The strictly lower triangular matrix K is nilpotent.
Iteration of (1.2), with no infinite-series issue, gives
e <= (I+K+...+K^n)b. A term from s to j is a strictly increasing
chain s=i_0<i_1<...<i_v=j. Put a=j-s and h_t=i_t-i_(t-1).
Then v<=a and sum h_t=a. For every point of the chain, (1.1)
bounds every visited endpoint coupling by C(2+gamma_j+a).
Consequently its weight
is at most

 max(1,C_0)^a L^(Da) 2^(qa)
                         [C(2+gamma_j+a)^A]^a,

since 1+h<=2^h for every positive integer h. There are exactly
2^(a-1) compositions of a when a>=1; they bound all the chains.
Absorb these exponential factors into C_1 a log(2+gamma_j+a).
This proves (1.4).

Thus the old-cell count L^(4a), polynomial collar factors and a
polynomial per-step response do not require separate contractions.
They do require an actual estimate of the form (1.2)--(1.3).

2. EXPONENTIAL BIRTHS AND THE MINIMUM-PERIOD CONDITION

Define

 ell(gamma) = [1+log(2+gamma)]^r,    r>=0,
 N_i=N_j L^(j-i),    x_j=N_j/ell(gamma_j).                 (2.1)

Suppose, for fixed B,c_0>0 and B_0>=0,

 b_i <= B_0(2+gamma_i)^B
                       exp[-c_0 N_i/ell(gamma_i)].        (2.2)

There are constants K_*,C_*,c_*>0, independent of n,j,gamma_j,
N_j,B_0, such that

 x_j >= K_* log(2+gamma_j)                                (2.3)

implies

 e_j <= C_* B_0 (2+gamma_j)^B exp(-c_* x_j).               (2.4)

If an additional final read has an incidence/cell-count kernel
of the form (1.3), incorporate that read as one more application
of the same triangular bound. Its possible fixed polynomial
prefactor is retained; it is not declared norm one.

Proof. From (1.1),

 (2+gamma_(j-a))^B <= C(2+gamma_j)^B(1+a)^B,
 ell(gamma_(j-a)) <= C ell(gamma_j)(1+a)^r.

Insert these bounds in (1.4). It suffices to bound

 sum_(a>=0) (1+a)^B
   exp[C_1 a log(2+gamma_j+a)
                         -c_2 x_j L^a/(1+a)^r].          (2.5)

Put h_a=L^a/(1+a)^r. The numbers

 d = inf_(a>=0) h_a,
 S = sup_(a>=1) a[1+log(1+a)/log(4)]/h_a

satisfy d>0 and S<infinity. Also

 log(2+gamma_j+a)
           <= log(2+gamma_j)+log(1+a).

Thus the positive exponent in (2.5) is at most
C_1 S log(2+gamma_j) h_a. Choose
K_*>=max(1/log(4),2 C_1 S/c_2). Condition (2.3) pays this
cost with half the negative exponent and also gives x_j>=1.
The remaining sum is bounded by

 exp[-c_2 d x_j/4]
       sum_(a>=0)(1+a)^B exp[-c_2 h_a/4].

The series is finite, since h_a grows exponentially up to a
polynomial denominator. This proves (2.4).

3. WHY THE SCALE CONDITION CANNOT JUST BE OMITTED

For fixed x>0 and A,c>0, consider the contribution

        gamma^(Aa) exp(-c x L^a).                         (3.1)

It is obtained, for example, from a scalar nearest-step recurrence
with response gamma^A and a birth a dates earlier with the displayed
winding factor. Constant gamma satisfies the upper comparison (1.1).
Choosing a=floor[(log log gamma)/(2 log L)] as gamma tends to infinity
makes the logarithm of (3.1) grow as a positive constant times
log gamma log log gamma, up to smaller terms. It exceeds the
logarithm of every fixed polynomial in gamma.

Therefore a polynomial-in-gamma prefactor at fixed x is not a
consequence of polynomial response alone. Section2 does not make
this false assertion. The finite-scale estimate in TURN52 Section12B
already withheld it for this amplification class.

4. THE PHYSICAL REFERENCE SCALE MAKES THE CONDITION UNIFORM

Suppose the chosen continuum-cutoff sequence has

 N_j=N_* L^(n-j),
 2<=gamma_j<=C_p[Gamma+(n-j)+1],    Gamma>=2.              (4.1)

The native positive-block profiles in TURN52 supply an upper
comparison of this form, with their fixed block/step constants.
The number N_* is the torus period in terminal coarse-cell units;
it is fixed as the ultraviolet cutoff n increases.

There is K_p, independent of n and Gamma, such that

 N_* >= K_p [1+log(2+Gamma)]^(r+1)                        (4.2)

implies (2.3) for every j. Indeed, for m=n-j,

 1+log(2+gamma_j)
             <= C[1+log(2+Gamma)](1+m).

The quantity sup_(m>=0) L^(-m)(1+m)^(r+1) is finite.
Taking K_p larger than K_* times the resulting constant proves
N_j >= K_* ell(gamma_j)log(2+gamma_j) simultaneously.
Rounding N_* up to an allowed lattice multiple only improves it.

This is a condition on the relation between reference box size,
reference lattice units and weak-coupling floor. It is not an
estimate for every box with arbitrary independent values of
N_* and Gamma.

For a prescribed tube radius delta>0 and fixed B_0, enlarge the
constant in (4.2), if necessary with an additive dependence on
log_+(B_0/delta). The right side of (2.4) can then be made less
than delta/2 at all dates. To see this without an asymptotic
shortcut, use x_j>=K log(2+gamma_j) to pay the factor
(2+gamma_j)^B with half the exponential and use the positive
uniform lower bound on x_j to pay 2 C_* B_0/delta.

5. SUMMING THE VOLUME-WEIGHTED OUTPUT

If the actual vacuum comparison at date j is bounded by
C N_j^4 e_j, then Sections2 and4 give

 sum_(j=0)^n C N_j^4 e_j
   <= C' B_0 N_*^4(2+Gamma)^B
       exp[-c' N_*/ell(Gamma)],                           (5.1)

after modifying constants depending on the fixed profile bounds.
This is uniform in the ultraviolet depth n.

Proof. In (2.4) substitute m=n-j and (4.1). With
x_*=N_*/ell(Gamma), the sum is bounded by a constant times

 B_0 N_*^4(2+Gamma)^B
   sum_(m>=0) L^(4m)(1+m)^B
            exp[-c x_* L^m/(1+m)^r].

Because x_*>=1 and L^m/(1+m)^r has a positive minimum and
exponential growth, splitting the exponent as in Section2
gives (5.1). The L^(4m) volume weight is included in the
convergent series; it is not discarded.

For a fixed integer cover multiplicity J, a factor J^4 in
the raw vacuum comparison is harmless but must be restored
in the final two-volume statement.

6. EXACT NATIVE ATTACHMENT AND WHAT IS STILL MISSING

The following items are established at the stated source/classical
premises in TURN52:

 * Flat raw Gaussian vacuum contributions have winding tails
   in the actual localized resolvent support catalogue.
 * Scalar and original-domain field perturbations, at FIXED
   reconstruction maps and cuts, enter a native component with
   a pointwise old-cell sum containing L^(4a) and polynomial
   collar factors.
 * The positive full component mass gives a response retaining
   its small activity and a fixed smaller spatial exponent.
 * Its raw supported connected expansion gives a fresh R response.

The present lemma proves that, IF the remaining full dated
E/R/live-B comparison map obeys (1.2)--(1.3) in one fixed weaker
comparison norm, all such inherited winding errors are summable.
There is no need to prove that this entire map is contractive.
The same stronger baseline support reserve must be retained at
every step; repeated weakening of the exponent is not allowed.

In particular, the lemma does not supply:

 (i) a small difference in the full original mixed live-B norm,
     or a proved replacement by a closed mass-weighted norm;
 (ii) equality/comparison of the complete nonlinear reads and
      hard-cut integration domains;
 (iii) a response estimate through an unverified regrouping
       of retained large-field regions;
 (iv) equality of the full raw finite-volume partition normalization;
 (v) local-observable bounds, continuum symmetry, reconstruction
     or physical nontriviality.

If a nonlinear map satisfies the local response bound only inside
a fixed comparison tube, the uniform delta/2 majorant can be used
in a finite first-exit induction, PROVIDED each step is defined
and the response inequality holds throughout that tube. This
does not establish native class admission or continuity across
changing hard cuts on its own.

CONCLUSION

The amplification/summation problem is settled at explicit
polynomial-response hypotheses and a reference-scale condition.
The remaining native boundary/read comparison must still be
proved before this becomes a full vacuum-cover theorem.
