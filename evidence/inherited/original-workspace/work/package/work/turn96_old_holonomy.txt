TURN96 -- THE OLD PLAQUETTE HAS AN ADJOINT BOUNDARY CURRENT
21 October 2026. holonomy96. Scoped mathematical note; no frontier edit.

QUESTION AND RESULT

Can the fact that the original source is a closed SU(2) plaquette yield
its dimension-four coarse-background response before all old raw reads
have been integrated? In this generality the answer is no. The exact
closed trace has a linear adjoint boundary-current term. A real SU(2)
plaquette with two fixed raw links and two coherent regular links attains
an O(eta) response. Its regular background can be flat. This rules out a
source-only argument that applies a regular curvature-square grading
uniformly to arbitrary remaining raw records.

This is a limitation of the proposed POINTWISE estimate, not an actual
Bałaban-history counterexample, not a lower bound on a Wilson correlation,
and not an obstruction to integrating the current with its genuine
weight. It suggests keeping a much more explicit low-jet target: the
adjoint boundary current and the derivative of its conditional weight.
No uniform marked-history estimate is claimed below.

1. EXACT SCALAR / ADJOINT SPLITTING

For Q,V in SL(2,C), put

 q0=Tr(Q)/2, Q0=Q-q0 I, v0=Tr(V)/2, V0=V-v0 I,
 h(U)=1-Tr(U)/2.

Direct multiplication, using Tr(Q0)=Tr(V0)=0, gives

 h(QV)-h(Q) = q0 h(V) - (1/2) Tr(Q0 V0).                (1)

This is an exact identity, with no commutativity assumption. For a
traceless 2 by 2 matrix X, Cayley-Hamilton implies X^2 is scalar. Hence

 h(exp X)=O(||X||^2),
 (exp X)_0=X+O(||X||^3)                               (2)

on each fixed matrix ball. The constants depend only on that ball.
Consequently, if X=eta^2 B, the first term in (1) is O(eta^4), but the
second is generally O(eta^2). The fact that the entire trace is gauge
invariant does not remove this term: under simultaneous conjugation,
Q0 and B both transform in the adjoint representation and Tr(Q0 B) is
invariant. A live raw record is precisely allowed to supply Q0.

For an original plaquette P(z) with P(0)=Q, let
A=Q^{-1} P'(0), so Tr(A)=0. Its exact first jet is

 d_z h(P(z))|0 = -(1/2) Tr(Q0 A).                       (3)

A genuine common gauge change has P'=[Y,Q], and its trace derivative
vanishes. A physical change of regular links with raw links held fixed
need not have this form. Closed-loop gauge cancellation and vanishing
physical response are different statements.

2. A FINE-LINK EXAMPLE AT THE ACTUAL LIVE-READ TYPE

Consider one oriented square. Let its two parallel raw links have
fixed values Q and R^{-1}. Let its two parallel regular links be
exp(eta t T), with the reverse traversal taking the inverse, where
T is anti-Hermitian and traceless. Its complete original plaquette is

 P(t)=Q exp(eta t T) R exp(-eta t T).                    (4)

The regular links are the restriction of a constant commuting, hence
flat, background in that direction. The raw link values stay fixed as
t varies. Their presence can make the actual plaquette nonflat.

Differentiating the closed trace yields

 d_t h(P(t))|0
   =-(eta/2) Tr(Q[T,R])
   =-(eta/2) Tr([R,Q] T).                              (5)

Thus the surviving first jet is an adjoint current [R,Q]. It is not an
open-link gauge artifact; it occurs in the derivative of the complete
closed original trace. Take the Pauli matrices and set

 Q=i sigma_1, R=i sigma_2, T=i sigma_3.

All real configurations in (4) belong to SU(2), and direct multiplication
gives

 Tr P(t)=-2 sin(2 eta t),
 h(P(t))=1+sin(2 eta t),
 d_t h(P(t))|0=2 eta.                                  (6)

Therefore neither O(eta^4) nor O(eta^2) first-response grading follows
uniformly from closedness, gauge invariance, a flat regular background,
and arbitrary fixed real raw link values alone. The example does not
assert that any specified complete native branch realizes this exact
one-parameter family. It tests an estimate using only those local
premises, which the example satisfies. Additional constraints from the
actual native inverse record may improve the answer, but must be used.

For general Q,R in SU(2), the second derivative in (4) is bounded by
4 eta^2 ||T||^2 exp(2 eta |t| ||T||), in the trace normalization above.
Taylor's integral formula consequently gives

 |h(P(t))-h(P(0))-t h'(P(0))|
 <=2 eta^2 |t|^2 ||T||^2 exp(2 eta |t| ||T||).           (7)

This separates a definite linear boundary-current coefficient from a
quadratic remainder. No source radius, logarithm of a partition function,
or inverse g factor enters this elementary estimate.

3. WHAT AN ACTUAL INTEGRATION WOULD HAVE TO CANCEL

There is a precise sufficient averaging mechanism. If mu is a probability
law on Q in SU(2), independent of V, and E_mu Q0=0, then (1) implies

 E_mu h(QV)-E_mu h(Q) = E_mu q0 h(V).                    (8)

In particular, a conjugation-invariant law has E Q0=0 by invariance of its
matrix mean, so V=exp(eta^2 B) gives the desired O(eta^4) difference in
this restricted frozen-law model. The mechanism is cancellation of the
adjoint mean, not a pointwise curvature bound for h(QV).

The native conditional integral is generally not of this form. Its law
may depend on the surviving background, and additional retained raw or
boundary variables can supply covariant directions. Let

 A(z)=int w_z(x) F_z(x) dnu(x), Z(z)=int w_z(x) dnu(x),
 m(z)=A(z)/Z(z),

on a real admitted domain with Z>0; assume the finite differentiations
are justified. At z=0 put S=partial_z log w_z. The exact first derivative is

 m'(0)= E F'_0 + Cov(F_0,S).                            (9)

The first term contains (3) or (5). The second is the weight-score term;
it is present even if the mark's own adjoint mean vanishes. In the
unnormalized native formulation the corresponding statement is simply

 A'(0)=int [F'_0 w_0+F_0 w'_0] dnu.                    (10)

No small component normalizer is needed to retain these two terms.

A simultaneous gauge symmetry of (w,F,external records) does not imply
independent conjugation symmetry of Q at fixed external records.
Accordingly it does not establish (8) for the actual conditional law.
After the last live record has been removed, an own-field coefficient's
Ward identities can impose further cancellations. That is a statement
about the integrated coefficient and its complete dependence, not
about a trace with arbitrary remaining raw arguments.

For the original source sum, the current (5) must retain its original
plaquette root and the actual transported generators/paths. A product
bound on four matrices does not remove the number of original roots.
The natural candidate estimate is therefore an integrated, connected
bound for the sum of these boundary-current terms together with their
weight-score partners. It may be smaller than an all-observable complex
posterior estimate, but its uniform native bound is still to be proved.

4. WHAT THE PRIMARY NATIVE DOMAIN STATEMENTS DO PROVIDE

I read Large-II printed374--376, especially (1.61)--(1.66), from the
existing supplied-PDF text extracts. Printed375 explicitly states that
the reconstructed extended U_k maps the new (U,J) domain into the old
U'_k domain. It explains why the admitted old E/R/B effective-action
terms may then be composed, and why the earlier terms' regularity
requirements remain admissible. This is useful for an integrated source
coefficient once that coefficient has been put in the same old class.
It is not a uniform norm estimate for an arbitrary old original-source
record which has not yet been put in that class.

Large-I printed187--191, (1.53)--(1.60) and the descending-domain statement,
were read through the existing extracts. The already proved TURN79
accepted-block result remains valid: an admitted source composed through
the actual nested maps has no repeated inverse-radius cost at this
pointwise pullback step. The rejected-branch group correction also costs
O(H), not O(H/g), and its previously proved numerical summability remains.

The unresolved cross-block source admission from TURN79 is not settled
by (1), (4), or the final source-free domain statement. In particular,
this note does not extend a smooth real switch to a complex old noise
coordinate. It neither assumes that the switch composition fails on an
actual history nor assumes that it is admissible.

5. CONSEQUENCE FOR THE TURN96 CHOICE OF TARGET

The regular primitive's curvature-square estimate in TURN83 is untouched:
it concerns a complete reference on its admitted regular minimizing
background. The original partially integrated raw source has additional
boundary currents. It is therefore unnecessarily strong, and generally
false at the stated raw-data premises, to ask that the same dimension-four
pointwise grading hold before those currents have been integrated.

A successful direct mixed-correlation proof must control their actual
connected response or show their cancellation with the weight-score and
remaining source terms. The exact low-jet identities above say which
terms are present; they do not supply their cutoff-uniform estimate.
No new literature input or inaccessible paper was needed for this result.
