CLAIM

Let W_real be a real compact-group lattice field with the B98(52) bound
||W_real(partial p)-I||<alpha0 eta^2 on the complete required reads,
eta=L^-k. Let W=exp(i eta A') W_real, X=exp(i eta A), with complex
Lie algebra A',A and ||A'||_infinity<=a, ||A||_infinity<=b. Fix a and
alpha0 below strict small ceilings in B98 Props6,7, and take b below
one further fixed ceiling. Let u be exactly the B98(106) return for
relative field X over W. Then at fixed L,d and group norm,

 ||u||_infinity, ||u^-1||_infinity <= exp(C b),
 ||u-I||_infinity, ||u^-1-I||_infinity <= C' b,             (A)

with constants independent of k and compatible finite volume. The maps
are jointly holomorphic in A',A on a slightly larger common supremum
ball. Constants may depend on the fixed strict a/alpha0 ceilings and L.

The same argument applies to the B109 endpoint mean convention after
the finite-primitive mean estimate in Step6 is inserted. The endpoint
identity and all gauges are those in
work/B98-B109-literal-endpoint-return-20261025.txt.

STEP1. TRUNCATION / RESCALING OF THE PUBLISHED ESTIMATES

Put s_j=L^j eta for 0<=j<=k. For j>=1 reinterpret the SAME first j
averaging steps at final spacing one. Its fine spacing is eta_j=L^-j,
with rescaled physical perturbations A'_res=s_j A', A_res=s_j A,
and real curvature parameter alpha0_res=s_j^2 alpha0. Indeed
eta_j A_res=eta A, and alpha0_res eta_j^2=alpha0 eta^2.
Thus B98 Props6 and7 apply with ceilings no larger than the original
ones, uniformly in j and k. At j=0 the claims follow directly from the
exponentials. Denoting W_j=M^jW, W_j^r=M^jW_real, and X_j the native
corrected relative field from(89)-(91), they give

 ||W_j(W_j^r)^-1-I|| <= C s_j a,
                    ||Log X_j|| <= C s_j b.              (1)

For the second statement use Prop7's uniform Prop4 conclusion; the
linear averaging term is uniformly bounded on the rescaled ball by
the estimate used in (131), or by the uniform analytic bound and its
vanishing at A=0 on a fixed larger rescaled ball. Equivalently repeat
the proof of(131) with the Prop7 factors displayed on printed43.
All constants are at fixed primitive L; no C^j is introduced.

Because W_j^r is unitary and C s_j a is uniformly less than 1/2,

 ||W_j(e)||, ||W_j(e)^-1|| <= exp(C_0 s_j a).             (2)

The inverse bound uses (I+E)^-1 and -log(1-||E||)<=2||E||.
This step controls matrices themselves in an operator norm, not only
a formal Lie algebra coefficient.

STEP2. LOCAL ENDPOINT RATIOS

Correction after root primary check: B98 blocks are lower-corner anchored,
while B109 blocks are centered. The safe bound below covers both.
For the B98 path convention, a level-j endpoint path has at most
q_L=d(L-1) edges. Write C_j=X_jW_j. The ratio

 T_j(y,z)=C_j(Gamma_(y,z)) W_j(Gamma_(y,z))^-1

is the ordered product of X_j edges conjugated by the corresponding
W_j partial paths. Reverse oriented edges can be treated with the
same bounds by writing their relative factor as a transported inverse;
all path transports have at most q_L edges.
By(2), every conjugation has norm at most exp(2C_0 q_L s_j a).
Using ||Log X_j||<=C s_j b in the finite product yields

 ||T_j-I||,||T_j^-1-I|| <= C_1 s_(j+1)b,                (3)

on a fixed smaller b ball. More explicitly the pre-smallness bound is
exp(C q_L exp(2C_0q_Ls_j a) s_j b)-1. Since q_L/L is bounded at fixed d
and s_(j+1)<=1, this implies(3) with a fixed C_1.
Choose b so every T_j lies within a fixed logarithm ball, say
||T_j-I||<=1/4. Then ||Log T_j||<=2||T_j-I||.

STEP3. CENTERED FACTORS IN THE ACTUAL RETURN

Let m_j(y)=|B(y)|^-1 sum_z Log T_j(y,z), a_j=exp m_j.
This is exactly B98(105). From(3), ||m_j||<=C_2 s_(j+1)b.
The centered factor appearing in(106) is

                  D_j(y,z)=a_j(y)^-1 T_j(y,z).

The elementary exponential-product estimates give

 ||D_j-I||,||D_j^-1-I|| <= C_3 s_(j+1)b.                (4)

No assertion that a logarithmic group mean is a multiplicative cocycle
is needed; the factor is bounded in its displayed order.

STEP4. HIERARCHICAL BACKGROUND TRANSPORT

The background transporter P^0_(j+1)(x_(j+1),x) in B98(106) is a
product of one endpoint path at each level ell=0,...,j. Each such
path has at most q_L edges OF ITS OWN LEVEL. Hence(2) implies

 ||P^0_(j+1)||,||[P^0_(j+1)]^-1||
      <= exp(C_0 q_L a sum_(ell=0)^j s_ell)
      <= exp(C_4 a s_(j+1)),                            (5)

because sum_(ell=0)^j s_ell=(s_(j+1)-eta)/(L-1).
Counting L^j paths and bounding every edge at the largest scale would
be a wrong estimate. The hierarchical expression contains only one
short path at each grade; the geometric spacings already account for
its fine-scale physical extent.

STEP5. MULTIPLY THE LITERAL RETURN FACTORS

B98(106), in the verified notation of the companion note, is

 u(x)= product_(j=k-1 down to0)
  Ad_([P^0_(j+1)]^-1) D_j(x_(j+1),x_j).

Call each displayed factor F_j. By(4),(5),

 ||F_j-I||,||F_j^-1-I||
       <= C_3 exp(2C_4 a s_(j+1)) s_(j+1)b
       <= C_5 s_(j+1)b.                                (6)

The fixed a ceiling is absorbed in C_5. Thus

 ||u||,||u^-1|| <= product_j (1+C_5s_(j+1)b)
                 <= exp(C_5 b sum_(j<k)s_(j+1)).

The geometric sum is

 sum_(j<k)s_(j+1)=L(1-L^-k)/(L-1) <= L/(L-1).

This proves the first bound(A). Product telescoping yields
||u-I||<=product_j(1+C_5s_(j+1)b)-1<=exp(Cb)-1<=C'b;
the inverse product in reverse order gives the same estimate.
Every formula uses the same finite joint analytic branches. Strict
smallness in Steps1-3 leaves a larger uniform ball for A',A, so the
finite product is jointly holomorphic there, with bound(A). Uniform
Cauchy bounds for finitely many parameter directions may then be taken
on any strictly smaller ball; no spatial smoothness is inferred.

STEP6. B109 ENDPOINT MEAN CONVENTION

B109 replaces the single coordinate-order endpoint path by the analytic
left/right-equivariant group mean of their finitely many transports,
(0.5)-(0.11), and uses this same endpoint in (0.12). The p254 transfer
statement imports B98's estimates for that convention.
For the elementary estimates above, the only added finite-primitive
fact is the following: uniformly over strict real regular local reads,
P_j(V;y,z) is holomorphic on a fixed complex neighborhood, with bounded
first derivatives after factoring an anchor endpoint path. This follows
from (0.6), local analyticity of the chosen group mean on a fixed
small-diameter neighborhood, and compactness of the finite real local
read at fixed L. All path differences are small loops controlled on
the native strict regularity domain. The dimension of this local read
and its constants depend only on fixed L,d and group.

Consequently(1) implies

 ||P_j(W_j)(P_j(W_j^r))^-1-I|| <= C_L s_j a,
 ||P_j(C_j)P_j(W_j)^-1-I|| <= C_L s_j b,

with inverse bounds on a smaller ball. P_j(W_j^r) is unitary by(0.9).
The same estimates as(3)-(6) follow, with fixed C_L in place of the
single-path constants. Hierarchical P remains an ordered product of
one such endpoint transporter at each level, so the geometric sum is
unchanged. This is a local finite-dimensional consequence, not a claim
of analytic domain admission for source localizers or complete currents.

FAILURE TESTS AND EXCLUSIONS

1. The real reference W_real and its complete curvature packets are
   required. Arbitrary complex backgrounds are not covered.
2. A whole noisy prefix must still be represented on ONE such chart,
   on all required source contours. Separate charts at separate real
   points do not furnish that representation.
3. No C1 estimate for u is claimed. B98 printed43 explicitly states
   returns(104)-(106) generally fail(165); native averaged tests(166),
   (167) replace that stronger condition.
4. Gauge invariance of a pair domain allows choosing representatives
   only for the simultaneous evaluated field/current pair. This note
   does not recompute the current or alter a real noise variable.
5. This bound is for one actual depth-k return, uniform in k. Products
   of returns from DIFFERENT ordinary-prefix stages require a separate
   sum of their actual b_j costs; if those costs are C delta_j, their
   product is exp(C sum delta_j), but this last hypothesis must be proved.
6. No masks, original normalizers, Jacobians, source contacts, or
   complement masses are removed by these pointwise estimates.
