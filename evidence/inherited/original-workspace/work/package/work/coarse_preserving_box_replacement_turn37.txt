COARSE-PRESERVING NORMALIZED BARE-BOX REPLACEMENT BY DISINTEGRATION
19 October 2026. admission_proof. Turn37 construction and exact bounds.

RESULT

The original normalized box replacement extends to arbitrary Borel
coarse statistics local to each separated core. Disintegrate the ACTUAL
conditional law, preserve its coarse marginal, and condition on the
proved window only on fibers whose window mass is at least 1-theta.
Leave every other fiber unchanged. This avoids identifying a singular
fiber measure with a bounded perturbation of product Haar.

The exact joint law of the exterior and all chosen coarse statistics
is preserved. Conditional discrepancy is at most theta after fixing
the coarse data, but its exterior-conditional mean is at most alpha,
the original bare tail. With eta=min(1,alpha/theta), the union of a bad
boundary, a bad fiber, and a discrepancy has joint-inclusion parameter
beta+(1-beta)eta. No extra +alpha or +theta is needed in that bound.

There is no native analytic coefficient/domain conclusion. The result
is an almost-everywhere disintegration theorem for the first actual
bare law and for statistics with the specified complete local reads.

1. ACTUAL CONDITIONAL PRODUCT AND COMPLETE COARSE READS

Let I be a finite family of separated box cores. In the bare Wilson
application, use vertex-disjoint embedded boxes and include EVERY box
boundary link in the common exterior omega. Then no plaquette depends
on two core interiors, and the original probability is exactly

 mu(domega,du)=mu_X(domega) product_i Q_i^omega(du_i).     (1.1)

The Q_i are the normalized compact conditional Wilson kernels, not
Gaussian replacements. The abstract proof below only requires (1.1)
on standard Borel spaces with measurable probability kernels.

For each i let B_i be an exterior-measurable bad-boundary event, and
let W_i(omega) be a measurable subset of its original interior space.
Assume, for a fixed 0<alpha<1,

 Q_i^omega(W_i^c)<=alpha on B_i^c,                         (1.2)
 mu_X(intersection_(i in S) B_i)<=beta^|S| for all S,       (1.3)

where 0<=beta<=1. The fixed-box archived theorem and the polylog-box
theorem supply (1.2) on their respective boundary windows. The latter
by itself does not supply (1.3); a boundary-array theorem is a separate
input whenever (1.3) is used.

Choose any Borel map

              Y_i=A_i(omega,u_i) in a standard Borel space E_i.

This may be a finite collection of group-valued/real constraints and
branch flags; it need not take finitely many values. Its COMPLETE
read may use omega and core i, but no other core interior. Define it
on ORIGINAL link variables. The forest gauge is used to define W_i,
not to silently replace the prescribed coarse statistic by an average
of gauge-transformed links.

Every additional coarse observable of the form
C(omega,Y_1,...,Y_|I|) will also be preserved exactly. Thus a constraint
spanning more than one core is covered if it factors through the
chosen separate local statistics. Recording all local path segments
may suffice in a particular map. Recording each whole core is always
possible but can make the construction trivial; see Section9.

Conditioning directly on a coupled statistic such as u_1+u_2 does
NOT in general leave the interiors independent. The product and array
claims below require the boxwise factorization just stated.

2. REGULAR CONDITIONAL PROBABILITIES AND NULL FIBERS

Let nu_i^omega=(A_i(omega,.))_* Q_i^omega. Parameterized regular
conditional probabilities give jointly measurable kernels
R_i^(omega,y_i) with

 Q_i^omega(du_i)=integral nu_i^omega(dy_i) R_i^(omega,y_i)(du_i),
 R_i^(omega,y_i){A_i(omega,u_i)=y_i}=1                    (2.1)

for the relevant almost-everywhere values. It is enough to construct
these by disintegrating the joint law of (omega,u_i,Y_i); all claims
then hold for mu_X-almost every omega and nu_i^omega-almost every y_i.

The exact joint exterior/coarse law is

 Lambda(domega,dy)=mu_X(domega) product_i nu_i^omega(dy_i). (2.2)

Given (omega,y), the actual interior conditional law is product_i R_i.
To prove this, test the proposed kernel against products of bounded
functions of the separate pairs (u_i,y_i), use (1.1),(2.1), and extend
by the monotone-class theorem. No independence of the boundary events
B_i is required.

Define

                    p_i(omega,y_i)=R_i^(omega,y_i)(W_i^c).

It is a measurable [0,1]-valued function, and the defining identity is

 integral p_i(omega,y_i) nu_i^omega(dy_i)
                         =Q_i^omega(W_i^c)<=alpha on B_i^c. (2.3)

An individual fiber often has zero Q_i mass because Y_i is continuous.
The theorem nevertheless gives an RCP for almost every coarse value;
it does not assert a bound or a canonical kernel at EVERY prescribed
value, including null exceptional values. Values of kernels there may
be assigned arbitrarily, with no effect on the constructed measures.
In particular no separate coarea or FP density formula has been proved.

3. GOOD FIBERS, THE EXACT REPLACEMENT, AND NORMALIZATION

Fix 0<theta<1. Put

 F_i={B_i^c and p_i>theta},
 G_i={B_i^c and p_i<=theta},
 q_i=1_(G_i) p_i.

F_i is the bad-fiber mark. Define the replacement kernel on a fiber by

 P_i^(omega,y_i)=R_i^(omega,y_i)(. | W_i) on G_i,
 P_i^(omega,y_i)=R_i^(omega,y_i) otherwise.                (3.1)

The first denominator is 1-p_i>=1-theta>0. The second line includes
both bad boundaries and bad fibers, including a fiber with p_i=1.
Every P_i remains concentrated on the SAME coarse fiber by (2.1).

The new measure is

 nu(domega,dy,dv)=Lambda(domega,dy) product_i P_i^(omega,y_i)(dv_i).
                                                                    (3.2)

It is a probability and has EXACTLY the original (omega,Y) law Lambda.
After forgetting y this remains a measure on the original fine links.
All chosen coarse values can be preserved pointwise in the coupling
of Section4, not merely in expectation.

Its Radon--Nikodym derivative relative to the ORIGINAL bare law is

 dnu/dmu=product_i M_i(omega,u_i),
 M_i=1_(G_i^c)+1_(G_i) 1_(W_i)(u_i)/(1-p_i),              (3.3)

where y_i=A_i(omega,u_i) in every coefficient. Conditional on (omega,Y),
each factor has original R_i expectation one. Thus the product does
too. Multiplying (3.3) into the unnormalized original Wilson measure
retains its total partition mass EXACTLY. The denominators are genuine
fiber probabilities; no Gaussian determinant or vacuum scalar replaces
them, and no source-dependent normalization has been introduced.

All bounded or integrable functions of (omega,Y), including their
full generating functions where defined, have identical expectations
under mu and nu. A global product density bound is unnecessary.

The local operator 'hold omega,Y_i fixed and redraw from P_i' is
idempotent on the supported law. Operators on different boxes commute
because their coarse maps, windows and selection rules do not read
another interior. All kernels and selection rules in this statement
are frozen from the original Q_i; they are not recomputed after a
partial replacement.

4. COARSE-PRESERVING COUPLING AND TWO DISCREPANCY SCALES

On G_i with p_i>0 let H_i=R_i(. | W_i^c); otherwise choose any
measurable probability kernel on the same fiber, whose coefficient is
zero. Then the exact identity is

                  R_i=(1-q_i)P_i+q_i H_i.                (4.1)

Sample (omega,Y) with Lambda. Independently across i conditional on
these variables, sample V_i~P_i, D_i~Bernoulli(q_i), and H_i as needed;
put U_i=V_i when D_i=0 and draw U_i~H_i when D_i=1.
Then U has the original law, V the replacement law, and

           A_i(omega,U_i)=A_i(omega,V_i)=Y_i a.s.         (4.2)

On a discrepancy, U_i is outside W_i and V_i inside. Otherwise the
fields are identical. Thus D_i is exactly the discrepancy indicator.

After fixing both exterior and coarse values, the D_i are independent
Bernoulli(q_i), with q_i<=theta. They therefore admit iid Bernoulli(theta)
domination on this conditional level.

After fixing only the exterior, the complete box experiments remain
independent because their Y_i are independent. Their discrepancy
parameters are

 d_i(omega)=integral p_i 1_(G_i) dnu_i^omega
                    <=alpha 1_(B_i^c).                  (4.3)

Consequently the discrepancy array, with coarse values integrated,
is stochastically dominated by iid Bernoulli(alpha), and

                 P(D_i=1 for all i in S)<=alpha^|S|.     (4.4)

For precision, the independent uniforms realizing theta domination
conditional on (omega,Y) need not be the uniforms realizing alpha
domination after integrating Y. The latter follows from the conditional
product Bernoulli parameters d_i(omega)<=alpha, or can be realized by
sampling those Bernoulli marks first and then the corresponding Y_i
conditional on them. It is NOT an alpha bound at each fixed fiber.

Expanding the products in (4.1) also gives the archived exact positive
failure expansion with Lambda as base and q_i as weights. The empty
term is weighted by product_i(1-q_i(omega,Y_i)); it is not a constant
multiple of nu. There is no fictitious positive native-history measure.

5. SHARP JOINT UNCONTROLLED-BOX BOUNDS

Let eta=min(1,alpha/theta). From (2.3),

              P(F_i | omega)<=eta 1_(B_i^c).             (5.1)

The bad-fiber law is unchanged by replacement because it is a function
of (omega,Y). Define the coupling's uncontrolled mark

                         C_i=1_(B_i) OR 1_(F_i) OR D_i.

These three alternatives are mutually exclusive at a given box. On
a good boundary, its exterior-conditional parameter is exactly

 c_i(omega)=integral [1_(p_i>theta)+p_i 1_(p_i<=theta)] dnu_i^omega.

For 0<=p<=1,

                1_(p>theta)+p 1_(p<=theta)<=p/theta.

Hence c_i<=eta on B_i^c and c_i=1 on B_i. Conditional on omega the
C_i are independent, so for every S

 P(C_i=1 for all i in S)
 <=E product_(i in S)[eta+(1-eta)1_(B_i)]
 <=[eta+(1-eta)beta]^|S|.                                (5.2)

The last line expands the finite product and uses only (1.3), not
independence of bad boundaries. The resulting parameter

                        kappa=beta+(1-beta)eta            (5.3)

is sharper than beta+alpha/theta+alpha or beta+alpha/theta+theta.
There is no need to charge a discrepancy separately after bounding
bad fibers: the same mean p controls their disjoint union.

The eta bound is optimal using only E[p]<=alpha. For alpha<theta,
take p=theta+epsilon on a fraction alpha/(theta+epsilon) of fibers
and zero elsewhere, then send epsilon down to zero. Independent
bad-boundary marks of parameter beta show the corresponding sharpness
of (5.3) within these probabilistic hypotheses.

Equation (5.2) alone is a joint-inclusion bound, not a claim of iid
stochastic domination for C. It implies, for t>=0 and finite A,

 E exp[t sum_(i in A) C_i] <=[1+kappa(e^t-1)]^|A|.        (5.4)

Choosing theta=sqrt(alpha) gives eta=sqrt(alpha), a uniform good-fiber
denominator at least 1-sqrt(alpha), and conditional discrepancy at
most sqrt(alpha). The annealed discrepancy bound stays alpha. Other
theta trade the good-fiber denominator against the number of excluded
fibers; there is no uniquely optimal threshold for all uses.

6. ACTUAL WINDOW DEFECTS ARE BETTER THAN THE UNCONTROLLED LABEL

On a good boundary the replacement's probability of being outside W_i
is

 r_i(omega)=integral p_i 1_(p_i>theta) dnu_i^omega<=alpha.

There is the exact mass identity

              d_i(omega)+r_i(omega)=Q_i^omega(W_i^c)
                                     on B_i^c.          (6.1)

Thus the original bad-window mass is divided into mass removed by
the coupling and mass retained on bad fibers. The replacement creates
no new window failure: in the coupling, its W_i^c indicator is at
most the original one, pointwise.

For the replacement's actual defect B_i OR {V_i outside W_i}, the
same conditional product proof gives the stronger joint bound

        [beta+(1-beta)alpha]^|S|.                         (6.2)

Unlike the unconstrained archived replacement, the present operation
cannot in general reduce the replacement defect to B_i alone. A fiber
may be wholly outside W_i and must then be left unchanged to preserve
its coarse value. The bound alpha in (6.2) cannot be universally
improved: take p_i=1 on a fraction alpha of fibers and zero elsewhere.

7. LOCAL COMPARISONS AND EXACT DENOMINATOR COST

For the joint law of (omega,Y) and the interiors in a finite set A,
the total variation distance, with convention sup_events, is exactly

 ||mu_(omega,Y,U_A)-nu_(omega,Y,V_A)||TV
   =E_Lambda[1-product_(i in A)(1-q_i)]
   =E_(mu_X)[1-product_(i in A)(1-d_i(omega))]
   <=1-(1-alpha)^|A|<=alpha |A|.                         (7.1)

On the replacement support the conditional density relative to the
original is at least one; its complement has original probability
1-product(1-q_i). This proves the equality, as does the disjoint-support
coupling. Forgetting some preserved coordinates gives an upper bound.

For a bounded observable F depending on these interiors and arbitrary
preserved (omega,Y),

 |mu(F)-nu(F)|<=2||F||infty alpha |A|.                    (7.2)

With finite/summable box oscillations the sharper bound is
alpha sum_i osc_i(F). Pure coarse/exterior observables have zero
error exactly, regardless of their oscillation as functions of Y.

The directed relative entropy is also controlled with actual
normalization:

 D(nu_(omega,Y,V_A) || mu_(omega,Y,U_A))
     =E_Lambda sum_(i in A) 1_(G_i)[-log(1-p_i)]
     <=|A| alpha/(1-theta).                             (7.3)

The reverse entropy can be infinite, since some original fine fields
are removed. The bounds contain no distant-box count. They retain
the ordinary bounded-observable norms and do not prove bounds on
physically renormalized tensor insertions by themselves.

8. GAUGE COVARIANCE AND NATIVE BRANCH QUALIFICATIONS

The original Q_i transform covariantly under the finite-lattice gauge
action. The good-boundary condition and W_i must be gauge invariant
when lifted back from the complete boundary-tree/forest convention.
If the coarse maps are invariant, or equivariant with a specified
action on Y_i preserving the sigma field generated by (omega,Y), then
conditional expectation uniqueness gives the corresponding covariance
of p_i and the replacement density (3.3), almost everywhere. Hence
the replacement MEASURE is gauge invariant. No assertion of a single
pointwise equivariant RCP at every exceptional coarse value is needed.

An arbitrary measurable statistic need not have this covariance, so
gauge invariance is an additional conclusion only under the preceding
equivariance condition. Exact preservation of that statistic itself
does not require it to be gauge invariant.

When an ordinary native average is only defined on a validity branch,
one can record the validity flag and use a distinguished invalid value
to make a total Borel statistic. One may also leave invalid branches
unchanged. Such extra exclusions preserve the coarse law and decrease
discrepancies, but an uncontrolled-box bound counting those extra
branches needs THEIR probability bound as well. They cannot silently
be included in beta or alpha/theta.

In particular validity of an ordinary average can read an exterior
collar larger than the bare box boundary. Good bare boundary data
alone need not imply that validity. No invalid-selector probability
is inferred unless W itself implies validity on the complete read,
or a separate collar estimate supplies it.

An actual finite measurable branch or density multiplier can be
included among the retained local statistics. Whenever the resulting
K(omega,Y) is integrable, its weighted integrals are preserved exactly:
integral K dnu=integral K dmu. The same holds with any further retained
coarse test. This is an equality of integrals, not a rarity bound
under the K-weighted law. A singular delta/gauge constraint is not
automatically such a finite measurable multiplier; evaluation on its
null fiber needs its own disintegration/version identification.

Likewise conditioning afterward on a selected coarse branch changes
the relevant denominator. The bound E[p|omega]<=alpha does not imply
the same bound after conditioning on an arbitrarily rare event in Y.
The theorem preserves that branch's probability exactly, but makes
no uniform claim about the normalized kernel within it.

9. SHARPNESS AND ABSENCE OF ANALYTIC CONTROL

If A_i records the entire fine core u_i, then R_i is a point mass,
p_i is either zero or one, and the operation is identically the
original law. It still satisfies every theorem above. Thus allowing
all chosen constraints is logically valid but does not guarantee a
nontrivial smoothing/resampling operation on those constraints.

Small p and small normalization logs imply no coarse-coordinate
derivative bound. Explicitly let Y be uniform on the circle, let
B be binary with conditional probability

              P(B=1|Y=y)=p(y)=alpha(1+sin(Ny)),
              W={B=0},  2alpha<=theta<1.

Then E p=alpha and EVERY fiber is good, but

 d/dy[-log(1-p(y))]=alpha N cos(Ny)/(1-p(y)).              (9.1)

Its supremum is at least alpha N, arbitrarily large with N. In the
complex extension, zeros of 1-p(z) can approach the real circle on
a distance of order N^-1, so no common holomorphic neighborhood is
implied either. This is a countertest of an inference, not a claim
about the particular Wilson kernel's dependence on its averages.

Disintegration and Markov's inequality therefore supply exact
normalization, preserved coarse law, and quantitative sparse marks;
they do not supply analytic native source coefficients, current
bounds, or an admissible complex original domain on each good fiber.

10. RELATION TO THE ARCHIVE AND TO THE POLYLOG BOX THEOREM

The archived normalized_box_replacement_research.txt proves the same
conditional-product replacement when only the exterior is preserved.
It uses the boundary-level probability Q_i(W_i^c), and therefore can
condition every good-boundary box on W_i. Its coupling, partition
normalization, local TV and entropy proofs are reused here after
enlarging the preserved data to (omega,Y).

The NEW step is the intervening disintegration and threshold split:
bare tail smallness holds only on average over coarse fibers. It gives
rare bad fibers, rather than a uniform fixed-fiber tail. The sharper
joint uncontrolled bound (5.2) and the discrepancy/retained-failure
identity (6.1) keep that distinction explicit.

For polylogarithmically growing bare boxes, substitute the proved
alpha from polylog_box_conditional_tail_turn37.txt, or its justified
real perturbation extension. When alpha is superpolynomially small
in g, theta=sqrt(alpha) preserves that property. If a compatible
beta boundary-array bound is also available, (5.2),(6.2) give the
corresponding sparse-array conclusions independently of ambient volume.

For the FIRST bare pushforward, one can choose all locally determined
coarse averages needed for that pushforward, together with exact
branch/frame data as above. The resulting entire coarse marginal
is unchanged. It does not follow that the operation equals the
published native R replacement, that its good fibers have native
analytic domains, or that later effective conditional laws retain
the original box product. Those are separate matching statements.

The exact coarse-law preservation has no box-count error. By contrast,
the generic fine-law TV estimate (7.1) need not vanish for all boxes
in a fixed physical region as the lattice is refined: their number
may grow faster than alpha decreases, especially when g runs only
logarithmically with the ultraviolet cutoff. No physical-region
fine-law convergence is inferred from the phrase 'superpolynomial
in g'. A better observable estimate would need its actual summable
box oscillations or another argument.
