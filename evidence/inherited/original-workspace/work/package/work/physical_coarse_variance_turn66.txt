RESULT

There is an actual original-Wilson-law variance theorem for a bounded
observable of fixed PHYSICAL size. For an edge-simple thin SU2 loop
of length l, choose m>=l/19 edges no two of which share an action
plaquette. With W=Tr U(loop)/2 and beta>=0, put

                 eta=exp[-c m/(1+beta)].

Uniformly in finite nondegenerate periodic volume and in every
conditioned exterior of those edges,

 |E(W | exterior)|<=eta,
 |E(W^2 | exterior)-1/4|<=(3/4)eta.                    (0.1)

Thus Var_W(W)->1/4 for a fixed physical square when a(1+beta)->0.
However, its covariance with ANY bounded observable avoiding the
chosen edges goes to zero at the same rate. Its adjacent-slice
increment tends to1/4, and its centered reflected covariance tends
to zero. This is a rigorous physical-size variance floor carried
by thin-loop noise, not a nontrivial continuum witness.

The underlying perimeter suppression is a classical phenomenon,
not a claim of a new general perimeter theorem. Sections1--5 give
a self-contained quantitative SU2 conditional proof, its variance
and reflected consequences, and an optional whole-law strengthening.

Sections6--8 return to the actual native coarse question. Large-I
does supply the old-marginal-preserving joint law, and Large-II
supplies a nondegenerate conditional reference at a fixed coarse
scale. These two facts do NOT transport its reference variance
to an original observable. On the literal native branch there
are explicit centered current tests with strictly positive
reference variance whose pullback to EVERY original field is zero.
This statement retains the source's normalizer and all old records.

The native nonlinear group average is not identified here with
a thin fine path. The thin-loop theorem therefore does not rule
out the averaged, thick, flowed, or renormalized alternatives.
The actual full-original coarse conditional floor remains unproved,
with its missing input identified precisely in Section8.

1. EXACT ORIGINAL CONDITIONAL LAW ON A SPARSE EDGE SET

Work on a four-dimensional finite periodic hypercubic lattice with
all periods at least4, action beta sum_p h_p, h_p=1-Tr U_p/2.
Haar measures are normalized, and the expectation always uses
the full original Wilson partition function.

Let C be a loop that uses each unoriented edge at most once.
In the conflict graph on its edges, join two edges if an elementary
action plaquette contains both. Each edge belongs to six plaquettes,
each having three other edges, so this graph has degree at most18.
A greedy independent set I={e1,...,em} has m>=l/19, up to harmless
integer rounding. The chosen edges are integrated, not fixed
boundary edges.

Condition on the sigma algebra E generated by ALL other links.
No action plaquette contains two chosen variables. The complete
conditional density therefore factorizes exactly:

       mu(dq1...dqm | E)=product_i f_(kappa_i,s_i)(qi)dqi,

 f_(kappa,s)(q)=Z(kappa)^-1 exp[kappa ReTr(s^-1 q)/2],
       0<=kappa_i<=6beta,  s_i in SU2.                  (1.1)

At zero staple choose s arbitrarily. This factorization does not
discard any Wilson plaquette or require a favorable exterior.
It is the same compact one-link conditional law as TURN45/65.
Its centering s_i and strength kappa_i are exterior-measurable.

Writing the loop in traversal order,

 U(C)=C0 q1^(eps1) C1 ... qm^(epsm) Cm, eps_i in{+1,-1}, (1.2)

all C_i are fixed after conditioning on E. Distinct selected
edges occur once; the proof would not apply unchanged to a
word that repeats one of them.

2. EXACT REPRESENTATION COEFFICIENTS AND A UNIFORM GAP

Write q=cos(theta)I+i sin(theta)n.sigma. The centered density
f_(kappa,1) is a class function. In the irreducible spin-j
representation of dimension d_j=2j+1,

 int D^j(q) f_(kappa,1)(q)dq=c_j(kappa) I_(d_j),
       c_j(kappa)=I_(2j+1)(kappa)/I_1(kappa),            (2.1)

with its continuous value0 at kappa=0 for j>0.
Here I_n is the modified Bessel function.

For an explicit normalization check, Weyl integration gives

 Z(kappa)=(2/pi)int_0^pi e^(kappa cos theta)sin^2(theta)dtheta
         =I_0(kappa)-I_2(kappa)=2I_1(kappa)/kappa,

 int chi_j(q)e^(kappa ReTr q/2)dq
         =I_(2j)(kappa)-I_(2j+2)(kappa)
         =2(2j+1)I_(2j+1)(kappa)/kappa.

Divide by d_j Z to obtain(2.1). The recurrences follow by
integrating the derivative of e^(kappa cos theta)sin(n theta);
positivity of I_n for kappa>0 follows from its power series.
No asymptotic Bessel substitution is needed in the bound below.

Let t=ReTr q/2 under f_(kappa,1), and set
S_kappa=E(1-t^2). There is an absolute c0>0 such that

                   S_kappa>=c0/(1+kappa).              (2.2)

For kappa<=2 the density relative to Haar is at least e^-4,
and E_Haar(1-t^2)=3/4. For kappa>=2 put u=1-t. Its unnormalized
density is e^kappa e^(-kappa u)sqrt(u(2-u)), 0<u<2.
The full integral is at most C e^kappa kappa^(-3/2).
On u in[1/(2kappa),1/kappa] the integral is at least
c e^kappa kappa^(-3/2); hence this band has probability
at least a constant. There 1-t^2=u(2-u)>=u>=1/(2kappa).
This proves(2.2) uniformly, including kappa=0.

For the fundamental and adjoint characters,

 c_(1/2)=E t,             c_1=(4E t^2-1)/3.

Their coefficients are nonnegative by(2.1), and

 1-c_(1/2)=E(1-t)>=S_kappa/2,
 1-c_1=(4/3)S_kappa.

It follows, after decreasing a single absolute c>0, that

 0<=c_(1/2)(kappa),c_1(kappa)
       <=exp[-c/(1+kappa)]
       <=exp[-c'/(1+beta)],  kappa<=6beta.              (2.3)

The last implication uses 1+6beta<=6(1+beta). Constants below
absorb this factor. In particular the bound is valid at beta=0.

Translations and inversion are harmless:

 E[D^j(q) | E]=c_j(kappa)D^j(s),
 E[D^j(q^-1) | E]=c_j(kappa)D^j(s^-1).                 (2.4)

Every remaining matrix has operator norm1. Conditional independence
in(1.1) therefore gives the exact identity

 E[D^j(U(C)) | E]
   =product_i c_j(kappa_i)
      D^j(C0 s1^(eps1) C1 ... sm^(epsm) Cm).            (2.5)

This retains every deterministic intervening link. Replacing a
conditional staple by the identity is unnecessary and would
not be an exact computation.

3. PHYSICAL-SIZE VARIANCE UNDER THE FULL ORIGINAL LAW

In SU2, (Tr U)^2=1+chi_1(U). Thus for W=Tr U(C)/2,

 W^2=(1+chi_1(U(C)))/4.

Use(2.3)--(2.5), |chi_j(U)|<=2j+1, and the definition of eta.
The two inequalities(0.1) hold for every exterior. Consequently

 Var(W | E)>=1/4-(3/4)eta-eta^2,
 |Var_mu(W)-1/4|<=(3/4)eta+eta^2.                      (3.1)

The second formula also uses |EW|<=eta. Thus there is a definite
positive floor, for example1/8 after eta is sufficiently small.
No selected-sector mass, weak-field chart, coupling calibration,
large-field deletion, or conditional denominator estimate appears.

For a square with fixed physical side ell>0, lying in a fixed
spatial time slice and not winding around the finite spatial torus,
l_a=4ell/a up to lattice approximation and m_a>=l_a/19.
If a(1+beta_a)->0, then eta_a->0 and

                         Var(W_a)->1/4.                (3.2)

Logarithmic beta growth satisfies this condition, but the proof
does not establish that beta growth or identify a renormalized
coupling. The region enclosed by the loop has fixed physical
area. This is therefore distinct from the one-plaquette witness
of TURN65, even though the fluctuation mechanism is still ultraviolet.

4. THE SAME THEOREM GIVES REFLECTED COLLAPSE

If H is E-measurable and |H|<=B, then(0.1) gives

 |E(H W)|<=B eta,
 |Cov(H,W)|<=2B eta.                                    (4.1)

No spatial separation estimate is being imported: H may depend
on every original link except the selected ones, and the bound
comes from their exact conditional averaging.

In particular let W_1 be the one-time-step translate of a spatial
loop W_0. The selected spatial edges on slice0 are absent from W_1.
Translation invariance and(4.1) yield

 d_a=E[(W_1-W_0)^2]/2=E W_0^2-E(W_1W_0),
                   |d_a-1/4|<=(7/4)eta_a.               (4.2)

Thus d_a->1/4, while the variance also tends to1/4.
This is a stronger conclusion than failure to prove d_a=O(a_time).

Take next a reflection with the loop a fixed positive physical
distance from its plane, and with the reflected loop edge-disjoint.
The reflected variable is E-measurable. In the reflection-positive
Wilson law,

 0<=E[(Theta(W_a-EW_a))(W_a-EW_a)]<=2eta_a ->0.          (4.3)

Every fixed separated translate has the same covariance collapse.
This conclusion DOES exclude a nonzero separated reflected
component for this family, unlike the weaker microscopic
one-step lower bound in TURN65.

It does not rule out thick loops, positive-flow-time loops,
unbounded or source-renormalized loop families, or native
nonlinear block averages. Their complete read or normalization
violates an input of this particular calculation.

5. OPTIONAL STRONGER CONDITIONAL HAAR LIMIT

The entire conditional holonomy law, not only its first two
characters, has a useful explicit bound. The recurrence

        I_n(kappa)-I_(n+2)(kappa)
                   =2(n+1)I_(n+1)(kappa)/kappa>0

implies for every integer n>=2 that I_n is at most I_2 or I_3,
according to parity. Thus(2.3) bounds ALL nontrivial c_j by
rho_beta=exp[-c/(1+beta)].

Each centered conditional density has

 ||f_(kappa,1)||_infinity<=C(1+kappa)^(3/2),
 ||f_(kappa,1)||_2<=C(1+beta)^(3/4).                    (5.1)

The first estimate follows from the spherical-cap lower bound
Z(kappa)>=c e^kappa(1+kappa)^(-3/2); the second follows by
integrating f^2<=||f||_infinity f.

The law in(2.5) is a deterministic translate of a convolution
of the m central conditional densities. This also follows
by moving the independent central factors past the deterministic
intervening links, conjugating them without changing their laws.
Plancherel, or contraction on each nontrivial Peter--Weyl block,
therefore gives, for m>=1,

 ||density(U(C)|E)-1||_1
    <=||density(U(C)|E)-1||_2
    <=C(1+beta)^(3/4) rho_beta^(m-1).                   (5.2)

Translations preserve both Haar and these norms. If one factor
has kappa=0 the conditional law is already exactly Haar.

For every fixed bounded class function f on SU2, (5.2) controls
the conditional mean of f(U(C)) minus its Haar mean, uniformly
in E. It also controls f^2. Whenever its right side tends to zero,
the full-law variance tends to Var_Haar(f), and covariances of
the Haar-centered observable with bounded exterior tests vanish.
The sufficient condition is

 c(m-1)/(1+beta)-(3/4)log(1+beta)->+infinity.             (5.3)

Physical squares at logarithmic beta satisfy it. Merely m/beta
diverging arbitrarily slowly is sufficient for(3.2), but not
automatically for the stronger estimate(5.2).

The result shows concretely how many bounded nonlinear thin-loop
tests can have nontrivial ordinary limiting laws but zero separated
OS norm. It does not identify a compatible continuum loop theory.

8. WHICH COARSE OBSERVABLE STILL NEEDS A FULL-LAW FLOOR

There are three different objects here.

1. A coarse holonomy assembled from prescribed fine path products.
For a macroscopic edge-simple loop this is the thin-loop theorem
of Sections1--5. Its bounded unrenormalized trace is not a
nonzero separated witness in the stated scaling regime.

2. A holonomy of the native ordinary nonlinear BLOCK AVERAGE.
The program's corrected-average bridge
nonflat_reference_averages_research.md, Section1, and the
flat_nonlinear_average_research.md definition distinguish this
from a single path. On their admitted neighborhood the flat
corrected differential averages parallel strings, schematically

 Q A_mu(y)=L^(-d-1)sum_(x in B(y))sum_(r=0)^(L-1)
                                      A_mu(x+r e_mu).

Endpoint gauge factors relate the ordinary and corrected maps.
Those factors cancel in coarse gauge-invariant loop tests, but
they do not replace the average over many strings by one string.
These archived analytic definitions retain their recorded
source/regularity scope; no new global averaging theorem is
claimed here. In particular conditional factorization(1.2)
is not proved for a loop of this nonlinear average.

3. The native RT current variable after reference replacements.
Its full-state floor concerns a further stochastic variable.
For an original candidate F(U_bare), the exact transported
first and second insertions are

normalization. The native reference f(current) is not
