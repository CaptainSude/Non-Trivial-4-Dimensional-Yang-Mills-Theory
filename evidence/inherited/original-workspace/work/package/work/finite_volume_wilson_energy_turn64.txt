FINITE-VOLUME ORIGINAL WILSON ENERGY DOMINATION -- TURN64
20 October 2026. Root derivation; based on the independently derived
one-slab transfer calculation in transfer_matrix_witness_turn64.txt.

RESULT AND SCOPE

For the SU(2) Wilson measure on any nondegenerate finite periodic
four-dimensional rectangular lattice, with positive homogeneous
plaquette coupling beta and normalized link Haar measures, put

 h(U)=1-(1/2)Tr U,   h_p=h(U_p).

For every finite plaquette set B and arbitrary numbers 0<=s_p<1/2,

 E_beta exp[sum_(p in B) s_p beta h_p]
              <= product_(p in B)(1-2s_p)^(-3/4).       (0.1)

In particular

 E_beta exp[(beta/4)sum_(p in B)h_p] <=2^(3|B|/4).      (0.2)

This is a bound under the FULL original finite Wilson probability
measure, with its exact partition normalization. There is no
thermodynamic-limit-first qualification or log(beta)/L surface term.
It does not assert a conditional bound after fixing arbitrary exterior
links, a bound for an effective RG density, or physical continuum
correlation control.

TURN39 already proved an arbitrary-plaquette-set exponential bound
after a periodic thermodynamic limit, using partition estimates and
chessboard inequalities. The new improvement is the finite-volume
scope and explicit constants. The proof below is independent of those
partition estimates and uses positive transfer operators and Schatten
Holder. It does not assume operator powers preserve Loewner order.

All periods can, for definiteness, be taken at least4. This avoids
degenerate short periodic cells and suffices for the intended limits.
The same proof allows positive couplings depending only on plaquette
orientation, replacing beta by beta_p throughout.

1. A SINGLE SU(2) LINK

Write W=cos(theta)I+i sin(theta)n.sigma, 0<=theta<=pi.
For central functions normalized Haar integration is

 integral f(W)dW=(2/pi)integral_0^pi f(theta)sin^2(theta)dtheta.

Set

 c(beta)=integral exp[-beta h(W)]dW
         =2 exp(-beta) I_1(beta)/beta,
 q_beta(W)=exp[-beta h(W)]/c(beta).                    (1.1)

The value at beta=0 is its continuous limit c(0)=1.
On the spin-j representation, of dimension d=2j+1, convolution
by q_beta has scalar eigenvalue

 lambda_d(beta)=I_d(beta)/I_1(beta), d=1,2,... .        (1.2)

Indeed the character is sin(d theta)/sin(theta), so its integral
against exp(beta cos theta) is I_(d-1)-I_(d+1)=2d I_d/beta.
Dividing by the representation dimension and by the integral
2I_1/beta gives (1.2).

These eigenvalues are positive and nondecreasing in beta. To check
monotonicity without importing a ratio bound, let

 Z_nu(beta)=integral_(-1)^1
                   exp(beta x)(1-x^2)^(nu-1/2)dx.

For nu>=1, the integral representation of I_nu gives

 d/d beta log Z_nu=I_(nu+1)/I_nu,
 d/d beta [I_(nu+1)/I_nu]=Var_(nu,beta)(x)>0.           (1.3)

Here the variance is for the displayed normalized positive integral.
The identity for the first derivative also follows by integration
by parts or the recurrence I_nu'=I_(nu+1)+(nu/beta)I_nu.
For d>1, lambda_d is the product of these increasing positive
ratios for nu=1,...,d-1; lambda_1=1. Hence, as positive operators,

                0<=Q_(beta-t)<=Q_beta, 0<=t<beta.      (1.4)

This is spectral/operator domination, not pointwise domination
of the two normalized kernels.

2. A SHARP ELEMENTARY ENERGY MOMENT BOUND

Under the normalized q_beta law, u=h(W)=1-cos(theta) has density
proportional to

             u^(1/2) exp(-beta u) (2-u)^(1/2) 1_[0,2](u).

It is a Gamma(shape3/2, rate beta) law tilted by the decreasing
nonnegative function w(u)=(2-u)^(1/2)1_[0,2](u).
For any increasing integrable phi and this decreasing w,

 E_gamma(phi w)<=E_gamma(phi)E_gamma(w).               (2.1)

For example, average
(phi(u)-phi(v))(w(u)-w(v))<=0 over two independent gamma variables.
Thus, for 0<=t<beta,

 c(beta-t)/c(beta)=E_(q_beta) exp(t h)
                       <=(1-t/beta)^(-3/2).            (2.2)

The same argument for phi(u)=u gives E_(q_beta)h<=3/(2beta).
No small-beta or large-beta approximation is used.

3. EXACT TRANSFER REPRESENTATION

Choose one coordinate as time and let its period be N. On a spatial
slice let E_s be the set of positively oriented spatial links.
Let H_phys be the gauge-invariant subspace of L2(SU(2)^(E_s),dHaar).
Let P be the normalized slice-gauge averaging projection.

With spatial plaquette action V_s, put M=exp(-V_s/2). It is a
bounded strictly positive multiplication operator preserving H_phys.
Let Q be the tensor product over spatial links of the convolution
operators Q_beta from Section1. Each factor is invariant under
simultaneous site gauge transformations of its two endpoint fields:
centrality of q_beta proves this even with link-dependent beta.
Thus Q commutes with P, and on H_phys

                         T=M Q M.                     (3.1)

Equivalently one may write P M Q M P on the full slice space.
T is positive, self-adjoint and trace class: the smooth compact-group
convolution has rapidly decaying Peter--Weyl eigenvalues, and M,P are
bounded. At positive beta it is injective on H_phys.

Integrating temporal links supplies the projection P. The exact
periodic Wilson partition function is

              Z_beta=c(beta)^(N |E_s|) Tr_phys(T^N).    (3.2)

No ground-state limit or informal temporal gauge fixing is needed.
Normalized gauge averages have mass one. With orientation-dependent
couplings the scalar factor is the product of the corresponding c's.

Now insert arbitrary temporal-plaquette factors exp(t_(j,e)h_(j,e)),
0<=t_(j,e)<beta, allowing DIFFERENT selected links and source strengths
on EVERY slab j=1,...,N. The source changes just that plaquette's
coefficient to beta-t_(j,e). Define

 Q_j=tensor_e Q_(beta-t_(j,e)),   T_j=M Q_j M,

with t=0 at unmarked links. By the common Peter--Weyl diagonalization
of Q_j,Q and (1.4), followed by restriction and sandwiching,

                         0<=T_j<=T.                   (3.3)

The normalized expectation of all these temporal insertions is
exactly

 product_(j,e) [c(beta-t_(j,e))/c(beta)]
               * Tr(T_1 T_2 ... T_N)/Tr(T^N).          (3.4)

The trace numerator is nonnegative because it represents the
original positive Wilson integral with the indicated real insertions.
The different T_j need not commute.

4. THE ARBITRARY-SLAB STEP

Generalized Schatten Holder gives

 |Tr(T_1 ... T_N)|
        <= product_j ||T_j||_N
         = product_j [Tr(T_j^N)]^(1/N).                (4.1)

For positive compact operators, (3.3) implies eigenvalue-by-eigenvalue
domination by the min--max principle. Therefore

                         Tr(T_j^N)<=Tr(T^N).           (4.2)

We do NOT claim T_j^N<=T^N as an operator: powers need not preserve
operator order. It is the ordered eigenvalue sum that is used.
Equations(4.1)--(4.2) show that the trace ratio in(3.4) is at most1.
Combining with(2.2) gives the full original-law bound

 E_beta exp[sum_(all selected temporal p) s_p beta h_p]
             <= product_p (1-s_p)^(-3/2), 0<=s_p<1.   (4.3)

This covers arbitrary temporal plaquettes on arbitrary slabs; there
is no penalty proportional to the number of slabs. The one-slab
argument alone would not justify successive replacements inside
a trace with an arbitrary nonpositive remainder. Schatten Holder
is what permits the simultaneous comparison here.

5. ALL PLAQUETTE ORIENTATIONS IN FOUR DIMENSIONS

Use all FOUR temporal orientation stars. Let B_mu contain every
marked plaquette having direction mu as one of its two directions,
for mu=0,1,2,3. Each plaquette occurs in exactly two stars. Write

 Y_mu=sum_(p in B_mu) s_p beta h_p.

The homogeneous original Wilson law has a transfer representation
in each of these directions on the same rectangular periodic lattice.
Equal side lengths or rotational invariance of the finite box are
unnecessary; the transfer time periods can differ.

Since sum_B s_p beta h_p=(1/2)sum_mu Y_mu, ordinary four-factor
Holder gives

 E exp[sum_B s_p beta h_p]
 <= product_(mu=0)^3 {E exp[2Y_mu]}^(1/4).

Each factor is bounded by(4.3) with sources2s_p. Each plaquette
occurs in two factors, so its total exponent is
2*(-3/2)/4=-3/4. This proves(0.1); s_p=1/4 proves(0.2).
No phase-dependent partition pressure or chessboard dissemination
has been used. These overlapping orientation stars are not independent
laws. The symmetric cover, suggested independently by tail47, improves
the earlier three-disjoint-star bound (1-3s_p)^(-1/2).

6. CONSEQUENCES FOR THE ORIGINAL LAW

6.1 Specified simultaneous microscopic bad plaquettes.

For thresholds u_p>0, exponential Markov and(0.1) imply

 P(beta h_p>=u_p for every p in B)
 <= product_p inf_(0<=s<1/2)
                         exp(-s u_p)(1-2s)^(-3/4).     (6.1)

For u_p>3/2 the minimizing s is (1/2)(1-3/(2u_p)),
and the factor is

                 exp(-u_p/2+3/4)(2u_p/3)^(3/4).       (6.2)

For u_p<=3/2 use the bound1. If all plaquettes belong to a single
time-direction star, (4.3) gives the stronger corresponding
Gamma(3/2,1) Chernoff bound.

6.2 Previously admitted local energy events, now at finite volume.

Suppose k selected bad-core events have finite plaquette footprints
D_i of size<=C R^4, with overlap multiplicity<=m_0, and the deterministic
geometric implication

          event_i => sum_(p in D_i)h_p>=c R^(-52).

Then on their joint event the UNION B has
sum_B h_p>=(c/m_0)k R^(-52) and |B|<=CkR^4.
Consequently (0.2) proves

 P(all k events)
       <=exp[k(C'R^4-c' beta R^(-52))].                (6.3)

The geometric implication is exactly an additional hypothesis;
the exponent52 is inherited from the previously proved local-gauge
criterion and is not rederived here. The gain over TURN39 is that
the probability inequality now holds before the thermodynamic limit,
uniformly in every allowed periodic volume and beta>0.

6.3 Fixed microscopic insertions.

A gauge-invariant fixed-stencil bilinear with |Y|<=C beta sum_D h_p
has uniform exponential moments and factorial absolute moments by
(0.1). The same holds for finitely many such marks of bounded footprint
overlap. Physical inverse powers of a remain in Y if its definition
contains them; this theorem does not remove those factors.

6.4 Infinite-volume limits.

At fixed beta the local exponential is bounded and continuous on
the compact link configuration space. The estimates pass to every
local subsequential limit of the periodic Wilson measures under
consideration. Their constants already hold before that limit.
They also hold along simultaneous sequences of beta and volume;
no relation such as log(beta)/L being bounded is needed for them.
Identifying physical continuum observables still requires separate
estimates and limiting constructions.

7. WHAT THIS DOES NOT PROVE

An upper microscopic energy bound gives neither a positive variance
at fixed physical scale nor a bound of order a on the adjacent-time
increment of a proposed physical observable.

The transfer-matrix witness criterion in the companion note reduces
one nontriviality task to those two original-law estimates. The present
bound supplies kinetic-plaquette control that can be used in its
increment estimates, but not the necessary nonlinear smoothing
sensitivity or physical variance lower bound.

Likewise the derived-minimum tests in native chi_k depend on more
than raw plaquette energies; their complete source definitions and
the actual current/effective law must be kept. A finite bare Wilson
bound cannot be relabeled as a bound for every native RG history.

The full nontrivial four-dimensional Yang--Mills existence proof
remains incomplete.
