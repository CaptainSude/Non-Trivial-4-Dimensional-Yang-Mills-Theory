BOUNDARY CURVATURE ADMISSION FOR THE FIXED-BLOCK SOURCE COMPARISON
TURN72. gauge47. 20 October 2026.

STATUS AND PROVENANCE

This short consequence attaches the new nonlinear mixed-source
comparison in turn72_dirichlet_wilson_root.txt to a gauge-invariant
boundary-curvature condition on a fixed four-dimensional rectangle.
It does not introduce a new bare coercivity theorem.

The stronger quantitative bare-box tail theorem and an exterior
collar with an original-law probability estimate already appear in
polylog_box_conditional_tail_turn37.txt and
polylog_box_collar_turn37.txt. Their polylogarithmic constants,
collar action cost and first ordinary-average admission must be
credited separately. Nothing below upgrades their native/history
scope or supplies a probability estimate for the present event.

1. THE BOUNDARY TREE AND ITS FINITE FILLINGS

Let B be a fixed nondegenerate rectangular cubical four-dimensional
box, with all boundary-subcomplex edges fixed by the conditional
exterior. Let E_boundary and P_boundary be its boundary edges and
plaquettes. The boundary is a cubical subdivision of S^3.

Choose a root o and a deterministic spanning tree T_boundary of
the boundary edge graph. For each remaining boundary edge e=(u,v),
its fundamental loop is the rooted tree path from o to u, followed
by e and the reversed tree path from o to v.

The boundary TWO-skeleton is simply connected. One direct
justification is that every loop contracts in S^3, and a relative
cellular approximation of its contracting disk uses only cells
of dimension at most two. Equivalently, attaching the boundary
three-cells does not change the fundamental group. Consequently
each of the finitely many fundamental edge loops is a product
of conjugates of boundary plaquette words and their inverses,
after cancellation of reversed adjacent edges.

Choose one such finite filling for each edge, once and for all,
and let A_B be the largest number of plaquette words in a chosen
filling, enlarged to at least one. Repetitions, orientations and
connecting paths are part of that fixed filling. Only finiteness
at this fixed box is asserted; no bound uniform in its side length
is obtained from this argument.

For the bi-invariant operator distance on SU(2),

 ||UV-I|| <= ||U-I||+||V-I||,
 ||WUW^-1-I||=||U-I||,
 ||U^-1-I||=||U-I||.

The word identity therefore proves the exact global estimate

 ||Hol(loop_e)-I||
       <= A_B max_(p in P_boundary)||U_p-I||.             (1.1)

This requires no smallness of the connecting transports.

2. A GAUGE-INVARIANT EXTERIOR EVENT

Define

 E_boundary(delta)
   ={max_(p in P_boundary)||U_p-I||<=delta}.              (2.1)

This is an exterior-measurable gauge-invariant event. In SU(2),
||U_p-I||^2=2h(U_p), so it is equivalently a condition on the
nonnegative boundary plaquette actions.

Gauge the boundary tree to identity, based at o. Each boundary
chord becomes the holonomy of its fundamental loop, up to the
irrelevant orientation convention. Thus (1.1) gives

 max_(e in E_boundary)||U_e^tree-I|| <= A_B delta.         (2.2)

For delta<=delta_*/A_B, with delta_* the weak-boundary radius in
the fixed-block source theorem, the entire boundary tuple belongs
to that theorem's admitted weak neighborhood.

There is no residual flat-holonomy exception. The boundary S^3
has trivial fundamental group, unlike a toroidal boundary. Nor
is an obstruction from pi_3(SU(2)) relevant: the transformation
here is an arbitrary map on finitely many vertices, not a required
continuous extension of a prescribed smooth boundary map.
It may be extended to interior vertices by any fixed values.

For comparison, the same argument works on the S^2 boundary of
a three-dimensional box. It need not work from boundary
plaquettes alone for a two-dimensional box, whose boundary is
a circle with no two-cells. The present statement is in d=4.

3. EXACT CONDITIONAL LAW AND THE WINDOW CONVENTION

Conditional on the complete exterior, the chosen boundary gauge
is deterministic. Extend it to the interior vertices and make
the corresponding product-Haar change of the free links.
Every plaquette term involving a free edge is retained in the
same conditional action. Gauge invariance therefore transports
the exact original Wilson conditional measure to the small
boundary data in (2.2), with no normalizing factor.

Then apply the interior anchored forest gauge of the Dirichlet
theorem, which is identity on all boundary vertices. It leaves
the small boundary tuple unchanged. The union of this forest
and T_boundary is a spanning tree of the whole box.

This combined construction also describes the original-coordinate
selection unambiguously. Its trees and all auxiliary conventions
must be frozen before source differentiation. A gauge change of
the original links changes the complete rooted representative
by the single conjugation at o. Accordingly:

 - gauge-invariant sources have exactly their original values;
 - a centered metric window is automatically invariant;
 - the projected product window is invariant if its chart,
   whitening and ambient embedding transform covariantly, with
   the resulting frame changes preserving its color balls.

The last property is a requirement on the actual window
convention. Arbitrary coordinate axes or an arbitrary embedding
cannot be declared covariant. With a fixed measurable convention
one still obtains a definite source-independent selection and
the conditional comparison for that selection, but gauge
invariance should only be claimed after the preceding residual
conjugation check. No equality between different tree-window
laws is asserted.

The unique weak-boundary minimizer is transported by the same
conjugation whenever the chosen coordinates are equivariant.
Its uniqueness follows from the fixed-block theorem, not from
a separate minimizer choice here.

4. THE NEW SOURCE CONSEQUENCE AND ITS LIMIT

Let F,H be the same original smooth gauge-invariant block
observables on both sides, and let w be the fixed projected
window transported by Section3. On E_boundary(delta), the
Dirichlet theorem gives

 |Cov_original(F,H | exterior)
       -Cov_selected(F,H | exterior)|
 <= C_B [g a_F+g^2 b_F][g a_H+g^2 b_H] exp(-c_B T^2),    (4.1)

with its original definitions of the first derivatives at the
actual minimizer and the global Hessian bounds. The constants
are uniform in the exterior subject to (2.1) at this fixed box.
They remain dependent on the box and the frozen conventions.

For a fixed weighted plaquette-action source F=sum_p f_p h(U_p),
the first derivative vanishes at the flat boundary minimizer.
Analytic dependence of the admitted minimizer and smoothness
of the source give a_F<=C_(B,f)delta on (2.1), with A_B absorbed
in the fixed constant. Thus for two such sources,

 |Cov_original(F,H | exterior)
       -Cov_selected(F,H | exterior)|
 <= C_(B,f,h) [g delta+g^2]^2 exp(-c_B T^2).              (4.2)

For the canonical rescaling F/g^2,H/g^2, this is
C_(B,f,h)(1+delta/g)^2 exp(-c_B T^2). A general weak curvature
threshold is not silently replaced by delta=O(g).

This is a transport of the new two-source conditional estimate,
not a new annealed or native all-history theorem. One must still
bound the complement of (2.1), identify the actual constrained
coarse fibers if those are used, and retain the exterior mean
and covariance contributions in a full-law comparison.
The TURN37 collar notes provide stronger bare first-scale
ingredients of a different declared scope; their existence
does not itself settle these source and history attachments.
