RESULT

Two exact statements identify the scope of the TURN65 Gaussian
feature factorization.

A. Every smooth bounded gauge-invariant finite-lattice observable
has a finite ambient Fourier representation to which the original
Wilson source theorem applies. Section2 proves the resulting
nonlinear adjacent-time inequality with the COMPLETE denominator.
However, Section3 proves that certifying d(F)=O(a) solely by its
absolute Fourier norm forces Var(F)->0 whenever a beta_max->0.
This is a limitation of that certification, not of the actual
observable or its actual increment.

B. There is no finite operator constant C such that

 [F,[F,T]] <= C T                                    (0.1)

for the exact finite-volume SU2 Wilson transfer matrix, already
for the bounded analytic physical plaquette observable

 F(U)=chi_2(W_p(U))/3
     =(4[Tr W_p(U)/2]^2-1)/3.                        (0.2)

Here chi_n is the character of the SU2 irrep of dimension n+1;
thus chi_2 is spin1. The obstruction holds with the ACTUAL spatial
Wilson multiplication in T. It follows from an explicit high-spin
physical subspace, not from changing the spatial potential.

The statement concerns an operator inequality over all physical
vectors. It does NOT say that the actual thermal trace of this
double commutator is unbounded, or disprove a state-dependent
Wilson estimate for a flowed magnetic observable. Bounded F has
bounded actual adjacent increment. The result explains why a
bounded feature-space multiplier or uniform relative commutator
shortcut cannot handle even all elementary nonlinear observables.

2. AN ACTUAL NONLINEAR FOURIER-CONSUMER INEQUALITY

Suppose a bounded physical observable has, on X, a representation

 F(u)=int_R^D exp[i b.u] nu(db),   D=4|E|,            (2.1)

with finite complex measure nu. A real F is the case needed in(1.2).
Define

 H_beta(nu)=int sqrt[2(1-exp[-sigma_b^2/2])] |nu|(db),
 R_beta(nu)=int min(1,sigma_b)|nu|(db).                (2.2)

Both are finite. Then

 d_N(F)<= (1/2) H_beta(nu)^2 <= R_beta(nu)^2.          (2.3)

For complex F the same proof bounds(1/2)E|F(V)-F(U)|^2.

Proof. Gauge invariance gives F(V)=F(V^h) for the actual temporal
alignment. Minkowski's inequality in the original normalized L2 law
and(1.1) yield

 sqrt[2d_N(F)]
  <=int ||exp[i b.u]-exp[i b.v^h]||_L2 |nu|(db)
  <=int sqrt[2(1-exp[-sigma_b^2/2])] |nu|(db).

The last integrand is at most sqrt2 min(1,sigma_b), proving the
second bound. All expectations retain the original Wilson
denominator. No independence, random-Jacobian substitution, or
intermediate-field equilibrium law is used.

This applies to EVERY smooth finite-lattice observable, including
the finite spatial-flow magnetic observables, as a finite bound.
Indeed choose a smooth radial cutoff equal to1 near radius1 and
supported inside(1/2,3/2). On the product of these annuli define

 F_ext(x)=product_e cut(|x_e|)
                       F((x_e/|x_e|)_e),

and set it to0 outside. This is a smooth compactly supported ambient
extension. Its ordinary Fourier transform is rapidly decreasing
and defines a finite measure nu in(2.1), with every polynomial
moment finite. This extension statement supplies no useful
cutoff-uniform control of H_beta or R_beta.

3. WHY AN ABSOLUTE FOURIER-NORM CERTIFICATE COLLAPSES THE VARIANCE

Assume the spatial graph has distinct edges, no loops and no
parallel edges, as for the ordinary periodic lattice with periods
at least4. Put beta_max=max_e beta_e and assume beta_max>=1.

For fixed u, independent site gauge averaging gives

 E_g u_e^g=0,
 E_g[(b_e.u_e^g)(b_f.u_f^g)]=0 if e!=f,
 E_g(b_e.u_e^g)^2=|b_e|^2/4.                         (3.1)

A distinct pair has an endpoint appearing in just one edge; its
normalized Haar average kills the cross term. Each single transformed
quaternion is uniform on S^3. Since F is gauge invariant, average
(2.1) over g and subtract the constant F_ext(0)=nu(R^D):

 |F(u)-nu(R^D)|
  <=int min(2, |b|/2)|nu|(db)
  <=2 sqrt(beta_max) R_beta(nu).                     (3.2)

The first bound uses Cauchy--Schwarz and(3.1); the second follows
from |b|<=sqrt(beta_max)sigma_b, separately for sigma_b<=1 and
sigma_b>=1. Consequently under EVERY probability mu on the links,

 Var_mu(F)<=4 beta_max R_beta(nu)^2.                  (3.3)

The sharper norm H_beta has the same limitation. Put
c0=sqrt[2(1-exp[-1/2])]>0. For all sigma>=0,

 sqrt[2(1-exp[-sigma^2/2])]>=c0 min(1,sigma).

Thus

 Var_mu(F)<= (4 beta_max/c0^2) H_beta(nu)^2.           (3.4)

In particular, if one certifies d_N(F)<=C a by demanding the
right side of either inequality(2.3) be O(a), then(3.3)--(3.4)
force Var_mu(F)=O(a beta_max). On logarithmic weak-coupling
sequences this tends to0.

This conclusion holds for any chosen finite Fourier representation,
and therefore also prevents evasion by taking an infimum over
representations with the same absolute-norm certificate. It does
not assert that a small actual d_N(F) forces small variance:
the losses from taking absolute values in(2.3) can be substantial.
State-specific cancellation or a different nonlinear representation
would have to do additional work.

4. AN EXACT PHYSICAL PLAQUETTE SUBSPACE

For the operator obstruction take homogeneous temporal beta>0.
Let p be one elementary spatial plaquette with its four distinct
links, and W=W_p its oriented holonomy. More generally the following
argument uses any simple loop of length ell>=1 with distinct edges;
the actual plaquette has ell=4.

Let chi_n be the SU2 character of dimension n+1, and set

 psi_n(U)=chi_n(W(U)),  n=0,1,2,...

These are physical gauge-invariant functions. Because one can
integrate one loop link while fixing the others, W has normalized
Haar law under the product slice Haar measure. Character
orthogonality therefore gives

 <psi_n,psi_m>=delta_nm.                              (4.1)

The central one-link electric convolution has spin-n/2 eigenvalue

 r_n=I_(n+1)(beta)/I_1(beta)>0.

Each of the ell loop links carries this same representation,
including oppositely oriented links by the conjugate representation.
All other links carry the trivial representation. Hence

 Q psi_n=a_n psi_n,   a_n=r_n^ell.                    (4.2)

This closed physical subspace is invariant under multiplication by

 F=chi_2(W)/3,   -1/3<=F<=1.

The elementary SU2 character product formula gives, for n>=2,

 F psi_n=(psi_(n-2)+psi_n+psi_(n+2))/3.                (4.3)

It follows either by multiplying the finite weight sums, or by
the spin1 tensor-product rule. Low-index boundary variants will
not enter the high-index test vectors below.

5. THE RELATIVE DOUBLE COMMUTATOR IS UNBOUNDED ABOVE

Write Gamma_Q(F)=[F,[F,Q]]. On finite sums of the psi_n define
the relative form

 G=Q^(-1/2) Gamma_Q(F) Q^(-1/2).                      (5.1)

No boundedness of this relative form is assumed. A form bound
Gamma_Q(F)<=C Q would require <u,Gu><=C||u||^2 on every
such finite sum.

The constant part of(4.3) cancels from the double commutator.
Direct multiplication gives, for n away from the boundary,

 d_n:=G_(n,n)
       =[4-2(a_(n-2)+a_(n+2))/a_n]/9,

 t_n:=G_(n,n+4)
       =[a_n+a_(n+4)-2a_(n+2)]
                            /[9 sqrt(a_n a_(n+4))].  (5.2)

All n to n+2 entries cancel exactly. There are no other off-diagonal
entries. Thus each residue class modulo4 is a scalar Jacobi block.

Here are the required asymptotics, proved directly from the positive
Bessel series at fixed beta. With m=n+1,

 I_m(beta)=(beta/2)^m/m! times
             [1+beta^2/(4(m+1))+O_beta(m^-2)].

For any fixed integer shift q the ratio of the bracketed factors
at m+q and m is 1+O_beta(m^-2). Put

 kappa=(2/beta)^(2ell).

Factorial expansion gives

 a_(n-2)/a_n
   =kappa[m^(2ell)-ell m^(2ell-1)+O_beta,ell(m^(2ell-2))],

 sqrt[a_n/a_(n+4)]
   =kappa[m^(2ell)+5ell m^(2ell-1)
                                  +O_beta,ell(m^(2ell-2))],

 sqrt[a_(n-4)/a_n]
   =kappa[m^(2ell)-3ell m^(2ell-1)
                                  +O_beta,ell(m^(2ell-2))].

Also a_(n+2)/a_n=O_beta,ell(m^-2ell), and
a_(n+2)/sqrt[a_n a_(n+4)]=1+O_beta,ell(m^-1).
Substitution into(5.2) proves

 t_n=(kappa/9)m^(2ell)(1+O_beta,ell(m^-1))>0,

 V_n:=d_n+t_n+t_(n-4)
      =(4ell kappa/9)m^(2ell-1)
                            +O_beta,ell(m^(2ell-2)). (5.3)

For ell=4 the positive leading term is
(16/9)(2/beta)^8 m^7.

For a finitely supported real sequence u in one residue class,
the Jacobi identity is

 <u,Gu>=sum_n V_n u_n^2
                 -sum_n t_n(u_(n+4)-u_n)^2.          (5.4)

Take a packet supported on n=K+4j, 1<=j<=J, where
J=floor(K^(3/4)), with normalized sine coefficients
u_(K+4j)=sqrt[2/(J+1)]sin[pi j/(J+1)], zero elsewhere.
Its squared nearest-neighbor difference sum is at most
pi^2/(J+1)^2. Uniformly over this packet, n is comparable to K.
Equations(5.3)--(5.4) give, for fixed beta and ell,

 <u,Gu>
   >=c_(beta,ell) K^(2ell-1)
                  -C_(beta,ell) K^(2ell)/J^2
   >=c_(beta,ell) K^(2ell-1)
                  -C_(beta,ell) K^(2ell-3/2)
     ->+infinity.                                   (5.5)

Every packet is a legitimate finite vector in the form domain:
the corresponding test f=Q^(-1/2)u is a finite character sum,
although its ordinary L2 norm may be large. Its Rayleigh quotient
is

 <f,Gamma_Q(F)f>/<f,Qf>=<u,Gu>.

Thus no finite relative operator upper bound exists for Q.

6. THE ACTUAL SPATIAL WILSON POTENTIAL DOES NOT REPAIR THAT OPERATOR BOUND

The preceding construction is already in the physical Hilbert space
of the original spatial lattice. On any fixed finite lattice, the
actual Wilson M=exp[-V_sp/2] is bounded, strictly positive and has
bounded inverse. F, M and the gauge projection are multiplication/
physical operators with the needed commutations. Exactly,

 [F,[F,T]]=M Gamma_Q(F) M.                            (6.1)

If(0.1) held, set h=M^(-1)f in its quadratic form. One would obtain

 <f,Gamma_Q(F)f><=C<f,Qf>

for every physical f, contradicting(5.5). This proves the failure
of(0.1) for the ACTUAL spatial Wilson potential, not just an
arbitrary replacement potential.

It also rules out any proposed feature factorization

 Gamma_Q(F)=F_feature* B_F F_feature,
 F_feature*F_feature=Q,
 B_F<=C I

with a finite uniform upper constant, since that would imply the
already disproved relative operator bound.

There is no contradiction with(1.2) or boundedness of actual d_N(F).
The high-spin test vectors are oscillatory physical states, not the
particular positive thermal remainder T^(N-1). To exploit that
remainder one must use additional state-specific information.
The trace may remain small even though the relative form has
arbitrarily large positive values on other states.

7. CONSEQUENCE FOR THE NEXT PROOF STEP

The Fourier-feature theorem does have a rigorous nonlinear consumer,
but its direct absolute Fourier-norm certification loses the desired
variance on the logarithmic ultraviolet sequence. A universal
relative commutator domination is not an available substitute:
it fails already for a simple bounded analytic plaquette character
with the actual finite Wilson transfer operator.

This does not obstruct a carefully proved temporal-continuum limit
at fixed spatial graph, nor a state-sensitive Wilson estimate for
the spatially flowed magnetic observable. Such arguments must retain
their limiting order or their actual state information; they cannot
be replaced by the false operator bound(0.1).

No nonlinear physical increment estimate of the desired cutoff-
uniform size, physical variance lower bound, or nontrivial YM4
existence proof has been established by these results.
