RESULT

A complete Wilson plaquette with retained multiplicative fluctuations can
respond at the fine POTENTIAL scale h=L^(-j), even when its background is
exactly flat. Taking the trace gains a fluctuation power, but does not by
itself gain another spatial h. An explicit SU(2) square realizes both
orders. Integration can cancel the response, including all mixed source
contacts, if the COMPLETE conditional measure and remaining reads admit
the corresponding real gauge transport. Centering or global color symmetry
is weaker. This identifies a useful integrated target instead of treating
a coordinate-dependent pathwise row as a necessary physical bound.

The native constrained response of the retained fluctuation itself is
included algebraically below but not bounded by this note. The exact
accepted native C/D/minimum chain is history47's complementary task.

1. COMPLETE PRODUCT IDENTITY WITH PHYSICAL EDGE ORIENTATIONS

Write the four positively oriented edge variables of a square as
U_e(t)=F_e(t)B_e(t), with F_e,B_e in SU(2), and take the oriented product

 P=U_1 U_2 U_3^(-1) U_4^(-1),
 Q=B_1 B_2 B_3^(-1) B_4^(-1).

All norms below are operator norms. Set

 b_e=dot B_e B_e^(-1), f_e=dot F_e F_e^(-1),
 d_e=||b_e||, rho_e=||F_e-I||,
 D=sum_e d_e, R=sum_e rho_e, A=sum_e ||f_e||,
 q=||Q-I||, K=||dot Q||.

For the four ORIENTED factors W_l of P, with prefix P_<l,

 Y_P=dot P P^(-1)=sum_l Ad_(P_<l)(dot W_l W_l^(-1)).       (1.1)

There is an identical formula Y_Q for Q. A positive edge has logarithmic
velocity f_e+Ad_(F_e)b_e. A negative traversal has velocity
-Ad_(U_e^(-1))f_e-Ad_(B_e^(-1))b_e. In particular, keeping F_e fixed
does NOT keep the left fluctuation in an arbitrary reversed-edge
factorization fixed. Formula (1.1) respects the original physical edges.

Telescoping unitary products gives ||P-Q||<=R. Also
||Ad_G Z-Ad_H Z||<=2||G-H||||Z|| for unitary G,H. Comparing each term
of (1.1) with its background term therefore gives

 ||Y_P-Y_Q||<=2 R D+A,
 ||dot P||<=K+2 R D+A.                                  (1.2)

The sharper fixed-F bound is 2 sum_l d_l sum_(k<=l)rho_k; for negative
traversals the last rho_l term can be omitted. The coarser (1.2) is
convenient and includes every edge with its actual orientation.

For w(P)=1-Tr(P)/2, the matrices Y_P,Y_Q are traceless anti-Hermitian.
Using ||P-I||=sqrt(2w(P)) gives

 |dot w(P)|<=||P-I||(K+2 R D+A),                         (1.3)
 |dot w(P)-dot w(Q)|<=R K+(q+R)(2 R D+A).                (1.4)

Indeed subtract the two traces as
Tr[Y_Q(P-Q)+(Y_P-Y_Q)(P-I)]/2. Equations (1.2)--(1.4) are real exact
inequalities and do not use an action minimum or a probabilistic law.

For a flat background path Q=I, hence K=q=0, and FIXED retained F,

             ||dot P||<=2RD,  |dot w(P)|<=2R^2 D.        (1.5)

The trace has one extra fluctuation factor R. If D=O(h), R=O(g),
these are O(gh) and O(g^2 h), not O(gh^2) and O(g^2 h^2).

2. EXACT SU(2) SQUARE: BOTH ORDERS ARE ATTAINED

Use Pauli matrices with U=exp(i x.sigma), and let h>0 denote a spatial
scale, not the action. Put G(t)=exp(i h t sigma3), with

 B_1=B_3=G(t), B_2=B_4=I,
 F_1=F_3=I, F_2=exp(i rho sigma1), F_4=exp(i rho sigma2).

Then Q(t)=I for every t. This background extends as a constant commuting
horizontal connection on a rectangular patch. Nevertheless

 P(t)=G(t) exp(i rho sigma1) G(t)^(-1) exp(-i rho sigma2),
 w(P(t))=sin^2(rho)[1+sin(2ht)],
 ||dot P(0)||=2h|sin rho|,
 dot w(P(0))=2h sin^2(rho).                             (2.1)

Conjugation sends sigma1 to cos(2ht)sigma1-sin(2ht)sigma2, fixing the
sign in (2.1). The background curvature and all its derivatives vanish.
Thus background curvature alone cannot bound the fixed-noise response.

For the canonical microscopic source E=beta0 w and rho=g0=beta0^(-1/2),
dot E(0)=2h beta0 sin^2(g0)->2h. The extra fluctuation power cancels the
canonical beta0; it does not produce a curvature-counting h^4 gain.
This is a deterministic example, not a claim that this path satisfies
the full native coarse constraints with its retained noise fixed. The
response of F_e through C, Dtilde, gauges and minima can change (2.1).

For a local gauge change removing the flat B, F_2 must also rotate at
its tail vertex. Holding F_2 fixed instead changes the physical full
field. Gauge invariance of the trace therefore does not null this row.

3. WHAT GAUSSIAN CENTERING DOES AND DOES NOT CANCEL

First, parity alone is insufficient. In (2.1) replace rho on the two
vertical edges by gX and gY, where (X,Y) is a centered jointly Gaussian
scalar pair of variances v_X,v_Y and covariance c. The SAME exact square
formula gives

 E dot w(P(0))
 =2h exp[-g^2(v_X+v_Y)/2] sinh(g^2 c).                  (3.1)

This is O(g^2 h) and nonzero for c!=0. Its quadratic leading term is
even under (X,Y)->(-X,-Y). This fixed-axis Gaussian is only a test of
parity; it is not the flat native color-covariant Gaussian.

Next assume the pair (V_2,V_4) in SU(2)^2 is invariant under SIMULTANEOUS
conjugation. Write V_i=c_i I+i v_i.sigma, and set
m=E(v_2.v_4)/3. Isotropy gives E[v_2 v_4^t]=m I. Consequently

 E w(G(t)V_2G(t)^(-1)V_4^(-1))-E w(V_2V_4^(-1))
                         =4m sin^2(ht).                 (3.2)

The first derivative at t=0 vanishes, but the whole response need not.
For a nondegenerate jointly isotropic Gaussian vector pair X,Y with
Cov(X^a,Y^b)=c delta_ab, V_i=exp(i g X_i.sigma), Taylor and finite
Gaussian moments give m=g^2 c+O(g^4). Thus the second background
derivative is 8h^2[g^2 c+O(g^4)]. A correlated Gaussian can satisfy
global adjoint symmetry and still fail the stronger cancellation below.

These examples preserve the distinction between an uncut Gaussian
calculation and the actual truncated correlated native Gaussian. An even
native window preserves joint sign symmetry; it does not make correlated
coordinates independent or impose independent site-color rotations.
