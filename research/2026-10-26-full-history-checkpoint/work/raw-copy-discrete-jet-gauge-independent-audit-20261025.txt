2. FIRST GAUGE: ROOT LINKS ARE EXACTLY THE IDENTITY

Put a_mu=A_mu(0) and

 g1(y)=exp(i sum_mu y_mu a_mu).

Then g1(0)=I, g1(eta e_mu)=exp(i eta a_mu)=U_mu(0), so

 U_mu^g1(0)=I, A_mu^1(0)=0                            (2)

EXACTLY on the chosen logarithm branch. This is not an O(eta) assertion.
Differentiated finite exponential products, with hD<=c, give

 |A1(y)|<=C b h^2(1+|y|),
 |partial_eta A1|<=C b h^2,
 [partial_eta A1]_alpha<=C_alpha b h^(2+alpha).         (3)

For completeness, decompose A=a+v. Then |v(y)|<=C b h^2|y|,
|partial v|<=C b h^2 and [partial v]_alpha<=C b h^(2+alpha).
For v=0 the gauged constant connection has zero root links, first
derivative O(h^2), and variation of that derivative O(h^3) per unit
distance. The coefficients of its convergent exponential series are
uniform for h|y|<=c. For the v-dependent difference, the ordinary lattice
product rule puts at most one derivative on v; Holder differences use
its stated Holder bound. Derivatives of the g1 coefficients contribute
an extra h. The resulting error terms are O(h^3), O(h^4 D), etc., hence
bounded by C h^(2+alpha) since hD<=c and alpha<1. Divided differences of
the exponentials are uniform as eta decreases. This proves (3).

Define b_mu,nu=partial_nu A1_mu(0). This tensor is O(h^2). Lattice Taylor
summation on coordinate paths gives

 A1_mu(y)=sum_nu b_mu,nu y_nu+r1_mu(y),
 ||r1||_(C1,alpha;box)
              <=C_alpha b h^(2+alpha)(1+D)^(1+alpha). (4)

Indeed each first-difference increment is compared with its value at
the root using (3); summing the differences is the discrete fundamental
theorem. The gradient Holder seminorm is unchanged by subtracting its
constant affine jet and is bounded directly by (3). No continuous Taylor
formula with an uncontrolled eta*h^2 remainder is used.

3. SECOND GAUGE: EXACT SHIFTED QUADRATIC

Set S_mu,nu=(b_mu,nu+b_nu,mu)/2. Define the Lie-algebra-valued polynomial

The exact forward difference is

The shift y_mu-eta is essential. Using y_mu^2/2 instead would leave a
constant eta*S_mu,mu/2, which is not small enough for the desired age
remainder. Also phi(0)=phi(eta e_mu)=0, so g2 preserves every root link
in (2) EXACTLY.

Let K_mu,nu=(b_mu,nu-b_nu,mu)/2 and L_mu(y)=sum_nu K_mu,nu y_nu.
The transformed logarithmic field A2 satisfies

 ||r||_(C1,alpha;box)<=C_alpha b h^(2+alpha) P(D),      (6)

where a fixed polynomial, e.g. C(1+D)^4, suffices. To see this, its
linear gauge change is A1-partial phi. This gives L+r1 by (4)--(5).
All remaining gauge/BCH terms contain at least two of phi, partial phi,
A1, or their first differences. Their C1 bound is C h^4 P(D), using
|phi|<=C h^2D^2, |partial phi|<=C h^2D and |partial^2 phi|<=C h^2.
Since h^4<=h^(2+alpha), these terms give (6). The exponential sums have
uniform constants because |phi|<=C c^2.

Separately, DIRECT product estimates give the actual domain bounds

Do not use the coarse polynomial remainder estimate (6) to infer (7)
