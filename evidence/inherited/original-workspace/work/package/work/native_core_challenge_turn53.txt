RESULT

The strongest potential type error is to treat a physical-coarse-field
determinant identity as an identity on the whole independent-(U,J)
domain. That inference would be false. It is unnecessary: native
off-physical bounds must come from the B109/B116 prepared construction,
while the determinant and Maxwell coefficient are compared after
physical restriction. Section1 makes this boundary explicit.

A new positive result is obtained in Sections2--5. Exact preservation
of the physical Maxwell coefficient follows directly from the flat
stationarity equation and transverse ellipticity, at each fixed
ordinary endpoint. It needs only the native physical polarization
kernel and the actual classical quadratic reference. It does not
need an all-arity pullback of every native pair-domain function into
the archived nonlinear strict-support quotient, or a large-endpoint
limit in the marginal-transport proof.

I found no contradictory gauge/Haar dimension factor. Section6 checks
the nested counts explicitly, including the scalar factors that the
normalized determinant comparison does not determine.

This does not verify the full native continuation class, prove the
Large-I partition identity, or construct the Yang--Mills continuum law.

1. A real domain distinction, with a counterexample to a stronger claim

There are two different objects:

 (a) a native extension E_j(X,U,J), on the B109 pair domain with
     independent J and all domain clauses, including derived minima;
 (b) its physical restriction
          e_j(w)=E_j(X,U_j(w),J_j(w)).

The native domain parameters can be uniform in j even though the
spaces are typed by j and the derived-minimum condition depends on
that type. This is not literally one unchanging raw coordinate ball.
The fixed core stores births in their own typed domains.

Equality of physical restrictions does NOT identify two holomorphic
pair-domain extensions. Already F(z,j)=j-f(z) vanishes on j=f(z) and
is nonzero elsewhere. A gauge-invariant version is a local quadratic
trace in J-J_phys(U), wherever that expression is defined.

Consequently the finite coarea identity for w cannot be extended by
the identity theorem to arbitrary independent J. Its valid analytic
continuation is in the common physical w variables. Nor can that
identity supply uniform bounds on an independent-current Gaussian.

This is the correct reading of the TURN52 construction: its native
pair-domain estimates are imported from the native prepared formulas;
the physical determinant is used for the marginal coefficient. The
following primary locations support precisely the former type:

 B109 pp262--263, (1.10)--(1.18): pair domains, their derived-minimum
 clause and constants independent of birth depth;
 B109 p268, following (2.13): the reduced exponential perturbation
 vanishes at g=0;
 B116 pp6--7, (1.19)--(1.23): the response circle, and pp14--15:
 analyticity of the localized birth on the native pair domain.

The off-physical removal of the constrained linear term must precede
the extension. A new g^(-1)<Hx,J> term on arbitrary independent J would
destroy the required weak-coupling estimate. The current producer
explicitly excludes that mistake.

Thus I do not find an invalid fixed-domain inference in the presently
qualified theorem. Its domain claim would become unsupported if
rephrased as a fixed raw link/current ball, or justified solely by
the physical stationary-amplitude calculation.

2. A weaker interface suffices for exact Maxwell transport

For the scalar Maxwell coefficient, take a compact simple gauge
group and a fixed invariant color quadratic form. A semisimple
product has a coefficient for each simple factor; the same argument
applies componentwise with the corresponding normalized references.

Work on the infinite homogeneous CURRENT unit lattice. The current
lattice link-angle variables and their counting inner products are
used throughout. Fix a finite allowed endpoint R and the literal
ordinary map F_R, composed from the chosen primitive mean.

Let S_M be the actual classical reference at prior depth M. At flatness
write q_M for its physical Hessian. Let a_R(w) be any actual local
minimizer representative for F_R(a)=w, with a_R(0)=0. Define

       H_R=Da_R(0),       Q_R=DF_R(0),
       q_out=D^2 S_(MR)(0).

The reference minimum comparison is
S_(MR)(w)=S_M(a_R(w)). The following assumptions are the precise
quadratic inputs used here:

 (Q1) q_M is unit-translation invariant, self-adjoint at real momentum,
      with exact gauge nullspace away from zero momentum. For the
      lattice gradient symbol d(k), put
      delta(k)=d(k)^*d(k)=sum_mu 4 sin^2(k_mu/2),
      P_T(k)=I-d(k)d(k)^*/delta(k).
      On transverse directions q_M(k)>=c delta(k) P_T(k).
      Its midpoint symbol has Maxwell normalization
      q_M(k)=|k|^2 I-kk^t+O(|k|^4), tensored with the fixed
      color quadratic form.

 (Q2) q_out has the same gauge identity and Maxwell normalization:
      q_out(p)=|p|^2 I-pp^t+O(|p|^4) in midpoint coordinates.
      In particular ||q_out(p)||<=C|p|^2.

 (Q3) Q_R is the actual finite-range ordinary constraint derivative;
      its cell Bloch matrix is bounded for fixed R. The actual
      linear lift and physical interaction Hessians have the
      analyticity/moments needed to define their output Fourier jets.

These are much weaker than an all-order nonlinear interaction norm
theorem. Uniformity in M follows if the displayed constants are

The retained flat-reference module supplies (Q1) through the actual

3. Exact stationarity and the gauge component

Choose the Lagrange sign convention

        grad S_M(a_R(w))=DF_R(a_R(w))^* lambda(w).

At flatness the action gradient and multiplier vanish. Differentiating
and using the envelope identity grad S_(MR)=lambda gives

       q_M H_R=Q_R^* q_out,          Q_R H_R=I.        (3.1)

All pairings in these identities are the same counting pairings.
The second derivative of the nonlinear constraint is multiplied by
lambda(0)=0 here; it is not being omitted from a nonflat Hessian.
It reappears in the nonflat determinant calculation.

A choice of gauge changes H_R by a fine gradient and does not change
its transverse part or the scalar quadratic substitution below.
The exact linear covariance of the ordinary map is

                 Q_R d_f=d_c E_R,                    (3.2)

where E_R restricts a fine site gauge to the output centers. Therefore

          d_f^* Q_R^* q_out=E_R^* d_c^* q_out=0.       (3.3)

The right side of the stationarity equation is transverse. There is
no unsupported assumption that Q_R^* itself is transverse.

Use the unitary cell Fourier transform, then the unitary alias basis
inside a cell. For coarse momentum p the fine momenta are

          k_a=(p+2 pi a)/R modulo 2 pi,
                  a in {0,...,R-1}^4.

In that basis q_M is diagonal in a, with direction/color blocks
q_M(k_a). Delta is diagonal with blocks delta(k_a)I. Hence Delta
COMMUTES with q_M and with P_T, because it is scalar on EACH alias
block. It need not be scalar on the whole folded fiber.

For real p!=0 sufficiently near zero, no alias is a zero mode.
Inverting q_M on the transverse subspace gives from (3.1)

    H_T=P_T H_R=q_M^dagger Q_R^* q_out,
    ||Delta H_T||<=c^(-1)||Q_R^*|| ||q_out||
                           <=C_R |p|^2.              (3.4)

This inversion never acts on gauge modes.

The commutation observation is essential. Generic form ellipticity
q>=c Delta would NOT imply ||Delta q^(-1)||<=C. For example take
Delta=diag(epsilon,1) and
q=Delta^(1/2)[[2,1],[1,2]]Delta^(1/2).
Then q>=Delta, but Delta q^(-1) has an entry
-1/(3 sqrt(epsilon)). That invalid inference is avoided in (3.4)
by the actual translation/alias structure.

4. Direct proof that extracted kernels remain extracted

Let e be a native physical invariant scalar interaction, with zero
total first derivative at flatness and analytic translation-invariant
Hessian Gamma(k). The first derivative vanishes by global adjoint
invariance; semisimplicity already suffices for that assertion.
The compact simple-group scope fixed above gives a single scalar
Maxwell coefficient beta. Set

                  K(k)=Gamma(k)-beta q_M(k).

The infinite Ward/Euclidean classification and the normalization of
q_M imply

             K(k)=O(|k|^4),   K(k)d(k)=d(k)^*K(k)=0.  (4.1)

With the recorded exponential moments, K is bounded on the momentum
torus. Thus, for real nonzero k,

          K(k)=delta(k)^2 A(k),    sup_k||A(k)||<infinity. (4.2)

No analyticity of A at zero is required. Away from zero delta has a
positive lower bound; near zero (4.1) proves the bound. This works for
signed or complex interaction coefficients by operator norm bounds.

In the alias basis apply (4.2) to every block. Gauge invariance removes
the longitudinal part of H_R, and (3.4) gives

 ||H_R^* K H_R||
    =||(Delta H_T)^* A (Delta H_T)||
    <=C ||Delta H_T||^2
    <=C_R |p|^4.                                     (4.3)

The displayed adjoints are for real coefficient kernels. For a complex
interaction, the Fourier Hessian is bilinear and uses the corresponding
transpose at -p; the same estimate uses
||Delta H_T(-p)|| ||Delta H_T(p)||. The conclusion is unchanged.

The ordinary chain rule has no first-derivative correction:

        D^2(e o a_R)(0)=H_R^* Gamma H_R.               (4.4)

Furthermore (3.1) and Q_R H_R=I imply the exact reference identity

                     H_R^* q_M H_R=q_out.            (4.5)

Combining (4.3)--(4.5) proves

      Gamma_out(p)=beta q_out(p)+O(|p|^4),
             beta_out(e o a_R)=beta_in(e).            (4.6)

The output Hessian is analytic by the actual local lift and kernel
moment hypotheses. Its order estimate at real p!=0 determines the
Taylor coefficients at zero. There is no choice of a pseudoinverse
value at the zero mode, no removal of a finite-volume constant mode,
and no limit through changing zero-mode normalizations.

This is exact for every fixed endpoint R. It uses neither a nonlinear
fixed-radius large-R limit nor an interchange of unrelated structural
limits. Any actual infinite homogeneous system supplying (Q1)--(Q3)
and the native physical kernels obeys it.

6. Nested gauge and coarea scalar counts

Let stages 0,1,2 have numbers of links e_0,e_1,e_2 and sites v_0,v_1,v_2
on one compatible finite regulator. Based gauge counts are

 s_01=v_0-v_1,  s_12=v_1-v_2,
                   s_02=v_0-v_2=s_01+s_12.

The physical fiber dimensions are

 d_ab=q[(e_a-e_b)-(v_a-v_b)],  q=dim g,
                         d_02=d_01+d_12.

Thus h0^(e_0-e_1-s_01) h0^(e_1-e_2-s_12)
equals h0^(e_0-e_2-s_02), and the Gaussian powers of g and 2 pi
multiply with the correct physical dimensions. The based gauge groups
use disjoint site-coordinate factors, so normalized Haar disintegrates
without a field-dependent factor.

The coarea identity itself is stronger than dimension counting:
the simultaneous constraint derivative (ordinary mean plus contour)
and the physical Lagrange Hessian must be those of the same measure.
In (b,h) coordinates the phase is f(b)+sum q(h^-1), and the ordinary
fiber measure is physical slice measure times normalized Haar dh.
This gives the h0^(s) factor in the retained-versus-physical stationary
amplitude identity. The constraint-curvature Hessian is retained.

The raw orbit normalizers for sequential gauge cutoffs need not equal
one macro normalizer at positive g. Their products are still scalar,
but their values and the flat determinant values belong in the vacuum
ledger. A normalized D identity cannot determine a two-cover scalar
defect or erase these constants. This is why the exact leading
cocycle is compatible with the still-open native vacuum problem.
