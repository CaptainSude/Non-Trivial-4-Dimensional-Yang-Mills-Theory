1. DATA AND REQUIRED COMMON CONTOURS

Let i<=r be the source birth and actual preparation grade. Put

Work in born-i lattice units on a fixed enlargement of an r-cell. Let
Sigma be a union of interfaces lying in faces of a bounded number of
r-grid cubes per r-cell. The actual fixed-width read and source halos
remain attached. Locally graded covers can use this lemma cell by cell
only after their geometry and overlap have separately been proved.

The source is a COMPLETE own-field pair-gauge-invariant family Z_i with
zero vacuum and zero full universal pointed curvature tensor. All Taylor
kernels through order four, their Ward identities, fifth-order remainder,
source-root labels and a fixed exponential spatial moment reserve are
kept. Let M_i be their strong born norm and r_i their actual safe finite
Cauchy radius. We use the same joint-current grading convention as in
the existing neutral preparation. We do not reinterpret a raw fine-bond
divergence as that normalized current without a scaling check.

On EVERY occupied native complex parameter contour, in one coherent
local frame for each protected read, require
 |A| <= C epsilon, |A(x+xi)-A(x)| <= C epsilon^2 |xi|,       (1)
and a branchwise first-order Taylor estimate
 |A(x+xi)-A(x)-D A(x)xi|
       <= C epsilon^(2+alpha) |xi|^(1+alpha), alpha>0,      (2)
when the segment and its required stencil stay in a single component
of the complement of Sigma. The derivative at a root on Sigma may be
chosen arbitrarily with norm C epsilon^2: its error will be charged to
the interface estimate. Bounds (1)--(2) include the actual amplitudes;
using a fixed absolute response ceiling is enough. They are not inferred

The independent OUTER current remains an input of the prescribed maps.

2. A ROOTED BOUND WITHOUT HOLDER CONTINUITY ACROSS SIGMA

For a source root x set d=dist_i(x,Sigma). The kernel radius ell is the
maximum displacement of its field/current arguments and their stencils.
If ell<d-C_stencil, (2) applies. Otherwise use the Lipschitz bound (1).
After reducing the spare exponential moment once, the whole-kernel
Taylor remainder therefore has the bound
 epsilon^(2+alpha) ell^(1+alpha)
             + C epsilon^2 ell 1_{ell>=d-C_stencil}.      (4)
The second summand, after summing the original kernels, costs
C epsilon^2 exp(-c' d), including its finite spatial moments.

The quadratic AA Ward identity first replaces each field by an anchored
increment. Zero full tensor removes the product of their linear Taylor
parts. One factor then has (4), and the other has (1). Thus its complete
bound is C epsilon^4[epsilon^alpha+exp(-c'd)].

At cubic field degree the complete constant and first spatial moments
tied to that tensor vanish. An expression left after subtracting its
constant/linear Taylor part has either two anchored increments (order
epsilon^5) or one remainder (4) times two fields (order
epsilon^(4+alpha)+epsilon^4 exp(-c'd)). At quartic field degree its
constant term vanishes; a remaining increment gives epsilon^5. The
fifth-order analytic remainder also gives epsilon^5. Reduce alpha to
min(alpha,1) if needed.

These are the complete Ward-kernel identities, not identities for a
kernel truncated to a component of the complement of Sigma. No
truncated moment is declared zero. All crossing-kernel contributions
are kept through the second term of (4). No smooth extension of a
branch across an interface is used.

For terms involving the current, the A leg of AJ has zero sum and
contributes an anchored increment epsilon^2. Equation(3) then gives
epsilon^5 plus epsilon^4 times a current-interface tail. JJ contributes
epsilon^6 plus such a tail. A^2 J contributes epsilon^5 plus such a
tail; all other cubic/quartic current monomials are at least as small.
Convolution with the reserved kernel decay moves the current-interface
tail to exp(-c''d) at x. First joint jets vanish by color invariance.

Consequently the sum of the complete analytic kernel majorants at x is

Any literal response-derivative factor remains additional, unless its

contained in a bounded number of r-cube faces, the number within born

 sum_x exp(-c''dist_i(x,Sigma)) <= C R^3.                (6)
