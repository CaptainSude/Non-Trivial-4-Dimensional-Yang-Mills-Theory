ROOTED NORMALIZED LIVE-HISTORY MASS: PRIMARY CHECK AND TWO EXACT COMPARISON TESTS
18 October 2026. Independent audit; this note does not assert a YM existence theorem.

1. PRECISE RESULT OF THE PRIMARY CHECK

Source: T. Balaban, Large field renormalization II, CMP 122 (1989),
https://scispace.com/pdf/large-field-renormalization-ii-localization-exponentiation-h8b7ih93xo.pdf
DOI 10.1007/BF01238433. Read the actual primary PDF text.

At pp379--380, (1.73)--(1.75), T(X)1 is positive on real fields; normalized T controls a mark by its supremum. The subsequent large-field estimate is for T(X)1. At pp389--390, (1.97)--(1.100), terms avoiding surviving domains become small bulk log activities, while terms meeting them become boundary terms. The alternative representation (1.103)--(1.104), pp391--392, extracts each surviving T(Y)1 and exponentiates the normalized boundary operations. The proof explicitly dispenses with tree factors for these fixed Y because they are not summed in that expansion. The resulting multipliers depend on V_k restricted to Y; the next RG operations leave them outside their integrations.

The lower bound at pp357--359, (1.2)--(1.11), is for the reference refill normalization with its constrained classical minimum extracted. It is not a lower bound for an actual history in which a specified surviving component has been removed. The lifetime argument (1.79)--(1.88) charges enlargements and mergers to the original bad regions. It supplies an absolute majorant, not a conditional denominator comparison.

Therefore this source passage does NOT establish the requested normalized rooted mass estimate. It identifies exactly where an additional argument must attach.

2. WHY NORMALIZING A COMPONENT DOES NOT RETAIN ITS RARITY

For positive stopped history densities write

 rho(V)=sum_H rho_H(V),    N(V)=sum_H rho_H(V) F_H(V).

If a designated history weight has rho_H=z_H b_H with z_H small, its
conditional mark is still F_H. A bound |T_H F_H|<=z_H B_H sup|F_H|
and T_H1<=z_H B_H does not bound T_H F_H/T_H1 by z_H sup|F_H|.

The elementary exact countermodel is

 rho=a+z b,    N=z b m,    N/rho=z b m/(a+z b).

For a=0 this equals m, for every z>0. This disproves the implication
from an absolute component bound to pointwise normalized rarity. It
is not a counterexample to the actual YM history probability: proving
that an appropriate competing a exists with a useful size is precisely
the additional mathematical obligation.

Likewise a regular current refill kernel cannot settle the issue. If
rho_h(V)=w_h K(V) with K normalized and common to all hidden histories,
then P(h|V)=w_h/sum w. Any lower bound or Gaussian tail for K controls
the new field, but it leaves the relative hidden history masses intact.
The source's separation of T(Y)1 from normalized T is consistent with
this distinction.

3. AN EXACT SUFFICIENT LOCAL COMPARISON, INCLUDING NORMALIZATION

Fix an exterior xi and integrate ALL variables in a removable merger
hull D, including those needed to change the determining set. Suppose
there is a map from histories containing a designated rooted bad
component C to admissible histories without that component, preserving
xi, such that their integrated densities satisfy

 W_bad(C|xi) <= exp[-P(C)+A nu(C)] D_ref(xi) F_out(xi),
 W_fill(C|xi) >= exp[-B nu(C)] D_ref(xi) F_out(xi).

Suppose the weighted number/Jacobian of inverse surgery choices is at
most exp[C_0 nu(C)]. The denominator of the actual conditional law
contains the fill contributions. Summing over inverse choices gives

 P(C occurs | xi) <= exp[-P(C)+(A+B+C_0)nu(C)].                 (3.1)

The same argument permits a nonnegative polynomial mark M(C), provided
the root animal sum of M(C) times the right side of (3.1) converges.
All vacuum, reference-normalization, coarea, gauge-fixing and classical
minimum factors must be identical in the two displayed comparisons.
An upper bound on W_bad and normalization of the reference kernel do
not imply the lower bound on W_fill. No global partition function is
used in this lemma, so the conclusion is independent of total volume.

A weaker integrated comparison could suffice: an exterior cost C_K(xi)
on the right of (3.1), with precisely the normalized scalar-source
moments needed for the exterior words. Uniformity over arbitrary complex
source configurations is not meaningful as a probability statement;
one needs either real positive sources or a normalized analytic
coefficient estimate on a stated common source disc.

4. A REAL LIMIT ON WHAT A 'GOOD COLLAR' CAN GUARANTEE

An unrestricted uniform collar theorem at a single threshold is false
already for a finite Dirichlet Maxwell box. Let the boundary data impose
constant mean curvature t_g=epsilon_g-g^2, with epsilon_g=g(log g^-2)^p.
The collar is strictly below epsilon_g. An interior curvature equals

 F=t_g+g Y,

where Y is a centered nondegenerate Gaussian with variance independent
of g. Therefore

 P(F>epsilon_g | this collar)=P(Y>g) -> 1/2.

No bound exp[-c(log g^-2)^nu] holds uniformly over such collars. This
does not exclude a comparison with a strict fractional safety margin,
or with a larger interior threshold. In particular the source's final
healing discussion uses a 2 epsilon_k curvature bound. One must check
the literal bad cutoff and admitted collar before applying this test;
it is not a disproof of the source's doubled-threshold geometry.

Topological or coarse-average constraints can also obstruct a refill
which keeps every coarse datum fixed. A valid surgery may need to
integrate the whole merger hull and change some interior coarse data.
The exact reference denominator still has to be shared after that change.

5. THE BARE WILSON RP ROUTE REALLY DOES GIVE NORMALIZED RARITY

Here is a direct finite-volume calculation which avoids dividing two
extensive bounds before localization. On a reflection-compatible Wilson
lattice with normalized Haar measure let

 Z_beta = integral exp[-beta sum_p S_p(U)] dU,    S_p>=0.

Choose a fixed reflection cell and one plaquette observable S in it.
Let n be the number of reflected cells, and assume the standard
chessboard inequality for this cell convention. Disseminating
f=exp[(beta/2)S] yields

 E_beta f <= (Z_dis/Z_beta)^(1/n)
          <= (Z_(beta/2)/Z_beta)^(1/n).                      (5.1)

The last inequality holds because only a subset of plaquettes was
reweighted; reducing all their positive Wilson coefficients to beta/2
increases the partition integral. Markov's inequality then gives

 P_beta(S>=t) <= exp[-beta t/2]
                  (Z_(beta/2)/Z_beta)^(1/n).                (5.2)

For a fixed cell, n is comparable to the number of lattice sites.
Restrict every link to a group ball of radius c beta^-1/2. Its Haar
volume is at least c_1 beta^(-dim G/2). Every plaquette action is then
at most C/beta. Thus

 log Z_beta >= -C N_sites(1+log beta),   Z_(beta/2)<=1,

and (5.2) implies the volume-uniform bound

 P_beta(S>=t) <= exp[-beta t/2+C(1+log beta)].               (5.3)

For t=c beta^-1(log beta)^(2p), p>1/2, this is the desired stretched
logarithmic kind of rarity. Precise cell constants depend on the
reflection-plane/plaquette convention; no claim that n equals the
number of all oriented plaquettes is needed.

This proves that extensive pressure estimates CAN be useful after a
chessboard root. It does not identify the native auxiliary-history
event with the bare local observable used in (5.1).

6. WHAT IS MISSING IN TRANSFERRING (5.3) TO A NATIVE FIRST CONTACT

There are three different mathematical objects:

 (a) a local bare plaquette event under the bare Wilson measure;
 (b) a current coarse-field event under the actual stopped positive law;
 (c) a surviving first-contact event in the auxiliary R history.

The preceding proof handles (a). For (b), sufficient inputs would be
reflection positivity for the actual coarse law and a uniformly
normalized pressure estimate for a fixed positive coarse Wilson-energy
tilt, with the same UV vacuum factor canceled first. Compactness alone
gives a cost proportional to gamma_b per cell rather than log gamma_b.
The native absolute lifetime bound does not supply that pressure
estimate merely by being a stability theorem.

For (c), mass preservation gives a positive dual function

 p_C(U_bare)=P(auxiliary first contact C | U_bare),  0<=p_C<=1.

But it does not make p_C a function of the links of a closed reflection
cell. Applying chessboard requires a positive cell-local majorant (or
a summably localized positive decomposition) and the matching
disseminated source estimate. Signed localized T coefficients are not
such a decomposition of a conditional probability. A naive proxy
'some original fine plaquette was bad in the ancestor cell' loses the
huge number of bare plaquettes and does not retain the renewal gain.

Reflection covariance and positivity of a resampling kernel alone do
not imply reflection positivity. For example, a reflection-symmetric
kernel may output the two-side spin pair (H,-H), H uniform in {+1,-1}.
The output law is reflection invariant and positive, but for the
positive-side function f=s_+ it has E[f theta f]=-1. A factorization
of the actual refill kernel across each reflection plane, or another
positive-kernel RP argument, must be proved rather than assumed.

Finally, a zero-source probability bound cannot simply be combined with
Holder and ordinary powers of a Wick F^2 insertion: coincident products
need not be uniformly bounded as the UV cutoff is removed. The needed
extension is a normalized source coefficient estimate for separated
scalar words, retaining their counterterms and vacuum cancellation.

7. DOES SURVIVING TO THE STOP SCALE FORCE AN EXTRA AGE PENALTY?

A component at the stop scale has a carrier in that scale's domain
class. This fact alone gives no diameter factor exponential in its
first-contact age. An ancient small ancestor may merge with a newer
neighbor, and a tree connecting two touching pieces need not contain
a long edge. The actual lifetime ledger is stronger than this simple
topological observation, but it is still a bound on absolute weights.
To obtain an additional fine-to-stop suppression one must exhibit an
unspent source factor for each such ancestral chain; it cannot be
inferred from the current component's minimum block size.

Even if a fixed-stop-scale lower density bound were available, the
hidden-history example in section 2 shows that the bound must compare
the SAME merger hull with a competing history. A lower bound on a
common current refill kernel is insufficient. The native final
representation leaves exactly this comparison between its surviving
T(Y)1 multipliers unresolved.

8. PRESENT STATUS AND THE NARROW NEXT LEMMA

The normalized estimate requested for early surviving histories has
not been proved by the source passages checked here. The valid results
are the absolute producer estimate, the normalized sup-mark estimate,
the healed bulk log-activity estimate, and the bare RP estimate (5.3).
They do not combine without another locality/denominator theorem.

The most direct sufficient next statement is (3.1), with the literal
original determining sets and source cutoffs. The RP alternative is
the positive dual-cell interface in section 6, together with the
normalized disseminated scalar-source bound. These are specific
additional proofs, not changes of notation for the existing absolute
T estimate. No claim here rules out either route for actual YM.
