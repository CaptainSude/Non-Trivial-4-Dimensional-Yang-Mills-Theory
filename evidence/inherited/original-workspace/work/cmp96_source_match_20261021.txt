1. EXACT METRIC AND ENDPOINTS

CMP96(2.45) sets B=union_(j=0)^k Lambda_j and identifies determining
vertices with their blocks. Formula(2.46), printed231, is

 d(y,y')=inf_Gamma sum_j (L^j eta)^(-1)
                         |Gamma intersect B^j(Lambda_j)|.

The infimum is over its admissible contours: the portion inside
B^j(Lambda_j) consists of bonds of the corresponding grade-j lattice.
Thus the direct lower bounds in mixed_geometry_20261021 are indeed
bounds for the native metric, not a guessed age metric.

The same paragraph extends the metric to fine points by block projection:

 d(x,x')=d(y^j(x),y^(j')(x'))
   when x in B^j(Lambda_j), x' in B^(j')(Lambda_(j')).

Consequently a fine-point Euclidean comparison needs bounded endpoint
offsets: |x-x'| <= s[d(x,x')+C_d] in a truncated geometry with all
scales <=s. A bare |x-x'|<=s d(x,x') would be false for two distinct
points in the same block, since their extended d is zero. TURN126's
use of 1+d already accommodates this correction.

B99 propagators, printed397, refers to CMP96(2.36). The original scan
confirms the relevant metric is (2.46); cite the latter explicitly.

2. EXACT NATIVE LEMMA AND CONSTANT CONVENTIONS

The ordinary physical distance in this paper is the l1 distance
(printed223). The geometric gap is (L^j eta)^(-1)
dist(Omega_j^c,Omega_(j+1))>RM, equation(2.2), printed224.

On printed233, the paper defines

 c0(u)=sum_(z in Z) exp[-u delta0 |z|].

The factor delta0 is part of c0's definition. Omitting it changes
the parameter condition. For 0<alpha<1, condition(2.59) is

 (alpha delta0 RM)/4 > 2d log c0(alpha/2)+1.           (A)

Lemma2.1, printed234, equations(2.60)--(2.63), includes

 sup_(y in B) sum_(y' in B) exp[-alpha delta0 d(y,y')]
        <=c1(alpha),
 c1(alpha)=12 [c0(alpha/2)]^d.                         (B)

Its grade-cost inequality(2.60) is

 d(y,y') >= RM max{|j-j'|-1,0}, y in Lambda_j,
                                      y' in Lambda_(j').

The triangle inequality is proved explicitly at(2.54), printed233.
Thus the polynomial-moment deduction below uses primary statements,
including their actual refinement-independent counting measure.

The summation in(B) is over determining vertices, one per native block,
not over every fine site inside each block. This is exactly why density
cancellation does not eliminate an additional fine-site multiplicity.

3. CMP102 CONSUMER: THE AVAILABLE DECAY IS delta0/8

CMP102 original printed308, equation(190), shows

 |delta H_mu(B,x)/delta B_nu(y')|, and its specified output jets,
   <= C [a_j^(-1),a_j^(-2),...,a_j^(-3),a_j^(-3)]
          a_(j')^(-d) exp[-delta0 d(y,y')/8],

for x in Delta(y), y in Lambda_j and y' in Lambda_(j').
Here y' is literally the root of the component B_nu(y'). This avoids
silently replacing every incident-bond endpoint by an unqualified
geometric point. Orient each incident bond from an endpoint in its
defining Lambda_j; the finitely many orientations give only a factor
at most 2d (times fixed matrix-coordinate constants). If another bond
orientation is used, its reversal/transport must be retained explicitly.
The sum need not be enlarged to all fine sites.

CMP96 equation(2.69), printed235, explicitly gives the mixed scalar
product sum_j sum_(y in Lambda_j) a_j^d lambda(y)lambda'(y).
This verifies the input measure corresponding to density a_j^(-d).
The component-kernel convention therefore leaves a counting sum over
the actual determining roots after multiplication by this measure.

4. POLYNOMIAL SCHUR CONSEQUENCE, WITH EXPLICIT GAP CHOICE

Let x0,y0 be determining roots in the common top patch, with
d(x0,y0)<=D. Set beta=delta0/8, the literal rate in CMP102(190).
For a determining root z,

 1+d(y0,z) <= (1+D)(1+d(x0,z)).

Let K_delta=sup_(t>=0) (1+t)^2 exp[-delta0 t/16], which is finite.
Then

 exp[-delta0 d(x0,z)/8] (1+d(y0,z))^2
   <= (1+D)^2 K_delta exp[-delta0 d(x0,z)/16].

Apply Lemma2.1 with alpha=1/16. Its sufficient geometric condition is

            delta0 R M1/64
                 > 2d log c0(1/32)+1,                   (C)

where M in the CMP96 gap is M1 in the B99/CMP102 geometry, NOT the
later M-cell carrier size in B109. Under(C),

 sum_c a_(q(c))^d a_(q(c))^(-d)
       exp[-delta0 d(x0,c)/8] (1+d(y0,c))^2
   <= 2d (1+D)^2 K_delta 12[c0(1/32)]^d,                (D)

up to the fixed matrix-coordinate norm factor. This is the precise
uniform top-output estimate required in TURN126(4.1). It actually
inherits the native lemma's all-output property whenever d(x0,y0)<=D;
the subsequent nonlinear argument uses it only on its homogeneous
top patch.

Condition(C) must be recorded at the initial choice of native geometric
constants. Merely satisfying(2.59) at alpha=1/2 does not automatically
justify spending a smaller rate alpha=1/16. One may enlarge the fixed
native gap threshold R1 before choosing the later B109 carrier M, then
fix that geometry for the construction. Equivalently, if existing fixed
constants already satisfy(C), no change is needed. No cutoff-dependent
choice is introduced.

5. LOCALIZATION / DOMAIN SCOPE

CMP96 includes the grade0 complement in its full-lattice partition;
B99's Dirichlet variant starts with a possibly smaller Omega0. The
remaining determining roots form a subset of the corresponding full
grade0-completed geometry. Thus counting over the completion gives
an upper bound; if contour paths are restricted by the Dirichlet
domain, distances increase and the exponential sum only decreases.
One must keep the actual metric convention used in the kernel, rather
than assigning zero length outside Omega0.
