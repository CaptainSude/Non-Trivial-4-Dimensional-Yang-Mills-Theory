PRIMARY NATIVE CURRENT RESPONSE AND A HOMOGENEOUS COARSE-COORDINATE LIFT
19 October 2026. Admission-proof agent. Turn 16.

Result and exact scope

The missing second-order response estimate is in the ORIGINAL published
variational theorem. It has now been read from the primary PDF, including
the displayed formula. Thus this part of H_min is no longer an assumption
supported only by the reconstructed account.

There is a closed, uniform-in-fine-refinement result on a homogeneous
determining lattice: a sufficiently regular real physical anchor admits
a holomorphic lift of one prescribed coarse-coordinate perturbation,
with all other coarse coordinates unchanged. The lifted field and its
ACTUAL Wilson current stay in the original smaller-radius sufficient
subclass of the analytic field domain. Every derived-minimum condition
then follows from the explicit primary implication on B109 p263.

One may first take the anchor to be a homogeneous native minimum. More
generally the same conclusion uses the native fluctuation chart at an
arbitrary sufficiently regular physical anchor: it is a constraint right
inverse, not an assertion that this new field is a minimum. The reason
is the actual zero-input identity in (179)--(180), not continuation of
an identity from the set of minimizing fields.

This theorem does NOT upgrade the original proposed graded local U0
anchor to a homogeneous field. It does not freeze arbitrary finer-tier
or artificial-boundary slots of a different tower. A compact local lift
is proved separately in primary_reference_field_class_turn16.txt,
Sections4--7, and independently audited in
compact_native_lift_independent_audit_turn16.txt. It requires one complete
coherent gauge patch and strict smaller-subclass/current margin;
arbitrary mixed-anchor coverage is not asserted.

2. THE ACTUAL RESPONSE ESTIMATE, INCLUDING ITS SCALE WEIGHTS

Use current-scale units. The fine spacing is eta=L^(-k), and a tier-j
block has side r_j=L^j eta. Let y in Lambda_j, y' in Lambda_j', and
x in the block Delta(y). Formula (190), printed p308, bounds the five
kernels

  delta H_mu(B,x)/delta B_nu(y'),
  nabla_x delta H_mu(B,x)/delta B_nu(y'),
  a localized beta-Holder first-derivative seminorm,
  D_U^{eta*} D_U^eta delta H_mu(B,x)/delta B_nu(y'),
  Delta_U^eta delta H_mu(B,x)/delta B_nu(y')

by, respectively,

  C [r_j^-1, r_j^-2,
     (||zeta||_beta^# + |zeta|) r_j^(-2-beta),
     r_j^-3, r_j^-3]
       r_j'^(-d) exp[-delta0 d(y,y')/8].                  (2.1)

The factor r_j'^(-d) belongs to a kernel integrated against the
source's r_j'^d counting measure. In the homogeneous current lattice
used below both r_j and r_j' are 1. Thus changing one coarse bond does
not introduce either a fine-site count or an eta^-d factor.

Proposition 9, p309, states analyticity in the small relative datum B,
the relative Landau-gauge bounds (19)--(21), and (190). It also extends
the construction to an arbitrary regular external gauge field U in
the specified spaces of the earlier propagator/gauge paper. Those
spaces include the actual second-order/current regularity: arbitrary
C1 fields are not being substituted for them.

The real minimum theorem, p279 Theorem1, separately supplies the
curvature/current regularity (2) and local first/second-derivative
bounds (9),(10). It is not necessary to infer these from analyticity.

3. THE CURRENT IDENTITY THAT CONVERTS (2.1) INTO ADMISSION

Write

  J_W(U) = D_U^{eta*} eta^-2 pi Im partial U,
  Im W = (W-W^-1)/(2i).

B109 (3.11), p272, states for any regular U the EXACT expansion

  J_W(exp(i eta A) U)
       = J_W(U) + D_U^{eta*}D_U^eta A + F(U,A).           (3.1)

Here F is local and depends only on U, partial U, A, nabla_U A.
In particular it has no uncontrolled second derivative of U or A.
Its small-field analytic bounds are uniform in eta. The original
derivation is referred to the lattice formulas (1.43)--(1.54) of
the regular-field/gauge paper; the same cancellation is visible in
CMP102 pp291--292, (90)--(96). In those formulas a discrete derivative
of a higher lattice correction is paid by its explicit eta factor.
The cubic-and-higher force bounds are summarized in CMP102 Prop4,
(97)--(98), p293.

Since F(U,0)=0, Cauchy on a fixed regular neighborhood, or the same
local expansion, gives

  ||F(U,A)||_infinity
       <= C_U (||A||_infinity+||nabla_U A||_infinity)     (3.2)

on a fixed small ball, with C_U uniformly bounded when the anchor
radii are bounded by the fixed native small constants. Consequently

  ||J_W(exp(i eta A)U)-J_W(U)||_infinity
       <= ||D_U*D_U A||_infinity
          +C (||A||_infinity+||nabla_U A||_infinity).     (3.3)

The scales in (3.1) are physical: a fine plaquette is eta^2 times
curvature, whereas J_W is a physical field of dimension three.
There is no extra eta^-1 to append to (3.3).

This is precisely the missing equation in the former C1-only
argument. A generic C1 perturbation has no bound for D*D A; (2.1)
supplies that bound for the native chart. No arbitrary independent
J variable is used: along the final path J is always J_W of the
same fine field.

4. HOMOGENEOUS LIFT THEOREM

Fix d,L and a finite current-scale periodic box/torus on which the
native homogeneous construction is defined, with all its coarse
links included in the determining datum. Its physical coarse size
and the constants M,M1 are fixed. Refine only eta=L^(-k).
There are no artificial finer-tier boundary rows in this construction.
An alternative boundary version would need its own full fixed-boundary
dictionary; it is not silently included here.

Let U_* be a real anchor in the original homogeneous regular space,
with strict margin in the smaller-(i)--(iii) sufficient subclass on
B109 p263. Concretely keep separate the following bounds, in current
units:

  eta^-2 |partial U_*-1| <= a_*,
  |J_W(U_*)| <= a_*,                                    (4.1)

and on every required O(LM)-sized local cube a real gauge with

  U_*^u=exp(i eta A_*),
  |A_*|+|nabla A_*| <= C_patch a_* .                      (4.2)

Also retain the original external-field regularity hypotheses of
Prop9, rather than replacing them by (4.2) alone. In particular,
a homogeneous native minimum with sufficiently small datum curvature
has all these properties by Theorem1. For a general physical anchor
one can state its original curvature/codivergence inequalities (2)
directly; they are explicit source hypotheses, not a newly named norm.

Let (a0',a1') be fixed smaller radii for which the implication on
B109 p263 gives the target class U^c_k(Z,a0,a1). Assume, with a fixed
margin nu>0,

  a_* <= c_nu a0',                                      (4.3)

with c_nu small enough to allow the fixed patch/minimum constants.
The target radii may depend on g_k; the constants c_nu,C below do not
depend on k. Let rho_native>0 be the actual relative-datum radius in
(172), with the source constants fixed once. At a weak anchor it is
permissible to apply that theorem on a FIXED small native radius and
then restrict to the smaller target disk.

Pick one oriented coarse bond c and a unit Lie algebra element T.
Define V(z) by

  V(z;c)=exp(i z T) M^k(U_*;c),
  V(z;b)=M^k(U_*;b) for every other positive coarse bond b,

and impose the corresponding inverse rule on reverse bonds. Then
there is a holomorphic fine-field path U(z), real for real z, with

  U(0)=U_*,                  M^k U(z)=V(z),             (4.4)

on a disk |z|<r, where one may choose

  r = c min(rho_native, nu a0', nu a1').                 (4.5)

For a fixed common real anchor gauge, or in its native relative
Landau representative, the estimates are

  |A(z)|+|nabla_U A(z)|+|D_U*D_U A(z)|+|Delta_U A(z)|
                                                     <= C |z|,
  eta^-2 |partial U_L(z)-partial U_*| <= C |z|,
  |J_W(U_L(z))-J_W(U_*)| <= C |z|.                       (4.6)

Here U_L(z)=exp(i eta A(z))U_* is the relative Landau representative.
The actual field U(z) is the original representative obtained by
undoing the native gauge map so that the ordinary averages in (4.4)
hold exactly. The target analytic class is a union of complex gauge
orbits; membership is therefore proved in U_L and applies to U.
This avoids claiming that an arbitrary representative has the same
pointwise complex matrix norm.

All constants in (4.5),(4.6) depend only on fixed native small radii,
patch geometry, d,L and the stated margin. They are uniform in prior
fine refinement. No growing-volume assertion is needed or made.

5. PROOF, INCLUDING THE ZERO-INPUT AND EXACT-CONSTRAINT POINTS

First suppose U_* is a homogeneous native minimum U_k(V_*).
Take the original analytic U_k(V(z)) of Proposition9. SectionG,
(172)--(173), gives its relative Landau form A(z)=H_{U_*}(B(z));
H(0)=0 and B(z) is the one-bond logarithmic datum. All output/source
tiers in (2.1) are k, so integrating the bounded derivative from 0
to z proves the first line of (4.6). The elementary plaquette product
expansion proves its curvature line. Equation (3.1) proves its
current line. This is already a source-attached complete subcase.

For the more general regular anchor, use the extension of the same
native chart through (179)--(180), as explicitly stated on p309.
Its zero-input identity can be checked without any minimum assumption.
The nonlinear function V in (180) is the one in (80):

  V(A') = -<H D3(A'),J>
          -<A',Delta H D(A')>
          +1/2 <H D(A'),Delta H D(A')>
          +V0(A'-H D(A')).                              (5.1)

D starts in degree two, D3 in degree three and V0 in degree three.
Thus V'(0)=0 for EVERY regular physical background, including one
that is not stationary for the averaging fiber. At B=0, equation
(180) has the solution A0=0, and the same contraction used there
makes it unique on the native small ball. Equation(179) then gives
H_U(0)=0. One must not reinsert a missing forcing term G J and call
this chart a new minimum: that is a different map.

The constraint identity is likewise algebraic. SectionsA--C start
with an arbitrary regular reference U0, subject to (14), not with
a stationary reference. Their change of gauge converts the original
averaging constraints into (20). The linearizing transformation is
defined by (48)--(50), so for A=A'-H D(A') it satisfies exactly

                   Q_U(eta A)=r_j Q_j A'.               (5.2)

In (179), A'=A0+H0 B, Q A0=0 and r_j Q_j H0 B=B. Thus the original
constraint (20) holds. Undo the SAME source gauge transformation
used to obtain (20). It returns the ordinary averaged datum V(z)
and fixes the zero perturbation. This step uses the source's
ordinary/corrected-average dictionary and its normalized inverse
gauge map; it does not identify those averages as the same map,
nor infer the identity by holomorphic continuation from minimizing
backgrounds. The original bijection is discussed on pp280--281;
the analytic inverse gauge construction is used again on p307.

Proposition9 explicitly preserves (19)--(21) and the kernel bounds
for the regular external-U extension. With the PHYSICAL background
current J_W(U_*), (2.1) and H_U(0)=0 therefore give (4.6) in exactly
the same way. This is a regular constraint lift; its nonstationarity
off the original minimum graph does not affect the argument.

Finally use U_* as the REAL factor in the definition of the target
complex field class. Its real curvature and local gauges satisfy
(i). The first line of (4.6) gives (ii). The curvature/current lines
give (iii), with the reserved smaller radii. The paragraph immediately
after B109(1.16), p263, explicitly proves that sufficiently smaller
(i)--(iii) imply all the derived-minimum conditions(iv), including
the real-factor version. This supplies the full domain and not only
its first-derivative part. Equation(4.5) pays each radius separately.

6. CONSEQUENCE FOR ONE FIXED NATIVE REFERENCE SCORE

Consider the reference of B122-II (1.2), with fixed Z,Lambda,tree/slice,
window,action weight and every branch label. Its primary analytic
extension before (1.10) is through V_k=M^k(U) with U in the original
complex field class. If the reference's varying external data are the
coarse data in (4.4), and the remaining prescribed slots are fixed
as in that definition, the path above supplies an admitted disk.
Its interior reference baseline V_Lambda is recomputed by the original
rule; it is NOT asserted to equal the fixed core data of the lift.

The normalized reference theorem from turn15 now yields, on an inner
disk at a real parameter,

  ||centered partial_z log w||_L2 <= C/r,
  |Cov(f,partial_z log w)| <= C(g_k/r) Lip_Lambda(f).      (6.1)

These constants include the fixed 100M-box slice dimension, quadratic
gap and Haar/window margins of that reference. They do not count fine
reconstruction variables. The B-independent Wilson action factor
cancels before the score estimate. No complex zero-free denominator
is used in that probability argument.

The deterministic derivative of f(exp(i g_k B)V_Lambda(z)) is still
separate. A fine-loop or already backward-propagated insertion needs
its own Lipschitz/response estimate. No sharp branch gate is
differentiated. Equation(6.1) is not a complete-law influence bound.

7. WHAT THIS DOES NOT SETTLE ABOUT THE GRADED LOCAL PROPOSAL

Formula(2.1) at a finer output tier j carries r_j^-3. A top-tier datum
perturbation therefore has only the bound

  C r_j^-3 exp[-delta0 d(y,c)/8] |z|                      (7.1)

there. One cannot delete r_j^-3 because the source datum is protected.
Additional distance-through-layers estimates can sometimes overcome
it, but they must be checked on the actual tower and the original
field-class target. More importantly, they do not repair an anchor
which already fails that target class on its artificial boundary.

The homogeneous theorem also does not fix ALL fine slots of a second
local tower merely because it fixes all homogeneous coarse averages.
If the reference implementation reads extra fine boundary slots
independently, the lift must preserve those too, or its full derivative
must retain their variation. Applying the global path in that different
slot problem would be incorrect.

The proposed compact smooth-bubble alternative can preserve genuine
fixed fine exterior slots. Its finite-dimensional inverse theorem is
not sufficient by itself: the inverse and analytic derivatives of the
ACTUAL iterated ordinary averaging map must be uniform in k. In
particular ordinary averages have an endpoint gauge term absent from
the straight/adjusted flat average. The candidate correction by a
compact gauge bubble has to be retained, and the nonlinear iterate
must be estimated with physical-scale summability, not a C^k product.
The separate completed proof is now in
primary_reference_field_class_turn16.txt, Sections4--7. Its auxiliary
corrected averaging trajectory has summable physical-step errors; an
exact ordered gauge product then controls the ordinary iterate. The
flat complete-star moment inverse, endpoint gauge correction and
uniform Cauchy bounds give the required holomorphic finite inverse.
The independent audit compact_native_lift_independent_audit_turn16.txt
passes these steps. This is an additional local result, not an expansion
of the homogeneous theorem's hypotheses.
