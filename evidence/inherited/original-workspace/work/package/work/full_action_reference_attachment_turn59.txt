2. THE ACTUAL INTEGRAL, WITH ALL FIXED DATA RETAINED

Fix one component Lambda, its geometry, copied determining data,
zeta, coupling g, native graph G0, and the admitted real exterior
center e0. Fix the corresponding analytic representatives. Write

 t_e=V_Lambda(e),      t0=t_(e0),
 V'=exp(i g B),
 S_e(v)=A(zeta,U_(k,Z)(v t_e)).

The real B coordinates lie on the prescribed G0-pinned linear slice.
Its actual characteristic function defines a fixed bounded domain D,
independent of the external parameter e in this branch. Let d be its
real dimension and

                    R_B=max(1,sup_(B in D) ||B||_2).      (2.1)

All link entries, including zero entries on G0, are included when
comparing this norm with the orbit-profile edge norm.
The maximum with1 only inflates the radius bound, not the domain.
It ensures W>0 also for a zero-dimensional slice; in that case
the integral is a point evaluation. The property gR_B->0 is
unchanged.

On D the native expansion has the literal form

 S_e(exp(i g B))/g^2
    =S_e(1)/g^2 + ell_e(B)+Q_e(B,B)/2+R_e(B).              (2.2)

Here ell is linear and is retained; R has zero jets through degree2.
Do not replace the local reference minimum by an unrelated global
minimum, or set ell to zero by declaring zeta-stationarity.

Up to a field-independent coordinate factor J_g, the denominator is

 Z_nat(e)=J_g exp[-S_e(1)/g^2] Z_red(e),
 Z_red(e)=int_D sigma(gB)
          exp[-ell_e(B)-Q_e(B,B)/2-R_e(B)] dB.             (2.3)

The relative Haar factor sigma(gB) and D do not depend on e.
The scalar J_g includes any chosen Haar-coordinate normalization
and g^d; it is unchanged in the modification.

3. QUANTITATIVE LOCAL NATIVE INPUT, NOT GLOBAL CONVEXITY

The source expansion, with a strict reserved native field margin,
gives on the real center and a declared admitted complex input disk

 Q_(e0)>=kappa I,       ||Q_e||<=K_Q,
 ||ell_e||<=L,          sup_D |R_e|<=R,
 0<sigma_-<=sigma(gB)<=sigma_+ on the real D.              (3.1)

The constants depend on fixed M, the prescribed current slice and
native hierarchy. They are uniform in previous fine refinement.
Choose the weak floor so R and L have fixed upper bounds and D
contains a fixed Euclidean ball of radius b>0 in its actual slice.
The source window has g R_B ->0 at that floor: its per-link radius
is the source's small weak-field threshold, and d=O_M(1).

For analytic e use ONLY a path whose complete current data are the
actual averages of an admitted original fine-field family. The
minimum-output function t_e and the coefficients in (2.2) are then
the actual native analytic functions. The homogeneous auxiliary-lift
dictionary in TURN16 Section8 applies when its anchor hypotheses hold.
It is not an admission theorem for arbitrary independent mixed data.

Here is an explicit local reduction which avoids assuming that
real positivity alone persists on an arbitrary complex domain.
Let e=e(z), |z|<r, be such an admitted disk centered at e0. If
||Q_e||<=K_Q there, Cauchy bounds on a fixed fraction of r give

              Re Q_e >= (kappa/2) I                     (3.2)

after shrinking r by a factor depending only on kappa/K_Q.
The same argument applies to any finite collection of directions
on their declared analytic norm ball.

The relative logarithm T(z)=log[t_e t0^-1] is taken on its local
branch. Its actual analytic minimum-output bound is retained.
For example, a bound
sup_(|z|<r) ||t_e t0^-1-I||<=B_t gives, on a smaller disk,

             ||T(z)||_infinity <= 4 B_t |z|/r.            (3.3)

Thus any fixed graph-dependent T radius below is obtained by
one further reduction of the admitted disk. This records the
minimum-output input explicitly; it does not manufacture a
uniform coarse-coordinate lift or ignore an exterior gauge.

The real coercivity consequence of (3.1) is only

 S_e0(exp(i g B))-S_e0(1)
       >=g^2[kappa ||B||_2^2/2-L||B||_2-R],    B in D.   (3.4)

It is sufficient here. Global coercivity, a globally defined
minimizer, and convexity outside D are not required or inferred.

4. AN EXPLICIT COMPLEX NORMALIZED-OPERATOR BOUND

For clarity, this bound can be derived directly from (3.1)--(3.2);
one need not infer it just from holomorphy of the final logarithm.
On the reserved disk put

 C_abs=sigma_+ exp(R)
        int_(R^d) exp[-kappa |B|^2/4+L|B|] dB,

 c_min=sigma_- Vol_d(B_b)
        exp[-L b-K_Q b^2/2-R].                           (4.1)

Then the absolute reduced integral is at most C_abs, whereas
Z_red(e0)>=c_min>0. Both constants are independent of previous
refinement, with fixed current geometry. In dimension zero the
same argument means evaluation at the single point.

On a further fixed fraction of the analytic disk, Cauchy's
estimate gives |Z_red(e)-Z_red(e0)|<=c_min/4. For instance,
after placing the uniform upper bound on a disk of radius r1,
one may use |z|<=r1 min(1/2,c_min/(8 C_abs)).
Consequently

 |Z_red(e)|>=3 c_min/4,
 int_D |d nu_e| <= C_M:=max(1,4 C_abs/(3 c_min)),          (4.2)

where d nu_e is the native reduced integral normalized by Z_red(e).
For real admitted e it is a probability law; for complex e it
is a normalized complex measure of total mass1.

This recovers a sufficient local version of the source's analytic
normalizer control, with every norming factor exposed. The large
exp[-S_e(1)/g^2] cancels exactly from (4.2).
The disk radius still scales with the actual admitted input radius
r1. No fixed, coupling-independent exterior-coordinate radius is
claimed. Constants such as C_M may be large functions of fixed M;
they are not silently called polynomial in M.

6. CHOOSE THE WIDTH BEFORE USING THE ZERO-FREE COMPARISON

Take C0=2 exp(2), C_R=sqrt(2)exp(3)/4, and the TURN58

 K_F=max(1,m log[(C_H/c_H)(2 C_F)^(3/2)]).

Use the explicit vanishing-modulus estimate proved in
soft_orbit_zero_free_turn58.txt, (5.6). With

 |phi_lambda(i g B,t_e)/phi0-1|
 <=exp[C0 C_F K_F tau+(9/4)a sqrt(K_F)+C_R a^2]-1,       (6.1)

provided the original trace-bound chart holds and
(C0/2)C_F tau+3a/(4sqrt(K_F))<=1/2.
It uses no native action or reconstruction outside D.

Choose eta=1/(8 C_M), and define fixed constants

 a_* = min(eta/(18sqrt(K_F)), sqrt(eta/(8C_R)), 1),
 tau_* = min(1/(4C0 C_F), eta/(8C0 C_F K_F)).

Take

                  W=R_B/a_*                            (6.2)

and reduce the admitted external disk once so ||T||<=tau_*.
The trace hypotheses and the extra condition in (6.1) then hold.

       sup_(B in D)|phi_lambda(i g B,t_e)/phi0-1|
                          <=eta.                       (6.3)

Finally choose the weak floor so gR_B<=a_*/2.
Then gW<=1/2, hence lambda>=4>=1, and max_e|i g B_e|<=1.
There is no inconsistency between making W wider than D and
the requirement lambda>=1: gR_B is the actual shrinking
native weak-link radius. No unbounded UV graph dimension

One may take any larger W for which gW<=1. For a family of

7. EXACT NORMALIZER AND CURRENT-TEST CONSEQUENCES

Let r_e(B)=phi_lambda(i g B,t_e)/phi0 and write

 H(e)=Z_soft(e)/(phi0 Z_nat(e))
     =int_D r_e(B) d nu_e(B).                           (7.1)

Equations (4.2),(6.3) prove

              |H(e)-1|<=C_M eta=1/8.                   (7.2)

Thus H and Z_soft are zero-free on the same reserved disk,
log H has its branch with the actual real value at e0,
and |log H|<=-log(7/8). In particular

 log Z_soft
   =log J_g-S_e(1)/g^2+log Z_red+log phi0+log H.          (7.3)

The classical background-action term is EXACTLY the same
as in the native reference. The positive phi0 and the
analytic log H are retained, including all their allowed
physical jets. The local comparison center t0 is fixed;
phi0 is not a universal vacuum constant as physical
centers are varied.

For bounded current tests F the normalized modified law is
d nu_e^soft=r_e d nu_e/H. Hence

 int |d nu_e^soft|<=C_M(1+eta)/(1-C_M eta),

 |nu_e^soft(F)-nu_e(F)|
 <=eta C_M(1+C_M)/(1-C_M eta) ||F||_infinity.             (7.4)

For real admitted e, positivity improves the last bound
to2eta/(1-eta) ||F||_infinity. These formulas hold for
bounded tests on the actual current integration variables.
No old-field observable identification is inferred.

There is also a pointwise normalized insertion. Put

                   Delta_e(B)=r_e(B)/H(e)-1.

It is analytic in the admitted external variables and
the admitted B variables wherever their original complex
chart is retained, and on the real B integration domain

 sup_D |Delta_e|
       <=(1+C_M)eta/(1-C_M eta),
                   nu_e(Delta_e)=0.                     (7.5)

Indeed r/H-1=[(r-1)-(H-1)]/H. Thus the comparison is a
legitimate centered small insertion on this SAME fixed
native branch, with its normalization performed against
the actual full-action law. For a required complex-B
supremum norm use (6.1)--(6.3) on that entire declared
complex chart, replacing R_B by its total norm radius;
the source action must also be admitted there. A bound
only on real D is not silently promoted to that norm.

This insertion can be used in a later admitted marked
calculation only after its actual typed gauge covariance,
complete support/locality and required complex input
domain have been checked. Those are not conclusions of
the pointwise formula (7.5).

Any smaller FIXED eta works by the same construction,
with its new width and weak floor chosen once. If a
later class instead requires eta=eta(g), for example
g^P, one must additionally verify

                   gR_B <= a_*(eta(g))/2.                (7.6)

For small eta the displayed choice has a_*=c_M eta.
The source fact gR_B->0 does not alone prove (7.6)
for an arbitrarily prescribed power g^P. The present
fixed small insertion is therefore not automatically
an input of every native g^P marked class.

Fixed-order derivatives of log H on a smaller admitted
disk obey the ordinary Cauchy bounds with its actual radius.
Such bounds include the profile's new physical quadratic
and any allowed linear term; they do not discard them.

8. WHAT THIS RESOLVES, AND WHAT REMAINS

The new fixed-slice reference has an actual full reconstructed
action, a positive real integral, and an analytic zero-free
normalizer at the admitted local scope. Its proof exposes
the precise uniform-in-refinement coercivity and minimum
inputs and preserves native classical-action cancellation.
This is more than an estimate of the orbit profile alone.

A strictly positive unrestricted soft profile has support
on every real current group field. The source construction
does not supply U_(k,Z) on all those data. Therefore simply
deleting D in (5.2) is not a defined use of the native regular
branch. This is a domain diagnosis, not a claim that no
other global action extension can be constructed.

Conversely, retaining D makes the full fixed-slice integral
well defined, but orbit invariance of phi alone does not
make the remaining product characteristic function an
invariant function on the full current group space.

There is a compatible finite-graph alternative, explained
in soft_full_action_route_turn59.txt, Section4: pull back
the COMPLETE supported slice weight by the axializer of
one fixed construction forest. It is then invariant and
has the same slice normalizer. Its action is evaluated
only on the admitted slice. This requires an exact
forest/anchor/Haar dictionary for the chosen native G0,
or its actual conditional coarea retained explicitly.
Calling G0 an axial graph alone does not certify that
complete dictionary. Changing a display integration
slice leaves this fixed invariant weight unchanged;
changing the construction forest can change the weight.

Compatible complete choice averaging and the original-law
basic-operation identity remain separate requirements
for either version of this modification.

The analytic input radius used above is the actual original
field-class radius with a reserved minimum-output neighborhood.
Any required coarse-coordinate/source parametrization must
be attached to that class, as in TURN16 where its hypotheses
hold. Full mixed-history domains and future hard-cut decisions
are not enlarged by this construction.

No bound for a full unrestricted fine-action normalizer,
no recursive modified-R stability theorem, and no Yang--Mills
existence conclusion is asserted.
