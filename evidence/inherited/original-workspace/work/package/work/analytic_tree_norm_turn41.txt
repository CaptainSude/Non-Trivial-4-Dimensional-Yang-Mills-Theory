ALL-ARITY TREE NORMS FOR THE LOCAL CLASSICAL SADDLE AND PREFACTOR
19 October 2026. Root producer. Turn41.

RESULT AND LIMITATION

The local KKT branch of local_saddle_and_prefactor_turn40 has more than
fixed-order spatial response bounds. Its analytic Taylor kernels have a
common exponentially weighted, absolutely summable tree norm, including
a bound with ANY external leg fixed. The same is true of the classical
action and complete leading Haar/coarea determinant, after excluding
their extensive vacuum constants. Thus their n-th derivatives have
C^n n! bounds in a specified spatial norm, with constants independent
of box volume. This is proved by an analytic tensor majorant, rather
than inferred from a collection of constants at separate orders.

This statement concerns the local classical saddle and its leading
prefactor. It does NOT estimate the entire finite-g integral, the
derivatives of a cutoff at arbitrary order, or repeated RG maps. It does
not claim individual gauge invariance of the resulting Taylor monomials.

1. EXACT INPUTS AND COMPLEX LOCAL VERTICES

Use the actual complete ordinary averaging map, local G0 forest,
fixed-crossing action and parameter locations of TURN40. Write

 G(z;u)=(grad S_e(x)+DF_e(x)^t lambda, F_e(x)-v),
 z=(x,lambda), u=(v,e), M0=G_z(0;0).

Here e denotes retained logarithmic link coordinates. It may be held
fixed when only framed v derivatives are wanted. The number of local
field/color types, read radius and incidence are bounded in terms of
fixed L and SU(N_c). Coarse rows and fine coordinates are assigned their
actual lattice positions, with finite multiplicity per site.

Each local action/map row is holomorphic in a common small complex
polydisc. This follows by taking a smaller disc inside the local
exponential/logarithmic ordinary-mean chart: only finitely many local
read and boundary types occur at fixed L. Holomorphic matrix inverses
used in that finite local chart remain nonsingular on its compact
smaller closure. Cauchy estimates there imply common geometric bounds
on ABSOLUTE Taylor coefficient sums, not just existence of each finite
derivative separately. In detail a row with at most D local variables
has coefficient sum at degree p bounded by
C r^-p binomial(p+D-1,D-1)<=C_1^p for p>=1, after enlarging C_1.
Transpose incidence is bounded as well, so the same bound is available
with any input leg fixed and the output summed.

The actual M0 inverse has uniform exponentially weighted row and column
sums from TURN40. Choose a positive exponent m below that decay rate.
No theorem concerning a massless coarse covariance is used here.

2. A RE-ROOTABLE TREE TENSOR NORM

For positions i_0,...,i_p let tau(i_0,...,i_p) be the shortest length
of a connected lattice tree containing those positions; additional
Steiner vertices are allowed. Repeated positions cause no difficulty.
For a tensor A(i_0,...,i_p), define

 ||A||_m = max_(0<=r<=p) sup_(i_r)
      sum_(all other indices) |A(i_0,...,i_p)|
                                  exp[m tau(i_0,...,i_p)].          (2.1)

Each index also includes its finite color/type multiplicity. The norm
of a two-leg kernel is its maximum weighted row/column sum. Kernels
with distinguished output legs are allowed; symmetry is required only
for the Taylor input slots where appropriate. For one-leg tensors the
norm is a supremum. Vacuum constants are treated separately.

TREE CONTRACTION LEMMA. Suppose tensors are joined by contracted legs
so their factor graph is a tree, and at least one external leg remains.
The norm (2.1) of the resulting tensor is at most the product of the
norms of the factors.

Proof. The union of the connecting trees inside the factors is connected
and contains all external positions. Therefore the external tree weight
is at most the product of their tree weights. Fix ANY chosen external
index and root the factor graph at its incident factor. Remove the leaf
factors successively, summing all their legs except their connection to
the remaining graph. The corresponding summation is bounded by (2.1)
with that connection fixed. At the last factor use the originally fixed
external index. This proves the claimed product bound for every root.
Symmetrizing external Taylor inputs averages such bounds and increases
none of them. Fixing only the output in the definition would not justify
this argument when the chosen root is an input leaf.

A local degree-p vertex has all its legs within fixed radius D_L of its
output. Its tree length is at most D_L p. The Cauchy coefficient sums
of Section1 thus give ||A_p||_m<=C_m^p. The finite set of boundary types
has the same bound. Local identities/inclusions have finite norm.

3. COMMON ANALYTIC MAJORANT FOR THE SADDLE

Expand around zero first. G(z;u)=M0 z+G_u(0;0)u+N(z,u), where N begins
at total degree2. Put K=M0^-1. The exact equation is

 z=-K G_u(0;0)u-K N(z,u).                               (3.1)

Let Z_n be the symmetric degree-n Taylor tensor of z(u), so Z_n=D^n z/n!.
Use (2.1) on ALL its external legs, including the output. The tensors
of N have common bounds C^p, and K has norm k. Substitution and the
tree contraction lemma bound the coefficient recursion by the scalar
nonnegative majorant

 w(t)=a t+k sum_(p>=2) C^p(w(t)+t)^p
     =a t+k C^2(w(t)+t)^2/[1-C(w(t)+t)].                (3.2)

The symmetrization factors in Taylor substitution are averages; the
remaining ordered degree choices are exactly those in scalar series
composition. Thus induction on n gives ||Z_n||_m<=[t^n]w(t).

Equation(3.2) has a unique analytic solution with w(0)=0: its derivative
with respect to w on the left-minus-right side equals1 at the origin.
Its radius and coefficient bound depend only on a,k,C. Taking a smaller
circle gives [t^n]w<=C_2^n. In particular

                    ||D^n z/n!||_m<=C_2^n.            (3.3)

This proves a COMMON radius and the all-arity weighted factorial bound.
It is not an inference from real C2 locality or fixed-order estimates.
The series converges absolutely for ||u||_infinity<C_2^-1 and agrees
with the unique sup-small KKT solution. The sum of its majorant bounds
also gives volume-independent operator estimates for bounded inputs.

The estimate is uniform around every background in a smaller complex
sup ball. Indeed M_*(u0)-M0 is finite range and O(||u0||_infinity) in
the same weighted row/column norm. Choosing that ball so
||K(M_*-M0)||_m<1/2 gives a uniformly weighted inverse by a Neumann
series, without a self-adjointness assumption at complex data. The
local vertex Cauchy bounds remain uniform on a smaller nested disc.
Apply (3.2) to increments z-z(u0),u-u0, with these uniform constants.

4. CLASSICAL ACTION KERNELS

For framed v and fixed exterior, the exact envelope identity is

                     D_v S_min=-lambda_*.             (4.1)

The n-th action Taylor tensor for n>=2 is, after distinguishing one
v leg, -1/n times the degree-(n-1) tensor of lambda(v). Consequently

 max_r sup_(v_r) sum_(other n-1 indices)
       |D^n S_min/n!| exp[m tau(all n indices)]<=C_3^n. (4.2)

Symmetry of the action derivatives and the any-leg bound in (3.3)
justify every choice of root. The first derivative is uniformly small
on the data ball, and S_min(0,e) is a separate vacuum/background term.
For joint retained-edge derivatives the envelope formula is instead
S_min,e=partial_e S_e+lambda dot partial_e F_e. The partial tensors
have bounded local incidence; substitution of (3.3) and the same tree
contraction gives the corresponding marked joint estimates. At a
nonzero exterior its own constant/forcing terms remain present.

Passing to the LOCAL insertion q->v(q,e) of a fixed coarse forest
section is a composition with finite-read analytic kernels, so it
preserves a common bound of this form. Passing from an arbitrary framed
field to its long-forest gauge representative is a different operation
and is not justified by this sentence.

5. MATRIX TAYLOR KERNELS AND THE MARKED TRACE LOG

For M_*(u)=G_z(z_*(u);u), subtract M_*(0). The degree-n matrix coefficient
B_n has two distinguished operator legs and n datum legs. Local vertex
composition with (3.3), using the tree contraction lemma, gives

                         ||B_n||_m<=C_4^n, n>=1.       (5.1)

The maximum in (2.1) includes either operator leg AND every datum leg.
This last fact is important for taking an extensive trace.

On the small data ball the analytic logarithm is represented by

 log det M_*(u)-log det M_*(0)
           =sum_(p>=1)(-1)^(p+1) Tr[(K B(u))^p]/p.      (5.2)

Use the determinant sign at the real base point to choose its branch;
for complex data (5.2) specifies the holomorphic continuation. The
unmarked constant log[(-1)^dimY det M_*(0)] is not estimated in a
rooted norm.

MARKED CYCLE ESTIMATE. A coefficient of order n>=1 in the p-th trace
has p vertices B_nj with nj>=1 and sum nj=n, joined in a cycle by p
K kernels. Fix any external datum index. Remove one K factor and bound
its entries by sup|K_ab|<=k. The remaining factor graph is a tree; the
two exposed end indices are still summed. Its union of connecting
trees contains every external datum. The proof of Section2 then sums
the remaining kernels with the chosen datum as root, including those
two endpoints. The resulting norm is at most k^p product_j||B_nj||_m.
No free vacuum volume remains, since an external datum is fixed before
any trace summation.

Therefore the all-arity coefficients of (5.2) are majorized by

     sum_(p>=1) [k b(t)]^p/p=-log[1-k b(t)],
     b(t)=sum_(n>=1) C_4^n t^n.                        (5.3)

This scalar function has a positive radius independent of volume. Its
Taylor coefficients are bounded by C_5^n. This proves the desired
common factorial bound for every marked derivative of the log
determinant, with the full tree weight. The inverse matrix can be
treated by the corresponding open-chain Neumann expansion, with both
operator legs retained; no cycle argument is needed for it.

6. COMPLETE HAAR/COAREA PREFACTOR AND A PRECISE INTERACTION NORM

The actual complete prefactor from TURN40 is

 W=.5 log[(-1)^dimY det M_*]-log j_C(x_*)+log j_J(v).     (6.1)

The two Haar logarithms are sums of fixed-range analytic link/row
functions. Their local vertex coefficient sums have common Cauchy
bounds. Composing with the saddle and datum response tensors, and
fixing any datum index before summing the local vertex location, gives
the same anchored norm by tree contraction. Thus for n>=1

 max_r sup_(u_r) sum_(other n-1 indices)
       |D^n W/n!| exp[m tau(all n indices)]<=C_6^n.      (6.2)

At the identity, the linear color-invariant jets vanish where the
previous adjoint argument applies. Equation(6.2) does not need such
vanishing at arbitrary background. Extensive W(0) remains in the
vacuum normalization. Every original fine/coarse Haar factor is kept.

These bounds give an explicit absolutely convergent analytic expansion
with tree-length weights. For h small, write the Taylor monomial terms
of S_min or W about a specified background and group them by the set
of distinct input coordinates. Assign each monomial a deterministic
shortest connecting lattice tree and the connected hull consisting of
that tree and its marked sites. Since the lattice spacing is fixed,
the number of sites in this hull is at most C_L(tau+1), including the
finite color multiplicity. For a sufficiently small positive animal
weight kappa, exp(kappa|hull|) is paid by part of exp(m tau).

For a root which is a marked coordinate, the sum of the absolute sup
norms of these monomials, with that animal weight, is bounded by
sum_(n>=1) n C_7^n ||h||_infinity^n. The factor n allows for occurrences
of the root among ordered Taylor slots. This converges at a common
radius. For an unmarked lattice root lying on the assigned connecting
tree, summing the possible displacement from one marked site costs
at most a polynomial in tau, absorbed by another fixed fraction of
the exponential weight. Equivalently split into tree-length shells,
use the marked bound with the larger exponent, and sum the shell
volume polynomial. The same uniform rooted animal bound follows.

This is an analytic spatial interaction expansion for the LOCAL
classical action and leading prefactor, with extensive constants
separated explicitly. Individual monomials and connecting hulls are
not asserted gauge invariant. Native renormalization conditions and
relevant/marginal extractions are not supplied by this decomposition.

7. WHAT REMAINS FOR THE ACTUAL RG MAP

The full finite-g action has the additional term -g^2 log(1+r_g).
TURN40 controls only two data derivatives of that term on its stated
boxes. Neither (3.3) nor (6.2) provides an all-arity tree norm for r_g.
A fixed smooth energy cutoff or a hard finite window also needs its
own complex-domain/large-field analysis; analyticity of the saddle
does not establish analyticity of the entire selected density.

The proof is for the already specified first-step local KKT system.
Uniform bounds for a changing all-scale quadratic reference, exact
gauge-compatible source transport, the actual exterior/coarse law,
and running-coupling/relevant-part control are separate requirements.
No continuum Yang--Mills or closed RG induction is asserted.
