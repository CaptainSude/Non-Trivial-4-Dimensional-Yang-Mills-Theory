ROOTED QUADRATIC GAUGE-WARD JETS: COVARIANT COMPLETION
18 October 2026. Working proof; ordinary coarse-link variables only.

Scope. The lattice is a finite periodic unit lattice, with fixed-size
J-blocks and fixed SU(2) color dimension. A block contains at most C_J
independent link/color slots. All constants below are independent of
the torus size. No assertion is made for a block containing an
unbounded fine-refinement fiber. The proof uses a fixed positive
spatial-exponent reserve. It does not itself prove that such a loss
can be repeated without bound along an RG history.

1. INPUT AND NORMS

Fix an external root block z. Let

             W_z(A)=sum_H W_{z,H}(A_H)

be holomorphic on the ordinary complex link ball ||A||_infinity<b0.
H is a finite strict support, and tau(z,H) is the length of a shortest
connected block graph containing z and H. Include a harmless additive
1 in all lengths below. Suppose

       sum_H exp(kappa0 tau(z,H)+a0 |H|)
                    sup_{||A_H||<b0}|W_{z,H}(A_H)| <= W0.     (1.1)

The total W_z is invariant under local gauge transformations whenever
both configurations are in its analytic domain. It has no nontrivial
external color index. In particular its first field derivative at
zero vanishes: a global SU(2) transformation would otherwise give an
invariant linear covector on a direct sum of adjoint representations.
Let

                Q_z(A)=(1/2) D^2 W_z(0)[A,A].                (1.2)

Differentiating the local gauge identity, and using DW_z(0)=0,
shows that the Hessian annihilates lattice gradients in either slot:

             Q_z(A+d lambda)=Q_z(A).                       (1.3)

This identity is on the actual finite torus, including all finite
volume modes. No infinite-volume or nonwinding limit is used.

First average each W_{z,H} over simultaneous CONSTANT compact color
conjugation of all its arguments. Its support and supremum do not
increase, and the total remains W_z. Thus each quadratic color tensor
may be taken invariant under simultaneous adjoint conjugation.

Expand the quadratic pieces into fixed-dimensional link slots:

          Q_z(A)=sum_{e,f} q_{z;ef}(A_e,A_f).               (1.4)

Here q is complex bilinear, not sesquilinear. One may retain the old
H label during the expansion and sum it afterwards. Coordinate
Cauchy and polarization give at most C_J |H|^2 coefficients per H,
with total bilinear norm bounded by C_J |H|^2 b0^-2 sup|W_H|.
For any fixed kappa1<kappa0, the polynomial |H|^2 is paid by the
remaining exponent, since |H|<=C(1+tau(z,H)). Consequently

 M := sum_{e,f} exp(kappa1 tau(z,e,f)) ||q_{z;ef}||
                         <= C b0^-2 W0.                    (1.5)

The main construction can equivalently take (1.3), color invariance,
and finite M in (1.5) as its input, without any parent W.

2. ONE GLOBAL SHORTEST-PATH TREE AND THE LINEAR IDENTITY

Choose a spanning tree of the finite graph rooted at a representative
vertex of z, such that the path P_x from the root to x is shortest.
This exists by breadth-first search. Fixed J gives

             length(P_x)<=C_J(1+dist(z,block(x))).          (2.1)

For an oriented link e=(e-,e+), let C_e be the rooted closed walk

                P_(e-) followed by e followed by P_(e+)^-1.

Repeated/backtracking edges are allowed. Its oriented linear sum is

 B_e(A)=sum_{p in C_e} epsilon_p A_p
       =A_e+lambda_A(e-)-lambda_A(e+),
 lambda_A(x)=sum_{p in P_x} epsilon_p A_p.                 (2.2)

Thus B=A+d lambda_A, with a sign convention absorbed in d. Applying
the finite-torus Ward identity (1.3) gives the exact polynomial
identity

                  Q_z(A)=sum_{e,f}q_{z;ef}(B_e(A),B_f(A)).  (2.3)

The SAME tree is used for every coefficient. Choosing an unrelated
gauge for each (e,f) would not justify (2.3).

Let ell_e be the number of oriented links in C_e and K_ef its two-loop
support, enlarged to J-blocks. Every path length is bounded by its
root-to-endpoint distance. Hence, with a fixed C_J,

 ell_e+ell_f+|K_ef|+tau(z,K_ef)
                       <= C_J(1+tau(z,e,f)).               (2.4)

This is the crucial linear support-enlargement bound. Expanding
quadratic coefficients BEFORE adding the paths avoids the potentially
quadratic support enlargement of adjoining root paths for every link
in a large original H at once. Winding paths on the finite torus are
allowed; shortest graph distance is the metric used in (2.1)--(2.4).

3. EXACT NONLINEAR GAUGE-INVARIANT COMPLETION

Put U_e=exp(i A_e), U_(e^-1)=U_e^-1, and let V_e(U) be the ordered
holonomy along C_e. Define

            L(V)=pi_g((V-V^-1)/(2i)),

where pi_g is the fixed complex-linear adjoint-equivariant projection
onto the represented complexified Lie algebra. For SU(2), V-V^-1
is already traceless. The projection can also be fixed using the
invariant trace metric. It is holomorphic and satisfies

             L(I)=0,       D[L(exp(i t B))]_(t=0)=B.       (3.1)

Under local gauge transformations a rooted holonomy transforms as
V_e -> g_z V_e g_z^-1. Equivariance of L and constant-color invariance
of q therefore make EACH summand in

             C(Q_z)(U)=sum_{e,f}q_{z;ef}(L(V_e),L(V_f))     (3.2)

exactly locally gauge invariant. Compact color invariance of q extends
to the connected complexified group by holomorphy; compact invariance
alone already suffices on the real physical fields.

The first derivative of L(V_e(exp(iA))) at A=0 is B_e(A).
Equations (2.3) and (3.1) show

          C(Q_z)(exp(iA))=Q_z(A)+terms of degree >=3.        (3.3)

Thus C(Q_z) has zero vacuum and linear terms and exactly the desired
quadratic homogeneous polynomial. Equivalently,
D^2 C(Q_z)(0)=D^2 W_z(0), with the 1/2 convention in (1.2).

If q is real on real Lie algebra fields, so is the completion on SU(2)
links. A translation-covariant family of rooted trees is obtained by
using a fixed ordering of directions relative to z. If a point-group
covariant choice is desired, averaging the finitely many transformed
completions preserves every conclusion and changes constants only by
a fixed amount. No rotational covariance is needed for the lemma.

4. UNIFORM HOLONOMY ESTIMATES AND THE CUBIC DIFFERENCE

Use a fixed submultiplicative matrix norm. For complex ||A||<=b,
successive product expansion gives, with absolute fixed constants,

 ||L(V_e)|| <= C ell_e b exp(C ell_e b),
 ||L(V_e)-B_e(A)|| <= C ell_e^2 b^2 exp(C ell_e b).          (4.1)

For completeness, expand each exp(+/-i A_p) into its absolutely
convergent series. The total degree-n coefficient norm of the product
is bounded by (ell_e b)^n/n! times a fixed norm constant. Removing
degrees 0 and 1 yields the second estimate, with the same argument
for the reversed inverse product. Projection pi_g is bounded.
Repeated links and inverses cause no extra volume dependence.

For one bilinear coefficient the first estimate gives

 |q_ef(L_e,L_f)|
       <= C ||q_ef|| b^2 ell_e ell_f exp(C b(ell_e+ell_f)).  (4.2)

To estimate the difference one MUST first use (2.3), obtaining the
strict representation

 C(Q_z)-Q_z = sum_ef [q_ef(L_e,L_f)-q_ef(B_e,B_f)].         (4.3)

Termwise subtraction of q_ef(A_e,A_f) would leave quadratic terms;
the cancellation is the global linear Ward identity, not a separate
identity for each coefficient. Bilinearity, (4.1), and telescoping give

 |q_ef(L_e,L_f)-q_ef(B_e,B_f)|
   <= C ||q_ef|| b^3 ell_e ell_f(ell_e+ell_f)
                                      exp(C b(ell_e+ell_f)). (4.4)

Choose positive output weights kappa2,a2 with
C_J(kappa2+a2)<kappa1/2. Then choose a fixed b_* small enough that
the exponential in (4.2)--(4.4), after (2.4), consumes less than a
further kappa1/4 of the input reserve. The remaining exponential pays
the displayed length polynomials. Grouping equal K_ef, or retaining
their coefficient labels, yields the rooted strict-support norms

              ||C(Q_z)||_(kappa2,a2;b) <= C M b^2,
        ||C(Q_z)-Q_z||_(kappa2,a2;b) <= C M b^3,            (4.5)

uniformly for 0<b<=b_*. Constants depend only on the fixed exponents,
J, and group/coordinate norms, not the torus. The sums converge
normally on smaller complex balls, including on an infinite lattice
if the same uniform rooted coefficient bound is supplied.

5. PRECISE APPLICATION AND LIMITS

For an actual gauge-invariant mark W and a linearly gauge-invariant
quadratic reference Q_free, W-C(Q_free) is exactly gauge invariant.
Its quadratic jet is Q_actual-Q_free; it has no quadratic jet only
when those two quadratic forms are equal. Both cases may be useful,
but they must not be conflated.

The completion error has analytic norm O(M b^3), hence after dividing
by the natural b^2 mark scale it is O(M b). This justifies an O(b)
per-step comparison cost when M is uniformly bounded in the stronger
input norm. It does not by itself establish that such costs are
summable over an unlimited history, nor that a repeatedly degraded
spatial exponent remains available. The current application must use
its already proved static neutral transport / rooted carrier bounds
and keep one fixed stronger kernel reserve for each new completion.

In particular this lemma repairs gauge invariance of the comparison
mark without assuming a nonlinear common Gaussian background chart.
The quantum response estimate, the actual/free quadratic comparison,
and their history bounds remain separate inputs. No full existence
or nontriviality conclusion is asserted by this construction alone.
