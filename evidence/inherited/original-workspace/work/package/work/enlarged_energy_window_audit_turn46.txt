ENLARGING THE ACTUAL ENERGY WINDOW WITHOUT A SMALL OUTER CUBIC EXPONENT
19 October 2026. TURN46. Independent audit/proof by native46.

VERDICT

PASS, with the hypotheses and qualifications stated below. The TURN45
conditions g Lambda^3+Ng Lambda<<1 are unnecessarily strong for comparing
the actual energy-window integral with its auxiliary product-cutoff
integral. They made the interacting correction uniformly bounded by a
constant throughout the outer integration domain. For a PURE-TAIL
comparison it suffices to retain coercive Gaussian decay there and pay
an exp(CN) amplitude cost.

The replacement conditions are a fixed small bound g Lambda<=epsilon,
the actual geometric coverage of the entire supported fiber in its
complete chart, and the original auxiliary cluster conditions. In
particular gT^3<<1 is still required on the auxiliary cube. The comparison
is joint in complex datum and source variables, and its logarithm uses
the auxiliary cluster lower bound, not a near-one Laplace ratio.

One quantitative change must be retained: the real positive-mixture
deficit now has an exp(CN) prefactor and a weaker Gaussian exponent,
exp(CN)m exp(-cT^2). The dimension-free earlier C m exp(-T^2/2) estimate
is not asserted in this larger window regime.

1. INPUTS, PARAMETERS, AND THE ACTUAL COVERAGE REQUIREMENT

Use exactly the complete fixed-L, fixed-crossing, G0-quotient/coarea
chart of TURN41--45, with all charged plaquettes and all affected coarse
rows. Retain the same original Haar and coarse-Haar normalizers. Write
K=ker Q, Pi for its Euclidean orthogonal projector, d=dim K, and m for
the full ambient Gaussian dimension, with d,m<=C_L N. The nonorthogonal
constraint-chart projector is not used in Gaussian decompositions.

The existing joint chart supplies the holomorphic saddle B_*(u), action
s(u), constrained Hessian H(u), complete Jacobian J, and the uniform
analytic Morse inverse b_M(y,u). In a fixed small complex neighborhood,

 |s(u)|<=C N ||u||_infinity^2,

 |R_3(b,u)|<=C||b||_2^3,
 |log[J(B_*+b,u)/J_*(u)]|<=CN||b||_2,                 (1.1)

and H, H^(-1), H^(+-1/2) have uniformly bounded operator norms. The
principal square-root branch is symmetric for the bilinear transpose.
The Morse map obeys

 f(B_*+b_M(y,u),u)-s(u)=y^t y/2,
 b_M(y,u)=H(u)^(-1/2)y+O(||y||_2^2),                 (1.2)

with the corresponding first-derivative remainder. It is defined on a

with epsilon small enough for ALL linear, Morse, angular and Stokes

Additionally require the actual geometric coverage of the supported

 C_L [g R_geom Lambda+R_geom ||e||+||Y-I||] <=epsilon_cov,

where R_geom is the relevant fine box diameter and e,Y are the entire

2. COERCIVITY ON THE LINEAR CONTOUR, INCLUDING COMPLEX DATA

Let xi be real in the ambient space and put
b=g H(u)^(-1/2) Pi xi. Because H(u) is symmetric and its analytic square
root commutes with H(u),

                 b^t H(u)b=g^2 |Pi xi|^2.             (2.1)

This is an exact BILINEAR identity, even though u and b can be complex.
It is not a claim that a complex matrix defines a positive Hermitian
form. Equation (1.1) gives for r=|Pi xi|,

 Re[(f(B_*+b,u)-s(u))/g^2] >=r^2/2-Cg r^3.            (2.2)

For r<=C_0 Lambda, choosing g Lambda small relative to C_0 gives

 Re[(f(B_*+b,u)-s(u))/g^2] >=c_1 r^2.                 (2.3)

The real actual support, after linear whitening, satisfies such a bound
r<=C_0 Lambda by chart coverage and the uniform real constrained gap.
The fixed linear comparison ball has r<=R and is also covered.

The complete Jacobian satisfies
|J/J_*|<=exp(CN g r)<=exp(CN). The plaquette estimate remains

 sum_p |h_p(B_*+b,u)| <=C(||b||_2^2+N||u||^2)<=CE0,   (2.4)

and therefore the full physical source factor costs at most exp(CN),
since E0/sigma^2<=N/c0^2. The same bound holds throughout the Morse
ball and swept side, after fixed changes of constants.

Crucially, the reference ambient Gaussian cancels only in K. Including
the untouched K-perpendicular factor, the absolute normalized density
is bounded by

 exp(CN) (2pi)^(-m/2)
       exp[-c_1|Pi xi|^2-|Pi_perp xi|^2/2].          (2.5)

This is bounded by an isotropic Gaussian of rate c_2=min(c_1,1/2),
whose normalization relative to the standard Gaussian costs
(2c_2)^(-m/2)<=exp(CN). Consequently its integral on the ambient
cube complement C_T^c is bounded by

                  exp(CN) m exp(-cT^2).              (2.6)

This argument explicitly pays both the weakened-Gaussian determinant
and the full ambient dimension. It does not use an incorrect product
Gaussian identity for the coordinates of Pi xi.

3. LINEAR BALL VERSUS THE ACTUAL AUXILIARY CUBE

 |L_R(u,w)-R_aux(u,w)|<=exp(CN) m exp(-cT^2).           (3.1)

Both integrals are holomorphic in u,w on the declared common domain:

At zero source and real data, the positive restricted-submeasure identity

 0<=1-a_aux(u;0)/a_E(u;0)<=exp(CN)m exp(-cT^2).        (3.2)

The constants in exp(CN) can change. Neither exact positivity nor full
normalization is lost, but the former sharper deficit is not retained.

4. LINEAR BALL VERSUS MORSE BALL: THE MOVING COMPLEX CONTOUR

The two oriented chains are

 |b_t-b_0|<=Cg^2R^2,
 ||D_xi(b_t-b_0)||<=Cg^2R,

The uniform Taylor bound gives

 (f(B_*+b_t,u)-s)/g^2=R^2/2+O(gR^3).

Its real part is >=R^2/4 under gR<=epsilon. This absorption requires
gR small, NOT gR^3 small. The leading R^2 is retained throughout.
The complete Jacobian and source factor on the side cost exp(CN) by
Section2; they need not be bounded by a dimension-independent constant.

Integrate the normalized holomorphic top-degree form used in TURN45.
It is closed; Stokes applies to this smooth real (d+1)-chain in C^d,

5. THE ACTUAL CUTOFF VERSUS THE MORSE BALL

On |v|<=3E0 and the full complex datum/source domain define

 h_w(v,u)=average_theta [A(sqrt(2v)theta,u)/A0(u)]

The uniform Morse chart applies if sqrt(6)gLambda is sufficiently small.
Its amplitude bound is

                       |h_w(v,u)|<=exp(CN).           (5.1)

Odd angular powers cancel, so h_w is holomorphic in v. No estimate of
h_w-1 or smallness of N gLambda is used. No assertion h_w(0,u)=1 is
made for nonzero sources.

Let a=d/2, t0=E0/2, q=t0-s(u). The exact actual normalized integral is
continued by

 R_E=1/[g^(2a)Gamma(a)] {
 q^a int_0^1 exp(-qz/g^2)z^(a-1)h_w(qz,u)dz
 +int_(t0)^(2E0) exp[-(t-s)/g^2](t-s)^(a-1)

The identity holds on the real family by the full supported-fiber Morse
change of variables. It defines a holomorphic extension because the

argument remains in |v|<3E0. The same branch choices as TURN42 apply.

The Morse-ball integral ends at v_R=E0/8. Deform the first radial path

In particular we never compare the entire Morse integral to one.

6. ZERO-FREENESS FROM THE LOCAL CLUSTER, WITH NO NEAR-ONE ASSUMPTION

The auxiliary cluster theorem on its UNCHANGED small cube supplies

Therefore

                   |R_aux(u,w)|>=exp(-CN).            (6.1)

This is valid on the full complex datum/source polydisc by the assumed

Combining (3.1), (4.2), (5.2), then dividing by (6.1), gives

 |R_E/R_aux-1|<=exp(CN)[m exp(-cT^2)+exp(-cLambda^2)]
                       =:delta_enlarged.             (6.2)

Choose the cutoffs so delta_enlarged<1/4. Then D=log(R_E/R_aux) is
holomorphic with ||D||<=C delta_enlarged, and

 log[a_E(u;w)/a_E(u;0)]
 =sum_X[F_X(u,w)-F_X(u,0)]+D(u,w)-D(u,0).             (6.3)

The exact leading prefactors and all vacuum/source-only contributions
are retained before forming this ratio. Datum derivatives of D cost
rho^(-k), source derivatives cost sigma^(2n), with the usual factorial
Cauchy bounds. A full-box carrier is paid by imposing

 T^2>=A[N+(p+1)log(1/g)+log(m+1)],
 Lambda^2>=A[N+(p+1)log(1/g)+1],

as well as the separate cube/plateau requirement Lambda^2>=C mT^2

7. WHAT LARGER WINDOWS ARE NOW PERMITTED

Suppose R_geom and N have fixed polylogarithmic growth and the exterior
family is weak enough for (1.5). Take, with a sufficiently small fixed
epsilon_* and the necessary coverage constants absorbed,

 Lambda=epsilon_*/(g R_geom),
 E0=epsilon_*^2/R_geom^2,
 rho=c0 epsilon_*/(R_geom sqrt(N)).                   (7.1)

Then gLambda=epsilon_*/R_geom is small, the geometric energy term in
(1.5) is epsilon_*, and the complex datum term obeys
R_geom rho=c0 epsilon_*/sqrt(N). The datum radius is still inside the
fixed chart. A native real family gZ with fixed polylogarithmic Z lies
well inside this larger disc for small g. The original auxiliary
polylogarithmic T has gT^3<<1 and mT^2<<Lambda^2.

Thus the ACTUAL WINDOW TAIL becomes

             exp[CN-c/(g^2 R_geom^2)],               (7.2)

up to constants depending on epsilon_* and the fixed geometry. This
statement is compatible with the recorded chart hypotheses; the old
restriction Lambda<<g^(-1/3) is not needed for this comparison.

The full comparison still contains exp(CN)m exp(-cT^2). It is incorrect
to report (7.2) as its sole error when T is only polylogarithmic. One may
retain the old pure-cube-tail budget while exploiting the stronger
omitted-energy exponent separately. Trying to enlarge T as far as
Lambda violates the auxiliary gT^3 condition and requires a new theorem.

Nor does (7.2) by itself bound every omitted original-Wilson history:
one still needs the relevant conditional denominator, actual coarse
constraint/current geometry, and history counting. This note repairs
the single-step energy-window estimate only.

8. AUDIT SUMMARY

* Complex coercivity is proved by exact bilinear whitening plus cubic
  absorption; it is not inferred from real positivity.
* The weaker ambient Gaussian and its dimension cost are explicit.
* Jacobian and physical-source amplitudes cost exp(CN), which is allowed
  only because these bounds multiply tail terms.
* The Stokes homotopy remains inside a fixed analytic chart and retains
  its R^2 phase; no complex cutoff is introduced.
* The radial common core cancels exactly; no false near-one estimate is
  used at the enlarged window.
* The local auxiliary cluster supplies the nonzero denominator.
* Complete real-fiber coverage and the unchanged auxiliary small-cube
  cluster are still indispensable hypotheses.
* The enlarged omitted-energy exponent does not erase the separate
  auxiliary-cube error or prove an all-scale construction.
