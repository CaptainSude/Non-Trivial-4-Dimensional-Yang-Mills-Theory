3. THE ACTUAL MR PARTITION COMPARISON

B119 printed255 defines each R_i to be a power of L. The rendered
printed256 equation(2.9) states, for n>m,

 R_n<=L R_m,
 R_m<=(L+1)(n-m)^beta0 R_n.                              (3)

Use adjacent dates. Since L>=2,
 R_m<=(L+1)R_(m+1)<L^2 R_(m+1).
Both sides' R ratio is a power of L, hence
 R_m<=L R_(m+1).
Iteration yields

                    R_i<=L^(j-i) R_j.                   (4)

On the original fine graph, the physical side of the grade-i MR_i
partition is L^i M R_i times the original fine mesh. The ratio of the
grade-j side to it is

                 L^(j-i) R_j/R_i,                       (5)

a nonnegative integer power of L by(4). Therefore the new partition
is a coarsening of each old partition when the ACTUAL common aligned
origin/offset and coordinate conventions are used. The group of lattice
translations/reflections/permutations preserving the new partition is
then a subgroup of the intersection of the corresponding old groups.
Different unrelated origins would invalidate this inference; numerical
side divisibility alone does not align partitions.

The same statement in current grade-j units uses old side
L^(i-j) M R_i, so no second rescaling factor is inserted. The mature/
young selector may still use its actual native threshold; (4) does not
alter it. It proves the needed common covariance subgroup for a total
