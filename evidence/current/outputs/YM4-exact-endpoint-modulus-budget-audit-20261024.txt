1. SPATIAL EXTRACTION AND THE TWO COUNTS

Starting from the displayed covariance bound for a test in B_n(b),
let r=d(b,q), D=d(P,b). For kappa<=lambda,

 exp[kappa d(P,q)] exp[-lambda(D-n)_+]
                        <= exp[kappa(r+n)].

This follows from d(P,q)<=D+r and D-(D-n)_+<=n. Thus extracting
the spatial factor exp[-kappa d(P,q)] gives exactly the two-parameter
sum in producer(3). The root count is O((1+r)^(d-1)); the bounded
test's support count is O((1+n)^d). The original shell factor in
the second cap is paid by the fixed eta-eta_bar reserve before
this sum. A density of original labels per current cell multiplies
the estimate. It is not removed by rooting the resulting activity.

2. SPLIT LINE, POWERS AND ZERO-RATE LIMIT

If u<=chi, N=log(chi/u)/eta_bar, the minimum changes at n=r+N.
Below that line it is u exp(-eta_bar r). At positive kappa, summing
n to that line costs (1+r+N)^d exp[kappa(r+N)]. The remaining r
series has exponent -(eta_bar-2kappa)r, so it is summable for
2kappa<eta_bar. Above the line the exponentially decaying n-tail
has the same boundary value and polynomial order. Hence the bound
is C u^(1-theta) chi^theta(1+log(chi/u))^d, theta=kappa/eta_bar.

If u>=chi, interchanging r and n interchanges the polynomial powers
d-1 and d. The logarithmic power needed at the split is then d-1,
which is bounded by the producer's common power d. The formula with
m=min(u,chi), M=max(u,chi) is therefore correct in both orderings.
At kappa=0 a finite sum of (1+n)^d costs one further power of its
upper limit, giving the stated d+1 upper logarithmic power. The
positive-rate constant is not asserted to stay bounded as kappa->0.
If either cap vanishes the original sum is exactly zero.

3. MODULUS CLAIMS HAVE THE CORRECT LOGICAL DIRECTION

For u(s) comparable to s^alpha and fixed positive chi, the particular
positive-rate bound has power alpha(1-theta) with the stated log.
With a required next-lattice rate floor and only the supplied bounded
input rate, blocking forces a positive lower bound on theta in this
proof. Repeating THIS majorant therefore loses a fixed power fraction.
This is not a lower bound on the actual physical response or a proof
that the actual function loses regularity.

Likewise at zero rate, for a nonzero concave modulus omega with
omega(0)=0, omega(tau s)>=tau omega(s). The numerical transformed
majorant omega(tau s) times the diverging logarithmic factor cannot
be bounded by a fixed multiple of omega(s). This shows failure to
close that majorant in the same modulus; a response bounded by it
could still obey a better estimate. The Dini example also correctly
shows why Dini continuity alone is insufficient for exponential
spatial composition. The producer explicitly requires additional
spatial input for its general modulus recursion.
