A bounded measurable cylinder needs no derivative of its own: projecting

general LII removed-family versus empty kernels with such a masking of
the B119 ordinary law. That law/read matching is the precise remaining

2. ONE BOUNDED LOCAL TEST NEEDS NO GRADIENT ARM

Let F=F(x_R) be any bounded real measurable cylinder, and set

 b_F=inf_(c real) ||F-c||_infty=osc(F)/2.

For an admitted smooth G, let T_R(x_R)=E_mu[G|x_R]. The exact identities
are

 Cov_mu(F,G)=Cov_mu(F,T_R),

 D_R T_R[v]=E_C[D_R G[v]]-Cov_C(G,D_R H[v]).                       (3)

Both terms on the right use the original CONDITIONAL normalizer.
Equation (3) follows by differentiating that numerator and denominator,
not by estimating their ratios separately. The finite compact chart and
fixed K_C justify this step by domination. Approximation extends the
result in the usual Sobolev class when needed.

Take W(i)=exp[-nu dist(i,R)] and A=M_nu/c_nu. Conditional weighted
covariance and the full mixed-Hessian Schur bound give, for every v on R,

 |Cov_C(G,D_R H[v])|
 <=A ||W_C grad_C G||_(L2(mu_C)) ||v||_2.                         (4)

Indeed W_R=1 and ||W_C^-1 H_CR v||_2<=M_nu||v||_2. Integration in
x_R and Cauchy--Schwarz for the two gradient blocks then yield

 ||grad_R T_R||_(L2(mu))
 <=sqrt(1+A^2) ||W grad G||_(L2(mu)).                             (5)

Apply the FULL-law Poincare inequality to T_R(x_R), rather than
asserting a new marginal Hessian theorem. Since sd(F)<=b_F,

 |Cov_mu(F,G)|
 <=b_F c0^(-1/2) sqrt(1+A^2) ||W grad G||_(L2(mu)).               (6)

No differentiability or Cauchy radius of F occurs. F need only have
the actual read R in these current coordinates. For complex bounded F,
one may use inf_(c complex)||F-c||_infty and the Hermitian variance
version, or apply the real result to real and imaginary parts with a
fixed constant loss. All displayed sharp oscillation constants below
are stated for real tests.

3. RETAINED-CURRENT SCORE AND LOCAL TOTAL-VARIATION RESPONSE

For a supported retained-coordinate path a(t), the complete score is

 S_t=partial_a H_pre(x,psi(a(t)))[b_t],
 b_t=Dpsi(a(t)) dot a(t),
 grad_x S_t=H_pre,IE'' b_t.                                     (7)

If b_t is supported on T, at distance d from R, weighted Schur gives

 ||W grad_x S_t||_2<=M_nu exp(-nu d)||b_t||_2.

Consequently (6) gives

 |Cov_mu(F,S_t)|
 <=b_F c0^(-1/2) M_nu sqrt(1+(M_nu/c_nu)^2)
                       exp(-nu d)||b_t||_2.                    (8)

The slightly larger convenient constant

 K_nu=M_nu+M_nu^2/c_nu

can replace M_nu sqrt(1+(M_nu/c_nu)^2). Root's direct differentiation
of E_C S_t proves that form as well: its direct mixed Hessian term
costs M_nu and its complement covariance costs M_nu^2/c_nu.

The native C1 switch is bondwise, with delta=lambda epsilon and
||Dpsi-I||<=C_lambda exp(-R_switch), for the explicitly chosen scaled
cutoff profile in survivor_normalized_score_20261023. It preserves T.
Integration of (8) therefore bounds (Q_a1-Q_a0)F by the same right-hand
constant times (1+C_lambda exp(-R_switch)) and the path length.
This gives the local marginal total-variation response by taking the
supremum over bounded local tests, with the chosen TV normalization.
Every point of the path must remain in the same strict admitted slice.

This genuinely improves the earlier opaque-source bound: a literal
bounded local compiled test has a distance gain without a test-gradient
arm. It does not prove that an arbitrarily compiled source has that
literal local read, or compare backgrounds/coordinate sets/cuts.

4. TWO BOUNDED LOCAL TESTS

Let F(x_R), G(x_T) be bounded real cylinders with disjoint R,T. Set
d=dist(R,T) and b_G=osc(G)/2. Condition the full law on x_R, and write
U(x_R)=E_C G. In its derivative, G has no explicit x_R derivative:

 D_R U[v]=-Cov_C(G,D_R H[v]).

Apply (8), or its proof with x_R as retained parameters, to this
conditional complement law. All constants remain valid by Section 1.
It follows that

 ||grad_R U||_2<=b_G c0^(-1/2) K_nu exp(-nu d).

The full-law Poincare inequality and sd(F)<=b_F give

 |Cov_mu(F,G)|<=b_F b_G K_nu c0^-1 exp(-nu d).                    (9)

This is a direct finite proof under the complete weighted Hessian
hypotheses. Strong convexity by itself is not being promoted to an
exponential mixing theorem. Smooth approximation of G can be avoided
by differentiating its fixed-domain conditional numerator directly;
G is bounded and the action derivatives are uniformly integrable.

5. AN EXACT HARD-DOMAIN-CHANGE PRODUCER WITH JOINT RARITY

Let E be ANY measurable event determined by x_T, p=mu(E), 0<p<1.
It may be a nonsmooth union of local success/failure tests. Define
mu_E=mu(.|E). For F as in Section 4,

 p(mu_E F-mu F)=Cov_mu(F,1_E).                                  (10)

Thus the complete partition mass of the changed domain appears ONCE
on the left. This identity includes the original denominator exactly;
no lower bound for p and no derivative of a hard boundary are needed.

For a nontrivial indicator b_(1_E)=1/2. The trivial bound and (9) imply

 |p(mu_E F-mu F)|
 <=b_F min{2p, [K_nu/(2c0)] exp(-nu d)}.                         (11)

For every theta in (0,1), min(u,v)<=u^theta v^(1-theta), so

 |p(mu_E F-mu F)|
 <=b_F (2p)^theta [K_nu/(2c0)]^(1-theta)
                                  exp[-(1-theta)nu d].          (12)

In particular a SAME-law rarity p<=C exp(-q) supplies joint
exp[-theta q-(1-theta)nu d]. The statement is equally valid with
1-p if that is the rare side. It is not a square of the rare factor.

If the desired comparison is the two complementary normalized laws,
then the exact identity is

 p(1-p)(mu_E F-mu_(E^c) F)=Cov_mu(F,1_E).                        (13)

For p<=1/2, (11)--(12) therefore control p times their difference with
at most factor 2. This is a real change of the permitted integration
domain, even if E makes that domain nonconvex. Convexity is only used
for the original unconditioned mu and its coordinate restrictions;
it is never falsely assigned to mu_E.

There is a stronger finite-radius option, proposed by root and checked
here, which preserves the FULL rare power. Set h_T(z)=E[F|x_T=z].
Its gradient is bounded uniformly by Section 3 on the principal
complement I minus T, with z as the retained coordinates:

 ||grad_T h_T||_2<=L_R,T,
 L_R,T=b_F K_nu c0^(-1/2) exp(-nu d).                            (14)

This uses whole-slice constants and the same fixed complement domain.
Since K_T is convex, its Euclidean diameter D_T gives

 osc_(K_T) h_T<=L_R,T D_T.

The event E reads ONLY x_T, so exact conditioning gives

 Cov_mu(F,1_E)
 =p(1-p)(E[h_T|E]-E[h_T|E^c]).

Consequently

 |Cov_mu(F,1_E)|
 <=p(1-p) min{2b_F,L_R,T D_T}.                                  (15)

This has the full p times exponential distance, at the explicit price
D_T=diam_2(K_T), bounded by the root sum of squared coordinate-ball
diameters. A large support T can therefore cost sqrt(|T|) times its
cut radius. There is no claim that this radius/volume cost is harmless
in an all-date native census. Bound (12) is a diameter-free alternative.

Moreover any nonnegative integrable reweighting depending only on x_T,
with positive mass, changes mu F by at most osc h_T. The reweighted
law is simply another mixture of the SAME conditional complement
laws. This controls normalized local hard masks without an inverse-p
price. All branch-dependent source functions, changed Hamiltonians,
or changed coordinates would still require their separate matching;
the mixture identity alone does not identify the native reset branches.

This subclass is nonempty on the actual ordinary fiber: choose a
nontrivial threshold inside the success ball of a remote coordinate,
or any local finite Boolean combination of such tests, and a bounded
test on a disjoint current coordinate set. It is a finite conditional
producer. Identifying a physically required original source and its
native geometry event with these literal cylinder reads is a separate
application, not supplied merely by this abstract example.
