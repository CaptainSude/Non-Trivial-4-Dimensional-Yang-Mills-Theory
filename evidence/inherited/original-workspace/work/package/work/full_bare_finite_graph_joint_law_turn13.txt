THE FULL BARE FINITE-GRAPH WILSON JOINT LAW
19 October 2026. Fixed-graph theorem; no refinement or volume uniformity.

0. PRECISE RESULT AND SCOPE

Let K be a finite connected cubical complex whose two-skeleton is simply
connected. A finite rectangular box with its plaquettes has this property.
Use free boundary conditions: every graph link is integrated, and gauge
transformations are allowed at every vertex. Fix one vertex and a spanning
tree only to give an exact rooted gauge parametrization. Let G be a compact
connected semisimple Lie group and rho a faithful finite-dimensional unitary
representation. Normalize each link Haar measure to have mass one.

The full Wilson probability law, including all configurations outside any
small coordinate window, has a unique zero-action point in the rooted tree
quotient. Its Hessian there is strictly positive. Consequently, for this
FIXED graph,

 Z(g) = c_K g^n (1+O_K(g)),
 n=(number of edges-number of vertices+1) dim(G),       (0.1)

and its complete compact-space distance from the identity has a Gaussian
tail on scale g. Fixed normalized polynomial marks have bounded moments,
exponentially small marked chart-complement tails, and Gaussian limiting
Gram matrices. A finite reference family with linearly independent leading
polynomials therefore has a uniformly invertible Gram matrix for small g.

This is a JOINT law of all the rooted link variables, not the fluctuation
law conditional on an already specified coarse datum. No fine-refinement,
large-volume, all-history, continuum, or physical-field-rank conclusion is
included. Rooted quotient dimension n is the REAL dimension: if m denotes
the number of non-tree edges, the denominator is g^(m dim G), not g^m.

1. EXACT ROOTED TREE GAUGE AND PRODUCT HAAR

Choose one orientation for each unoriented edge. For an edge e=(v,w), use

 (h.U)_e = h_v U_e h_w^(-1).

Choose a root r and orient the spanning tree away from r, reversing edge
variables when necessary. Define k_r=1 and, recursively on tree edges,

 k_w=k_v U_(v,w).

Then W_e=k_v U_e k_w^(-1) equals 1 on every tree edge. The remaining W_e,
one for each non-tree edge, are unrestricted elements of G. Conversely,

 U_e=k_v^(-1) W_e k_w,       W_e=1 for tree edges.      (1.1)

Thus the full link space is smoothly and bijectively parametrized by
G^(|V|-1) times G^m. The Jacobian statement is exact, with no local chart:

 product_e dU_e = product_(v!=r) dk_v product_(e notin T) dW_e. (1.2)

For tree variables this follows successively from left/right Haar
invariance; conditional on those variables, every chord change in (1.1)
also preserves Haar. Inversion on a reversed oriented edge preserves Haar.

Every plaquette product changes by conjugation, so the action is independent
of k. Integration over the k variables gives exactly one. In particular
the full partition function and every gauge-invariant moment equal their
integrals on the compact smooth rooted space

                         X=G^m.                     (1.3)

The quotient by gauge transformations with h_r=1 is free and is exactly X.
The additional root gauge acts on all W_e by simultaneous conjugation.
We DO NOT divide by that possibly singular orbit space. For physical
observables we retain simultaneous-conjugation invariance on X. This does
not produce a null Hessian at the identity: the conjugation orbit of the
identity tuple is a single point.

2. THE UNIQUE ZERO AND THE ROLE OF TOPOLOGY/FAITHFULNESS

Write d_rho=dim rho and define

 v(u)=d_rho-Re Tr rho(u),
 S(W)=sum_(plaquettes p) v(W_p).                       (2.1)

Fixed strictly positive plaquette weights may be included throughout.
For a unitary matrix, each eigenvalue has real part at most one; equality
of their total real part with d_rho forces every eigenvalue to be one.
Thus

 v(u)>=0,       v(u)=0 iff rho(u)=I iff u=1.           (2.2)

Faithfulness is used in the last implication, including on the center.
At zero action EVERY plaquette holonomy is therefore the identity in G.

To see that every chord is then the identity in tree gauge, use the usual
explicit presentation of the fundamental group of a finite cell complex:
after collapsing the spanning tree, the chord loops generate pi_1, and
the attaching loops of the two-cells give its relations. A flat link
assignment defines a homomorphism pi_1(K)->G by path-ordered products;
backtracking and each plaquette homotopy preserve the product. Since
pi_1(K)=0, every based loop product is 1. The chord coordinates are exactly
these based fundamental-loop products in the tree frame. Hence

                    S(W)=0 iff W=1_X.                (2.3)

This argument does not require G itself to be simply connected. No
nontrivial bundle is introduced by this model: its variables are globally
specified finite graph links. Conversely, nontrivial spatial cycles can
carry zero-action global holonomy. A nonfaithful rho can permit nonidentity
plaquette holonomies in ker rho. Neither situation is covered by (2.3).

3. THE TREE-SLICE HESSIAN IS POSITIVE

Use the positive invariant inner product on the Lie algebra

 <X,Y>_rho=-Re Tr(d rho(X) d rho(Y)).                  (3.1)

Faithfulness makes d rho injective, so this is positive definite. Put
W_e=exp(x_e) on chords and x_e=0 on tree edges. For small x, the first
variation of the logarithm of a plaquette is the signed ordinary coboundary

 (d_1 x)_p=sum_(e in boundary p) incidence(p,e) x_e.

Taylor expansion of the exact matrix product in (2.1) gives

 S(exp x)=1/2 sum_p |(d_1 x)_p|_rho^2 + O_K(|x|^3)
         =1/2 <x,H x> + O_K(|x|^3).                  (3.2)

Here H is the Hessian on the non-tree coordinates, with the inner product
in (3.1) on each chord. There is no replacement by an infinite-volume or
nearest-neighbor inverse.

If d_1 x=0, the Lie-algebra-valued closed one-cochain x is exact: integrate
x along paths from the root. The result is path independent because its
sum around every plaquette is zero and pi_1(K)=0. Thus x=d_0 a, with a_r=0.
Since x vanishes on the spanning tree, a is identically zero. Hence x=0.
Therefore H is a strictly positive finite matrix:

                  0<lambda_K I<=H<=Lambda_K I.       (3.3)

Choose a fixed simultaneous exponential chart B_(2r) in the chord Lie
algebra, small enough that the coordinate map is injective, its image is
conjugation invariant, and

 c_K I <= D^2 S(exp x) <= C_K I       (|x|<2r).        (3.4)

In particular c'_K |x|^2<=S(exp x)<=C'_K |x|^2 there.
The pullback of product Haar is j(x) dx, with j smooth, positive and
bounded above/below on this chart, and j(0)>0. All constants in this
paragraph may depend on K, its chosen tree, G and rho.

4. THE FULL NORMALIZER, NOT A WINDOW NORMALIZER

Define on the COMPLETE compact rooted space

 Z(g)=int_X exp[-S(W)/g^2] dW,
 mu_g(dW)=Z(g)^(-1) exp[-S(W)/g^2] dW.                (4.1)

By compactness and (2.3), the complement of any fixed neighborhood O of
1_X has a strictly positive action gap:

             Delta_O=min_(W notin O) S(W)>0.         (4.2)

Take O=exp B_r. The integral over O is at most C_K g^n by (3.4) and the
Haar bounds. The remaining integral is at most exp[-Delta_O/g^2]. For a
lower bound integrate only |x|<=g, for g<=r: its Haar volume is at least
c_K g^n and its action is at most C_K g^2. Thus, for 0<g<=g_0,

                  c_K g^n<=Z(g)<=C_K g^n.            (4.3)

In particular the normalized complement estimate, with its denominator,
is explicitly

 mu_g(X\O)<=C_K g^(-n) exp[-Delta_O/g^2].             (4.4)

It is incorrect to drop the factor g^(-n) before proving (4.3); it is
harmless after that proof, being absorbable into half the fixed action
gap. If m=0, the rooted space is a point and the conclusions have their
trivial n=0 interpretation.

For the asymptotic constant, rescale x=g y in O. With H from (3.2),

 Z(g)/g^n -> c_K^0=j(0)(2pi)^(n/2)(det H)^(-1/2).     (4.5)

Indeed both S(g y)/g^2 and y.Hy/2 are bounded below by c|y|^2 on |g y|<r,
and their difference has absolute value at most C g |y|^3. For nonnegative
A,B bounded below by c|y|^2,

 |exp(-A)-exp(-B)|<=|A-B| exp[-min(A,B)].

Together with |j(g y)-j(0)|<=C g |y|, this gives an integrable O(g) error
after multiplication by any FIXED polynomial weight in y. The excluded
Gaussian tail and the compact complement in (4.4) are exponentially small.
This proves (0.1) with the explicit constant in (4.5).

5. GLOBAL GAUSSIAN AND MARKED TAILS

Choose a bi-invariant Riemannian metric on G and its product metric on X;
let d(W)=dist_X(W,1_X). It is invariant under simultaneous conjugation.
By (3.4), chart comparability, (2.3), and compactness away from 1_X, there
is a FIXED c_*>0 such that

                      S(W)>=c_* d(W)^2              (5.1)

on the whole compact space. This is not a global Hessian-convexity claim.
On d(W)>=t, split the Boltzmann factor into equal halves and use (5.1):

 mu_g(d>=t)
 <= exp[-c_* t^2/(2g^2)] Z(sqrt(2)g)/Z(g)
 <= C_K exp[-c_* t^2/(2g^2)]                         (5.2)

for g small enough that both couplings lie in (4.3). This bound includes
all chart-complement configurations without altering their law.

Integrating (5.2), or using Cauchy-Schwarz and the resulting moments,
gives for every fixed k<infinity

 E_mu (1+d/g)^k <= C_(K,k),
 E_mu [(1+d/g)^k 1_(d>=t)]
       <= C_(K,k) exp[-c_* t^2/(4g^2)].               (5.3)

Thus every mark satisfying |M_g(W)|<=A(1+d(W)/g)^k has the corresponding
normalized marked tail. Any fixed smooth R which vanishes to order d_0
at 1_X satisfies |R(W)|<=C_R d(W)^(d_0) globally: use Taylor locally and
boundedness with d bounded away from zero outside the chart. Consequently
g^(-d_0)R is covered by (5.3). Products of a fixed finite number of such
marks are also covered, with the sum of their degrees.

For a mark known only to satisfy a global bound A g^(-D), (4.4) instead
gives the explicit full complement estimate

 E_mu[|M_g| 1_(X\O)]
 <= C_K A g^(-D-n) exp[-Delta_O/g^2].                 (5.4)

For any fixed D this is smaller than every prescribed power of g. The
degree is fixed before taking g small; no all-degree uniform bound is
being asserted.

6. WEIGHTED GAUSSIAN LIMITS AND FINITE GRAM INVERTIBILITY

Let Y_g=log(W)/g on O and set Y_g=0 on its complement. The latter convention
is only a way to discuss a probability pushforward, not a change to mu_g.
Let gamma_H be the centered Gaussian on R^n with covariance H^(-1).
The estimate used in (4.5), normalization by the full Z(g), and (4.4)
show that for every fixed k,

 int (1+|y|^k) |d Law(Y_g)-d gamma_H|(y) <= C_(K,k) g. (6.1)

The absolute measure includes the small atom at zero contributed by the
chart complement. Equation (6.1) is a weighted total-variation statement,
not just weak convergence.

Let R_1,...,R_d be fixed smooth functions on X. Suppose R_a vanishes to
order d_a, and in the chart

 R_a(exp x)=P_a(x)+O(|x|^(d_a+1)),                    (6.2)

where P_a is homogeneous of degree d_a. Allow R_0=P_0=1,d_0=0 when a
constant reference is wanted. Put F_(a,g)=g^(-d_a) R_a. Taylor's theorem,
the global marked bounds and (6.1) yield, for every fixed finite product,

 E_mu product_a F_(a,g)^(k_a)
  =E_gamma product_a P_a(Y)^(k_a)+O_(K,R,k)(g).         (6.3)

The same statement holds with complex conjugates. One may first compare
F_(a,g) to P_a(Y_g) in each fixed L^p: the chart remainder is bounded by
C g |Y_g|^(d_a+1), and the complement has the bound (5.4). Then apply
Holder and (6.1). This proves (6.3) without discarding any measure sector.

In particular, for the full Hermitian Gram matrix

 G_g(a,b)=E_mu conjugate(F_(a,g)) F_(b,g),
 G_0(a,b)=E_gamma conjugate(P_a(Y)) P_b(Y),

one has ||G_g-G_0||<=C g. If the polynomials P_a are linearly independent,
G_0 is strictly positive: a nonzero polynomial cannot vanish almost
everywhere for a Gaussian with positive density on R^n. Hence for small g

        1/2 lambda_min(G_0) I<=G_g<=C I.              (6.4)

For centered references use P_a-E_gamma P_a; the analogous covariance
claim requires independence MODULO constants. All identities/contact
coefficients and centering constants must be retained. A zero leading
polynomial, a Bianchi relation, or another actual linear relation cannot
be removed by calling the formal labels independent.

7. EXACT GLOBAL GAUGE-INVARIANT REFERENCE FUNCTIONS

The finite reference family in Section6 need not be an arbitrary gauge-
coordinate family. Let P_a be any specified homogeneous polynomial in
the chord Lie-algebra coordinates invariant under simultaneous Ad(G).
Choose a smooth Ad-invariant cutoff chi supported in exp B_r and equal
to one on exp B_(r/2). Define exactly on X

 R_a(W)=chi(W) P_a(log W),                            (7.1)

extended by zero outside the chart. It is globally smooth because the
cutoff has compact support strictly inside the chart, and it has precisely
the prescribed leading polynomial. On the original link configuration,
each W_e is the based Wilson-loop holonomy associated with the tree and
that chord. Under the FULL gauge group these loops conjugate together at
the root. Therefore (7.1) is an exact gauge-invariant smooth function of
based loop holonomies, not merely an infinitesimal invariant.

The cutoff can be chosen from the invariant product norm of the logarithms.
Neither its support nor a product link-coordinate window is silently
declared gauge invariant before tree reduction; the globally defined
function (7.1) itself is invariant. The measure remains the FULL Wilson
measure; chi belongs only to the chosen observable.

This realizes every fixed independent invariant polynomial family as
finite global reference functions with the Gram bound (6.4). It does not
claim that these functions are already the desired continuum differential
polynomials, that their number is uniform in refinement, or that any
particular formal list of continuum fields has independent leading
polynomials on this graph. Those are separate matching questions.

8. WHY THIS IS A JOINT-LAW ATTACHMENT

For each fixed g>0, (4.1) is an actual probability law on all of X. Every
finite family of the global functions (7.1), and every product thereof,
is integrable and has its ordinary full joint moments under that same
law. Since each function is bounded for fixed g, the joint moment-
generating function is entire in finitely many source parameters.

Any genuinely defined measurable coarse map q on X supplies the actual
joint law (id,q)_*mu_g and its coarse marginal q_*mu_g. Conditional laws
are disintegrations of this joint law, rather than replacements for it.
There is no claim here that a locally defined block logarithm has already
been extended to a physical global q.

If such a q is smooth near 1_X, has q(1_X)=1, and in compatible local
coordinates obeys log q(exp x)=Qx+O(|x|^2), define Z_g=log q(W)/g when
q(W) lies in a chosen bounded coarse logarithm chart, and Z_g=0 otherwise.
This is only a bookkeeping random coordinate; neither q nor mu_g changes.
Continuity near 1_X makes the omitted event a subset of a fixed fine
chart complement, so it has an exponential bound. The same estimates give
Z_g-QY_g=O(g) in every fixed L^p and hence the finite joint Gaussian limit
(Y_g,Z_g)->(Y,QY), in distribution and in every fixed polynomial moment.
No joint TOTAL-VARIATION claim is made for a nonlinear q: its graph and
the limiting linear graph can support mutually singular joint measures.
If Q is surjective the limiting
coarse covariance Q H^(-1) Q^T is positive. This observation is limited
to a fixed, actually specified map and fixed graph. It supplies neither
an iterated source construction nor uniform control of q as the graph
is refined.

A concrete exact q is provided by compatible_tree_bare_block_map_turn13.txt:
embed a coarse graph by internally disjoint fine edge paths, lift its
spanning tree, remove one fine edge from each coarse-chord path, and extend
the remaining embedded tree to a fine spanning tree. In these compatible
quotients, each coarse chord holonomy is exactly the corresponding selected
fine chord variable. Thus q is a GLOBAL coordinate projection G^m->G^m_c,
and its logarithm is the surjective linear projection Q. The original full
Wilson density is then a genuine joint density in selected and unselected
variables. This supplies an explicit fixed-graph example of the preceding
joint-law statement, rather than an unspecified conditional fiber.

9. BOUNDARY, TOPOLOGY, AND REFINEMENT LIMITATIONS

(a) Free boundary means all graph links are integrated. If exterior links
are frozen and gauge transformations must be 1 on fixed vertices, the
rooted tree argument must preserve every frozen link and its holonomy.
It cannot gauge-transform frozen exterior data. With compatible identity
data on a connected fixed boundary subcomplex, the corresponding relative
flat problem can have the same uniqueness: H^1(K,B)=0 when K is simply
connected and B is connected. Disconnected frozen root components can
leave continuous relative flat transports. We have not included arbitrary
boundary data in the theorem.

(b) Small nonzero fixed boundary data would require a boundary-dependent
minimizer and tails centered there. Fixed nonzero boundary data cannot be
treated as lying in an identity-centered shrinking O(g) window. Frustrated
boundary holonomy may eliminate zero-action configurations altogether.

(c) A periodic box has harmonic/global holonomy modes. They must be retained
or separately fixed; (3.3) on the stated coordinates would fail. Faithful
rho eliminates invisible central plaquettes, not topological global modes.

(d) lambda_K, the chart radius, the action gap, and every moment/Gram
constant may deteriorate with K. Compactness alone has no uniform content
when the number of fine links grows. In particular path-holonomy blocking
is not a substitute for native volume averaging and its matched precision:
the compatible-tree construction just described permits choosing a coarse
chord whose path has M fine links. Put exp(theta H/M) on those links and
identity on all other links. Every other coarse edge remains identity;
a coarse plaquette containing the chord has nonidentity holonomy
exp(+/-theta H). At most 6M fine plaquettes change in four dimensions,
each by O(theta^2/M^2), so the total action is O(theta^2/M). This is a
gauge-invariant nonflat coarse datum, not a gauge-dependent open-path
claim. It is proved explicitly in Section7 of the compatible-tree note.
Thus fixed-graph Laplace bounds cannot establish uniform prior-refinement
coarse coercivity for this map.

(e) The argument is a bare positive Wilson-law theorem. A signed large-field
history is not this law, and a retained fluctuation integral at an already
fixed coarse value is not the joint law constructed here. Adding inherited
interactions, constraints, exact blocked action, or source-dependent chart
factors requires a new theorem controlling their WHOLE compact law.

10. WHAT HAS ACTUALLY BEEN ESTABLISHED

All statements above follow from exact finite Haar disintegration, the
faithful Wilson action, elementary cellular topology and finite-dimensional
Laplace estimates proved here. The proof does not import an unverified
Yang--Mills renormalization estimate. It supplies a complete fixed-graph
bare joint source law with controlled small-g local coordinates AND their
original compact complement. The substantive next attachment is to compare
an actual blocked joint law, its low reference matrix and its native source
norms uniformly along refinement; this theorem does not assert that step.
