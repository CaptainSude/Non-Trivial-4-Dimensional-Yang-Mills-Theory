AN EXACT NONLINEAR RADIAL-FLOW HESSIAN TEST -- TURN65
20 October 2026. Root derivation; independent audit required.

STATUS

This is a finite-dimensional deterministic theorem for the spatial
SU(2) Wilson flow used in TURN63--65. It is not a claim about the
probability of the tested configurations under the Wilson law.

The raw flow-map tangent growth found by gauge47 does not automatically
imply growth of the derivative of a radial magnetic observable: its
first derivative vanishes in the direction tested below. Nevertheless,
the actual radial observable has a growing SECOND derivative along a
unit, gauge-orthogonal initial tangent, on configurations with
beta h_p <= 1 for every plaquette.

Consequently a cutoff-uniform pointwise Hessian bound for this observable
cannot be obtained merely by imposing that microscopic energy condition.
An averaged, weighted, or more structured derivative estimate could
still hold in the original Wilson law. No failure of that estimate,
or of a Yang--Mills continuum limit, is asserted.

1. NORMALIZATION AND THE HOMOGENEOUS INVARIANT FAMILY

Take a cubic spatial torus with n vertices and spacing a. Every oriented
positive link has the Pauli/2 metric: i x.sigma/2 has squared norm |x|^2.
In quaternion coordinates q=(q0,qvec) on the unit S^3, this metric is
four times the round metric.

Let S_sp=sum of h_p over elementary spatial plaquettes, with
h_p=1-Tr U_p/2. Define physical flow time s by

                 dV_s/ds = -4 a^-2 grad S_sp(V_s).        (1.1)

Use homogeneous links

 U1=c I+i sqrt(w) n1.sigma,
 U2=c I+i sqrt(w) n2.sigma,  U3=I,
 c=sqrt(1-w),  |n1|=|n2|=1,  z=n1.n2.                   (1.2)

Then

                    h12=2 w^2(1-z^2), h13=h23=0.        (1.3)

Translation symmetry, interchange symmetry of the first two links,
and the gradient equations preserve this equal-amplitude family.
For clarity, each link's metric in alpha,n coordinates, w=sin^2(alpha/2),
is dalpha^2+4w|dn|^2. Differentiating S_sp=n h12 gives

 w_s=-8 a^-2 w^2(1-w)(1-z^2),
 z_s= 8 a^-2 w z(1-z^2).                                (1.4)

These equations also follow directly from the full link gradient:
the homogeneous restriction has the same factors n in its metric
and its action. The other links remain the identity.

Set

 r=w/(1-w),  I=z r,  u=I^2,  tau=8s/a^2.

The quantity I is constant along the flow, and

 dr/dtau=-(r^2-u)/(1+r),
 h12(s)=H(r,u):=2(r^2-u)/(1+r)^2.                        (1.5)

Initial r0>0 is held fixed when differentiating with respect to u.
For |I|<r0 and finite tau, the scalar equation is smooth, including
at u=0. On its decreasing solution,

             tau=int_r^r0 (1+x)/(x^2-u) dx.              (1.6)

The subsequent derivatives are derivatives of this finite-dimensional
equation, with all constants and metric factors retained.

2. EXACT SECOND RESPONSE TO RELATIVE AXIS ANGLE

At the orthogonal baseline z0=0, hence u=0, equation (1.6) becomes

             tau=1/r-1/r0+log(r0/r).                    (2.1)

Write r_u for the derivative of the solution at fixed tau,r0, evaluated
at u=0. Differentiating (1.6),

 r_u = r^2/(1+r) int_r^r0 (1+x)/x^4 dx
     = [1/(3r)+1/2-C0 r^2]/(1+r),
 C0=1/(3r0^3)+1/(2r0^2).                               (2.2)

Here and below a derivative of h12 includes the solution r(tau;r0,u);
it is not a partial derivative of H(r,u) at fixed r. Thus

 d h12/du
   = 4r r_u/(1+r)^3 - 2/(1+r)^2
   = [-2/3-2r-2r^2-4C0 r^3]/(1+r)^4.                  (2.3)

Similarly,

 d h12/dr0 = 4r^3(1+r0)/[r0^2(1+r)^4].                 (2.4)

If r -> 0 and r/r0 -> 0, with r0 bounded, then

              d h12/du -> -2/3.                        (2.5)

Indeed C0 r^3=(r/r0)^3/3+(r/r0)^3 r0/2 ->0.
This response is strictly negative. There is no cancellation that
would follow merely from the vanishing of the first radial variation.

3. A GENUINE GEODESIC, RATHER THAN AN AXIS PARAMETRIZATION

One must distinguish a second derivative on a curved coordinate
path from a Riemannian Hessian. Use the following exact product
geodesic through the baseline n1=e1,n2=e2. At every spatial site,
in quaternion coordinates,

 q1(epsilon)=(c,sqrt(w0)e1) cos(sqrt(w0)epsilon)
                                +(0,e2)sin(sqrt(w0)epsilon),
 q2(epsilon)=(c,sqrt(w0)e2) cos(sqrt(w0)epsilon)
                                +(0,e1)sin(sqrt(w0)epsilon),
 q3(epsilon)=(1,0),  c=sqrt(1-w0).                      (3.1)

The summands in each expression are orthogonal unit vectors.
Thus (3.1) is a constant-speed great circle on each S^3 factor,
and hence a geodesic for the Pauli/2 metric too. Its initial full
spatial norm is

                         |J|^2=8n w0.                  (3.2)

Along this initial geodesic the amplitudes stay equal, so the exact
flow reduction above applies for every sufficiently small epsilon.
Its initial quantities obey

 w0(epsilon)=w0+w0(1-w0)epsilon^2+O(epsilon^4),
 r0'(0)=0, r0''(0)=2r0,
 z0(0)=0, z0'(0)=2,
 u'(0)=0, u''(0)=8r0^2.                                (3.3)

Therefore the ordinary second derivative along (3.1) is exactly the
Riemannian Hessian in direction J, and

 Hess(h12 circ V_s)(J,J)
        =2r0 (d h12/dr0)+8r0^2(d h12/du).                (3.4)

All first derivatives in this direction vanish.
If r/r0 ->0 and r->0, equations (2.3)--(2.4) imply

 Hess(h12 circ V_s)(J,J) = -(16/3)r0^2[1+o(1)].          (3.5)

The relative error here follows because
2r0(d h12/dr0)/r0^2=8(r/r0)^3(1+r0)/(1+r)^4 ->0.

The direction is physical in the elementary gauge-quotient sense.
With left-trivialization J_i=U_i' U_i^-1, its matrices are

 J1=i sqrt(w0(1-w0))sigma2-i w0 sigma3,
 J2=i sqrt(w0(1-w0))sigma1+i w0 sigma3,  J3=0.            (3.6)

At every vertex the adjoint of the infinitesimal gauge action is
sum_i(J_i-U_i^-1 J_i U_i). The first two contributions are
-2i w0 sigma3 and +2i w0 sigma3, respectively, so their sum is zero.
Thus J is orthogonal to all local periodic gauge directions.
No claim that the full nonlinear quotient is nonsingular is needed.

4. THE ACTUAL BOUNDED RADIAL MAGNETIC OBSERVABLE

Write the flowed plaquette as P12^s=p0 I+i p.sigma. Then
|p|^2=1-p0^2=2h12-h12^2 exactly. For fixed g_*>0 and alpha>0 use
the same type of bounded physical probe proposed in TURN63:

 F_s,a(U)=exp[-alpha |2p12(V_s;x)/(g_* a^2)|^2]
        =exp[-4alpha(2h12-h12^2)/(g_*^2 a^4)].            (4.1)

It is a smooth gauge-invariant function at finite cutoff, with values
in (0,1]. The chosen point x is immaterial on the homogeneous family.
For the unit initial tangent j=J/sqrt(8n w0), (3.4) gives the exact
Hessian formula

 Hess F_s,a(j,j)
   = -[8alpha(1-h12)/(g_*^2 a^4)] F_s,a
       [2r0(d h12/dr0)+8r0^2(d h12/du)]/(8n w0).         (4.2)

There is no squared-first-derivative term: that derivative is zero.

Take a sequence of cubic tori of fixed physical side L, so n=(L/a)^3.
Fix s>0 and let beta(a)->infinity with beta(a)=o(a^-2). Choose

                w0=(2beta)^-1/2, r0=w0/(1-w0).           (4.3)

Every initial spatial 12-plaquette has beta h12=1, and every other
initial spatial plaquette has h=0. With constant temporal links I
and the same spatial configuration on every time slice, the same
statement holds for every four-dimensional plaquette.

The condition on beta ensures tau r0 ->infinity. Equation (2.1)
then implies r tau ->1 and r/r0 ->0. In particular,

 h12(s)/a^4 -> 1/(32s^2),
 F_s,a -> exp[-alpha/(4g_*^2s^2)].                       (4.4)

Using r0^2/w0=w0[1+o(1)] in (4.2), obtain

 Hess F_s,a(j,j)
  = [16alpha/(3g_*^2 L^3)]
       exp[-alpha/(4g_*^2s^2)] (w0/a)[1+o(1)]
  = [16alpha/(3sqrt(2)g_*^2 L^3)]
       exp[-alpha/(4g_*^2s^2)]
          (a sqrt(beta))^-1[1+o(1)] -> +infinity.        (4.5)

The baseline expectation is not involved in this calculation.
In particular logarithmic beta growth meets the displayed conditions,
but no running-coupling theorem or identification of beta(a) is assumed.

The limit r tau ->1 can be checked directly from (2.1): with y=tau r,
the equation is 1=1/y-1/(tau r0)+tau^-1 log(tau r0/y).
Since r<=r0 and r0 is bounded, the logarithmic contribution tends
to zero; the same equation bounds y away from zero and infinity.
Alternatively integrate r_tau=-r^2/(1+r), then bound 1+r between
1 and 1+r0, and use r0->0 to squeeze tau r to one.

5. EXACT LIMITS OF THE COUNTEREXAMPLE

This disproves any deterministic bound

 sup_(all configurations with every beta h_p<=1)
                   ||Hess F_s,a||op <= C(s,L,g_*,alpha)

uniformly along the displayed sequences. The direction is a unit
vector in the complete spatial product metric and is gauge orthogonal.
It is not a pure gauge artifact, nor the Hessian of a coordinate path
whose connection correction was omitted.

It also shows why the original-law plaquette exponential estimate,
viewed ALONE as information about a probability measure, is insufficient
to establish such an averaged supremum-Hessian bound. Put a point mass
on the four-dimensional homogeneous configuration and, if desired,
average over all site gauge transformations. For any finite set B and
0<=s_p<1/2,

 exp[sum_B s_p beta h_p] <= exp[sum_B s_p]
                        <= product_B(1-2s_p)^(-3/4),     (5.1)

because -(3/4)log(1-2s)>=3s/2>=s. Thus this family obeys the entire
TURN64 energy-moment inequality, while the observable's Hessian
operator norm grows as in (4.5). Gauge averaging preserves the energy
and the gauge-invariant Hessian operator norm.

This auxiliary measure is NOT the Wilson measure. It is not asserted
to be reflection positive or to obey the Wilson conditional link law.
There is consequently no claim that the averaged Hessian in the
actual Wilson law diverges.

Nor does (4.5) disprove a bound on a weighted diagonal trace, a
second-variation contraction against a particular innovation
covariance, a fixed single-link derivative, or the actual temporal
increment d=E(F1-F0)^2/2. The tested direction is a coherent spatial
variation. Those more structured quantities may have cancellations
or may suppress this geometry probabilistically.

The useful conclusion is narrower: the proposed physical radial
observable cannot be attached to the earlier affine squared-influence
argument simply by claiming that its entire nonlinear Hessian is
uniformly bounded on configurations with bounded beta h.

An actual-law derivative estimate must use additional Wilson
structure, multiscale restrictions stronger than plaquette smallness,
or a consumer that does not require this false uniform Hessian bound.
The complete nontrivial YM4 existence proof remains unproved.
