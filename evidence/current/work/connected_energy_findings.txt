1. SETUP AND EXACT ENERGY IDENTITY

Let E_b,...,E_T be the actual complete opened stage spaces. Let F_e be
a normalized positive kernel from E_(e+1) to E_e with

                    mu_(e+1) F_e = mu_e.                    (1)

All mu are probabilities. Every row below is in the appropriate
L2(mu_e); products are therefore integrable. Rows may be complex.
Define bilinear covariance and Hermitian variance separately:

 Gamma_e(u,v) = F_e(uv) - (F_e u)(F_e v),
 V_e(u) = F_e|u|^2 - |F_e u|^2,
 sigma_e(u)^2 = mu_e |u-mu_e u|^2.                         (2)

The covariance in Gamma has NO complex conjugation, matching the
source-jet convention. Positivity implies

        |Gamma_e(u,v)| <= sqrt(V_e(u) V_e(v)).              (3)

For an arbitrary stage sequence v=(v_b,...,v_T), not necessarily a
conditional-mean sequence, define the spacetime energy seminorm

 ||v||_E^2 = sum_(e=b)^(T-1) mu_(e+1) V_e(v_e)
                                     + sigma_T(v_T)^2.    (4)

This is an actual Hilbert seminorm: map v to the tuple of conditional
fluctuations v_e(x)-F_e v_e(z) in
L2(mu_(e+1)(dz)F_e(z,dx)), together with v_T-mu_T v_T.
Its norm is (4), so Minkowski applies. No global path-space or
conditional-independence construction is needed.

If v_(e+1)=F_e v_e, the one-step variance decomposition is

 sigma_e(v_e)^2 = mu_(e+1)V_e(v_e)+sigma_(e+1)(v_(e+1))^2.

It telescopes to the exact identity

                        ||v||_E = sigma_b(v_b).            (5)

Thus the total energy includes the terminal variance; it is not lost
when the conditional variances telescope. Identity (5) uses actual
matching marginals. A hybrid/neutral suffix does not inherit (5) from
mere absolute continuity or positivity.

2. ONE EMISSION IS PAID ONCE THROUGH EVERY LATER COVARIANCE

Let r_a be emitted at stage a, b<=a<=T. Set r_e=0 for e<a and
r_(e+1)=F_e r_e for e>=a. Applying (5) to the tail gives

                         ||r||_E = sigma_a(r_a).           (6)

For any sequence v, (3), conditional integration, and Cauchy--Schwarz
over the finite direct sum give

 sum_e mu_(e+1)|Gamma_e(r_e,v_e)|
                + |Cov_(mu_T)(r_T,v_T)| <= ||r||_E ||v||_E. (7)

In particular, when g is an actual conditional-mean sequence,

 sum_(e=a)^(T-1) mu_(e+1)|Gamma_e(r_e,g_e)|
        + |Cov_(mu_T)(r_T,g_T)|
              <= sigma_a(r_a) sigma_a(g_a)
              <= sigma_a(r_a) sigma_b(g_b).                (8)

The first inequality uses only the tail of g's energy, hence is
slightly sharper than applying (7) with its full norm. This bound
controls the integrated ABSOLUTE conditional covariance, not merely
the absolute value of its signed integral. Its coefficient 1 is
sharp: take r=g, real. Every conditional variance and terminal
variance is nonnegative, and equality is (5).

If |r_a|<=b_a on actual support, then sigma_a(r_a)<=b_a. The factor is
b_a, not 2 b_a. For a real row known to lie in a deterministic interval
of width w, sigma<=w/2. More generally |r_a|<=A and mu_a|r_a|<=eta
give sigma_a(r_a)<=sqrt(A eta). The latter spends a square-root rarity
gain. Constants, even large constants, have zero energy; centering
before estimating can be materially better than an uncentered cap.

3. ARBITRARY MOVING REFERENCE AND ALL MIXED REMAINDERS

Let f_(e+1)=F_e f_e and g_(e+1)=F_e g_e be the actual source means.
Choose any reference sequences s_e,t_e defined on the SAME complete
actual stage states. They need not satisfy a source propagation rule.
Set

 r_e=f_e-s_e,       u_e=g_e-t_e,
 delta_e=F_e s_e-s_(e+1),
 eta_e=F_e t_e-t_(e+1).                                  (9)

Then exactly

 r_(e+1)=F_e r_e+delta_e,
 u_(e+1)=F_e u_e+eta_e.                                  (10)

For a>=b define P_(e:a)=F_(e-1)...F_a for e>a and identity for e=a.
The discrete variation-of-constants formula is

 r_e=P_(e:b)r_b + sum_(j=b)^(e-1)P_(e:j+1)delta_j.        (11)

Each summand is viewed as zero before its own emission. Equations
(6) and Minkowski give

 ||r||_E <= B_f := sigma_b(r_b)
                       + sum_(j=b)^(T-1)sigma_(j+1)(delta_j),
 ||u||_E <= B_g := sigma_b(u_b)
                       + sum_(j=b)^(T-1)sigma_(j+1)(eta_j). (12)

For every e, exact bilinearity gives

 Gamma_e(f_e,g_e)-Gamma_e(s_e,t_e)
   =Gamma_e(r_e,g_e)+Gamma_e(f_e,u_e)-Gamma_e(r_e,u_e).    (13)

The same identity holds for the terminal covariance. Applying (7)
to each term and (5) to f,g proves

 sum_e mu_(e+1)|Gamma_e(f_e,g_e)-Gamma_e(s_e,t_e)|
 + |Cov_(mu_T)(f_T,g_T)-Cov_(mu_T)(s_T,t_T)|
 <= B_f sigma_b(g_b)+sigma_b(f_b) B_g+B_f B_g.             (14)

This directly estimates every mixed remainder created by the exact
moving-reference decomposition, including remainder/remainder, and
the terminal mismatch. It has no extra number-of-reset factor.
At finite cutoff (14) is valid even when its right side is large.

With

 Q(s,t)=sum_e mu_(e+1)Gamma_e(s_e,t_e)
                              +Cov_(mu_T)(s_T,t_T),

the actual covariance Cov_(mu_b)(f_b,g_b)=Q(f,g) differs from Q(s,t)
by at most the right side of (14). Q(s,t) is a comparison functional,
not automatically the covariance of a separately propagated physical
source. In particular its terminal term remains part of the target.

Qualified near/near estimates can bound Gamma_e(s_e,t_e) only for
reference summands actually satisfying their hypotheses on that
opened stage. A selector depending on a branch being integrated, or
an inserted survival-history event, is not silently qualified by (9).
Record changes, mismatched reads and all ungated complements remain
in the exact reference choice or in its residual/injections.

4. SUMMING INJECTIONS: WHAT A VARIANCE BUDGET REALLY MEANS

The sufficient budget in (12) is the sum of standard deviations,
not the square root of the sum of variances. The latter needs extra
orthogonality in the direct-sum Hilbert space in Section 1.

Counterexample to an automatic squared budget: let the stage law be
uniform X in {-1,+1}, with identity kernels for n dates and a final
averaging kernel to a one-point state. Emit delta_j=X/sqrt(n) at each
of the n prefinal dates. Then sum_j Var(delta_j)=1. All emitted tails
are coherent, so the accumulated row at the final averaging step is
sqrt(n)X. Its energy is sqrt(n), and its covariance with g=X at that
step is sqrt(n). Thus no bound independent of n follows from the
sum of emission variances alone. This is an abstract budget
counterexample, not a lower bound for the YM model.

If actual energy images of the emissions are pairwise orthogonal,
Pythagoras does permit the squared budget. Such orthogonality must be
proved for the emitted tails, including their terminal components;
centering each emission separately does not prove it. Cancellations
may alternatively be retained by estimating the combined residual
energy directly before applying (14).

For two collections of emissions delta_j and eta_k, the same argument
also gives the pairwise tail bound

 sum_e mu_(e+1)|Gamma_e(r_e,u_e)|+|Cov_T(r_T,u_T)|
      <= (sum_j sigma_(j+1)(delta_j))
                         (sum_k sigma_(k+1)(eta_k)),       (15)

when initial residuals are zero. The more precise bound for a fixed
pair starts at max(j+1,k+1), using their actually propagated variances
there. Neither formulation supplies source-position decay.

6. CONTACTS, CLOSING STAGES AND ENDPOINTS

For an actual logarithmic jet with independent mixed row h, the exact
update remains

                 h_(e+1)=F_e h_e+Gamma_e(f_e,g_e).        (20)

Thus its final logarithmic coefficient equals

 mu_T h_T+Cov_T(f_T,g_T)
                   =mu_b h_b+Cov_(mu_b)(f_b,g_b).         (21)

Energy controls the covariance-generated part and its comparison via
(14); it does not estimate the independent initial contact mu_b h_b.
If additional independent mixed contacts k_e are emitted at stage
e+1, replace (20) by h_next=Fh+Gamma+k_e. Equation (21) then acquires
sum_e mu_(e+1)k_e on its right. A sufficient absolute contact budget
is mu_b|h_b|+sum_e mu_(e+1)|k_e|, separately from (12).

Equivalently, propagate a reference contact with the same actual F
but reference covariance Gamma_e(s_e,t_e). The integrated difference
of its generated contacts is bounded by the first term on the left
of (14), plus any independently mismatched contact budget; terminal
covariance comparison is the second term on that left. This is
comparison bookkeeping, not a new exact physical first-source jet
unless its injection corrections are also included.

Every later Y-closing or q-closing kernel must be included as another
actual stage in (1)--(4). The energy lemma holds after such an exact
refinement, but an opened near-source estimate does not automatically
bound the reference covariance at those closing stages. Similarly,
(14) estimates the terminal mismatch, not the unknown reference
terminal covariance itself. It cannot be dropped by naming it a
contact or a scalar remainder.

7. PRECISE ADVANCE AND REMAINING GATES

This finite result removes a needless cost proportional to the number
of later resets when a bounded or L2-budgeted source remainder has
already been emitted. It supplies an exact consumer for mixed
remainder terms, and a definite fixed-record capture budget (19).
It also identifies a weaker possible next target: centered actual-law
L2 summability of the moving-reference emissions, instead of proving
that every compiled remainder is a new local static source.

It does NOT produce: smallness of surviving compiled defects;
summable reset recenter/read-switch budgets; a valid fresh-near
representation of all references; spatial decay for their remainder
crosses; summability over calibrated original roots/preparations;
calibration-uniform source variances; bounds on much older proper
layers or independent contacts; reference closing/terminal covariance
control; continuum/volume limits, reconstruction or interaction.

In particular a small fixed-record bound without separation decay
cannot replace the spatial census needed for the continuum theory.
All source/date sums and original A_R calibrations must remain visible.
