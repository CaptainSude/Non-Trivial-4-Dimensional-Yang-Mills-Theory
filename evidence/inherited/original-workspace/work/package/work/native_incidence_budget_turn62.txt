The decay-exponent requirement exposed in TURN61 is compatible with
the primary parameter hierarchy. Kappa is not a universal small
physical mass: the native theorem permits an arbitrarily large
FIXED localization exponent, with the localization block size M
chosen afterward and the weak-coupling floor chosen last.

This does not mean one may increase kappa while keeping an already
fixed construction, its M, or its analytic domains unchanged.
Nor does small alpha compensate an insufficient exponential decay
rate. Both distinctions are essential.

There are two quantitative deductions:

 (A) On the cubic touching graph the TURN61 counting and cube

     O(R_k^d) M-cells. Its geometric candidate entropy and the

     The actual raw mass exp[-a0 p0(g_k)] pays them when the
     threshold exponent p exceeds d r, with R_k of order
     (log g_k^-2)^r. This is a numerical budget for the actual
     raw mass, not yet the unchanged-measure/removal identity.

2. SHARPER CUBIC GRAPH COUNT

Use the touching graph of a periodic or finite rectangular
cubic grid in dimension d. Its degree is at most

                 D=3^d-1.

Let Z be fixed and let admissible C include every component
of Z it meets whole, with R=C\Z nonempty. The graph metric
d_Z(C) is the minimum number of edges of an ordinary tree
inside C spanning R, exactly as in TURN61.

There are two improved finite constants.

2A. At most J=2^d distinct retained components neighbor any
one outside cube.

The neighboring cube offsets lie in{-1,0,1}^d. Partition
these offsets into2^d sets by choosing, coordinatewise,
either{-1,0} or{1}. Each resulting set is a clique for
touching adjacency. Two vertices from different connected
components of Z cannot lie in the same clique. The central
cube, if included in the offset partition, is outside Z
and is ignored. Thus at most J distinct components meet
the neighbors of a fixed outside vertex.

Consequently at most J|R| components can neighbor R.

2B. The number of embedded rooted trees with m edges is
at most(4D)^m.

Order each vertex's neighbors once. Encode a tree by the
rooted ordered tree shape from its ordered depth-first
traversal and by the ambient neighbor index for each
downward edge. There are at most4^m rooted ordered shapes
and D^m downward edge choices. Invalid embeddings only
overcount. Return edges are already determined.

Choose R among the m+1 tree vertices and choose the
included retained components among its at most J|R|
neighbors. The same injective reconstruction as TURN61 gives

 #{C: x in C\Z, d_Z(C)=m} <= B A^m,                      (2.1)
 B=2^(J+1),             A=4D B.

Therefore, for h>log A,

 sum_(C: x in C\Z) exp[-h d_Z(C)]
                  <= K_graph(h):=B/(1-A exp(-h)).       (2.2)

For a fixed component X, the incident bound remains
K_graph(h)|partial_out X|. The improved J also implies
that at most J|R| retained components need encoding; it
does not make the boundary of a distinguished large X
uniformly bounded.

2C. Native face connectivity and the touching-tree auxiliary.

An additional primary check is necessary here. B109 printed
p257 defines localization domains by chains of cubes sharing
a (d-1)-dimensional face. This is stronger than connectivity
in the touching graph. The geometric proof above is stated
first in the single touching graph; its native use must not
silently redefine components.

Use two graphs on the same M-cubes. Let G_f be the face graph,
of degree D_f=2d, and G_t the touching graph, of degree D_t=3^d-1.
Assume C is G_f-connected and includes whole G_f-components
of Z. Choose the minimizing tree spanning R inside C using
G_t edges, as supplied by the continuous-tree conversion.
Every included G_f-component of Z must meet R across a FACE:
take a G_f path in C from that component to R; different
G_f-components of Z cannot be directly face-adjacent.
There are therefore at most D_f|R| candidate components.

The ordered-tree count is still(4D_t)^m. The reconstruction
from R and the chosen whole G_f-components is unchanged.
The resulting bound has B_f=2^(D_f+1), A_f=4D_t B_f.
Since D_f<=2^d for every d>=1, the larger B,A in(2.1)
remain valid without change. A distinguished retained X
has a FACE boundary root, which is also a touching boundary
root, so the earlier boundary bound remains safe.

Thus Sections3--6 may use exactly the displayed conservative
constants for either the single-graph touching convention,
or the source face-connected convention with this explicit
two-graph proof. No assertion equating the two component
decompositions is needed. If a different raw-component
convention is used, its whole-component and adjacency
dictionary must still be identified.

3. SHARPER CONTINUOUS-TREE CONVERSION

Let ell be the length of a finite rectifiable tree inside
the domain which meets every required outside M-cube.
Its depth-first traversal has length2ell.

For any r<M/2, samples at traversal spacing r give at most
1+2ell/r balls of radius r covering the tree. Every such
ball meets at most2^d closed grid cubes: its projection on
each coordinate axis has length strictly smaller than M.
The C-cubes meeting the tree form a connected touching graph
and include every outside cube. Hence

 d_Z(C) <= J(1+2ell/r)-1.

Let r increase to M/2 and then take the infimum over trees.
With d_cont=ell_min/M, this proves

 d_Z(C) <= (J-1)+c_d d_cont(C),       c_d=4J.             (3.1)

The argument for a possible nonattained infimum uses
approximating trees. Periodic grids use periodic touching
adjacency; repeated offset representatives only decrease
the count.

For any nu>0,

 exp[-nu d_cont(C)]
   <=exp[nu(J-1)/c_d] exp[-(nu/c_d)d_Z(C)].              (3.2)

Thus a sufficient continuum relative-exponent condition is

                         nu>c_d log A.                 (3.3)

A corresponding explicit incident constant is

 K_cont(nu)=exp[nu(J-1)/c_d] K_graph(nu/c_d).             (3.4)

These constants are deliberately not optimized. They are
finite and independent of volume and retained-component size.

For d=4, D=80, J=16, c_d=64,
 B=131072, A=41943040.
Thus c_d log A is approximately1123.3. The older safe
conversion required4*3^4 log(80^2*2^81), about21030.
The numerical improvement is useful, but the fundamental
point is that BOTH are fixed thresholds.

For example, a fixed native kappa>=2048 already exceeds
the sharpened threshold when nu=kappa; one must also meet
the source's other lower thresholds. Its actual noninterior
bound has nu=(1+2 beta)kappa, so discarding that extra factor
is safe for this estimate.

If kappa is prescribed below the threshold, small alpha
does not make the geometric series converge. No such
compensation is asserted.
