1. Reflection and centering

For A_t=exp(itX_+)-m(t), the antilinear reflection gives
Theta A_t=exp(-itX_-)-conjugate(m(t)).
Reflection invariance gives E exp(-itX_-)=conjugate(m(t)).
Expansion therefore gives exactly
  E[(Theta A_t)A_t]=q(t)-|m(t)|^2.
The constant-centered vector is orthogonal to the vacuum vector.
Reflection positivity makes the displayed number real and nonnegative.

2. Gaussian witness and comparison margin

For equal variance v and covariance c,
q(t)=exp[-t^2(v-c)] and |m(t)|^2=exp[-t^2 v].
At t^2=1/v their difference is e^-1(e^(c/v)-1)>=c/(ev).
The errors in the two specified characteristic functions give
  |q_actual-q_G| + ||m_actual|^2-|m_G|^2| <= 3 epsilon,
since both one-point functions have modulus at most one.
Thus epsilon<=r/(6e), r=c/v>0, gives the claimed r/(2e) margin.
No differentiation in the source is used.

3. Maximal-correlation bound

The Hermite calculation is valid for complex f:
  centered reflected form = sum_(n>=1) |a_n|^2 r^n.
For 0<=r<1 this lies between zero and
  r sum_(n>=1)|a_n|^2 = r Var(f).
The extension from polynomials to L2 follows by completeness and
Cauchy–Schwarz for the cross expectation. For |f|<=1, Var(f)<=1.
The bound remains valid for cutoff-dependent bounded f.
Its coefficient r is sharp on the full L2 class (the first Hermite
mode); no assertion of sharp attainment within |f|<=1 is needed.

also follow directly. The example concerns one-probe witnesses; it
