RESULT

There is a genuine volume-uniform weak-interaction extension of the
Gaussian product-ball covariance comparison: globally small bounded
local Mayer activities permit a connected expansion in which every
full-minus-selected coefficient has one exponentially small Gaussian
tail insertion. Section2 proves this for a bounded-degree interaction
graph and quadratic marks on finitely many coordinate blocks.

The actual TURN48 selected interaction does NOT presently satisfy that
global hypothesis. Its analytic remainder, Jacobian and inherited input
are admitted on the weak chart and on the product-ball Gaussian domain.
The bound C(g T^3+epsilon gT/b) is not an untruncated Gaussian norm.

This distinction cannot be bypassed using only local analyticity and
partition stability. Section4 gives a compact positive analytic model
with a bounded Gaussian-normalized partition function, the required
small local even interaction, and an order-one covariance correction
coming from distant wells. The source in that example is a fixed
bounded quadratic observable, not the total classical action.

There is also a positive actual compact-group check. For a single SU(2)
Wilson plaquette, global radial action coercivity proves an exponential
full-minus-selected bound for quadratic-growth observables, including
the normalized plaquette action. This does not supply the corresponding
coupled constrained-fiber theorem.

2. A PROVED WEAK-INTERACTION EXTENSION WITH COMPLETE DENOMINATORS

2A. Precise theorem

Let G=(I,E) be a finite graph of maximum degree D. At each vertex put
xi_i in R^q with standard Gaussian probability gamma. Let

 gamma_T = gamma(. | |xi|<=T),  c_T=gamma(|xi|<=T),  T>=1.

For every edge e={i,j} let V_e(xi_i,xi_j) be real and measurable. Suppose
the GLOBAL Mayer activities satisfy

       sup_(R^q x R^q)|exp V_e-1| <= epsilon.          (2.1)

Fix k0 and kappa>0. There is epsilon_0(D,q,k0,kappa)>0 such that the
following holds when epsilon<=epsilon_0. Define the actual probabilities

 dmu_inf=Z_inf^-1 exp[sum_e V_e] product_i dgamma_i,
 dmu_T  =Z_T^-1   exp[sum_e V_e] product_i dgamma_(T,i). (2.2)

Thus mu_T is exactly mu_inf conditioned on ALL |xi_i|<=T. In particular

 P_mu_inf(all good)=c_T^|I| Z_T/Z_inf,                 (2.3)

which need not be near one. No global lower bound on this probability
is assumed or used.

Let A,B be sets of at most k0 sites. Let F_A,H_B be quadratic
polynomials in their respective coordinate blocks, with

 |F_A(xi)|<=M_F(1+sum_(i in A)|xi_i|^2),
 |H_B(xi)|<=M_H(1+sum_(i in B)|xi_i|^2).              (2.4)

The same proof works for measurable marks satisfying these growth
bounds and admitting the stated complex exponential integrals.
Then uniformly in the graph volume,

 |Cov_mu_inf(F_A,H_B)-Cov_mu_T(F_A,H_B)|
   <=C M_F M_H exp[-c T^2] exp[-kappa dist_G(A,B)].    (2.5)

When A and B lie in different interaction components, the left side
is zero. When A intersects B, take distance zero. Constants depend
on D,q,k0,kappa and the fixed smallness margin, not on |I|.

This is an all-orders interacting statement. It is not obtained by
bounding Z_inf^-1 and Z_T^-1 separately by extensive exponentials.
It does require the much stronger global input (2.1).

2B. A small signed base-measure derivative

Interpolate the ONE-SITE probability by

                gamma_s=(1-s)gamma_T+s gamma,  0<=s<=1.

Let nu=gamma-gamma_T. For every fixed 0<eta<1/2,

 integral exp(eta|xi|^2) |nu|(dxi) <=C_(q,eta)e^(-c_eta T^2).
                                                               (2.6)

Indeed gamma-gamma_T equals gamma on the complement of the ball and
equals -(1-c_T)gamma_T on the ball. Its weighted variation is bounded
by the corresponding Gaussian weighted tail plus
(1-c_T) E_gamma_T exp(eta|xi|^2). Since T>=1, c_T is bounded below
by c_1>0. Both terms have the asserted Gaussian tail estimate.
Polynomial factors can be absorbed by decreasing c_eta.

For a union of at most 2k0 sites the derivative of the product gamma_s
is a sum of at most 2k0 products with one nu factor. Thus (2.6)
also controls that derivative, including weights exp(eta sum|xi|^2).

Normalize the marks by their M_F,M_H and introduce complex sources
z,w on a fixed disc |z|,|w|<=r0, where r0 depends only on q,k0.
The bound (2.4) and Gaussian exponential moments allow r0 to be
chosen so that the local source partition factor differs from one
by less than 1/4, uniformly in s and T. Consequently its normalized
complex measure has uniformly bounded variation, and its s derivative
has variation at most

                         C exp[-c T^2].              (2.7)

For disjoint A,B, make them two source blocks. For overlapping A,B,
use their union as one block. Other vertices remain singleton blocks.
Contracting these source blocks gives a graph of degree at most a
fixed D'=2k0 D, allowing parallel edges. The bounded interactions
internal to a source block can be included in its local measure:
because epsilon<1/2, |V_e|<=2epsilon, and their number is bounded
in terms of D,k0. This changes only the fixed constants and retains
(2.7) after further decreasing epsilon_0,r0.

All local normalization factors are retained. For disjoint A,B their
logarithms depend on at most one of z,w and hence have zero mixed
derivative. If A and B overlap, their local mixed derivative difference
is O(exp[-cT^2]) by (2.6)--(2.7) and Cauchy.

2C. Connected expansion and its uniform majorant

For the remaining edges write w_e=exp V_e-1. Expanding the finite
product product_e(1+w_e) gives an exact hard-core polymer gas:
a polymer X is the vertex support of a connected nonempty edge set,
with activity

 zeta_s(X;z,w)
  =sum_(F connected, V(F)=X)
       integral product_(e in F)w_e product_(i in X)dnu_(i,s;z,w).
                                                               (2.8)

Here the nu_i are the just-normalized local complex measures.
They have variation bounded by a fixed L; away from source blocks
their variation is one. All logarithms of source-block partition
factors have already been retained separately. The polymer gas
partition function multiplies their product, so this is the exact
interacting source integral, not a replacement denominator.

A simultaneous source/s majorant is

 a(X)=L^|X| sum_(F connected, V(F)=X) epsilon^|F|,
 |zeta_s(X)|<=a(X),
 |partial_s zeta_s(X)|<=C e^(-cT^2)|X| a(X).          (2.9)

The derivative statement follows by differentiating one normalized
local measure at a time and applying (2.6)--(2.7). Internal source-block
normalization derivatives are included in that bound.

For completeness, the needed smallness follows directly from graph
counting. A connected edge set on k vertices has a spanning tree.
After choosing one, summing all additional edges costs at most
(1+epsilon)^(D' k/2). The number of rooted tree embeddings with
k vertices is at most (4D')^(k-1), by ordered rooted tree shapes and
at most D' choices for every edge. Thus, with
H=L exp(a)(1+epsilon)^(D'/2),

 sup_i sum_(X contains i) exp(a|X|)a(X)
      <= H sum_(k>=2)(4D' epsilon H)^(k-1).           (2.10)

Multiple edges are counted with their labels. Taking
a=kappa+4 and then epsilon_0 sufficiently small makes the right
side, for example, less than 1/10. No volume appears.

The hard-core connected-log expansion is

 log Xi_s
  =sum_(m>=1)1/m! sum_(X_1,...,X_m)
         phi^T(X_1,...,X_m) product_l zeta_s(X_l),     (2.11)

where phi^T is the alternating sum of connected incompatibility graphs.
Its absolute value is bounded by the number of labelled spanning trees
whose edges join intersecting polymers. One proof partitions connected
graphs into intervals indexed by a rooted spanning tree: the alternating
sum on each interval is zero or has absolute value one.

Here is the rooted convergence estimate used below. If
sup_i sum_(X contains i)a(X) exp[(kappa+2)|X|]<=1,
then at a fixed root polymer X the tree sum of descendants, with
weights a(Y)exp[(kappa+1)|Y|], is at most exp|X|.
To see this, truncate the possible number of descendants. If the
subtree factor at Y is at most exp|Y|, summing the unordered children
at X is bounded by

 exp[sum_(Y intersects X) a(Y)exp[(kappa+2)|Y|]]
                                                 <=exp|X|.

Induction starts at leaves; monotone convergence removes the truncation.
Rooting an entire cluster at a polymer containing a prescribed site
then bounds the cluster sum uniformly. The extra cardinal reserves
in (2.10) also pay the factor sum_l |X_l| in (2.9).
This proves absolute convergence of (2.11), its source derivatives,
and its s derivative on the fixed smaller source discs. It also
establishes the required nonzero local analytic branch of Xi_s.

2D. The difference has a tail insertion and connects both marks

Differentiate (2.11) in s. By (2.9), each term has ONE differentiated
local measure and is bounded by C e^(-cT^2) times the common absolute
cluster majorant, with the cardinal factor already paid.

For the mixed source derivative, only clusters whose union meets
BOTH source blocks contribute. Every such union is connected in the
contracted interaction graph. For disjoint source blocks it therefore
has total polymer cardinality at least dist_G(A,B)+1. The exponential
reserve extracts exp[-kappa dist_G(A,B)]. Root at one source block;
there are at most k0 original roots, already included in C.

Integrating s from zero to one and applying Cauchy in z,w gives
C e^(-cT^2)e^(-kappa dist(A,B)) for the normalized marks.
Restoring M_F M_H proves (2.5). The overlap case includes its local
source-block normalization term as described in Section2B.

Finally,
partial_z partial_w log E_mu exp(zF+wH)|_(0,0)=Cov_mu(F,H).
Thus the exact logarithms estimated above really give the covariance
difference in (2.5). The factor c_T^|I| in (2.3) is source independent
and cancels only at this derivative; the source-dependent interacting
partition functions were never removed.

For marks written as absolutely summable sums of finite-read quadratic
pieces, (2.5) can be summed when the corresponding marked support norms
are available. A nonlocal physical pullback requires that support
estimate; replacing its coefficient matrices by unrelated local marks
would not be valid. The Gaussian companion proves its own exact
projection/whitening support bounds. The present theorem does not
silently extend (2.1) to all-arity unbounded interactions.

3. WHY THE ACTUAL TURN48 HYPOTHESES DO NOT SUPPLY SECTION2

The selected local construction has three separate missing inputs for
the untruncated replacement:

 (i) a definition of the complete same-source phase, Jacobian and
     inherited amplitude for all Gaussian coordinates used;
 (ii) identification of that enlarged integral with the actual omitted
      portion of the original constrained compact gauge fiber;
 (iii) a global weighted connected expansion or domination estimate
       strong enough to retain one tail mark with two physical sources.

The bounded-domain estimate (1.4) provides none of these automatically.
For example g^2 xi^4 is analytic and even, and is small on |xi|<=T
when gT^3 is small. But E_gamma exp(g^2 xi^4) is infinite for every
real g>0. Local analyticity does not even imply real integrability
of a formal untruncated extension.

Keeping exact compact-group nonlinearities avoids this particular
divergence but does not make the global Mayer sup small. Already for
one SU(2) plaquette, in its principal logarithm coordinate xi with
U=exp(i g xi.sigma), the Gaussian-relative interaction is

 V_g(xi)=|xi|^2/2 -(1-cos(g|xi|))/g^2
                         +2log[sin(g|xi|)/(g|xi|)].   (3.1)

At |xi|=r/g with a fixed 0<r<pi, its leading term is
[r^2/2-(1-cos r)]/g^2>0. Therefore exp V_g is exponentially large
there as g->0; at the cut locus the Jacobian vanishes. Extending the
density by zero beyond the principal chart gives w=-1 outside.
None of these facts meets (2.1), despite the favorable global real
bound proved next.

The published/native large-field action census and the retained full
Wilson energy inequalities concern their actual actions and geometry.
They cannot be reinterpreted without proof as uniform small global
activities in the moving constrained xi variables. In particular a
current relative chart event is not the same as a collection of large
original plaquette actions. This note supplies no such identification.

4. ANALYTIC COMPACT COUNTERMODEL: STABILITY PLUS LOCAL SMALLNESS IS NOT ENOUGH

Take a physical scalar b in [-2,2], with positive reference db and

 S(b)=b^2(1-b^2)^2/2,       Q(b)=b^2.

For real g>0 consider the exact positive compact law
mu_g(db)=Z_phys(g)^-1 exp[-S(b)/g^2]db.
Its source Q is fixed and bounded on that physical interval.
Around the saddle b=0 set b=gx. After extracting the Gaussian
normalization, its sourced partition function is

 Z_g(z)=(2pi)^(-1/2) integral_(|x|<=2/g)
               exp[-x^2/2+V_g(x)+z g^2x^2]dx,
 V_g(x)=g^2x^4-(g^4/2)x^6.                            (4.1)

The interaction is a polynomial, even in g and x, and on the entire
complex disc |x|<=T obeys

             |V_g(x)|<=|g|^2 T^4+(|g|^4/2)T^6.       (4.2)

It obeys the selected small-domain budget when |g|T^3 is small.
There is no hidden failure of local joint analyticity.

The classical action has three minima, at b=0,+1,-1, with

                   S''(0)=1,   S''(+1)=S''(-1)=4.

The normalized partition has the explicit limit

                         Z_g(z)->1+exp z              (4.3)

locally uniformly for complex z in bounded discs. To prove this,
write Z_g=(sqrt(2pi)g)^-1 integral_[-2,2]
exp[-S(b)/g^2+z b^2]db. Split into small fixed neighborhoods of the
three minima and their complement. The complement is exponentially
small. In each neighborhood b=b_*+g y, Taylor's theorem and a
strict local quadratic lower bound give dominated Gaussian convergence.
The contribution is exp(z b_*^2)/sqrt(S''(b_*)).
Their sum is exactly 1+exp z. The same domination is uniform on
bounded complex z discs; Cauchy therefore also gives source-jet
convergence. In particular Z_g(0)->2: the partition normalization is
uniformly of its expected Gaussian order.

Consequently the normalized law of Q converges to the distribution
which puts mass1/2 at0 and mass1/2 at1, and

                         Var_mu_g Q ->1/4.            (4.4)

Now select the declared saddle chart by |x|<=T(g), where
T(g)->infinity and gT(g)^3->0. Its exact selected source logarithm
uses the SAME density and the SAME Q, conditioned on that event.
Equation(4.2) tends uniformly to zero there and
0<=Q<=g^2 T(g)^2->0. Its normalized source generating function tends
locally uniformly to1, so

                         Var_selected Q ->0.          (4.5)

Meanwhile the Gaussian probability of the omitted |x|>T(g) tends
to zero exponentially. The interaction has promoted Gaussian-rare
neighborhoods of x=+/-1/g to order-one physical mass.

This example is not a gauge theory and not a counterexample to the
actual Yang--Mills claims. It is a counterexample to inferring a
full-source correction bound from the displayed local analytic
budgets, evenness, positivity, and a Gaussian-order partition bound.
The source Q is NOT the total S. A total-action source can be blind
to the relative weights of equal-depth saddles; no contrary statement
is inferred here. The example addresses the proposed general physical
quadratic-source comparison.

5. POSITIVE ACTUAL CHECK: ONE COMPACT SU(2) WILSON PLAQUETTE

Let U in SU(2), normalized Haar measure, h(U)=1-Tr(U)/2,
and density proportional to exp[-h(U)/g^2], 0<g<=1.
Use U=exp(i b.sigma), 0<=|b|<pi. Haar measure is

               (2pi^2)^-1 [sin|b|/|b|]^2 db.

With b=gxi and J_g(xi)=[sin(g|xi|)/(g|xi|)]^2, the actual normalized
law of xi has density proportional to

 1_(|xi|<pi/g) exp[-(1-cos(g|xi|))/g^2] J_g(xi) dxi. (5.1)

This is precisely the Gaussian-relative representation(3.1), including
its finite principal-chart domain. For 0<=r<=pi,

              2r^2/pi^2 <=1-cos r<=r^2/2.            (5.2)

Thus the density before normalization is bounded above by
exp[-2|xi|^2/pi^2]. On |xi|<=1 it is bounded below by
(sin1)^2 exp[-|xi|^2/2], uniformly in g<=1.
The normalized xi-law nu_g therefore satisfies

 c<=Z_gauss_relative(g)<=C,
 nu_g(|xi|<=1)>=c,
 E_nu_g[(1+|xi|^k)1_(|xi|>T)]<=C_k exp[-c_k T^2]    (5.3)

for every fixed k and T>=1. If T>=pi/g the tail is empty.
The constants do not involve an unnormalized rare-event denominator.

Let nu_(g,T)=nu_g(. ||xi|<=T). For observables satisfying

 |F(xi)|<=M_F(1+|xi|^2),  |H(xi)|<=M_H(1+|xi|^2),

the elementary identity

 E_nu_g F-E_nu_(g,T)F
 =E_nu_g[F1_(bad)]-nu_g(bad)E_nu_(g,T)F               (5.4)

and (5.3), with k=2,4, prove

 |Cov_nu_g(F,H)-Cov_nu_(g,T)(F,H)|
                       <=C M_F M_H exp[-cT^2].       (5.5)

Indeed the same argument bounds the product moment difference, and
the individual means are uniformly bounded. The selected denominator
is bounded below because T>=1, so every conditional moment used in
(5.4) is controlled. This proof does not require F,H to be polynomial.

In particular Q=h(U)/g^2 obeys 0<=Q<=|xi|^2/2, so (5.5) applies to
the normalized physical action. For the unscaled h, it gives the
explicit covariance correction C g^4 exp[-cT^2]. For independent
copies of this single-plaquette model the local estimate extends
immediately without a whole-volume normalization loss.

This is a genuine nonlinear compact Wilson integral, but only a single
plaquette (or an independent product). Its special input is the global
coercivity(5.2) in the actual chart. The coupled four-dimensional
constrained fiber has neither independent plaquettes nor this global
single-saddle inequality supplied by TURN48. Formula(5.5) must not be
promoted to that setting without the corresponding action, coarea,
source and connected-support estimates.

6. WHAT HAS CHANGED, AND THE NEXT ACTUAL ESTIMATE

The question is no longer whether a tiny local analytic interaction
should heuristically preserve the Gaussian cutoff correction. It
does preserve it under the explicit global high-temperature hypothesis
in Section2, and it can fail badly without a global hypothesis even
when the partition normalization is stable.

For the present YM route, a useful next theorem would retain the
actual full constrained compact measure and prove a connected
two-source expansion with one charged omitted-coordinate/branch
insertion. A weighted activity estimate may replace the overly strong
supremum hypothesis(2.1), as the single-plaquette calculation shows.
But it must retain global stability under overlapping activities,
the actual coarse source, the moving physical/gauge constraints, and
the complete denominator. Local holomorphy of V on C_T is insufficient.

No untruncated extension of the TURN48 kernel, no full-law source
correction, and no four-dimensional continuum construction has been
proved by this note. The proved additions are the explicit interacting
sufficient theorem, the compact analytic obstruction to the shortcut,
and the exact nonlinear one-plaquette tail comparison.
