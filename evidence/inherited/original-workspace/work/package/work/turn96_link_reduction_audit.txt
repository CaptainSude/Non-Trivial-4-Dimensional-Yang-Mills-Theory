1. CONDITIONAL LAW AND REGULATOR GEOMETRY

For an unfixed original link of the stated periodic four-dimensional
lattice, exactly six distinct Wilson plaquettes depend on it, each once.
Orientation reversal is an orthogonal operation on the four real
quaternion coordinates. The conditional weight is therefore exactly
exp(beta u dot V) relative to normalized S^3 Haar measure, V=sum v_p,
with six unit vectors v_p. Rotational invariance and elementary S^3
integration give z(s)=2 I1(s)/s and E u=m(s)V/r, m=I2/I1, s=beta r.
All nonincident Wilson terms are part of the original exterior marginal;
they have not been discarded or replaced by another law.

For an open box, the same formulas need the actual number k of incident
plaquettes in place of six. A fixed boundary link cannot be integrated
with this kernel. The producer avoids those issues by its periodic
periods-at-least-four assumption. Arbitrary values of all surviving
exterior links are allowed under that assumption.

2. GLOBAL BESSEL SLOPE AND CONVEXITY PROOF

The stated Riccati equation m'=1-m^2-3m/s is correct for I2/I1. Put g=sm.
Differentiation gives

 g'=s-(g^2+2g)/s,
 g''=2-(2g+3)g'/s.

Near zero, m=s/4+O(s^3), so g'=s/2+O(s^3) and g''=1/2+O(s^2).
For s>0, m>0 and m'=Var_s(u dot n)>0, hence g'>0. If g' first reached
one from below, then

 g+1=sqrt(s^2-s+1),
 g''=2-[2sqrt(s^2-s+1)+1]/s<0,

contradicting the derivative sign at first contact. The square-root
inequality follows from s^2-s+1=(s-1/2)^2+3/4; there is no omitted small-s
case. Thus 0<g'<1 globally.

At a zero of g'', direct differentiation gives

 g'''=2(1-g'^2)/s>0.

This forbids a first zero reached from g''>0. Consequently g is strictly
convex on (0,infinity), continuous with g(0)=0. These arguments exclude
both crossings and tangential first contacts.

For s*=6 beta and s=beta r, convexity gives g(s)<=s g(s*)/s*, while the
slope bound gives g(s*)-g(s)<=s*-s. Therefore

 m(6 beta)D <= T_beta(D) <= D, D=6-r.

Both endpoints r=0 and r=6 are included by continuity. No weak-field or
large-beta assumption occurs here.

3. GAMMA ESTIMATE AND THE REFINED REMAINDER

Under the tilted S^3 law, z=s(1-u dot n) has Gamma(3/2,1) weight times
w_s(z)=(1-z/(2s))^(1/2) 1[0<=z<=2s]. This is nonincreasing in z.
Its mean is at most the unweighted Gamma mean3/2 because
Cov_Gamma(z,w_s)<=0. Hence 1-m(s)<=3/(2s), proving

 0<=D-T_beta(D)<=[1-m(6 beta)]D<=D/(4 beta).

For the sharper Section4 estimate, differentiating log w_s on its support
gives z/[4s^2(1-z/(2s))], so the displayed covariance formula for
b'(s), b=s(1-m), has the correct factor and sign. The endpoint contributes
zero to the first derivative: the original density vanishes there and
the resulting inverse-square-root singularity is locally integrable.
The normalizing integral is uniformly bounded below on 0<=z<=1 for s>=1.
The numerator integral on s<=z<=2s is at most C s^(7/2)e^(-s), as stated,
and is therefore uniformly bounded. This proves 0<=b'(s)<=C/s^2.

Since D-T=[b(6 beta)-b(beta r)]/beta, integration yields the claimed
C(beta r0)^(-2)D bound for r>=r0>0, beta r0>=1. The global D/(4 beta)
comparison remains the appropriate bound when r approaches zero.

4. CLOSED LOOPS AND PROFILE CORRECTION

For six unit vectors,

 sum_(p<p') |v_p-v_p'|^2=36-|sum_p v_p|^2.

Rationalizing 6-r therefore gives exactly

 D=2/(6+r) sum_(p<p') (1-v_p dot v_p').

The two staples have the same endpoints; their product with inverse
orientation is a closed exterior loop. Its quaternion dot product is
half its SU(2) trace. Thus D is gauge invariant, local, and independent
of the conditioned link. It satisfies 0<=D<=6 on every real exterior.

With qbar=(1/6)sum q_p and delta_p=1-n dot v_p, one has sum delta_p=D
and delta_p>=0. The exact profile correction is

 m(beta r)sum_p(q_p-qbar)delta_p,

with absolute value at most omega(q)D. At r=0, any n gives sum delta=6,
and m(0)=0. The alternate expression

 -[m(beta r)/r]sum_p(q_p-qbar)v_p dot V

extends with m(beta r)/r=beta/4 at zero and is smooth in the real
quaternion variables. No profile-independent constant was hidden in this
bound. The source profile must actually be scalar and smooth across
orientations to replace omega(q) by a spatial gradient times the mesh.

5. FULL ORIGINAL COVARIANCE AND ITS MEASURABILITY

Each plaquette has four edges, so S_q=(1/4)sum_e S_(q,e). The separate
conditional expectation at each e preserves the pairing with a random
variable F precisely when F is measurable outside e. Hence the stated
condition that F be measurable outside every e in E(q) is sufficient.
Summing the six-star scalar gives

 (1/4)sum_e c_beta qbar_e=(c_beta/6)sum_p q_p.

This verifies both the expectation identity and the global covariance
identity, including the normalization and the factor1/4.

For deterministic original action sources, link-disjoint support suffices
for the one-sided identity. For the two-sided identity one needs the
complete exterior-star read of the first transformed source to avoid
the other source edges; this is exactly the producer's extra condition.
No independence of overlapping heat-bath updates has been assumed.

A history-owned raw source need not be exterior measurable, even when
its original plaquette locations are far away: ownership and deciding
labels can depend on e. Conditioning the Gibbs measure on those labels
also changes the conditional density. The producer correctly excludes
that application without an additional proof.

D=6-|V| is itself nonsmooth at V=0. The exact conditional combination

7. ACTUAL WILSON COMPLEX-POSTERIOR POLES NEAR A RAW EXTERIOR

The complex-domain caveat has a concrete example within the very same
six-staple conditional law. Assign the exterior staple matrices

 v1(t)=exp(t i sigma3), v2=-I, v3=I, v4=-I, v5=I, v6=-I.

Here the notation identifies unit quaternions with SU(2) matrices for
real t. These six staples can be realized independently: set their
outer legs equal to I and choose the distinct middle links of their
three-link paths. On the stated periodic lattice those middle links are
distinct. All other exterior links can be fixed arbitrarily.

Their quaternion sum is V(t)=v1(t)-I, so the holomorphic continuation of
its Euclidean squared norm (WITHOUT complex conjugation) is

 V(t) dot V(t)=4 sin^2(t/2).

Choose the odd analytic branch r(t)=2 sin(t/2); the normalizer is even in
this branch and equals

 z_beta(t)=2 I1(2 beta sin(t/2))/(2 beta sin(t/2)).

Let j be any positive simple zero of J1. The identity I1(i j)=i J1(j)
then gives zeros at

 t_beta=2 i asinh(j/(2 beta))=i j/beta+O(beta^-3).

These are simple for sufficiently large beta. Simplicity can also be
seen directly: a nonzero common zero of J1 and J1' would contradict
uniqueness for its second-order differential equation. At such a zero,
I2 is nonzero by the recurrence I1'=I2+I1/s. Therefore the normalized
conditional total-star source

 6-r(t) I2(beta r(t))/I1(beta r(t))

has a pole at t_beta. Subtracting its flat-exterior scalar does not
remove the pole. This is an actual finite Wilson conditional integral
with allowed real raw exterior data; it is not the generic two-point
posterior example from TURN95.

Consequently no complex neighborhood of radius c beta^-1/2 around EVERY
real raw exterior can support a uniform holomorphic normalized-star
source: these zeros occur at distance O(beta^-1). This does not rule out
a source-specific native history domain which avoids the family, and it
does not affect any real identity above. The UNNORMALIZED marked integral
and its normalizer remain entire; the obstruction is introduced by
forming the conditional ratio. Any later native use must retain or
estimate that denominator on its actual domain rather than infer
admission from the real boundedness of the conditional expectation.

No numerical value of the Bessel zero is needed. The elementary fact
that J1 has positive zeros follows from its real oscillatory Bessel
solution, or equivalently its standard Sturm-Liouville equation. This
section is an analytic scope check, not an assertion about zeros of the
full lattice partition function.
