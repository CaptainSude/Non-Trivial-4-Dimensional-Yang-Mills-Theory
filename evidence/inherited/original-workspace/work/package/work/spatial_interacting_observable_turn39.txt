No product of disconnected source expectations is being estimated as

This is a genuine interacting finite-box statement, not a formal

Use polylog_box_real_source_comparison_turn38 without changing its
law or gauge quotient. Boundary links are identity. Let

 S_t(U)=S(U)-sum_a t_a R_a(U),
 I_g(t)=integral exp[-S_t(U)/g^2] dU,
 p_g(t)=log[I_g(t)/I_g(0)].

The fixed finite source family consists of real gauge-invariant local
functions with bounded word lengths/read overlap, zero constant and
first field jets, and |sum t_a R_a|<=rho S for a fixed rho<1. It includes
the literal common-root curvature bilinears of turn38. Profiles can
depend on R within their stated uniform coefficient bound. They are
not arbitrary calibrated effective sources with an unbounded coefficient.

In forest logarithms x=gz, write the homogeneous field Taylor jets

 S_t(exp(gz))/g^2
       =Q_t(z)+g V_{3,t}(z)+g^2 V_{4,t}(z)
                   +g^3 V_{5,t}(z)+O(g^4|z|^6),        (1.1)

where anti-Hermitian Lie coordinates are used; exp(ix) in Hermitian
coordinates is the same convention. Q_t=z^tH_tz/2. A source has jets

       g^{-2}R_a(exp(gz))=P_a+g A_a+g^2 B_a
                                  +g^3 R_{a,5}+O(g^4|z|^6).

Thus P_a,A_a,B_a have degrees2,3,4 and
Q_t=Q_0-sum t_a P_a, V_{3,t}=V_3-sum t_a A_a,
V_{4,t}=V_4-sum t_a B_a. Let E_t and kappa_t be expectation and ordinary
joint cumulant in the normalized Gaussian with covariance C_t=H_t^{-1}.
Use the SAME real t-dependent covariance in every term.

All finite Taylor orders used here have constants independent of R:
each local word has bounded derivatives and only bounded overlap.
The established estimates are

 n<=CR^4,  cR^{-8}I<=H_t<=CI,
 E_t|z|^q<=C_q R^{6q},
 S_t>=cR^{-20} outside a fixed product chart of radius cR^{-8}.

The product exponential-Haar density is denoted j(x). Its logarithm is

       log[j(gz)/j(0)]=g^2 J_2(z)+O(g^4|z|^4).         (1.2)

There is no linear or cubic term. With the usual real determinant on
the Lie algebra,

       J_2(z)= (1/24) sum_e Tr_ad[(ad z_e)^2].          (1.3)

It is negative semidefinite for compact anti-Hermitian coordinates.
Formula(1.3) follows from

2. THE ACTUAL ORDER-g^2 PRESSURE AND A QUANTIFIED REMAINDER

Define

 L_2(t)=E_t[J_2-V_{4,t}]
                       +(1/2)kappa_t(V_{3,t},V_{3,t}). (2.1)

The second cumulant equals the second moment because the cubic is odd.
Then, for each fixed N, the error in (0.1) satisfies

 max_{|alpha|<=N} sup_t
 |partial_t^alpha{p_g-p_G-g^2[L_2(t)-L_2(0)]}|
                          <=C_N g^4 R^{12N+72}.       (2.2)

PROOF. Use the same symmetric mesoscopic ball as turn38,

       |z|<=A_N R^6 sqrt(log(1/g)+log(2+R)).

For small g in the polylogarithmic R range, this lies inside the chart
after scaling by g and g|z|^3 is uniformly small. Taylor expand the
scaled integrand through order g^3. Its order-g coefficient is -V_3,
and its order-g^2 coefficient is J_2-V_4+V_3^2/2. The order-g^3
coefficient is

       -V_5+V_3 V_4-V_3^3/6-J_2 V_3,                 (2.3)

which is odd. Both odd coefficients integrate to zero against the
even Gaussian over that symmetric ball.

After k source derivatives, the source insertions have their own
Taylor expansions through order g^3. Every total odd power of g has
odd polynomial parity. The remaining absolute error is bounded by
C_N g^4[1+|z|^{2k+12}], using g|z|^3<=c. Gaussian integration costs
at most C_N g^4R^{12k+72}. Differentiating the integral coefficient
at g^2 gives exactly partial^alpha[J_t L_2(t)], where
J_t=integral exp(-Q_t)dz; the cubic and quartic source contacts are
therefore included, not omitted by differentiating only the Gaussian.

Both complements are retained exactly as in the turn38 proof. The
chart outside the mesoscopic ball has exponent

 CR^4 log(2+R)+2k log(1/g)+C_k log(2+R)-cR^{-8}B^2.

Choose A_N larger so its normalized contribution is <=g^6. The
full compact complement has exponent

 CR^4[log(1/g)+log(2+R)]+2k log(1/g)
                         +C_k log(2+R)-cg^{-2}R^{-20},

and is also <=g^6. These bounds include g^{-n}, Haar factors and all
raw differentiated marks; there is no discarded outside-chart law.
The missing Gaussian polynomial tails obey the same estimate.

It follows that the actual relative normalizer is

 I_g(t)/(g^n j(0)J_t)=1+g^2L_2(t)+r_g(t),
 |partial^alpha r_g(t)|<=C_Ng^4R^{12|alpha|+72}.

The normalized Gaussian derivative/partition bounds give
|partial^alpha L_2|<=C_NR^{12|alpha|+36}. Since g^2R^{36} tends to zero,
expanding the real logarithm retains (2.2): the square of the first
correction has size g^4R^{72}, and differentiation distributes total
order |alpha| without increasing the additional +72. Subtract the
value at t=0. This proves the claimed actual-law expansion.

No constant is asserted uniform in N in this real-contour argument.
Section8 gives a stronger common-radius consequence from the separately
proved complex contour construction; it is not needed for (2.2).

3. THE COMPLETE CONNECTED COVARIANCE CORRECTION

At t=0 abbreviate V_3=T, V_4=V, and kappa=kappa_0. Then

 Cov_W(g^{-2}R_a,g^{-2}R_b)
     =kappa(P_a,P_b)+g^2 K^{(2)}_{ab}
                                  +O(g^4R^{96}),       (3.1)

where the EXPLICIT coefficient is

 K^{(2)}_{ab}=
   kappa(A_a,A_b)
  +kappa(B_a,P_b)+kappa(B_b,P_a)
  -kappa(A_a,T,P_b)-kappa(A_b,T,P_a)
  +kappa(J_2-V,P_a,P_b)
  +(1/2)kappa(T,T,P_a,P_b).                            (3.2)

Proof: for any t-dependent Gaussian polynomial F,
partial_a E_t F=E_t partial_a F+kappa_t(F,P_a).
The analogous derivative of a cumulant differentiates each argument
and appends P_a. Apply these exact identities twice to (2.1), using
partial_a T_t=-A_a and partial_a(J_2-V_t)=B_a. This gives (3.2).

All seven terms are needed. Keeping only the interaction insertion
kappa(J_2-V,Pa,Pb)+(1/2)kappa(T,T,Pa,Pb) would miss the nonlinear
observable's own cubic/quartic corrections. Replacing the last
cumulant by E[T^2 Pa Pb] would reintroduce disconnected vacuum pieces.
Equation(3.2) is already normalized and centered under the actual law
through its derivation from log I_g.

For separated two-source pressure, subtract both one-support pressures
as in turn30. Any counterterm e_a(s)+e_b(t) cancels exactly. Its order
g^2 mixed coefficient is still (3.2); no spatial cancellation is bought
merely by naming these single-support terms vacuum subtractions.

4. EXPLICIT LOCAL WILSON VERTICES

For a plaquette with oriented anti-Hermitian link variables Z_1,...,Z_4,
fixed forest links contribute zero. Define F_0=C_0=D_0=0 and update

 F_l=F_{l-1}+Z,
 C_l=C_{l-1}+(1/2)[F_{l-1},Z],
 D_l=D_{l-1}+(1/2)[C_{l-1},Z]
       +(1/12)[F_{l-1},[F_{l-1},Z]]
       +(1/12)[Z,[Z,F_{l-1}]].                        (4.1)

The logarithm of product_l exp(gZ_l) is gF_4+g^2C_4+g^3D_4+O(g^4).
With inner product <X,Y>=-Re tau(XY), the plaquette vertices are

 Q_p=(1/2)<F_4,F_4>,

Thus T=sum_p T_p and V=sum_p V_p. A scalar plaquette profile has

5. SPATIAL KERNEL: CONNECTED WICK GRAPHS BEFORE ANY COLLAPSE

Resolve each source and action into its local vertices, retaining their
positions and original read sets E_v. Write the coordinate covariance
as C(e,a;f,b), with link and color indices. For homogeneous polynomial
vertices F_1,...,F_m of degrees d_1,...,d_m, the Gaussian cumulant is

 kappa(F_1,...,F_m)
  =sum_{P: pairing of all legs, connected on vertices1,...,m}
       contraction of [coefficient tensors of the F_i]
                       with product_{(l,l') in P} C(l,l'). (5.1)

Internal self-pairings are allowed; connectivity refers to the graph
after contracting each polynomial to a labeled vertex. Formula(5.1)
follows by Wick's rule and the set-partition formula for cumulants:
a pairing with more than one connected component has total coefficient
zero in that formula; a connected pairing has coefficient one.

In (3.2) the largest degree list is (3,3,2,2), so every term has at
most ten legs and five propagators. There are at most 9!!=945 pairings
before color/read multiplicities, which are fixed for these local
vertices. Every surviving term contains a connected path joining the
two marked source vertices. This is the precise spatial gain retained
by the coefficient; it is lost if each cumulant is bounded by separate
ordinary moments.

For an explicit positive majorant, put

 c(v,w)=max_{e in E_v,f in E_w} ||C(e;f)||color,

and replace each covariance in (5.1) by c(v,w) and each finite vertex
tensor by its absolute coefficient bound. Sum the action vertex
positions only AFTER this replacement. This gives a finite, computable
connected kernel K_conn(x,y) with

 |K^{(2)}_{ab}|<=C sum_{x,y}|f_a(x)f_b(y)| K_conn(x,y). (5.2)

The kernel includes three-edge direct-source diagrams and the
three-edge diagram with one integrated quadratic Haar vertex;
four-edge diagrams contain either a quartic action vertex or a cubic
action vertex together with a cubic source vertex. Five-edge diagrams
contain two cubic action vertices. The degree lists in (3.2) specify
all of them without ambiguity. Source tensors
with several local read types are summed over those finite types.

This is NOT a proof that K_conn has the physical summable bound wanted
in the continuum problem. C is the actual FOREST-coordinate covariance.
Its coefficientwise absolute values need not inherit the good separated
curvature-projection kernel of the Gaussian leading term. Ward/derivative
cancellations must be performed before an inappropriate absolute bound.

6. EXACT LOCAL SUBTRACTIONS AND WHAT RENORMALIZATION STILL REQUIRES

Let Delta_C=sum_{a,b} C_ab partial_a partial_b. Gaussian normal ordering
gives, for each quartic vertex F_4,

 F_4=:F_4:_C+(1/2):Delta_C F_4:_C
                              +(1/8)Delta_C^2 F_4.    (6.1)

These are respectively its quartic remainder, a quadratic local
contraction, and a scalar vacuum. For a cubic vertex,

                    F_3=:F_3:_C+(1/2)Delta_C F_3.

The Wilson cubic in (4.2) has the Lie structure tensor f_abc. The
quadratic action and the stated color-invariant sources have covariance
diagonal in color. Contracting any two of the cubic's color indices
therefore gives zero; Delta_C T_p=0. The same observation applies to
the cubic of a literal transported clover bilinear, whose degree-three
terms are inner products with one Lie commutator. It is not a claim
about an arbitrary analytic cubic source.

Consequently the one-vertex local ledger contains the Haar quadratic
J_2, the quadratic and scalar contractions of V_p, and the analogous
quadratic/scalar contacts from B_a. The two-cubic term retains its
connected two-vertex contractions. They cannot all be replaced by one
single-support vacuum number. Equation(6.1) is an exact finite-cutoff
decomposition, with every local coefficient computable from C on the
complete read.

In particular an individual local quadratic contraction in forest
coordinates may look like an A^2 term. It is NOT an independently
allowed gauge-theory counterterm. Showing its cancellation/reassembly
with the Haar and two-cubic terms into gauge-invariant curvature

The finite21 curvature algebra identifies the possible dimension-four

A declared finite mixing R_a->R_a+g^2 sum_c Z_ac R_c changes (3.2) by

 sum_c Z_ac kappa(P_c,P_b)+sum_c Z_bc kappa(P_a,P_c).   (6.2)

7. USEFUL SCALE OF THE RIGOROUS REMAINDER; SHARP REMAINING GAP

For any fixed polylogarithmic R(g), the g^4R^{96} error in (3.1) tends
to zero faster than g^2 times every fixed power of R. Hence (3.2)
identifies the actual first interacting correction in that regime,
not merely a coefficient at each separately fixed graph. The same
statement holds after summing a fixed polynomial number of source
pairs: for O(R^4) local source positions the completely unspecialized
pairwise remainder sums to at most Cg^4R^{104}. This is useful on the
growing bare box; it does not reach a fixed physical box under AF.

The available Hessian data alone cannot turn (5.2) into a physically
summable bound. For a finite-range positive matrix with spectrum
[cR^{-8},C], polynomial approximation to its inverse only gives an
off-diagonal length of order R^4 (the inverse square root of the gap).
That exceeds the box diameter. The conclusion supplies no useful
uniform separation estimate within this box.

There is also a concrete test against treating bounded variances and
massless decay as enough for every absolute loop sum. The kernel

                       c(x,y)=1/(1+|x-y|^2)

on Z^4 is positive definite: it is the positive mixture
integral_0^infty e^{-t} exp[-t|x-y|^2]dt of Gaussian positive-definite
kernels. Its diagonal is one, but

            sum_{1<=|v|<=R} c(0,v)^2 is comparable to log R.

Indeed the shell count is order r^3 and the squared kernel is order
r^{-4}. Thus an absolute two-line loop can retain a logarithmic
volume dependence. This is a Gaussian analytic countertest, not a
calculation of a YM divergence or a denial of a Ward cancellation.

The smallest remaining interacting spatial assertion is a bound for
the COMPLETE combination (3.2), after its specified local vacuum and
mixing subtractions, retaining at least the separated curvature/source
cross-kernel factor. A new bound on its separate absolute moments does
not supply that. The compact-law remainder is already quantified here;
the pending work is this actual connected/gauge-compatible kernel
estimate, and its extension beyond the polylogarithmic bare geometry.

No total-volume-uniform local Schwinger bound, full continuum hierarchy,
or Yang--Mills existence conclusion is claimed.

9. AN EXACT COMPACT ONE-PLAQUETTE CHECK OF THE SIGNS

As a separate normalization check, take one independent SU(2) plaquette
U=exp(i x.sigma/2) with action h(U)=1-Tr(U)/2 and Haar measure. This
is not the multi-plaquette box's spatial estimate. In x=gz coordinates,

 Q=|z|^2/8,       T=0,       V=-|z|^4/384,
 J_2=-|z|^2/12,   C=4 I_3.

The Gaussian moments E|z|^2=12 and E|z|^4=240 give

                 L_2(0)=-1+240/384=-3/8.

For the literal action source R=h, all action jets are multiplied by
1-t while the covariance is C_t=C/(1-t). Thus every term of (2.1)
scales as (1-t)^{-1}, including the Haar term, and

 p_g(t)=-(3/2)log(1-t)
       -(3g^2/8)[(1-t)^{-1}-1]+O(g^4).

In particular the actual centered compact-law variance is

              Var_W(g^{-2}h)=3/2-(3/4)g^2+O(g^4).

This checks the Haar sign, the positive -V contribution, and both
source derivatives of the coefficient against a fully specified
compact law. It also illustrates why the first interacting correction
cannot be assumed nonnegative merely from positivity of the measure.
Its use here is an explicit coefficient test, not a substitute for the
connected spatial-kernel bound in Section7.
