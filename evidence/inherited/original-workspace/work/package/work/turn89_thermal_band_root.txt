TURN89 -- EXACT THERMAL PAIRING ON THE ORIGINAL WILSON SEQUENCE
20 October 2026. Scope: a spectral reduction, NOT a YM4 existence proof.

1. Setup and the distinction from TURN88

Use the actual finite Wilson transfer operator T=M Q M on the physical
(gauge-invariant) spatial Hilbert space, at positive Wilson coupling.
It is positive, injective and compact; the powers used below are trace
class. Write its eigenvalues lambda_i>0 and Z_N=Tr(T^N), for an even
temporal period N. The temporal lattice spacing is a and the physical
time circumference is P=Na. The spatial regulator may vary with a.

Let Phi be multiplication by the SAME real gauge-invariant slice
observable phi in [0,1] in both factors. In our application phi is the
fixed nonnegative unit-mass spatial smear of the bounded spatial-flow
observable in TURN88. The spectral argument needs only these properties.
The thermal expectation is E_N. Center A=Phi-E_N(phi)I when discussing
variance; its matrix elements between unequal eigenspaces equal Phi_ij.

No limit N->infinity at a fixed spatial regulator is taken in this note.
In particular, no uniform spectral gap or ground-state dominance is used.

Define a finite positive measure on (0,infinity):

 mu_(a,N) = Z_N^(-1) sum_(lambda_i>lambda_j)
               lambda_i^N |Phi_ij|^2 delta_(E_ij),
 E_ij = a^(-1) log(lambda_i/lambda_j)>0.                         (1)

Each unequal-eigenvalue pair is counted once, with the larger eigenvalue
first. Degenerate pairs do not enter. Formula (1) is invariant under
basis changes within degenerate eigenspaces. Since its mass is a subset
of the nonnegative terms in Tr(T^N A^2)/Z_N,

                  mu_(a,N)((0,infinity)) <= Var_N(phi) <= 1/4. (2)

This is a measure of positive ENERGY DIFFERENCES in a finite thermal
trace. It is not an assertion that a finite thermal reconstruction has
a nonnegative Hamiltonian with this spectral measure.

2. Exact filtered boundary and reflected formulas

Choose nonnegative weights w_k of sum one, with t_k=ak in [T0,T1],
where T0>0 and T1 are fixed physical constants. Let d be a positive
integer, delta_a=ad in [delta_min,D], with delta_min>0. Set

 F=sum_k w_k [phi(k)-phi(k+d)],   G=phi(0),
 kappa=E_N(FG),                  N_F=E_N((Theta F)F).

All original reads of F must lie strictly in the chosen reflected half:
in particular P>2(T1+D), with the buffers required by TURN88. Reflection
is through time zero and the opposite fixed slice. Spatial flow stays
within each original time slice. Stationarity gives E_N F=0.

For E>0 put

 L_-(E)=sum_k w_k exp(-t_k E),
 L_+(E)=sum_k w_k exp(+t_k E),
 K(E)=L_-(E)(1-exp(-delta_a E)).

Then the following identities hold for the ORIGINAL finite Wilson law:

 kappa = int (1-exp(-delta_a E))
          [L_-(E)-exp(-(P-delta_a)E)L_+(E)] mu_(a,N)(dE),     (3)

 N_F = int (1-exp(-delta_a E))^2
          [L_-(E)^2+exp(-(P-2delta_a)E)L_+(E)^2]
                                                   mu_(a,N)(dE). (4)

Proof. For 0<=s<=N the two-point trace is
 Z_N^(-1) sum_(i,j) lambda_i^(N-s) lambda_j^s |Phi_ij|^2.
For a pair lambda_i>lambda_j set r=lambda_j/lambda_i=exp(-aE).
The two directions give lambda_i^N(r^s+r^(N-s))|Phi_ij|^2/Z_N.
The boundary difference at k,k+d is therefore
 lambda_i^N(1-r^d)(r^k-r^(N-k-d))|Phi_ij|^2/Z_N.
The reflected double difference at k,l is
 lambda_i^N(1-r^d)^2[r^(k+l)+r^(N-k-l-2d)]|Phi_ij|^2/Z_N.
Sum the nonnegative weights. Equal-eigenvalue terms cancel exactly.
Each initial correlation is finite and bounded. Pair regrouping can
also be justified by finite spectral truncation and the bounded trace
form; the displayed paired kernels are nonnegative in the stated
geometry, so the final sums converge monotonically. This proves (3)-(4).

The conditional original-boundary product law from TURN88 still gives
N_F>=4 kappa^2. Here (3) also gives kappa>=0 directly; strict positivity
under the nonconstant-observable conditions remains the TURN88 result.

3. Uniform control of the backward thermal terms

Define positive forward quantities

 kappa^+=int K(E)mu(dE),   N_F^+=int K(E)^2mu(dE).

Let R1=P-T1-D and R2=P-2T1-2D. If both are positive, then

 0 <= kappa^+-kappa <= D/(4e R1),                            (5)
 0 <= N_F-N_F^+       <= D^2/(e^2 R2^2).                     (6)

Indeed L_+<=exp(T1 E), 1-exp(-delta_a E)<=D E and mu mass<=1/4.
The suprema of E exp(-R1 E) and E^2 exp(-R2 E) are respectively
1/(eR1) and 4/(e^2 R2^2). No spectral gap occurs in these estimates.

Consequently, on ANY simultaneous sequence of original finite Wilson
regulators with P_a->infinity, the two backward errors tend to zero
uniformly in the spatial volume, the spectrum and the cutoff. Coupling
constants can vary with a. This removes the ground-FIRST order of limits
from this particular spectral reduction; it does not establish the
continuum Wilson law or interchange any unproved field limits.

4. Equivalent quantitative target on a fixed physical band

Keep T0,T1,delta_min,D fixed and P_a->infinity. A positive lower bound
for kappa_a is equivalent to retaining positive mu_a mass on some fixed
band [epsilon,Lambda] with 0<epsilon<Lambda<infinity. A positive lower
bound for N_F,a is equivalent to the same property.

Explicit forward direction: if kappa_a>=c>0, then kappa_a^+>=c. Since
the total mass is at most 1/4, necessarily c<=1/4. Choose

 epsilon=c/D,
 Lambda=max{2epsilon, T0^(-1) log(1/c)}.

The contributions to kappa_a^+ below epsilon and above Lambda are each
at most c/4, because K<=D E and K<=exp(-T0 E). Since K<=1,

                    mu_a([epsilon,Lambda]) >= c/2.           (7)

Conversely if this band has mass at least b>0, then

 kappa_a^+ >= b exp(-T1 Lambda)(1-exp(-delta_min epsilon)),    (8)
 N_F,a^+ >= b exp(-2T1 Lambda)(1-exp(-delta_min epsilon))^2.   (9)

Subtract (5) for kappa; (4) gives N_F>=N_F^+ with no subtraction.
For the norm-to-band direction, (6) gives a positive lower bound on
N_F^+ eventually whenever N_F has one, and K^2<=K gives kappa^+>=N_F^+.
Apply the same low/high argument with a smaller positive constant.
These implications use uniform floors, not merely strict positivity
at each finite cutoff.

At finite period there is also a direct boundary lower bound if
P>=Pmin>2T1+D:

 kappa >= b exp(-T1 Lambda)(1-exp(-delta_min epsilon))
                    (1-exp(-(Pmin-2T1-D)epsilon)).           (10)

For each time t the kernel in (3) is
(1-exp(-delta_a E))exp(-tE)[1-exp(-(P-2t-delta_a)E)].
This proves (10) by positivity. The stronger support requirement
P>2(T1+D) is retained whenever F is used in the original reflected half.

5. Subsequences and the bounded scalar limit

If w_k are exact cell weights for a fixed nonnegative time profile of
mass one supported in [T0,T1], and delta_a->delta>0, then K_a converges
uniformly on E>=0 to
 K_infinity(E)=(int w(t)exp(-tE)dt)(1-exp(-delta E)).
The bound sup_(E>=0) E exp(-T0 E)=1/(eT0) controls cell-placement errors
and the delta error. A harmless slightly smaller T0 handles boundary
cells. The same holds for K_a^2. Both limiting kernels are continuous
and vanish at zero and infinity.

The mass bound gives a subsequence of mu_a converging weak-* on
C_0((0,infinity)) to a finite positive Radon measure. Mass may disappear
at zero or infinity. These two kernels see neither escaped component,
so (3)-(6) identify the limits of the filtered correlations and norms
with the integrals of K_infinity and K_infinity^2 against that measure.
This extraction can be taken along the common bounded-scalar subsequence
of TURN87. The physical-period divergence is essential for dropping
the backward terms in this statement.

When the infinite-time stationary reflection-positive scalar law and
its strongly continuous semigroup are available as in TURN88, the
filtered class is (I-exp(-delta H)) applied to a positive-time average.
It annihilates the entire kernel of H. A surviving reflected norm
therefore gives a genuinely positive-energy scalar sector. The thermal
measure above is not thereby identified with a full local YM field
spectral measure, and locality, Euclidean rotations, coupling matching,
joint field/source estimates and YM reconstruction remain separate.

6. What remains unproved

No lower bound on mu_a([epsilon,Lambda]), kappa_a, N_F,a, or the ordinary
variance criterion studied in the companion TURN89 note is proved here.
The exact original source row still is
 Z^(-1) int w_B G Z_+ Z_F dB,
with the original boundary and opposite-half weights retained. Native
unmarked bounds and a normalized new-slot reference replacement do not
estimate that signed old-observable row. This note strengthens the
consumer of a future quantitative estimate; it does not supply one.
