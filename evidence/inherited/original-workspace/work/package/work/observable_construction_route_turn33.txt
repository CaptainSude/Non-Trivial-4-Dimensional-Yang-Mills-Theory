RESULT

The most useful new algebraic simplification is specific to SU(2): the
entire normalized multi-loop moment functional is a positive linear
average of SINGLE-loop word expectations. The actual lattice loop
equations can consequently be written linearly on this infinite word
space. This permits a different prospective producer: uniform local
area/shape jets of one normalized Wilson-loop functional, with complete
contact subtractions, rather than a separate exponential source family
for every composite. The algebra is exact; the required continuum jet
estimate is not supplied by the papers inspected.

Two precise limitations are also established below. All scalar local
coupling jets do not determine the gauge-orbit law even at a fixed finite
lattice. And the available random-data spatial Yang--Mills heat-flow
theorem does not cover the equal-time roughness of a four-dimensional
free gauge potential. Neither limitation is a no-go for interacting
point splitting or for a future spatial-flow construction.

1. AN EXACT POSITIVE SU(2) REDUCTION OF ALL MULTI-LOOP MOMENTS

Use w(A)=Tr(A)/2. Cayley--Hamilton for B in SU(2) gives

              w(A)w(B) = [w(AB)+w(AB^{-1})]/2.              (1.1)

This is [KZ](1.1) in normalized-trace convention. Transport the based
holonomies of any finite collection of loops to a common vertex by fixed
lattice paths. Each trace is unchanged. Apply (1.1) successively:

 product_(j=1)^n w(A_j)
     = 2^{-(n-1)} sum_(epsilon_2,...,epsilon_n in {+,-})
                        w(A_1 A_2^{epsilon_2}...A_n^{epsilon_n}). (1.2)

The formula is pointwise, not a factorization of expectations. It holds
under the SAME normalized Wilson law, any source-deformed positive law,
and indeed any probability law of these matrices. The weights have total
mass one. There is no normalization denominator to compare, no large-N
limit and no factorial cost from this trace reduction. Ordinary joint
moments determine cumulants by the usual partition formula if desired.

Complete reads matter. The single words contain the common-base paths,
their inverse segments and every original loop. The union may be much
larger than an individual loop. A product supported on separated sets
does not become a local one-loop observable in either set. The equality
is exact despite this path dependence of the individual terms.

[KZ](2.31) makes the SU(2) lattice loop equation linear on single-loop
expectations after this reduction. It is an INFINITE loop-space equation;
plaquette deformations increase word length. Their finite-length bootstrap
does not prove continuum convergence or uniqueness from short-loop data.
The discussion pp32--33 explicitly leaves convergence and selection of
physical solutions as further questions. [SSZ] independently supplies
the exact normalized finite-N loop identities for the Wilson law.

Thus a claim that SU(2) necessarily retains an irreducible multitrace
hierarchy would be too pessimistic. A claim of finite-jet or finite-length
closure would be too optimistic.

2. EXACT QUADRATIC AND ORIENTED CUBIC LOCAL LOOP WORDS

Write P=p0 I+i p.sigma, Q=q0 I+i q.sigma and R=r0 I+i r.sigma,
with p0^2+|p|^2=1 etc. All loops in this paragraph have a common base.
Direct Pauli multiplication gives

 p.q = w(P)w(Q)-w(PQ)
     = [w(PQ^{-1})-w(PQ)]/2,                               (2.1)

       det(p,q,r) = [w(PQR)-w(PRQ)]/2.                     (2.2)

The second identity retains orientation: the vector part of PQ is
p0 q+q0 p-p cross q, so its scalar triple product contributes with the
displayed positive sign. These are finite local gauge-invariant loop
observables, including when the plaquette planes have no shared edge.
They are not functions solely of the separate plaquette traces.

For smooth small plaquettes in the convention F=F^a sigma_a/2,
P=exp(i g a^2 F_A+o(a^2)), one has p=g a^2 F_A^components/2+o(a^2)
at FIXED nonzero g. This is a classical benchmark. Uniform use along a
running g(a)->0 requires the stronger error o(g(a)a^2), with the
corresponding differentiated remainder bounds if jets are taken.
Consequently the leading terms of (2.1),(2.2) are respectively

       (g^2 a^4/4) F_A.F_B,
       (g^3 a^6/8) det(F_A,F_B,F_C).                        (2.3)

The trace DIFFERENCE in (2.2) therefore has coefficient g^3 a^6/4.
This supplies an exact orientation-sensitive lattice representative,
without assuming that an O(3)-invariant free quadratic net contains it.
It does not establish its interacting continuum renormalization.

There is also an exact connection to link integration by parts. Suppose
P and Q each traverse the same oriented link once and are based at that
link's initial vertex. For left generators X_a with exp(i t sigma_a/2),

 X_a w(P)=-p_a/2,
 Gamma_e(w(P),w(Q)):=sum_a X_a w(P) X_a w(Q)=p.q/4.          (2.4)

With E_P=1-w(P), |p|^2=(1-p0)(1+p0)<=2E_P, so

 |Gamma_e(w(P),w(Q))| <= sqrt(E_P E_Q)/2
                         <=(E_P+E_Q)/4.                   (2.5)

This is a genuine pointwise estimate under the full Wilson law, with no
weak-field qualification or history normalization. Opposite orientations
require the appropriate signed/transported link derivative. If the loops
share no link, this particular Gamma_e is zero; (2.1) still applies to
their based loop words. We do not use a fictitious shared-edge formula.

The useful limit of (2.5) is equally explicit: division by g^2 a^4 costs
exactly those canonical factors. Scalar vacuum-subtracted correlations
do not bound the uncentered positive E_P/a^4 by themselves. Likewise
|det(p,q,r)|<=(8 E_P E_Q E_R)^{1/2} is not a uniform renormalized cubic
bound. The contact/subtraction problem remains after this algebra.

2A. EXACT SOURCE-NORMALIZATION PRICE

At lattice spacing a define the literal canonical representatives

 Qhat_(A,B)=4(g^2 a^4)^{-1}[w(P_A)w(P_B)-w(P_A P_B)],
 That_(A,B,C)=4(g^3 a^6)^{-1}[w(P_A P_B P_C)-w(P_A P_C P_B)].

Their smooth classical limits are F_A.F_B and det(F_A,F_B,F_C),
respectively. For a physical smearing a^4 sum_x f(x), a source coupled
to Qhat multiplies the raw loop difference by 4 f/g^2, whereas a source
coupled to That multiplies it by 4 f/(g^3 a^2). These are the coefficients
in the exponent, if an exponentiated deformation is used. Fixed-degree
nilpotent differentiation avoids requiring that whole exponential disc,
but it does NOT remove these factors from the coefficient estimates.

For example the source derivative of a normalized expectation is exactly
Cov_mu(O,Qhat(f)) or Cov_mu(O,That(f)). Formula(2.5) alone leaves an
uncentered energy on the right after these divisions. A scalar contact
subtraction cannot be discarded before estimating that covariance.
Thus no inference from a bounded raw loop to an admitted canonical
dimension-six source is hidden in the proposed route.

3. NORMALIZED SCHWINGER--DYSON: WHAT IT DOES AND DOES NOT SOLVE

Let S=sum_p beta_p E_p on a finite graph with product Haar measure, and
mu_J=Z(J)^{-1}exp[-S+sum_p J_p E_p]dH. All J below are real, or complex
near a point where the finite partition function is nonzero. Put
Delta_e=sum_a X_a^2, Gamma_e(f,g)=sum_a(X_a f)(X_a g).
Haar integration by parts, BEFORE any absolute value, gives

 mu_J[Delta_e f-Gamma_e(S,f)+sum_p J_p Gamma_e(E_p,f)]=0.     (3.1)

For f=E_q, a plaquette traversing e once, Delta_e E_q=3 w(Q)/4.
Hence

 sum_p(beta_p-J_p) mu_J[Gamma_e(E_p,E_q)]
                            =3 mu_J[w(Q)]/4.               (3.2)

Only incident plaquettes occur. This is an exact finite-volume,
normalized, interacting identity. Differentiating produces normalized
covariances and the explicit derivative of beta_p-J_p. No vacuum factor
or independent conditional normalizer is introduced.

But (3.2) determines a weighted sum, not all off-diagonal Gamma insertions.
For example, one J derivative also produces unknown covariances of each
Gamma insertion with E_r. Iteration enlarges the insertion catalogue.
Equations(1.1) and(3.1) reduce it to loop words; they do not truncate word
length or provide an inverse of the loop equation with a cutoff-uniform
norm at beta~log(1/a). That inverse/estimate is the new analytic problem.

An exact Langevin interpretation is possible: for L=Delta-Gamma(S,.),
L(fg)-fLg-gLf=2Gamma(f,g). It shows which dynamic response adds Gamma to
the scalar algebra. Static scalar-coupling moments alone do not supply
this generator or its continuum domain. No continuum stochastic
quantization construction is inferred.

4. ALL SCALAR COUPLING JETS STILL LEAVE FIBER INFORMATION

Here is an elementary finite-lattice check, independent of free color
parity. Let a free-boundary four-dimensional cubical graph have n>=2
vertices in each direction. It has V=n^4 vertices, E=4(n-1)n^3 links,
and P=6(n-1)^2 n^2 elementary plaquettes. On the generic SU(2) orbit
stratum the gauge quotient has dimension

                 D=3(E-V)=9n^4-12n^3,
                 D-P=3n^2(n^2-2)>0.                       (4.1)

For completeness: tree gauge leaves E-V+1 independent SU(2) holonomies;
generic residual simultaneous conjugation has dimension3. Two
noncommuting cycle holonomies ensure the discrete generic stabilizer.

The smooth map from this stratum to the P separate plaquette traces
has rank at most P. Near a maximum-rank point the constant-rank theorem
therefore gives positive-dimensional fibers. Individual plaquette traces
do not separate all gauge orbits. Tree-gauge loop words do: pair traces
recover vector Gram data and triple traces recover orientation, including
degenerate cases by continuity. On a regular-rank open patch the quotient
Haar law has a smooth positive density; disintegration in constant-rank
coordinates has positive density along the positive-dimensional fibers.
A loop word differing along a fiber can be chosen nonconstant on such
an open fiber patch. Its conditional variance is positive on a set of
positive quotient measure. Thus some bounded invariant loop observable
is not measurable, even almost everywhere, in the plaquette-trace sigma
field. This uses the positive-density local structure, not topological
noninjectivity alone.

Let mu_0 be product Haar on the gauge quotient, choose bounded invariant
f not measurable in that sigma field E, and set

                 h=f-E_mu0[f|E].                          (4.2)

For sufficiently small real epsilon, (1+epsilon h)mu_0 is a different
positive gauge-invariant probability law. Yet its partition function
under EVERY plaquette-energy tilt equals the original Haar tilted
partition function, since E_mu0[h|E]=0. All scalar local-coupling jets
are identical, including at a Wilson tilt. The conditional fiber laws
differ.

This is an information statement, not an alternative Wilson theory or
an RP counterexample: the modified law is not claimed to satisfy the
complete Wilson SD identities or RP. It proves that a passage from all
scalar source jets to all loop/curvature fields requires additional
structure. Knowing the original lattice measure is Wilson does not
remove the need to CONTROL its extra observables in the continuum.

6. A NEW PRECISE TIME-ZERO/SPATIAL-FLOW MISMATCH

At finite lattice cutoff a deterministic time-slice map commuting with
reflection preserves RP, because its pullback preserves the positive
half-algebra. Spatial flow is compatible with this fact. On an infinite
slice it has spatial tails; a strictly local finite-region version must
declare its boundary data. Neither formulation by itself gives a local
rotation-covariant continuum net.

[CC] assumes covariance bounded in position space by G_alpha, whose
Fourier coefficients are |k|^{-alpha}. Theorem1.19 requires

                alpha>max(d-4/3,d/2).                     (6.1)

It gives a local random-time smooth heat-flow solution and convergence
of Fourier truncations under the additional stated moment assumptions.
In spatial dimension3, (6.1) is alpha>5/3. This includes the 3D GFF
covariance |k|^{-2}; it does NOT cover the time-zero restriction of a
4D free potential. Indeed, for a transverse polarization,

 integral_(R) dp0/(2pi) 1/(p0^2+|k|^2)=1/(2|k|).           (6.2)

Thus its spatial covariance exponent is alpha=1, and its positive trace
singularity is |x-y|^{-2}, too singular for the G_alpha bound with any
alpha>5/3. The transverse projector does not improve this power.
Already the Gaussian UV reference therefore fails this imported flow
theorem. This calculation does not prove impossibility of renormalized
spatial flow; it identifies the stronger random-data theory and Wilson
estimates that would be needed. A 3D Euclidean YM state-space theorem
cannot simply be applied to the equal-time law of 4D YM.

7. THE CONCRETE NEXT ESTIMATE SUGGESTED BY THE SINGLE-LOOP ROUTE

For each finite list of desired invariant-polynomial labels, choose
actual local small-loop words such as(2.1),(2.2), parallel-transported
finite differences for covariant derivatives, and ALL required lower
field/contact subtractions. Denote the resulting signed loop-word
combination for an n-point test F by nu_(a,F). Repeated(1.1) turns its
normalized moment exactly into the LINEAR functional

                    G_(a,n)(F)=m_a(nu_(a,F)),
             m_a(C)=E_Wilson,a[w(C)].                      (7.1)

This is only a representation. The coefficients of nu contain inverse
powers of a and g; ordinary |m_a(C)|<=1 destroys the cancellations.
The useful missing estimate is instead a bound on the COMPLETE signed
combination, for common successive cutoffs and renormalized local words,

 |m_(a/2)(nu_(a/2,F))-m_a(nu_(a,F))|
              <= A^n (n!)^B epsilon_a p_(s n)(F),
                  sum_(dyadic a) epsilon_a<infinity,       (7.2)

with fixed s,B and the appropriate collision-test space; compatible
one-cutoff distribution bounds and field identities must accompany it.
One whole-correlation epsilon is sufficient for the direct OS approach;
no arbitrary-copy operator-norm gain is claimed necessary.

Equation(1.2) means that, BEFORE canonical subtractions, passage from
joint loop moments to a single-loop functional costs total coefficient
mass1. The problem in(7.2) is shrinking-lobe/shape regularity and local
renormalization, not an unavoidable multitrace factorial explosion.
A useful research target is a weak-coupling loop-equation estimate on
this signed local-jet space. Neither [KZ] nor [SSZ] supplies it.

For separated loops, common-base connectors make the representation
nonlocal term by term. Their exact cancellations must remain before
estimates; no perimeter-divergent thin-loop limit is silently inserted.
Positive-flow words would still need a separate flow-to-local limit.

The criterion is non-circular in the following limited sense: (7.2) is
an inequality for explicitly given, normalized ORIGINAL Wilson integrals
of finite loop combinations. It assumes neither a continuum cubic field
nor its source theorem. It could be proved by a different method, such
as a loop-equation estimate with a uniform norm on these signed jets.
If it holds simultaneously for a countable compatible catalogue, with
the stated fixed-arity growth and the needed coincident-point extensions,
the moment limits are constructed directly. Local support and lattice
reflection identities pass along those limits. Full Euclidean covariance,
nonzero Gram matrices for the new labels and the required ultraviolet
YM matching remain separate tests; (7.2) by itself does not assert them.

In particular the nonzero classical coefficient in(2.3) is not a lower
bound on the normalized interacting two-point function of That. Conversely
the free quadratic O(3) closure obstruction does not invalidate(2.2):
its ordered trace words are additional observables, not words in the
separate quadratic trace-energy net. This is an honest enlargement of
the finite-cutoff observable catalogue.
