NEUTRAL SCALAR SOURCE WARD IDENTITIES AND CLASSICAL SCALE GAINS
TURN70. 20 October 2026. Root producer, submitted for independent audit.

RESULT AND SCOPE

A linear neutral scalar test source can be kept as an external
argument of the effective action. Joint translation and full
hypercubic covariance then remain available even though evaluating
that argument at a particular profile breaks these symmetries.
The source is a numerical probe, not a new dynamical field.

At this level the unique reflection-even marginal field vertex is
the scalar source times the Yang--Mills action. It is determined
by setting the source equal to one. A residual with that marginal
removed has the same joint low-momentum orders 4,3,2 at field
degrees 2,3,4 as in the homogeneous extraction.

There is an additional useful identity absent from the usual
momentum-conserving two-field vertex: the two field momenta are
independent, the source carries their negative sum, and both
one-leg marginal kernels vanish. The quadratic density can thus
be written with TWO field increments, anchored at the source.
No integration by parts is performed onto the test function.

This note proves the algebra and the scalar classical scale
estimate under precise kernel hypotheses. A separate carrier
localization proof and the exact source interpolation/update
admission are needed before claiming a complete sourced RG
induction. Neither an original full-law correlation theorem nor
YM4 existence follows from this lemma alone.

1. SOURCE MODULE AND ITS SYMMETRIES

For definiteness take SU2 on a four-dimensional cubic lattice.
Use positive-bond Hermitian angle coordinates A_mu(y), with
midpoint xi(y,mu)=y+e_mu/2. Let f_x be a scalar at lattice sites.
Consider a family linear in f,

 E(f,A)=sum_x f_x e_x(A).                              (1.1)

The field-independent term E(f,0) is tracked separately. The
following statements concern E(f,A)-E(f,0).

The density may have a carrier representation

 e_x(A)=sum_(H contains x) E_(H,x)(A|H),               (1.2)

with complete connected carriers, fixed cell size J, a positive
exponential support reserve and a fixed holomorphic field radius.
Individual E_(H,x) need not be gauge invariant.

Assume the TOTAL E(f,A) is gauge invariant for every finitely
supported f, on its actual common weak-field domain. Equivalently
each summed density e_x is gauge invariant there. Also assume
joint lattice translation, full signed-permutation covariance
and reality:

 E(Tf,TA)=E(f,A),   E(Rf,RA)=E(f,A).                  (1.3)

The scalar f has no color or pseudoscalar transformation.
Gauge transformations act on A and leave f fixed.

These conditions define a linear source module. Quotient
equivalence must mean equality for EVERY f and A (with source
vacua handled separately). Equality after f=1 alone is weaker
and is not used as the definition of this module.

Semisimple color invariance gives D_A e_x(0)=0. Write its
field Taylor tensors as K_(n,x), n>=2. Exponential carrier
bounds imply finite source-anchored spatial moments of every
fixed order at fixed field degree. All constants can retain
dependence on J and the fixed field radius.

For an infinite translation-covariant family, anchor x=0 and
take the Fourier transform in ALL n field displacements:

 Gamma_n(p_1,...,p_n).

The source momentum is -(p_1+...+p_n); there is no constraint
on the n displayed momenta. Their independence is essential.
Field midpoint phases are used. The scalar source is anchored
at its site, so joint inversion gives

 Gamma_n(-p_1,...,-p_n)=(-1)^n Gamma_n(p_1,...,p_n).
                                                               (1.4)

All field slots remain permutation symmetric. For SU2 the
quadratic color tensor is a multiple of the invariant form.

2. WARD EQUATIONS WITH THE NEUTRAL LEG

At each fixed f, differentiate exact lattice gauge invariance
at A=0. The Ward equations through field degree four are the
same equations for K2,K3,K4 as in the retained total-kernel
theorem, with the scalar source position carried along:

 K2(A,d lambda)=0,
 K3(A,B,d lambda)
     =K2(A,L_lambda B)+K2(B,L_lambda A),
 K4(A,B,C,d lambda)
     =sum_cyclic K3(A,B,L_lambda C)
        +2sum_cyclic K2(A,Q_lambda(B,C)).             (2.1)

Here d,L,Q are the fixed lattice gauge generators in the
positive-bond convention. They are nearest-neighbor linear
operations on the kernel list. There is no derivative or
gauge variation of f in these equations.

The quadratic Fourier relations are therefore

 sum_mu hat p_mu Gamma_(2,mu nu)(p,q)=0,
 sum_nu hat q_nu Gamma_(2,mu nu)(p,q)=0,
 hat p_mu=2sin(p_mu/2).                               (2.2)

Differentiate the second relation in q_alpha at q=0.
Since q is independent of p and d hat q_nu/dq_alpha|0
=delta_(nu alpha), one obtains

 Gamma_(2,mu alpha)(p,0)=0.

The other leg gives, identically on the analytic domain,

                    Gamma_2(p,0)=Gamma_2(0,q)=0.      (2.3)

In position space, each source-anchored quadratic kernel has
zero marginal sum in either field leg, with color and bond
direction fixed. This is an exact cancellation of the summed
density, not of each original carrier.

3. THE UNIQUE SCALAR MARGINAL

The constant quadratic momentum coefficient vanishes by (2.2).
Joint inversion removes odd total degrees. At total degree two,
(2.3) removes the pure p^2 and pure q^2 parts. Thus

 Gamma_(2,mu nu)^[2](p,q)
       =sum_(alpha,beta) T_(mu alpha nu beta)
                                      p_alpha q_beta.          (3.1)

Equation (2.2) at degree three makes T antisymmetric in
(mu,alpha) and in (nu,beta). Full signed-permutation covariance
then gives exactly

 Gamma_(2,mu nu)^[2](p,q)
       =c_E[(p.q)delta_(mu nu)-q_mu p_nu].             (3.2)

To check the tensor classification directly, a coordinate
reflection forces every coordinate label in a nonzero invariant
rank-four tensor to occur an even number of times. The possible
pairings are the three Kronecker pairings and the all-equal
hypercubic tensor. Antisymmetry kills the latter and the pairing
internal to each antisymmetric pair; it fixes the relative signs
of the remaining two. Permutations of axes leave one coefficient.

A Levi-Civita tensor would be an additional possibility with
only orientation-preserving rotations. It changes sign under
a single coordinate reflection and is excluded here. Joint
inversion alone would NOT exclude it in dimension four.

Setting f=1 imposes q=-p, and (3.2) becomes minus c_E times
the transverse Maxwell polynomial. Hence c_E is determined by
the ordinary constant-source Maxwell coefficient.

Let S(f,A) be the actual gauge-invariant jointly covariant
weighted reference, with nonzero corresponding c_S. Define

 alpha(E)=c_E/c_S,
 F(f,A)=E(f,A)-alpha(E)S(f,A).                        (3.3)

This subtracts the COMPLETE reference, not just its quadratic
jet. F remains gauge invariant for every f.

Its quadratic vertex has no joint momentum terms below
degree four. In the cubic Ward equation, the right side then
has no degree-two term. The degree-one polynomial P1 of the
residual cubic vertex is separately transverse in the three
independent field momenta. Such a polynomial is zero:
write P1=A p+B q+C k; p-transversality first forces B=C=0
from mixed monomials, and q-transversality then forces A=0.
Parity already removed the cubic constant term.

In the quartic Ward equation the residual lower vertices
produce no degree-one term on the right. Contracting the
constant quartic tensor with an arbitrary independent gauge
momentum therefore forces that constant tensor to zero.
Parity removes the degree-one quartic term.

Consequently, in the exact infinite-kernel setting,

 Gamma_2^F=O(p_*^4),
 Gamma_3^F=O(p_*^3),
 Gamma_4^F=O(p_*^2),                                  (3.4)

with p_* the maximum of the field momentum magnitudes.
The constants are bounded by the corresponding absolute
source-anchored kernel moments. These are simultaneous
low-momentum statements, not estimates at arbitrary momentum.

4. THE EXACT TWO-INCREMENT QUADRATIC REPRESENTATIVE

Fix a source site x. For each field direction mu choose its
base bond (x,mu), and put a_mu=A_mu(x). By (2.3),

 (1/2)sum_(mu,nu;y,z) K_(2,x;mu nu)(y,z)
                              A_mu(y)A_nu(z)
  =(1/2)sum K_(2,x;mu nu)(y,z)
             [A_mu(y)-a_mu][A_nu(z)-a_nu].            (4.1)

Color contractions are understood. Both cross subtractions
and the added base-base term vanish by the one-leg marginal
identities. The test factor f_x is untouched.

Using tails rather than midpoints in the displacements of
(4.1) does not alter the mixed first moment: the shift in
each direction is constant and is multiplied by a zero
one-leg marginal. Thus the absent degree-two tensor in (3.4)
implies

 sum_(y,z) K_(2,x;mu nu)(y,z)
                    (y-x)_alpha (z-x)_beta=0         (4.2)

in every relevant color/direction component.

This anchored identity is stronger than merely controlling
the homogeneous restriction Gamma_2(p,-p). It is the useful
reason for retaining the neutral source argument.

5. CLASSICAL SCALAR SCALE ESTIMATE

Let L>=1 be an endpoint scale ratio. On the admitted ordinary
classical prefix, the retained complete C^(1,alpha) bounds give
current link angles A satisfying, with 0<alpha<1,

 ||A|| <= C b/L,
 ||Delta A|| <= C b/L^2,
 |A_mu(y)-A_mu(x)-(y-x).Delta A_mu(x)|
       <= C b L^(-2-alpha)|y-x|^(1+alpha).            (5.1)

Use consistent coordinate paths and periodic lifts. The
bounds hold in the complete supported output norm as well
as pointwise under the earlier classical input theorem.
For the present section only their pointwise consequence
is being claimed.

In (4.1) write each increment as its displayed linear
spatial Taylor term plus Rem. The product of the two linear
terms vanishes by (4.2). A linear term times Rem is bounded
by C b^2 L^(-4-alpha) times its displacement moments; the
two-remainder product is smaller. Therefore

 |F_2(f,A)| per source root
       <= C ||f||infinity M_2 b^2 L^(-4-alpha),       (5.2)

where M_2 is a sufficiently high fixed absolute kernel
moment. No spatial derivative of f was used.

For the cubic term expand its three fields about x. The
constant and all terms linear in spatial displacements
cancel by the zero degree-zero and degree-one moments
from (3.4). A single Rem times two base fields costs
b^3 L^(-4-alpha); terms with two or more increments
cost at most C b^3 L^-5. Thus

 |F_3(f,A)| per root
       <= C ||f||infinity M_3 b^3 L^(-4-alpha).       (5.3)

For the quartic term the constant spatial moment is zero.
Subtracting its base-field product leaves at least one
increment, so its bound is C ||f|| M_4 b^4 L^-5.
The sum of field Taylor orders n>=5 also has scalar
size C epsilon L^-5 on an admitted smaller field domain,
by radial analytic projection, once L exceeds its fixed
classical containment constant.

Fixed-order Cauchy makes M_n<=C_n b^-n epsilon from a
carrier norm epsilon. Hence after the O(L^4) fine-root
count per final four-dimensional cell, these scalar
bounds have size

                  C epsilon ||f||infinity L^-alpha. (5.4)

This is the scalar power gain. To use it as an operator
contraction in the source quotient, the new representatives
must be localized in the same prescribed output carrier
norm, with all source roots and complete classical reads.
That construction is not replaced by this pointwise bound.

6. FINITE-PERIOD VERSION AND WHAT IS ONLY APPROXIMATE

On a finite compatible torus, form the short source-anchored
lift of K_(n,x), cutting off the diameter of the tuple that
includes x and ALL field slots. Use a cutoff T=floor(N/16),
as in the retained finite-volume Ward extraction.
This preserves joint translations, signed permutations and
field-slot permutations. The source root is never discarded.

For kernels with uniform exponential source-anchored moments,
the lifted Ward defect and each required weighted moment
are O(epsilon exp(-cN)). Indeed Ward operations only shift
or merge finitely many neighboring slots and commute with
periodization; their short lifted range is injective under
periodization. The discarded tail has the displayed bound.
This is the same geometric argument as the finite-volume
extraction, now retaining the additional source leg.

Differentiating the lifted quadratic Ward defect in the
second momentum at zero bounds Gamma_2(p,0), and in
position space its one-leg marginal, by the corresponding
defect moment. Thus (2.3) is only approximately zero for
this finite lift. Both the marginal defect and its spatial
moments must be kept in (4.1).

For exact compatibility with the established finite constant
channel, define alpha_N(E) to be its existing extraction
applied to E(1), with the existing reference S(1). Its reference
normalization is bounded away from zero by the retained
classical flat-reference theorem. This is a definition of
the augmented finite-regulator extraction.

The source-anchored short lift and the old constant-source
field-only short lift need not commute with evaluation at
one. Their difference consists of terms for which the source
is far from the field tuple or the tuple itself lies in the
discarded tail. Source-anchored exponential bounds control
the required fixed moments of that difference by
C epsilon exp(-cN), with a fixed exponent reserve. Thus the
two extraction prescriptions agree only up to that error.
It is not permissible to declare them exactly identical.

With alpha_N defined through the old constant extraction,
the source-anchored finite-dimensional tensor algebra
then gives residual low jets O(epsilon exp(-cN)).
All new remainder terms have their scalar field-size
bounds and explicit exponentially small kernel factors.
For example a quadratic marginal error at the classical
prefix is bounded per source root by
C epsilon exp(-cN)L^-2; after a root count it is bounded
by C epsilon L^2 exp(-cN).

For a direct prefix from N=L N_out current sites per side,
N_out>=N_min, this displayed error tends to zero uniformly
as L grows. Finite composition also has extraction leakage
and its already defined reference normalization. Neither
exact finite-torus vanishing nor regulator consistency is
asserted merely by proving the infinite-kernel polynomial
identity. A full iteration must retain these errors.

8. RETAINED SCOPE

No new scalar matter field is introduced. The neutral f is
only a bookkeeping argument for physical action insertions.
No source-dependent windows or covariance charts are varied.
The original full Wilson probability law is not replaced by
the selected branch, and an isotropic-to-anisotropic source
identification remains a separate obligation.

The proof above does not control the source vacuum cumulants,
the mixed contribution after separately transported marks
meet, every native R/B history, or a nonzero continuum
correlation. It supplies a new algebraic and scalar scale
estimate for the actual source problem when its stated
module hypotheses have been verified.
