The bound is linear in its original typed-domain norm. It has

2. Mark class and its elementary incidence bound

Let D_i(H) be a marked E-type potential on its original E
support/domain, or an R-type or B-type mark on its own type.
In the B case take the supremum also over admitted histories
and live variables. All linear covariance, locality and
support conditions of that input type are retained.

Fix kappa_c above the relevant animal-entropy constants and
below the unmarked native strong exponent. Put

 epsilon_i = sup_(H,roles)
       exp[kappa_c d_i(H)] ||D_i(H)||_(original full domain).
                                                               (2.1)

If several rooted roles occur, include their finite multiplicity
in this definition or in a fixed prefactor. Since the number
of connected H of size m through one native cell is at most
C exp(c_ent m), choosing kappa_c>c_ent gives

 sup_x sum_(H contains x,roles) ||D_i(H)||
                                      <= C epsilon_i.    (2.2)

There is deliberately NO assertion that the same sum with
weight exp(kappa_c d_i(H)) is finite. This distinction prevents
using up an unrecorded spatial reserve at every step.

For a current component X, fixed-map admission and old-cell
counting therefore give, pointwise on every admitted history,

 |sum_(i,H,roles in X) D_i(H;Phi)| <= E_k v_k(X),
 E_k=C sum_(i<=k) L^[4(k-i)] P(R_i,R_k) epsilon_i.          (2.3)

Additional finite preparation costs (1.1) can be included in
E_k. They do not change its permitted fixed polynomial/
exponential dependence on age and endpoint couplings.

An E mark is not renamed R to exploit the latter's smallness
bound: the admissible supports differ. A B mark keeps its live
A type. Before extraction one may simply retain the whole
marked potential. If scalar or quadratic jets are separated,
they stay explicitly in the marked state and are recombined
exactly; they are not dropped or absorbed by resetting g.

3. Root-protected arbitrary-mark localization

Fix one H and suppress its date. By N1,

 f(s)=D(H;Phi_s)

is analytic for all |s_Delta|<R_s and satisfies

                  |f(s)| <= ||D(H)||.                   (3.1)

The key fact is that the bound is on the ENTIRE function.
No expansion in its individual field coordinates is used.
In particular no factor exp(c|H|) is introduced by summing
Taylor monomials of an arbitrary input function.

First work with finitely many decoupling parameters. Iterated
fundamental theorem of calculus gives the exact identity

 f(1)=sum_(S subset parameters) integral_[0,1]^S
              (partial_S f)(s_S,0_(S complement)) ds_S. (3.2)

For m=|S| the mixed derivative uses each parameter ONCE.
The polydisc Cauchy estimate about s_S in [0,1]^S yields

 |partial_S f| <= (R_s-1)^(-m) ||D(H)||.                 (3.3)

Use a slightly smaller contour if a closed-radius equality
is inconvenient. There is NO m!: the derivative multiindex
has every nonzero entry equal to one, so its factorial is
the product of m copies of 1!.

By N2, a set S containing a disconnected component which
does not meet the protected root contributes zero. The
remaining terms have a complete support Y containing H_0.
Regrouping at FIXED H and FIXED complete output carrier Y
uses only the choices of added blocks in that carrier. It
does not sum over every place where a branch can attach to
an arbitrarily large H_0. For sufficiently large fixed kappa_1
this gives

 ||W_(i,H;Y)|| <= C_(i,k) ||D_i(H)||
                    exp[-c kappa_1 n_new(Y,H_0)].        (3.4)

Here n_new denotes the actual added localization/walk blocks,
with the native bounded collar convention. All read blocks
are retained. A walk leaving and returning to H_0 cannot be
declared to have no added support.
There is no unaccounted |H_0|^m factor in (3.4). When we later
sum over output carriers or move the root, the number of
attachments to H_0 can grow with its size. For example a
bounded-degree graph admits a bound C^(|H_0|+m) on connected
unions with m added blocks, rather than a uniform C^m bound
after summing over their placements. The root factor, collars,
and native block-volume choices are charged to a fixed
exp[delta kappa_c d_i(H)] reserve in Section4 and to the
explicit polynomial volume factors in E_k. They are not
silently absorbed into an absolute per-new-block constant.

The static term S=empty is retained on H_0. If the native
preparation uses D(Phi(B))-D(Phi(0)), apply the identity to
each value, or use its prescribed finite fluctuation
interpolation. Finite Taylor jets can be represented by
Cauchy integrals before the s localization. Their finite
radius/age costs are precisely (1.1). This does not alter
the per-new-block bound or introduce a derivative order
equal to the number of field coordinates in H.

The asserted theorem is finite-volume, with constants uniform
in that volume. An infinite-parameter limit also needs
consistency or convergence of each fixed finite localizer
under exhaustion, in addition to its summable majorant.
Only after importing that further consistency does dominated
convergence give the corresponding infinite-volume formulas.
Neither cover matching nor that consistency follows from
N1--N2 alone.

This is a fresh arbitrary-mark extension of the imported
localization maps. It uses their analytic range and locality,
not an identity theorem on a lower-dimensional physical slice.

4. One fixed spatial exponent

The original factor exp[-kappa_c d_i(H)] in (2.1) survives

the sum over H and marked root placements. The remaining

native inequalities B116(1.31)--(1.32).

At conversion to the output scale use the ACTUAL native
linear-size inequalities and cluster reserves, not a claim
that adjacent rounded MR_j meshes have physical ratio L.
B116 p19 (2.36) gives

                    2 d_k(Z_i) >= L d_(k+1)(Z_i').

Its (2.38) retains (1-8delta)L/2 after activity grouping,
and its final (2.41) retains (1-10delta)L/2 after the
connected-log and root-volume summations. Thus the
sufficient fixed-reserve condition is

              (1-10delta)L/2 > 1+4b,                    (4.1)

not the earlier proposed L(1-2delta)>1+4b. Choose L>2
and fixed small delta,b>0 meeting (4.1), with delta kappa_c
and b kappa_c above the original-support, attachment and
root-placement entropy thresholds. Choose kappa_1 large
enough for the new-block sums and the remaining native
constant restrictions. All choices precede the weak floor.
If the already chosen constants lack these reserves, the
same-exponent return is conditional on supplying them.

The new regular marked T birth then retains a pointwise
exponent (1+4b)kappa_c in output units. The source uses

are unchanged. This also agrees with the improved-T input

For old dates i<k, the B122-II(1.66) calculation can be run

exp[-(1+3b)kappa_c d_k(H_0)]. The former pays the old

The regular T/bulk output therefore returns the same full

5. The actual occupied-support coefficient disc

After the preceding preparation, let W_Y be the complete
marked fluctuation potential and let V_Y be the unmarked
native potential. Keep fixed the current numerical g,
the reference Gaussian and every actual cut domain.
Insert a scalar coefficient lambda by

                         V_Y -> V_Y+lambda W_Y.           (5.1)

B116(2.1)--(2.3) retains the fluctuation cutoff on the union
of OCCUPIED interaction supports and expands its complement
only. Treat W exactly as another occupied potential. Its
domain of evaluation is consequently the same admitted
domain used in the preparation; it is never evaluated on
an unrestricted Gaussian fluctuation outside that support.

For each local factor one has

 |exp(V+lambda W)-exp(V)|
       <= |lambda| |W| exp(|V|+|lambda| |W|).             (5.2)

Use N4 quantitatively. By (3.4) and Section4, a source of
size |lambda|E_k changes the dimensionless occupied-support
majorant by at most

 C |lambda| E_k g_k^(-A)(1+|log g_k|)^B

on a fixed small neighborhood. Choose P>A+1 and fixed c>0
so that, throughout the admitted weak interval,

 C c g_k^(P-A)(1+|log g_k|)^B <= eta_*/2.

Then rho_k=c g_k^P is an admissible radius in the normalized
source size: |lambda|E_k<=rho_k. This is a polynomial LOWER
bound on the available radius, not an inference from an
unspecified positive margin. The elementary bound follows
because g^(P-A)(1+|log g|)^B tends to zero as g tends to zero.
All dated preparation factors are already in E_k; none
is discarded in taking this uniform current-scale margin.

This is a PER-CELL condition, independent of total volume.
It does not require |lambda| times the total number of cells
to be small. The extra exponential in (5.2) is an extensive
but locally bounded action, exactly the situation controlled
by the native polymer census.

The unchanged Gaussian domination and the absolutely
convergent supported polymer series therefore define all
local connected coefficients holomorphically on this disc.
Equivalently, differentiating the product singles out one
marked activity; B119(3.44) is the corresponding actual
one-distinguished-insertion numerator construction.

After normalizing the incoming weighted dated majorant
to E_k=1, Cauchy on a smaller disc gives

 sup_(regular output Y) exp[kappa_c d_out(Y)]
        ||d/dlambda L_Y(lambda)|_(lambda=0)||
                             <= C rho_k^(-1).            (5.3)

Restoring the input size gives C E_k/rho_k. For a finite
difference, the same bound holds with a fixed extra factor
provided E_k<=rho_k/2 along the admitted segment. Its cost
rho_k^(-1) is a fixed power of the inverse coupling, which
fits the polynomial response class of the winding lemma.
Formula (5.3) concerns the occupied-support REGULAR connected
coefficients. It is not the norm of the complete unresummed
post-R B file. That file has the relative-boundary/interior
split stated at the end of Section4.

Scalar static terms are carried outside the fluctuation
logarithm or included exactly in its local scalar file.
Their exponentials cause no zero. Quadratic marks are
small bounded occupied potentials on this disc; their
subsequent extraction has its finite Cauchy cost recorded
separately. No artificial adjustment of the baseline
coupling is made while proving (5.3).

6. Raw large-field source: precise adjoining primitive

On a fixed actual component history, the same marked action
has the bound (2.3). In the RAW component formula introduce
exp(lambda Delta_X) and retain all original numerator and
denominator roles. Against its positive native majorant,

 |exp(lambda Delta_X)| <= exp(|lambda| E_k v_k(X)).

If the latter per-cell cost is below the retained native
spatial/action reserve, the unchanged positive census
gives the same raw component mass bound with a fixed
smaller reserve. Cauchy then yields a one-mark raw
operator estimate of the form

 ||delta T_X|| <= C (E_k/rho_k)
                exp[-c p0(g_k)-kappa_c d_k(X)].           (6.1)

This adjoining argument requires the actual source to
enter all its local action roles with the stated bound.
It is not a theorem about a component known only through
its integrated mass. In particular one does NOT divide
by T_X(lambda)1. Such a mass may have complex zeros on
an inverse-volume scale; the raw estimate needs no
componentwise zero-free disc.

The regular connected logarithm in Section5 is justified
by its occupied-support cluster expansion. It must not
be confused with a logarithm of an arbitrary retained
large-field mass. Source-dependent normalizers elsewhere
in the actual formula must either be fixed classical
normalizers or retain their separately proved localized
response. Their treatment is part of the companion raw
source analysis, not inferred from positivity alone.
