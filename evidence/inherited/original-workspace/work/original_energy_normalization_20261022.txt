RESULT

The raw insertion beta_W(a) sum_p f_p h_p is a BARE canonical action
density. It is not automatically the canonically normalized scalar at a
FIXED physical endpoint coupling. An endpoint-conjugate definition gives

 O_a(f) = t_R (d t_a/d t_R) sum_p f_p h_p,
 Z_E(a) = [t_R (d t_a/d t_R)]/t_a,                       (A)

where t_a is the ACTUAL coefficient multiplying the normalized Wilson
plaquette sum, and t_R is the endpoint coefficient in the same convention.
Centering can be added without changing separated covariances.

If dt_a/dt_R stays bounded above and below, (A) removes the growing bare
coefficient from the elementary insertion bound. At one-loop t_a=t_R+bN,
so dt_a/dt_R=1 and O_a=t_R sum f h exactly. This is a substantive reason
not to pay a bare t_a power before deciding the physical normalization.

The archive supplies a uniform CONSTANT-source tangent in its homogeneous
SELECTED frozen-window scheme. It does not supply a differentiable inverse
of the complete native trajectory, nor local inhomogeneous source bounds.
An analytic beta function alone would not supply those local bounds.

1. KEEP THE COUPLING CONVENTIONS DISTINCT

Throughout this note

 h_p(U)=1-(1/2)Tr U_p,  H_f=sum_p f(x_p)h_p,
 dmu_a=Z_a^-1 exp[-t_a H_1] product_b dU_b.               (1)

Plaquettes are counted once for each mu<nu. f is sampled at the plaquette
center, consistently with its orientation. There is no extra a^4 in H_f:
the smooth-field expansion already places a^4 inside h_p.

In conventional SU(2) notation with generators sigma_a/2,
t_a=4/g_0,phys^2. B109(0.2), printed252, uses the normalized trace and
starts with exp[-g_0,native^-2 A], where A=sum h_p in d=4. Thus its
native inverse coupling IS t_a, and g_0,phys=2g_0,native under the above
generator convention. The archive notation beta_bare=g_0^-2 is legitimate
when g_0 is native. It must not simultaneously be read as the conventional
physical coupling without the factor four.

For U_p=exp[i g_phys a^2 F_mu,nu^a sigma_a/2+...],

 h_p=(g_phys^2 a^4/8) sum_a(F_mu,nu^a)^2+...,
 (4/g_phys^2)H_f ~ integral f E,  E=(1/2)sum_{a,mu<nu}F^2. (2)

This is the E used by work/construction_route_20261021.txt, whose three-
color Maxwell covariance is 9/(pi^4 |x-y|^8). Using g_phys^-2 H_f with
the SAME physical g would instead give E/4, and covariance 9/(16 pi^4
|x-y|^8). This factor-four warning is a convention audit, not a statement
that the archive's native beta_bare is intrinsically wrong.

Equation(2) is classical calibration, not a quantum composite-operator
renormalization theorem.

2. AN EXACT FINITE-CUTOFF ENDPOINT-CONJUGATE DEFINITION

Fix one endpoint scale, one real endpoint t_R>0, and a chosen bare
trajectory a -> t_a(t_R). Assume locally for the moment that t_a(T)
is C^2 in an endpoint parameter T, with t_a(t_R) the SAME baseline bare
coefficient used in (1). Define

 q_a=t_a'(t_R), r_a=t_a''(t_R),
 c_a=t_R q_a, d_a=t_R^2 r_a,
 O_a(f)=c_a H_f.                                         (3)

This is a literal local gauge-invariant observable under the original
finite Wilson measure. The multiplicative renormalization relative to
the raw action insertion t_a H_f is Z_E=c_a/t_a. One may subtract
c_a E_mu H_f; it only changes scalar first jets.

The literal exponential insertion exp[s O_a(f)+t O_a(h)] is EXACTLY
the Wilson integral with affine plaquette coefficients

                 t_a - c_a s f_p - c_a t h_p.            (4)

There is no bare mixed insertion in this affine source convention.
For f=h=1 its first tangent agrees with decreasing the endpoint T by
t_R s. The full nonlinear endpoint curve is a different source family.

To keep that distinction explicit, define the finite integral with
plaquette coefficient t_a(t_R-t_R s f_p-t_R t h_p). Its logarithmic
source exponent has first/mixed jets

 D_f=c_a H_f,    D_h=c_a H_h,    D_fh=-d_a H_(fh).         (5)

Consequently, at the SAME baseline measure,

 partial_st log[Z_a(s,t)/Z_a(0,0)]|0
       =c_a^2 Cov_mu(H_f,H_h)-d_a E_mu H_(fh).           (6)

For disjoint sampled supports f_p h_p=0, the second term is exactly zero.
If the endpoint is instead parameterized exponentially, T=t_R exp[-s f-t h],
the mixed insertion is -(c_a+d_a)H_(fh). These are contact terms from
parameterization; neither may be omitted when supports overlap.

Equations(3)--(6) require no continuum theorem. Applying t_a pointwise to
T(x) merely DEFINES an inhomogeneous bare source family. It does not claim
that inhomogeneous RG is obtained by pointwise substitution into the
homogeneous beta function.

For any finite number of distinct source parameters in the affine
convention(4), each order-n jet is multiplied by c_a^n relative to raw
H-insertions. Relative to t_a H-insertions it is multiplied by Z_E^n.
The elementary single-plaquette bound is 2|c_a|, not 2t_a. Raw scalar
vacuum/contact terms remain part of the exact sourced partition integral.

3. THE TANGENT OF A DISCRETE RG MAP

If the full endpoint map is Phi_a:t_a -> t_R and has Phi_a'(t_a) !=0,
the inverse-function formulas are

 q_a=1/Phi_a'(t_a),
 r_a=-Phi_a''(t_a)/(Phi_a'(t_a))^3.                       (7)

Thus the missing scalar input is a uniform nonvanishing endpoint
Jacobian, not merely existence of a tuned bare coupling. In a purely
scalar recursion t_(j+1)=t_j-b_j(t_j),

 p_(j+1)=(1-b_j'(t_j))p_j,  p_0=1,
 v_(j+1)=(1-b_j'(t_j))v_j-b_j''(t_j)p_j^2,  v_0=0,       (8)

and Phi'=p_N, Phi''=v_N. If, in addition to a positive drift,
|b_j'|<=C t_j^-2 and |b_j''|<=C t_j^-3 uniformly, then
sum t_j^-2=O(t_R^-1), sum t_j^-3=O(t_R^-2) give

 q_a=1+O(t_R^-1),  r_a=O(t_R^-2),                       (9)

uniformly in depth, at sufficiently weak endpoint. This is an elementary
conditional deduction. The actual recursion also carries irrelevant
actions and prior coupling dependence; its total coupled derivatives
must replace (8). A claim that beta is analytic, without uniform derivative
bounds on a common domain and control of that coupled response, does not
prove (9).

The exact one-loop test t_a=t_R+bN has q=1,r=0. It yields

 Z_E=t_R/t_a,        c_a=t_R,

while the raw insertion t_a H corresponds to (t_a/t_R) times the
endpoint-conjugate candidate. This test exposes the potentially
unnecessary bare-power cost; it is not a proof of the interacting limit.

In physical-coupling variables, T=4/g_R^2, t_a=4/g_0^2,

 Z_E=(g_R/g_0)(d g_0/d g_R),
 O_a(f)=4 g_R^-2 q_a H_f.                               (10)

If an exact autonomous RG flow were available, its tangent would be
dg_0/dg_R=beta(g_0)/beta(g_R). Formula(10) would then agree with the
usual beta/g ratio for the scalar density. This autonomous-flow identity
is not an assertion that the native discrete beta_j is one such function.

4. WHAT THE ARCHIVE ACTUALLY SUPPLIES FOR THE SCALAR TANGENT

B109 printed255--256(0.18),(0.20) defines discrete coupling changes
through effective-action beta coefficients. Printed259 Theorem2 announces
endpoint tuning but says its proof will be supplied separately. LII
printed355 explicitly retains the weak-coupling trajectory assumption
because that perturbative proof was unpublished there. These statements
do not supply (7)--(9).

The newer archive's physical_determinant_drift_turn49.txt section10.5
proves finite selected endpoint shooting by continuity and an intermediate
value argument. It explicitly does not prove uniqueness or consistency
of the tuned choices as the depth changes. Continuous shooting alone
cannot be differentiated to obtain q_a.

There IS a useful stronger local normalization device in
constant_original_source_trajectory_turn49.txt, sections1--6. With every
baseline window and chart frozen, the actual constant bare insertion
exp[zeta H_1] produces a selected effective coefficient

                t_R - alpha_N(zeta),

where alpha_N(0)=0 and, on a depth-uniform source disk,
alpha_N(zeta)=zeta+O(S_N zeta) in the stated Wiener norm,
S_N=sum_j g_j^4=O(t_R^-1), under that note's hypotheses.

For S_N small, alpha_N'(0) is nonzero. Define an analytic local coordinate
by the inverse alpha_N(zeta)=t_R-T, and set

 t_a,fr(T)=t_a(t_R)-zeta(T).

Then at T=t_R,

 q_a,fr=1/alpha_N'(0),
 r_a,fr=alpha_N''(0)/(alpha_N'(0))^3.                    (11)

The fixed source disk gives depth-uniform bounds on these derivatives;
q_a,fr=1+O(S_N). No claim of the sharper r=O(t_R^-2) is needed here.
This gives a concrete bounded normalization coefficient c_a=t_R q_a,fr
for a literal ORIGINAL Wilson observable (3), with the same baseline
bare trajectory. The coordinate's calibration was defined by the
selected frozen-window extraction, and this qualification must remain.

It is NOT the inverse of re-running all windows/cutoffs as the bare
coupling changes. It does NOT prove that the complete original law has
the selected endpoint response or the canonical local energy limit.
The full-source comparison is still needed. Nevertheless it is a valid
finite-regulator normalization convention, better justified than simply
assuming Z_E=1, and it can avoid an unnecessary bare t_a factor in the
next source-admission problem. A fixed multiple t_R H_f is also an
explicit candidate; the finite calibration factor q_a,fr then remains
to be matched in the eventual physical comparison.

5. WHY TRACE ANOMALY IS A CHECK, NOT A CONSTRUCTIVE SHORTCUT

Suzuki, arXiv:1304.0533, section2, uses connection curvature with action
(4g_0^2)^-1 integral F^2. Its equations(2.15)--(2.21) relate the
renormalized scalar operator and the trace anomaly through the beta
function, with vacuum subtraction and an established renormalized
theory. After converting curvature conventions this is consistent with
the beta/g ratio in (10). It does not establish the needed nonperturbative
Wilson local-operator limit or identify native discrete beta_j with that
renormalization scheme. Importing that identity as an already constructed
YM4 energy field would assume the missing local theory.

The finite-cutoff coupling derivative in sections2--3 is the sound input
here. A future local renormalization theorem must additionally identify
its non-vacuum scalar component, control possible irrelevant/contact
terms, and establish separated Schwinger bounds. A constant coupling
derivative controls only an integrated response. It does not provide
arbitrary f,h, spatial source-arm decay, all histories, or rotation limits.
Even uniform q_a does not bound vacuum cumulants of Z_a.
