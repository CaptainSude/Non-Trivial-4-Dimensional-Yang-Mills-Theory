The domain inclusion is exact because every cohort uses the SAME absolute
reference-grade array K_ref L^(d(r-j))rho and fixed read/test geometry.
Increasing rho enlarges every witness inequality and hence its orbit
union. This is not an inclusion between arbitrary mixed-history domains.

The strict growth estimate is correct: before capping, b_n>=a_i and
C_R delta<= (1-lambda_*/Lambda)a_i imply
Lambda(b_n-C_R delta)>=lambda_* b_n. The separately stated absorbing-cap
condition keeps a capped cohort at rho_core. That condition must remain
valid at every later date under consideration, as is ensured by the
declared weak regime; it is not only a statement about its first age.

For every uncapped target age n, the hitting-time bound gives
n<=C log x_i. Bounded signed x increments keep all those x comparable
to x_i at a common weak floor. The derivative of log a(x) is bounded
by C_q/x, so the accumulated relative change is O(log x_i/x_i), strictly
less than log lambda_* at that floor. Hence a_(i+n)<lambda_*a_i,
whereas rho_(i,n)>=lambda_*^n a_i for n>=1. Capped cohorts instead
contain a_(i+n) because every fresh radius is below rho_core.

The index matching is right: at current step j the old cohort's target
age is j+1-i, and the new background class is at absolute grade j+1.
Their inclusion supplies precisely the common domain required before
the source map at noise delta_j is applied. It does not compare an old
grade-i domain directly with a grade-(j+1) field in unchanged units.

The lemma therefore supplies the common B_out needed by the new-ledger
pre-integration compiler and new quadratic births. It preserves physical
values only with the separately stated minimum/mother-cube sandwich.
It proves no catalogue norm, marginal eligibility or current-stabilizer
symmetry, core admission, or boundary/reset class. Those limitations in
the source lemma are accurate and should be retained.
