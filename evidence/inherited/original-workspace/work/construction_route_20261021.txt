1. PRECISE BENCHMARK AND NORMALIZATION

Take three independent colors of free Maxwell field in R^4, with

  E A_mu^a(x) A_nu^b(y) = delta_ab delta_munu /(4 pi^2 |x-y|^2)

in a convenient covariance gauge. Only separated curvature correlations
are used, so the formula can equivalently be interpreted through the
gauge-independent curvature covariance. Put

  E(x) = (1/2) sum_{a=1}^3 sum_{mu<nu} :F_mu,nu^a(x)^2: .       (1)

This is the conventional (1/4) sum over all ordered mu,nu. At distinct
points x,y, its connected covariance is

  K_0(x,y) = A |x-y|^(-8),       A = 9/pi^4.                   (2)

Derivation, including factors. If r=x-y and
I_mu,nu(r)=delta_mu,nu-2r_mu r_nu/|r|^2, differentiation of
(4 pi^2 |r|^2)^(-1) gives the curvature covariance matrix on the six
coordinates mu<nu as

  C_mu,nu;rho,sigma(r)
    = [I_mu,rho I_nu,sigma-I_mu,sigma I_nu,rho]/(pi^2 |r|^4).

The induced matrix wedge^2 I is orthogonal on this six-dimensional
space. Hence sum_{mu<nu,rho<sigma} C_mu,nu;rho,sigma(r)^2
=6/(pi^4 |r|^8). Wick's rule gives a factor 2 for each squared pair;
the factors (1/2)^2 in (1), and three colors, yield
3*(1/4)*2*6=9. This proves (2).

No coincident value of E(x)^2 or classical probability law for E is
asserted. All pairings below have separated supports.

2. A FIXED SMOOTH PROFILE WITH AN EXPLICIT POSITIVE-ENERGY MARGIN

Fix a physical length tau>0. Let rho be any nonnegative C_c^infinity
function on R^4 of integral one, supported in the Euclidean ball of
radius epsilon <= tau/1000 about zero. Define

  f_0(x) = rho(x-tau e_0),
  f_1(x) = rho(x-2tau e_0),
  F = E(f_0)-E(f_1).

Let theta be time reflection. The reflected Gaussian Gram norm is

  D_0 = sum_{i,j=0}^1 (-1)^(i+j) K_0(theta f_i,f_j).           (3)

Then

                    D_0 >= A/(400 tau^8).                    (4)

Proof. Replacing each profile by the point mass at its center gives

  A tau^(-8) d,
  d=2^(-8)-2*3^(-8)+4^(-8)>1/300.

Every point in each reflected-positive pair lies within 2 epsilon
of the corresponding center separation; that separation is at least
2 tau. The function |z|^(-8) has gradient norm 8|z|^(-9).
Thus each normalized pair average differs from its center value by at
most 16 epsilon (2tau-2epsilon)^(-9). The sum of absolute coefficients
in (3) is four, so the total error is at most

  64 A epsilon (2tau-2epsilon)^(-9)
    <= (64/1000) A (1.9)^(-9) tau^(-8)
    < A tau^(-8)/1200.

Consequently D_0 > A tau^(-8)(1/300-1/1200)
=A/(400 tau^8). The inequalities are deliberately conservative.

All supports in (3) are uniformly disjoint. The construction uses a
bounded number of fixed smooth tests, not a cutoff-dependent optimization.
The continuum source f_0-f_1 has integral zero. A translated discrete
representative has exactly zero lattice sum as well.

3. ORIGINAL-LAW COMPARISON CRITERION

Let O_a(f) be a consistently defined renormalized local gauge-invariant
energy insertion under the original normalized lattice measure mu_a.
Its multiplicative normalization and field-dependent mixing must be
specified by the construction. Subtracting a scalar mean has no effect
on the connected covariances below.

Let f_{0,a},f_{1,a} be compatible lattice representatives of the profiles
in section 2, with f_{1,a} an exact positive time translate of f_{0,a}
by tau_a tending to tau. Suppose the four reflected covariance entries
satisfy, for all sufficiently small a,

 | Cov_mu_a(O_a(theta f_{i,a}), O_a(f_{j,a}))
                - K_0(theta f_i,f_j) |
       <= A/(3200 tau^8),             i,j in {0,1}.           (5)

Then the exact original-law reflected norm of

  F_a=O_a(f_{0,a})-O_a(f_{1,a})

satisfies

             OS_a(F_a,F_a) >= A/(800 tau^8).                 (6)

Indeed expand the connected reflected norm into its four entries.
The error is at most four times (5), namely A/(800 tau^8).
Subtract this from (4). Mean subtraction can be made explicitly; with
exact time translation and translation-invariant mu_a the two means
are equal and cancel. No diagonal same-support variance is used.

If the state is translation and reflection invariant, the two off-diagonal
entries agree, so only three real numbers need be estimated. Without that
identity retain all four entries; nothing is discarded.

For actual lattice comparison it is equally valid to replace K_0 in (5)
by a verified lattice Maxwell benchmark, provided its four-entry error
relative to (3) has already been allotted part of the fixed margin. A
quadratic Taylor coefficient by itself is not this comparison theorem.

4. WHY THIS IS A DYNAMICAL WITNESS, CONDITIONAL ON RECONSTRUCTION

Suppose one common local continuum hierarchy and an OS reconstruction
have separately been established, and the four entries in (3) with the
actual interacting field are their compatible limits. Write v for the
OS vector of O(f_0), and let H>=0 be the reconstructed time-translation
generator. By positive time translation, the limit vector of F_a is

                     w=(1-exp(-tau H))v.

Equation (6) proves ||w||^2 >= A/(800 tau^8). Since
1-exp(-tau E)=0 at E=0, w is orthogonal to the complete zero-energy
subspace. Thus it cannot arise solely from a mixture of different
constant vacuum values. This is positive-energy content, not a mass gap.

This argument neither assumes convergence of Euclidean same-support
variances nor a common complex disc for the full source exponential.
The mixed source derivatives at separated supports suffice for this
particular consumer. The local hierarchy, full Euclidean covariance,
physical field identification and reconstruction remain independent
obligations. A free field can pass this witness; passing it alone does
not establish interacting non-Abelian Yang--Mills identification.

5. WHICH MODEL ESTIMATE WOULD ACTUALLY ADVANCE THE CONSTRUCTION

For the conventional SU(2) Wilson action beta sum_p h_p with
h_p=1-(1/2)Tr U_p and beta=4/g_0^2, a formal classical normalization is

  beta sum_p f(x_p) h_p ~ integral f(x) E(x) dx.

This follows from U_p=exp(i g_0 a^2 F^a sigma_a/2+...) and
h_p=g_0^2 a^4 |F|^2/8+... per orientation. It identifies a candidate
insertion only. It does not determine the renormalized Z_a or mixing
needed for (5), and no quantum estimate is inferred from the expansion.

A useful next complete theorem would prove, on an actual finite-volume
weak-coupling trajectory, a bound of the form

  |C_a(f_i,f_j)-K_0(f_i,f_j)| <= eta(g_R) tau^(-8)+r_a,
  eta(g_R) -> 0 as g_R -> 0,    r_a -> 0,

for the fixed reflected pairs above, with the original denominator and
all histories retained. Fix one sufficiently small but positive g_R
once, then take a -> 0; do not send the entire theory to its free limit.
The numerical threshold in (5) supplies a concrete acceptance test.

The difficult estimate is exactly this full-law comparison, including
integrated/crossing sources, used ancestry credits, normalized retained
covariances and regenerated marginal tensors. Neither the positivity
of the unmarked measure nor finite-jet differentiation removes it.
The current homogeneous smaller-core result is a potential local input,
not a proof of this comparison.
