RESULT

Two improvements are concrete. Constraint ranks need not be interpolated:
padding absent multiplier rows by identities gives an exact common-space
resolvent whose distinguished defect includes the FULL changed averaging
footprints. Also, on one genuinely weak coarse patch, a tapered ordinary
datum admits an analytic homogeneous minimum by the actual CMP102 theorem.
Two such completions agreeing near a source have exponentially close local
minimum/current reads. This is an anchored error, not a raw-history owner.

The general adaptive-to-ordinary comparison remains unproved. The native
domain does not automatically put the adaptive reconstruction, the new
completion and every localization contour in one common minimum chart.
The actual source's near+far preparation offers a separate route that
does not require this new comparison; see the companion neutral-source
preparation note.

2. THE COMMON UNION-ROW INVERSE

At the SAME background/gauge chart on a fixed finite field space, index
all rows present in either geometry by their union. Let P_a select active
rows, and extend Q_a by zero on absent rows. Set

 L_a = [ Delta_a, Q_a^t ; Q_a, I-P_a ],       a=0,1.       (2.1)

The inactive multipliers decouple with an identity. On the active block
this is the original constrained saddle problem. Thus its invertibility
is exactly that of the original problem; no false inverse on zero rows
is taken and no varying-rank path is needed. The exact identity is

 L_1^-1-L_0^-1 = L_1^-1(L_0-L_1)L_0^-1.                  (2.2)

For prescribed linear right sides d_a, its field/multiplier solutions
satisfy

 y_1-y_0 = L_1^-1[(d_1-d_0)+(L_0-L_1)y_0].              (2.3)

Every term has a distinguished datum or operator difference. If its
primitive inputs differ only on actual row supports S, the expanded
paths visit S. Q is a finite-support HYPEREDGE: every link in its whole
average/read footprint is retained. The dummy-row defect also belongs
to that complete row, not merely to its displayed root.

The native gauge precision depends on the geometry. Its difference is
expanded product by product, using the inverse identity again for its
row-space and massive scalar inverses. It is generally not a diagonal
operator supported on S. The correct assertion is that its expanded
paths contain a changed row. Comparing Delta at two different unknown
minima instead would introduce a field difference everywhere and would
not prove this support statement.

There is an exact nonlinear analogue on a COMMON admitted chart. Write
the two equations as L_a y_a=h_a(y_a). Then

 y_1-y_0=(I-L_1^-1 A)^-1 L_1^-1
       [h_1(y_0)-h_0(y_0)+(L_0-L_1)y_0],                 (2.4)

where A=integral_0^1 Dh_1(y_0+t(y_1-y_0))dt. When the native small
Picard bound applies to this whole segment, its Neumann series retains
the distinguished difference and every complete read. Equation(2.4)
does not itself admit that segment or assign the two equations the same
domain. It isolates exactly what the common-domain theorem must supply.

3. EXPLICIT ORDINARY COMPLETION ON ONE WEAK COARSE PATCH

Here the input is a finite coarse patch P with all endpoint-average,
gauge and source collars included, in one coherent analytic frame.
Suppose its coarse link datum v has logarithms A_e with

       ||A||_infinity <= delta,

on that whole patch, with a strict margin. Keep P a fixed number of
current coarse cells; constants below may depend on its size, not on
the fine mesh or total volume. Native proper-cube gauges and relative
averaging give this input on a protected weak patch when their full
footprints lie inside that cube. It is not inferred at a mixed seam.

Choose a fixed scalar cutoff chi on the coarse bonds, equal to1 on an
inner protected collar P0 and0 near the outer edge. Define the complete
ordinary coarse datum by

 v_chi(e)=exp(chi(e) A_e) inside P,   v_chi(e)=1 outside.   (3.1)

This is an explicit holomorphic map. For real compact-group data in
the chosen weak frame it stays compact-group valued. All its links
are delta-small, hence every datum plaquette is bounded by C delta.
For the ordinary homogeneous geometry there is no new mixed-level
interface in this datum. Take the flat real base V0=1 in Proposition9;
choose its epsilon1 a fixed sufficiently large multiple of delta.
For delta below a fixed threshold, (172) and Theorem1's hypotheses
hold on a slightly larger complex ball. Therefore

                 U_chi=P_hom(v_chi)                     (3.2)

exists analytically, has its actual physical current, and obeys the
native local estimates with amplitude C delta. Its coarse averages
equal v on P0. Neither original unmarked kernels nor old source
representatives have been altered by this mathematical construction.

A concrete analytic frame can be obtained on the finite coarse patch
by a fixed rooted tree. Products along the tree define its gauge
holomorphically. In an initially small frame, all remaining links in
that tree gauge stay C_P delta-small, with C_P depending on the finite
coarse tree. Under a local gauge transformation the tree-gauge datum
changes by root conjugation. Tapering its logarithm commutes with that
conjugation. A complete gauge-invariant scalar composed with(3.2) is
therefore independent of the arbitrary input frame in this sense.
Translation/reflection covariance requires transforming the tree and
patch labels too, or a prescribed symmetric finite family; no new
unlabelled symmetry is assumed for a single chosen tree.

The fixed amplification C_P is real. Taking delta smaller in absolute
value does not make C_P delta fit a saturated old ball of radius delta.
For a born-i germ read at a protected proper scale n, the existing
maturity test instead uses the actual old-coordinate ratios, with
h=L^(i-n): potential C_P a_n h, derivative C_P a_n h^2 and physical
current C_P a_n h^3. The original full old-domain/slack test must be
performed with these ratios. This note does not assert it for every
young source or every arbitrary independent-pair contour.

4. QUANTITATIVE ANCHOR AMBIGUITY

Take TWO small ordinary data v_0=exp A_0 and v_1=exp A_1 constructed
as above, with A_0=A_1 on P0. The path exp[(1-t)A_0+t A_1] stays in
the same flat relative datum ball. Apply the actual derivative(190)
and integrate this path. If a source read K lies distance rho from
the support of A_1-A_0, measured in the homogeneous coarse block
metric, summing the derivative kernels gives

 ||U_1-U_0||_(scaled local C1/Holder/current,K)
       <= C delta exp(-c rho).                           (4.1)

The norm is in the common admitted gauge/orbit chart. The datum volume
factor in(190) cancels the determining-cell count; a fixed slice of
exponential decay pays shell growth. The fine mesh introduces no new
cardinality factor. Complete Wilson-current stencils belong to K.

For an analytic own-field coefficient D on a common old domain with
strict inner margin b along the ENTIRE interpolated completion path,
not just its endpoints, Banach Cauchy in the displayed direction yields

 |D(U_1,J_W(U_1))-D(U_0,J_W(U_0))|
       <= C ||D|| (delta_old/b) exp(-c rho),               (4.2)

where delta_old includes the ACTUAL rescaled field/current allowances.
This directional estimate does not count independent fine coordinates.
It applies coefficientwise to finite source jets and keeps scalar
coefficients. All source roots and frames remain in the read.

Equations(4.1)--(4.2) are a source-attached homogeneous completion
comparison, deduced from Proposition9, not an assumed global weak
extension of a large-field configuration. The anchor support is the
place where the two completions differ. Unless that place is attached
to a genuine changed native row, it is an ARTIFICIAL anchor and receives
no raw-history credit. A fixed exp(-c rho) error is not automatically
summable over an unbounded number of RG steps.

5. WHY THIS DOES NOT YET COMPARE ALL ADAPTIVE READS

For the intended adaptive/ordinary pair, the common regular current
rows agree away from the genuine fine/boundary determining region.
Their full footprint enlargement must be included in that region.
If two admitted equations and a common chart existed with only those
differences, (2.2)--(2.4) would give the desired first changed-row arm.
There are still specific admission issues, not an algebraic rank issue:

* (3.1) is known on a controlled proper patch, whereas the adaptive
  problem can retain finer rows and fixed active exterior values beyond
  it. Their complete mixed datum plaquettes are part of condition(7).
* The source's original derived-minimum clause covers its prescribed
  determining towers. It does not quantify over every new union of
  rows or every boundary datum produced by a taper.
* Complex localization parameters must stay in the simultaneous old
  source, minimum, current and gauge domains. Endpoint regularity alone
  supplies no such common contour or spare old radius.
* Matching endpoints inside a collar leaves the explicit anchor error
  of Section4. Removing that error from a full telescope requires the
  SAME local map and datum at each repeated occurrence, before masks,
  with all source scalar terms. It is not cancellation between two
  independently constructed local minima.

Large-II(1.55) is an actual successful matched-layer identity for its
specified four maps; (1.56) still retains the exterior data. Its(1.65)
admits that original composition. It has not been found to assert the
new simultaneous adaptive-to-ordinary domain just listed. The exact
union-row comparison is ready once that actual endpoint attachment is
proved; it is not a substitute for it.
