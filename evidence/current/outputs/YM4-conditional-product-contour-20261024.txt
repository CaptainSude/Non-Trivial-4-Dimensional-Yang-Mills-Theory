PURPOSE AND SCOPE

The previous local pre-Q1 result leaves a conditional Gaussian mean of
order r at the retained boundary. An absolute-value estimate on the
unshifted real contour can charge its imaginary part on every occupied
ordinary coefficient. The following product-chain identity removes that
particular loss. After the shift, only explicitly selected surface
coordinates have a residual complex displacement. Their cost is paid
by their own large radius. No small probability of a global cutoff event
and no division by a conditional good-event mass is used.

The exact identity is elementary and finite dimensional. Its estimate
is for a localized Gaussian integral with a specified occupied read.
It is NOT, by itself, a connected cluster theorem, a zero-free theorem,
an own-field support theorem, or a theorem about arbitrary P0/P1 fibers.
Native localization of the mean and shifted interactions remains a
separate obligation. The companion native note addresses that matching.

1. ONE BALL, THEN THE PRODUCT

Let B_R be the real Euclidean ball in R^m, m=3 for a free SU(2) bond.
Let mu in C^m and let f be holomorphic on a neighborhood of
{y+t mu: y in closure(B_R), 0<=t<=1}. Lebesgue integration of the
holomorphic volume form on each translated ball has the real-coordinate
parametrization shown below. Differentiating under the finite integral
and applying the real divergence theorem to the complex vector mu f
gives the EXACT identity

 I_R f = T_mu f - S_mu f,
 I_R f = integral_(B_R) f(y) dy,
 T_mu f = integral_(B_R) f(y+mu) dy,
 S_mu f = integral_0^1 integral_(sphere_R)
                          f(y+t mu) (mu dot n(y)) dS(y) dt.       (1)

Indeed d/dt integral f(y+t mu)dy equals the sphere integral in (1).
The dot product is bilinear; no conjugation or absolute value enters
the identity. Thus both translated terms remain holomorphic in mu and
in any other parameter for which the indicated corridors stay admitted.
The vector mu can depend on external parameters but must not depend
on the integration variable y in this operation.

For a finite set I of coordinate blocks and a vector mu=(mu_b), apply
(1) to each block. The operators for different blocks commute. Hence

 integral_(product B_R) f(A)dA
 = sum_(S subset I) (-1)^|S|
        [product_(b notin S) T_(mu_b)]
        [product_(b in S) S_(mu_b)] f.                         (2)

In the term S, write A=y+mu-v, where

 v_b=0 for b notin S,
 v_b=(1-t_b)mu_b for b in S,
 y_b in B_R for b notin S, y_b in sphere_R for b in S.          (3)

In particular A-mu=y-v is REAL at every block not in S. A single
simultaneous global homotopy would not have this property: at its
intermediate time the residual imaginary mean would occur at all
unmarked coordinates. Product expansion (2) is essential.

2. CONDITIONAL GAUSSIAN FACTOR, WITH ITS RAW SCALAR

Let D be complex symmetric with uniformly positive Hermitian part,
Re D >= lambda I, and ||D||<=Lambda. For a linear term b write

 mu=-D^(-1)b,
 (1/2)A^T D A+b^T A
       =(1/2)(A-mu)^T D(A-mu)-(1/2)b^T D^(-1)b.              (4)

All transposes in (4) are bilinear. The analytic Gaussian normalizer is

 G(D)=(2pi)^(m|I|/2) det(D)^(-1/2),                          (5)

with the branch obtained by continuation from positive real D in the
accretive domain. For h(A)=A^TDA/2+b^TA-V(A), its cutoff integral is

 Z=G(D) exp[b^TD^(-1)b/2] sum_S (-1)^|S| J_S,              (6)

where J_S is (2) applied to the NORMALIZED centered Gaussian density
times exp[V], with centered variable (3). If desired, exp[V(mu)] can
be factored out and V(mu+y-v)-V(mu) used in J_S. This is an exact
algebraic operation. Both exp[V(mu)] and the scalar in (6) remain in
the raw outer measure. They may have boundary-size logarithms and are
not asserted to be small. The determinant also stays in that measure.

In the actual conditional fiber D=D_CC and b=D_CB z, while the
baseline subtraction H0(0,z) is already retained in its outer measure.
Formula (6) does not authorize discarding or counting that baseline
twice. The convention for V must be the complete native first action.

3. LOCAL GAUSSIAN MARGINAL, WITHOUT A VOLUME LOSS

For the estimate now assume that, after the relevant Mayer and exterior
cutoff expansions, a term has a complete occupied read Y containing S.
Every variable outside Y is integrated over its FULL centered real
Gaussian contour, and the remaining insertion depends on coordinates
in Y. This is an explicit premise, not an assertion about a still
truncated exterior. It is the place at which the native occupied-body
construction must be matched.

Integrate the complement exactly. The normalized marginal on Y has
covariance C_Y=(D^(-1))_(YY) and precision Q_Y=C_Y^(-1). It is analytic
with the consistent Gaussian branch. Uniform bounds follow directly:

 Re D^(-1) = D^(-*) (Re D) D^(-1) >= lambda Lambda^(-2) I,
 ||D^(-1)||<=lambda^(-1),
 Re C_Y>=lambda Lambda^(-2) I, ||C_Y||<=lambda^(-1),
 Re Q_Y>=q_- I, ||Q_Y||<=q_+,
 q_-=lambda^3/Lambda^2, q_+=Lambda^2/lambda.                 (7)

The conservative constants in (7) suffice. In particular the absolute
Gaussian normalization is at most a fixed constant to power m|Y|.
There is NO determinant loss proportional to the entire volume |I|.
Taking absolute values before marginalizing a fully centered exterior
could introduce precisely that unnecessary volume loss.

Let the remaining supported insertion F(w), w=A-mu, satisfy

 |F(w)|<=M_F exp[a ||w||^2],   0<=a<=q_-/16,                (8)

on the real/complex integration pieces actually used. This is a
pointwise growth premise and includes all allocated interaction tilts.
It does not assert that an arbitrary nonlinear native interaction
satisfies (8). At the literal bare step a uniform local supremum bound
can give (8) with a=0; its native proof is separate.

For real y and the displacement v in (3),

 Re[(y-v)^T Q_Y(y-v)]
 >= (q_-/2)||y||^2-C_Q||v||^2,
 C_Q=2q_+^2/q_-+q_+.                                      (9)

This follows by expansion and Young's inequality, with the last
quadratic term bounded below by -q_+||v||^2. Combining (8),(9) gives

 |exp[-w^TQ_Yw/2] F(w)|
 <= M_F exp[-c||y||^2+C_1 sum_(b in S)|mu_b|^2],
 c=q_-/8, C_1=C_Q/2+q_-/8.                               (10)

Only the selected set S occurs in the positive exponent.

4. SURFACE ACTIVITY AND THE SAME GAUSSIAN BUDGET

For b in S, the surface Jacobian in (1) is at most |mu_b| and
the sphere measure is |sphere_1| R^(m-1). Extend the other y integrals
to all real space in the bound (10). Including the marginal determinant
and all fixed dimensional constants gives

 |J_(Y,S)(F)|
 <= M_F C_0^|Y| product_(b in S)
        [|mu_b| R^(m-1) exp(-cR^2+C_1|mu_b|^2)].            (11)

Here J_(Y,S) denotes the localized normalized mixed-contour integral,
including its t integrals and surface Jacobians. Some nonselected
variables may still have upper cutoffs; dropping those in the bound
only improves the right side. The exact equality in Section3 requires
full real integration only OUTSIDE Y.

If native bad-cutoff marks P are also present, keep them disjoint from
S and inside the full occupied Y. They impose |y_b|>R at real blocks.
The same factorized Gaussian bound (10), with a fixed rate reserve,
gives an extra factor

             C(1+R)^m exp(-c R^2/2)                       (12)

for each b in P. Both (11),(12) use the SAME quadratic estimate (10);
no coordinate is charged a second Gaussian tilt. Overlapping geometric
read cubes do not duplicate actual block labels. The native support
enlargement and counting must still be paid in a cluster application.

Suppose uniformly |mu_b|<=M r, set R=K r, and choose the FIXED K so
c K^2>=2C_1 M^2, with a strict reserve. Then, for all sufficiently
large r, each bracket in (11) is bounded by

                 exp[-c K^2 r^2/4].                       (13)

The factor M r (K r)^(m-1) has been paid by the remaining exponential.
If mu_b=0 the surface contribution is exactly zero. A sharper bound
retains |mu_b| in (11), hence any proved spatial decay of mu_b from
the retained boundary. For r proportional to (log g^-2)^p, p>1/2,
the factor in (13) is smaller than every fixed power of g r^k for
each fixed k and any prescribed positive power, at a weak floor.

5. HOLOMORPHIC PARAMETERS, SOURCES, AND NATIVE DOMAIN OBLIGATIONS

The identities (1)--(6) contain mu, never Re mu or Im mu, and are
holomorphic identities on their admitted corridors. Real/imaginary
parts occur only in estimates. One may therefore differentiate an
original source insertion exp[s O_p(A)+t O_q(A)] under these finite
integrals whenever the original source and physical reconstruction
are holomorphic there. No source-dependent scalar is dropped. First
and mixed logarithmic jets require a separate nonvanishing/log theorem;
they do NOT follow just from (11).

For the guarded first fiber, |z_b|<=3r on its needed boundary read.

replace4r only after rechecking ALL affected prior curvature/gauge

g r tends to0 this is compatible with a fixed small analytic chart,

The following native obligations cannot be replaced by (11). Their

6. REFINEMENT: A SMALL VOLUME LOSS INSTEAD OF C_0 TO BODY SIZE

The crude constant C_0^|Y| in (11) is insufficient if a native
implementation enlarges the real body to a long background carrier.
For example, a carrier cube of side M can have O(M^4) noise variables,
while its walk weight decays on a length of order M. A fixed positive
constant per noise variable cannot simply be absorbed by that walk.
The following refinement preserves the SMALL per-volume loss used in
the native Gaussian account.

Assume in addition that the actual normalized marginal precision has

 Q_Y=A+iB, A,B real symmetric,
 q_- I<=A, ||Q_Y||<=q_+, ||B||<=C_rho rho,                    (14)

where rho is a freely small perturbation/contour budget, uniform in Y.
For example D=D_real+E, with D_real positive and real and ||E||<=rho,
implies (14) by the resolvent identity, principal restriction and a
second inversion, using (7). In a native localized Gaussian one must
check (14) for the ACTUAL quadratic being integrated; it cannot be
inferred just from smallness of the undeformed physical background.

Choose tau>0 and a>=0 with tau+2a<q_-/4. The growth premise (8) and
Young's inequality with this SMALL tau give

 |exp[-(y-v)^T Q_Y(y-v)/2] F(y-v)|
 <=M_F exp[-y^T A_t y/2+C_(tau,a)||v||^2],
 A_t=A-2(tau+2a)I,
 C_(tau,a)=q_+^2/(4tau)+q_+/2+2a.                           (15)

The imaginary displacement is still confined to S. The exact absolute
normalization ratio between Q_Y and the real tilted Gaussian A_t is

 |det Q_Y|^(1/2)/(det A_t)^(1/2)
 <=exp[C(rho^2+tau+a)|Y|].                                 (16)

To verify the rho^2 factor, put T=A^(-1/2)BA^(-1/2). It is REAL
symmetric. Then
 |det Q_Y|^(1/2)/(det A)^(1/2)
 =product_j(1+lambda_j(T)^2)^(1/4)
 <=exp[Tr(T^2)/4]<=exp[C rho^2 |Y|].
The remaining positive determinant ratio A/A_t is bounded by
exp[C(tau+a)|Y|] by its eigenvalues and the fixed lower gap.

Now integrate every unmarked coordinate in Y exactly in the positive
Gaussian with precision A_t, rather than bounding its normalization
coordinate by coordinate. Its marginal on T_mark=S union P has
precision with eigenvalues bounded above and below by fixed constants.
Its density is therefore at most C^|T_mark| exp[-c||y_T_mark||^2].
The existing surface/tail integrations yield

 |J_(Y,S,P)(F)|
 <=M_F exp[C(rho^2+tau+a)|Y|]
   product_(b in S)[C |mu_b| R^(m-1)
                         exp(-cR^2+C_(tau,a)|mu_b|^2)]
   product_(b in P)[C(1+R)^m exp(-cR^2)].                   (17)

Here S and P are disjoint actual block labels. The arbitrary body Y
has only the freely SMALL per-volume loss in (17). Constants per mark
can be paid by their rare weights. This includes S=P=empty, where
the bound has no spurious fixed constant per integrated coordinate.

The native budget order can now be respected: fix geometry/walk
reserves, choose rho,tau,a sufficiently small for its allowed
per-volume Gaussian loss, then choose fixed K so
cK^2>2 C_(tau,a) M^2 for |mu_b|<=M r, and only then take the weak
floor. Since K is fixed, polynomial surface factors are still paid
by exp(-c' r^2), and the larger complex physical corridor still
shrinks like g r. No second tilt is used on a marked coordinate.

If the native localized body/exterior quadratic differs from its
published Gaussian reference, it must first be normalized by its
OWN determinant. Its determinant ratio to that reference must be
retained and bounded by the existing small per-volume comparison.
Only then can (17) be applied. Merely treating an imperfectly
localized inverse identity as exact would be invalid.
