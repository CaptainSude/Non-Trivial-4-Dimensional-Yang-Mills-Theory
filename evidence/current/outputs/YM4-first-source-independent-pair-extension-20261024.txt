LEMMA. Let a be holomorphic on a product of color tubes
{|Re beta_e|<b, |Im beta_e|<t}, invariant under constant SU(2).
If beta and Ad(v)beta both belong to this product for v in SL(2,C),
then a(Ad(v)beta)=a(beta).

PROOF. Use polar decomposition v=k exp H, k unitary, H Hermitian
traceless, and remove k from the endpoint by constant invariance.
For each beta_e, in a basis diagonalizing H,

 F_e(s)=||exp(sH) beta_e exp(-sH)||_HS^2
       =sum_(ij) exp[2s(h_i-h_j)] |(beta_e)_(ij)|^2.

It is convex on [0,1], hence bounded by the larger endpoint value.
Also Re tr(beta_e^2)=||Re beta_e||_HS^2-||Im beta_e||_HS^2
is constant under conjugation. Consequently BOTH real and imaginary
norms along this path stay below the corresponding larger endpoint
norm. This is exactly the separate tube condition, not a total-norm
replacement that would shrink the real radius to t. The entire path
therefore stays in the same product domain. Holomorphic differentiation
of constant real SU(2) invariance gives its complexified infinitesimal
identity; integrate it along exp(sH). This proves the claim. END.

Here Re/Im mean the Hermitian real form of sl(2,C), with a fixed
Hilbert-Schmidt/color normalization. The argument is coefficientwise
and is independent of the number of parameters. If the two endpoints
are in strict tubes, the path has the same strict endpoint margin.
