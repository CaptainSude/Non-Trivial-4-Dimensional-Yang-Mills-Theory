1. THE SAME PHYSICAL SOURCE ON BOTH SIDES

Let the ambient chord space be X=direct-sum_(i in V) R^q, with fixed
color dimension q and n=|V|. Write gamma for its product standard
Gaussian probability law and mu_T for gamma conditioned on

       C_T={|xi_i|<=T for every i},       T>=1.

Thus mu_T is a product of normalized q-dimensional ball Gaussians.
Its selected probability is c_T^n, where

       c_T=P(|Z_q|<=T).

At a real admitted background u, use the ACTUAL TURN48 constrained
tangent space, physical Hessian and whitening:

       P=P_u,       K=H_u^(-1/2)P_u,       b=g K xi.

Their rank and dimensions are fixed on this branch. The averaged
normal window W_u(b) in TURN48(2.1) gives exactly this selected law
for the Gaussian physical tangent measure. Both its physical and
dummy normal Gaussian factors are retained. In particular

       E_(physical Gaussian at u) W_u = c_T^n,             (1.1)

independently of u. This follows by the orthogonal decomposition
xi=a+eta used in TURN48 Section3.

For physical affine-quadratic functions on this tangent coordinate,
write their ambient pullbacks as

 F=c_F+g l_F^t xi+(g^2/2)xi^t A xi,
 H=c_H+g l_H^t xi+(g^2/2)xi^t B xi,                         (1.2)

where A,B are symmetric. If the physical source polynomial is
c+a^t b+(1/2)b^t D b, then l=K^t a and A=K^t D K.
In particular the physical pullbacks satisfy P l=l and P A P=A.
The algebraic formulas below also hold for arbitrary symmetric
ambient matrices, but that alone does not make them physical sources.
For a source already expanded through its nonlinear constrained
chart, D is its FULL second derivative in b, not a bare plaquette
Hessian with the chart's second derivative omitted.

All of g,T,K,P,c,l,A,B are held fixed under the two source
differentiations. Both legs in the covariance are the same old
physical source polynomials on the two sides of the comparison.
No source is substituted on the dummy normal energy.

The constants c_F,c_H are retained. They cancel from covariance at
fixed u, but may depend on u and contribute to a later outer
covariance; Section8 displays that contribution explicitly.

2. EXACT RADIAL MOMENTS AND EXPLICIT EXPONENTIAL DEFECT

Put

 v=E_(mu_T)|xi_i|^2/q,
 m4=E_(mu_T)|xi_i|^4/[q(q+2)],
 kappa=m4-v^2,
 a_q=2^(1-q/2)/Gamma(q/2).

The radial Gaussian density is a_q r^(q-1)exp(-r^2/2).
Let tau=P(|Z_q|>T). Integration by parts gives

 E[|Z_q|^2 1_(|Z_q|>T)]
       =q tau+a_q T^q exp(-T^2/2),

 E[|Z_q|^4 1_(|Z_q|>T)]
       =q(q+2)tau+a_q T^q(T^2+q+2)exp(-T^2/2).

Subtract these from the full radial moments and divide by c_T.
Consequently, with

 d_T=1-v=a_q T^q exp(-T^2/2)/(q c_T),                       (2.1)

we have the EXACT identities

 m4=1-d_T(1+T^2/(q+2)),
 kappa=d_T(1-T^2/(q+2))-d_T^2.                             (2.2)

Since 0<=v<=1, 0<=d_T<=1. Hence

 0<=1-v^2<=2d_T,
 |kappa|<=d_T(2+T^2/(q+2)).                                (2.3)

For fixed q and T>=1, c_T>=c_1>0. In particular

 d_T+|1-v^2|+|kappa|
    <=C_q(1+T)^(q+2)exp(-T^2/2)
    <=C'_q exp(-T^2/4).                                   (2.4)

All constants are independent of the number of blocks. Equation(2.1),
not a bound on c_T^n from below, is what controls the local defect.

3. EXACT AFFINE-QUADRATIC COVARIANCE FORMULA

Rotational invariance within each color block gives

 E_T xi_(i,a)xi_(j,b)=v delta_(ij)delta_(ab).

The fourth cumulant is zero unless all four block indices agree,
and at a common block it is

 kappa(delta_ab delta_cd+delta_ac delta_bd+delta_ad delta_bc).

This follows from the radial fourth moment m4 and independence of
distinct blocks. The law is invariant under xi->-xi, so the
linear-quadratic covariance is zero. Thus

 Cov_gamma(F,H)=g^2 l_F.l_H+(g^4/2)Tr(AB),                   (3.1)

 Cov_T(F,H)
  =g^2 v l_F.l_H+(g^4/2)v^2 Tr(AB)
    +(g^4 kappa/4) sum_i[
                  tr A_ii tr B_ii+2 tr(A_ii B_ii)].         (3.2)

Here Tr is the full matrix trace and tr is the q-color block trace.
The matrices need not commute with each other or with P.

For clarity, the quadratic calculation expands
E[(xi^t A xi)(xi^t B xi)]. The pairing
E(xi_alpha xi_beta)E(xi_gamma xi_delta) cancels with the product
of means. The other two Gaussian pairings each give v^2 Tr(AB).
The same-block fourth cumulant gives the displayed diagonal term.
Multiplication by g^4/4 proves(3.2). The linear term follows from
the second moment, and parity disposes of both mixed terms.

Therefore the desired EXACT correction is

 Delta_T(F,H):=Cov_gamma(F,H)-Cov_T(F,H)
   =g^2 d_T l_F.l_H
    +(g^4/2)(1-v^2)Tr(AB)
    -(g^4 kappa/4)sum_i[
                 tr A_ii tr B_ii+2 tr(A_ii B_ii)].          (3.3)

This is already an actual two-source computation, not a statement
that a selection susceptibility would suffice if it were known.

The corresponding means, needed to keep the normalization explicit,
are

 E_gamma F=c_F+(g^2/2)Tr A,
 E_T F=c_F+(g^2 v/2)Tr A.                                 (3.4)

If p(s,t) is the probability of C_T in the source-tilted full
Gaussian reference, then exactly

 p(s,t)=c_T^n E_T exp(sF+tH)/E_gamma exp(sF+tH),
 partial_st log p(0,0)=-Delta_T(F,H).                       (3.5)

The full Gaussian exponential is finite in a neighborhood where
I-g^2(sA+tB)>0 for real sources; the bounded selected integral is
finite for all real sources. These facts suffice for the derivatives.
Equation(3.5) retains the exponentially small mass c_T^n and proves
that it does not force a large connected source correction.

There is also an exact real covariance ordering in this polynomial
class. Let R have the radial distribution of the uniform ball of
radius T. The ball Gaussian is its decreasing radial tilt
exp(-R^2/2). For independent copies R,S,

 (R^2-S^2)(exp(-R^2/2)-exp(-S^2/2))<=0.

Taking expectations proves
Cov(R^2,exp(-R^2/2))<=0. Therefore its tilted mean squared radius
is at most the uniform-ball value q T^2/(q+2), or

 v<=T^2/(q+2),       kappa=d_T[v-T^2/(q+2)]<=0.             (3.6)

For real symmetric A, Tr A^2 and every tr(A_ii^2) are nonnegative.
Equation(3.3) with F=H consequently gives Delta_T(F,F)>=0.
Applying this to every real linear combination of finitely many
affine-quadratic sources proves positive semidefiniteness of their
full-minus-selected covariance matrix, and hence

 |Delta_T(F,H)|^2<=Delta_T(F,F)Delta_T(H,H).                 (3.7)

This order is only asserted for this Gaussian polynomial class.

4. COMPLETE SOURCE CARRIERS AND A VOLUME-UNIFORM ROOTED BOUND

Use a common finite cell graph containing the operator block locations,
the old source anchors, and all background reads. Its metric is denoted
d_G. A carrier X is a finite connected set. An operator coefficient
assigned to X must depend only on background data in X and must include
its operator legs and its source anchor.

For two source families, expand for example

 l_F(f)_i=sum_(x,X) f_x l_(x,X)(i),
 A_F(f)_ij=sum_(x,X) f_x A_(x,X)(i,j).

Every nonzero term has x,i in X, or x,i,j in X respectively.
The source weights here are the actual declared interpolation of the
old source; a different profile is not substituted after pullback.
Use the uniform analytic supremum of each coefficient on the common
admitted background tube in the following norms:

 L_F =sup_z sum_(x,X containing z) e^(k|X|)
                                      sum_i |l_(x,X)(i)|,
 A_F =sup_z sum_(x,X containing z) e^(k|X|)
                              sum_(i,j)||A_(x,X)(i,j)||op.  (4.1)

Define L_H,A_H similarly. These are COMPLETE-root, strong
coefficient-sum norms. Mere spatial decay of the total matrix does
not imply them. If necessary symmetrize each matrix carrier by
(A_X+A_X^t)/2; its complete support is unchanged and the entry-sum
norm does not increase.

Construct a two-source scalar catalogue for(3.3) by contracting one
F carrier with one H carrier and assigning their union Z=X union Y.
For the vector term the carriers share i. For Tr(AB) they share i,j.
For the fourth-cumulant terms they share the common block i.
Thus Z is connected and includes both source anchors and ALL reads.

Let ||Delta_T||_(k;2src) be

 sup_z sum_(x,y,Z containing z) e^(k|Z|)
                         sup_background |Delta_(x,y,Z)|.

Then

 ||Delta_T||_(k;2src)
  <=2 g^2 d_T L_F L_H
    +g^4[ q(1-v^2)+(q^2+2q)|kappa|/2 ] A_F A_H             (4.2)
  <=2 g^2 d_T L_F L_H
    +g^4 q d_T(q+4+T^2/2) A_F A_H.

PROOF. For a union containing z, assign the root to X or Y,
overcounting harmlessly when both contain it. The weight of a union
is at most the product of weights. If z is in X, sum the F carrier
at z, and use the H norm rooted at the contracted index i. The
opposite root assignment gives the factor2.

For vectors use |l_i.m_i|<=|l_i||m_i|. For matrix contractions use

 |tr(A_ij B_ji)|<=q||A_ij||op||B_ji||op,
 |tr A_ii tr B_ii+2tr(A_iiB_ii)|
       <=(q^2+2q)||A_ii||op||B_ii||op.

The already included sums over operator legs in(4.1) pay all their
choices. This yields respective factors 2,2q,2(q^2+2q).
Multiplying by the three prefactors in(3.3) proves the first line
of(4.2); equation(2.3) gives the second. There is no volume factor
and no exponent loss in this bilinear contraction.

In particular

 ||Delta_T||_(k;2src)
  <=C_q exp(-T^2/4)
                  [g^2 L_F L_H+g^4 A_F A_H].                (4.3)

The raw scalar coefficient, including its zero-background value,
is INCLUDED in this norm. It has not been discarded as a vacuum.

5. WHAT K PULLBACK COSTS

Here is an explicit sufficient attachment of(4.1) to the actual
whitening catalogues, rather than an inference from l2 boundedness.
Let K=sum_X K_X, with complete connected read carriers, and define

 M_e=max{
   sup_i sum_(j,X) e^(k|X|)||K_X(i,j)||op,
   sup_j sum_(i,X) e^(k|X|)||K_X(i,j)||op },

 M_b=sup_z sum_(i,j,X containing z)
                              e^(k|X|)||K_X(i,j)||op.       (5.1)

The second norm includes roots internal to background reads and sums
over BOTH operator legs. It is stronger than an ordinary row bound.
Its marked-walk/marked-tree origin is the complete-root construction
in TURN47 Section3 and the fixed further exponent reserve for the
square root in Section4. These are retained input estimates on the
proved coherent branch; an unmarked row/column estimate alone is not
enough to claim them for a new action.

If the physical first and second source tensors have norms L_phys
and D_phys of the form(4.1), then direct carrier multiplication gives

 ||K^t a||_(k;src) <=(M_e+M_b)L_phys,

 ||K^t D K||_(k;src)
                <=(M_e^2+2 M_b M_e)D_phys.                 (5.2)

To verify these inequalities, locate a prescribed root in the physical
source tensor or in a K factor. In the first case sum the K tails by
their external row/column norms. In the second case sum that entire
marked K by M_b, the attached source tensor using its leg-rooted
subsum of(4.1), and the remaining K by M_e. This accounts for the
two terms in the vector case and the three terms in the matrix case.
Shared contracted legs keep every union connected.

Consequently finite-read source tensors with bounded incidence, and
the actual uniform complete-root K catalogues, give the required
volume-uniform norms after pullback. General analytic source
composition needs its corresponding complete supported first/second
derivative catalogue; (5.2) does not manufacture that input.

For the actual weighted Wilson source, TURN70 Section9 records its
full first and second jets after chart composition and whitening.
Those may be used directly in(4.1). The stationarity sum of first
jets vanishes only for a CONSTANT source; a varying source must retain
the g^2 d_T term in(3.3).

6. SEPARATION, PROJECTION AND GAUGE SCOPE

If f,h are supported in sets S_F,S_H at graph distance D, every
nonzero contributing carrier Z contains a source anchor in each set.
Connectedness implies |Z|>=D+1. Rooting at the source anchors on
the smaller side therefore gives

 |Delta_T(F(f),H(h))|
  <=||f||infinity||h||infinity min(|S_F|,|S_H|)
          exp[-k(D+1)] ||Delta_T||_(k;2src).                (6.1)

For complete carriers in cells of physical size ell, this gives the
usual exp[-c distance/ell] separation factor, together with one
source-volume count. Fixed collars and the actual interpolated
source supports must be included in that distance.

Projection dependence has not disappeared. For the full constant
physical action, the constrained first jet is zero and A=B=P.
Since Tr P^2=rank P=d, equation(3.3) becomes

 Delta_T
  =(g^4/2)(1-v^2)d
    -(g^4 kappa/4)sum_i[(tr P_ii)^2+2tr(P_ii^2)],           (6.2)

which is precisely the untruncated-minus-selected version of
TURN48(5.3). Replacing P by I would source the wrong normal modes.
For general weighted sources A and B must both be kept; they cannot
be replaced by a rank count. A constant source over the entire volume
can have an extensive covariance despite the rooted norm being uniform.

Under the actual compatible block-orthogonal frame changes,
l_i transforms at its block and A_ij transforms at both legs.
Every contraction in(3.3) is then invariant. With covariant input
carriers the constructed scalar carriers are individually invariant.
No Haar average on a weak angle ball or new global gauge domain is used.

For analytic background coefficients the identities extend as
bilinear holomorphic identities on the same admitted weak tube.
Transpose, not complex conjugation, is used there. Their uniform
supremum bounds are exactly the input norms in(4.1),(5.1).
The probability interpretation is asserted on real backgrounds.
No uniformity for a later generated action is inferred unless its
same complete-support inputs have been proved.

7. HOW SMALL THE GAUSSIAN SOURCE CORRECTION CAN BE

Choose T^2=A log(1/g) for small g with A fixed. The explicit bound
(2.4) is a power of g times a fixed logarithmic power; the weaker
form(4.3) gives exp(-T^2/4)=g^(A/4).
Thus any specified power suppression in this Gaussian rooted
correction is obtained by a sufficiently large fixed A.
At the same time g T^3 tends to zero, as required by the selected
local construction.

This conclusion concerns the connected correction per complete root,
not the global probability c_T^n. That probability may tend to zero
with volume even when(4.3) is very small. Equations(3.5) and(4.3)
therefore provide a genuine quantitative example of the TURN71
selection-susceptibility mechanism.

8. OUTER BACKGROUND COVARIANCE AND THE NONLINEAR GAP

Even within the Gaussian reference, a random background must be
handled correctly. Let nu(du) be an outer law, with the same fixed n,
g and T at every u. Equation(1.1) makes the zero-source selection
probability c_T^n independent of u, so selection leaves nu unchanged.
Write a(u)=Tr A(u), b(u)=Tr B(u). Total covariance and(3.4) give

 Cov_(nu gamma)(F,H)-Cov_(nu mu_T)(F,H)
  =E_nu Delta_T(F,H)
    +(g^2 d_T/2)[Cov_nu(c_F,b)+Cov_nu(a,c_H)]
    +(g^4/4)(1-v^2)Cov_nu(a,b).                            (8.1)

This follows by expanding the two outer covariances of the means
c_F+g^2 a/2 and c_F+g^2 v a/2, and similarly for H.
Thus the uniform fiber correction cannot be renamed the whole
two-point correction without treating these additional terms.
No locality or mixing of nu is assumed or proved here.

The nonlinear current fiber instead has the factor

 exp[V_g(xi,u)],
 V_g=-r_u(gK_u xi)/g^2+log[J_u(gK_u xi)/J_*(u)],

and inherited source/activity contributions. The TURN48 catalogue
controls this factor on the selected product domain. The untruncated
Gaussian samples arbitrarily large xi, and may leave the chart where
that physical expression is defined. Neither(3.3) nor local
holomorphy supplies a full-domain definition, integrable majorant,
or rare-tail marked cluster bound for this nonlinear weight.

For an actual analytic physical source, (1.2) is its first two
fluctuation jets unless the source is exactly affine-quadratic.
Higher jets and V_g produce additional covariance coefficients.
In particular, when first jets are nonzero the complete order-g^4
nonlinear covariance includes more than the quadratic-polynomial
Gaussian term. They have not been omitted from an asserted equality
for the physical law: that equality is not claimed.

What is proved here is the exact Gaussian reference susceptibility,
including both old source legs, its first-jet term, the full physical
projection, and a volume-uniform complete-carrier locality bound.
Extending this estimate to the full nonlinear current measure,
then to the original complementary histories and coarse law, remains
the substantive next attachment.
