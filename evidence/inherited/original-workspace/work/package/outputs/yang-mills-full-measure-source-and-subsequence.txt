1. WHAT CAN NOW BE CONSTRUCTED FROM THE SELECTED ESTIMATES

Fix ONE selected cutoff family, one physical torus and terminal mesh,
and the coherent sources from its finite-dimensional terminal vertex
profile space admitted by TURN70. At a fixed physical resolution r,
define the centered conditional mean

 Y_(a,r)(f) = E_sel,a[F_a(f)-E_sel,a F_a(f) | coarse record through r].

Resolution increases with r. The fixed-resolution source range bound
puts Y_(a,r)(f) in a compact interval independent of the ultraviolet
cutoff a. Its interval can grow with r; this is not a uniform bound
as r tends to infinity.

Retain these conditional means for a basis of the finite terminal
profile space. Their compact boxes at the countably many resolutions
give a single subsequential joint law. Identified compact coarse-link
tuples may optionally be retained too; compactness of every auxiliary
record or continuity of a gauge section is not assumed. Every finite-
cutoff martingale identity passes against continuous cylinder tests,
and then against bounded measurable tests. Thus the limiting hierarchy
retains consistent conditional expectations with respect to its
retained coordinates. All profiles on this fixed grid follow by
linearity from the same basis extraction.

The separated fine-scale remainder from TURN70 tends uniformly to zero.
Consequently, for its admitted separated coherent profiles,

 lim_(a along subsequence) Cov_sel,a(F_a(f),F_a(h))
       = lim_(r->infinity) E[Y_r(f)Y_r(h)].

This is a stronger conclusion than extracting unrelated scalar
correlation limits. A cross-cutoff Cauchy theorem or uniqueness of the
limit is unnecessary for this subsequential construction.

Its limitations are material:

 - the hierarchy is for the SAME selected law at each cutoff;
 - changing terminal restrictions or the selected law for different
   observables would require a new compatibility proof;
 - appended conditional means need not be deterministic functions of
   the limiting coarse field, without an additional continuity input;
 - no same-support uniform L2 bound or final random composite field
   follows from off-diagonal estimates;
 - the hierarchy can be trivial;
 - selected positivity and reflection invariance do not supply
   reflection positivity or full Euclidean symmetry.

Thus this is a limited selected-sector existence result, not a
four-dimensional Yang--Mills quantum field theory.

2. THE EXACT MISSING FULL-LAW CORRECTION

The selected construction already records a source-independent weight
0<=w<=1 against the original law, with its prescribed mass-one auxiliary
extension if needed. Write P for that augmented original probability
law, F,H for observables of the ORIGINAL field, and p=E_P w>0.

Under real source tilt define

 p(s,t)= E_P[w exp(sF+tH)] / E_P[exp(sF+tH)].

Keep the window w frozen. Direct differentiation gives

 Cov_full(F,H)
    = Cov_sel(F,H) - partial_s partial_t log p(s,t)|_0.       (2.1)

Equivalently, only the connected part

 log p(s,t)-log p(s,0)-log p(0,t)+log p(0,0)

matters. Its source-independent and one-source terms cancel.
The extensive value of log p(0,0) is not itself the obstruction.
Indeed an independent all-good selection can have exponentially small
probability and change no physical correlation.

For a product of successive frozen selections, log p telescopes into
the sum of the logarithms of the actual successive acceptance weights
under earlier selections and the original source tilt. Hence a
summable localized bound on their mixed source derivatives would
suffice for an upper-bound transfer.

This is an exact reduction, NOT an estimate of the correction.
It retains the unboundedly many omitted histories through p(s,t).
It also retains the covariance of conditional means under the full
coarse law. No native R-history completion is inferred from the
auxiliary positive completion of the selected measure.

4. A WEAKER CONVERGENCE REQUIREMENT AND A DYNAMIC NONTRIVIALITY TEST

For an actual physical Hamiltonian observable, let rho_a be its positive
centered spectral measure. Fix a positive time-window width delta and

 R_a(t)=integral exp(-2tE)[(1-exp(-delta E))/E]^2 rho_a(dE).

 nu_a(dE)=exp(-2t0 E)[(1-exp(-delta E))/E]^2 rho_a(dE)

and all of its time derivatives converge on the SAME subsequence

strictly later times. This result requires no full-sequence
convergence, but the required full-law upper bound is still missing.

A later positive norm alone does not exclude a merely static
zero-energy mixture. A sufficient stronger dynamic witness is

 R_a(t1)-R_a(t1+s)>=m>0,   t1>t0, s>0.

This and the earlier upper bound put a uniform positive amount of
spectral mass in a fixed interval [epsilon,Lambda] contained in

This is an alternative sufficient check to using a separate

For the actual fixed-spatial magnetic slab source with original

2. EXACT CONNECTED SOURCE COMPARISON

Define, with the unsourced law and w FIXED,

 Z(s,t)=E_P exp(sF+tH),
 Z_w(s,t)=E_P[w exp(sF+tH)],
 p(s,t)=Z_w(s,t)/Z(s,t).                                    (2.1)

For real sources near zero, p(s,t) is the probability/weight of the
same selection under the tilted ORIGINAL law. No window or coupling
inside w is retuned. Z(0,0)=1 and Z_w(0,0)=p.

Write

 K(s,t)=log Z(s,t),
 K_sel(s,t)=log[Z_w(s,t)/p].

Then exactly

 K(s,t)=K_sel(s,t)-log[p(s,t)/p].                            (2.2)

For any source function A with A(0,0)=0, write its connected two-source
part as A^c(s,t)=A(s,t)-A(s,0)-A(0,t). Define

 L_w^c(s,t)=log p(s,t)-log p(s,0)-log p(0,t)+log p.           (2.3)

Subtracting the three instances of(2.2) gives

 K^c=K_sel^c-L_w^c.

Differentiating the finite integrals proves

 Cov_mu(F,H)
   =Cov_sel(F,H)-partial_s partial_t log p(s,t)|_(0,0).
                                                               (2.4)

PROOF OF THE DERIVATIVE STATEMENT. For a positive finite measure nu,
partial_s log int exp(sF+tH)dnu is its tilted expectation of F.
Differentiating in t gives its tilted covariance of F,H. Applying
this once to Z and once to Z_w proves(2.4); no probability comparison
or division estimate using a lower bound on p is involved.

Consequently the following is a valid comparison inequality:

 |Cov_mu(F,H)|
 <= |Cov_sel(F,H)| + |partial_st log p|_0|.                  (2.5)

This identifies exactly the additional quantity needed to transfer
the selected upper bound. For a lower-bound transfer the same error
must be small compared with a separately proved positive selected
reference margin; TURN70 does not supply that margin.

An optional analytic consumer is also exact. If L_w^c has a holomorphic
extension to |s|<r_F, |t|<r_H, continuous on the corresponding circles,
and |L_w^c|<=epsilon there, the two-variable Cauchy integral gives

 |partial_st log p|_0|<=epsilon/(r_F r_H).                  (2.6)

Indeed integrate L_w^c(s,t)/(s^2 t^2) over both circles and take
absolute values. A uniform zero-free logarithm and these radii are
additional inputs; finite-regulator entire source integrals alone
do not supply them uniformly.

3. A TINY SELECTED MASS IS NOT THE OBSTRUCTION

The possibly extensive constant log p cancels exactly from(2.3).
Pure-s and pure-t terms cancel as well. Mixed source-dependent
scalar terms do not cancel.
All logarithms and derivatives are taken at a finite regulator with
p>0; no logarithm of a zero limiting selected mass is taken.

For example, append N independent Bernoulli(q) flags independent of
the original field, and select all flags. Then p=q^N can tend to zero
exponentially. Nevertheless p(s,t)=q^N for every source, so the
physical selected law equals mu and(2.4) has zero correction.

More generally E_P[w | sigma(F,H)]=p implies p(s,t)=p by conditional
expectation. This is sufficient, not necessary: only the mixed
coefficient of log p is required for covariance transfer.

A sequential form makes the target compatible with a chronological
selection without assuming independent histories. Let

 W_0=1,  W_j=product_(i=1)^j w_i,  W_n=w,
 a_j(s,t)=E_P[W_j exp(sF+tH)]/
                         E_P[W_(j-1) exp(sF+tH)].

All factors are nonnegative and the denominators are positive near
real zero on the admitted selected sequence. Algebraic telescoping
gives

 p(s,t)=product_j a_j(s,t),
 partial_st log p|_0=sum_j partial_st log a_j|_0.             (3.1)

For indicator choices, a_j is the acceptance probability of the next
choice conditioned on all earlier acceptances, under the original
source tilt. Weighted choices have the same formula, or can be
realized with an extra independent uniform flag.

The expected useful estimate is on these connected source
sensitivities, possibly through a two-source localized expansion
and a scale sum. It is not an estimate on product_j a_j(0,0) being
near one. Unconditioned count bounds cannot be substituted for bounds
under arbitrary earlier rare selections in(3.1).

4. CONDITIONAL FIBERS RETAIN THE COARSE COVARIANCE

Let Y be any retained statistic on the SAME original augmented law.
Suppose p_Y=E_P[w|Y]>0 almost surely, so the selected conditional
kernel is defined on the full Y support. This support hypothesis
must not be presumed for our weak-chart selection.

Set

 L_Y(s,t)=log { E_P[w exp(sF+tH)|Y]/
                              E_P[exp(sF+tH)|Y] }.

Let X_F^sel(Y), X_H^sel(Y), C^sel(Y) be the mean, mean and covariance
under the w-weighted conditional law at fixed Y. Conditional
differentiation gives

 E_P[F|Y]=X_F^sel(Y)-partial_s L_Y(0,0),
 E_P[H|Y]=X_H^sel(Y)-partial_t L_Y(0,0),
 Cov_P(F,H|Y)=C^sel(Y)-partial_st L_Y(0,0).

The law of total covariance therefore gives the exact identity

 Cov_P(F,H)
  = E_(P_Y)[C^sel-partial_st L_Y]
    +Cov_(P_Y)(X_F^sel-partial_s L_Y,
                            X_H^sel-partial_t L_Y).          (4.1)

PROOF. Subtract the conditional means from F,H. Their residuals have
zero conditional expectation, hence their cross terms with any
function of Y vanish. This yields the total covariance identity;
insert the three differentiated conditional formulas above.

The average and final covariance here use the FULL P_Y. The selected
coarse law is instead p_Y P_Y/p. Uniform selected conditional covariance
alone cannot control the second line. Even perfect within-fiber
centering leaves the fluctuations of the conditional means.

Where p_Y=0 one must retain the original conditional contribution
separately. In particular, a theorem proved only for weak coarse Y
does not apply to the whole first line of(4.1) without treating its
complement. The unconditional formula(2.4) avoids this support problem
but still retains its full contribution in log p.

There is a useful direct covariance diagnostic. With
f=F-E_PF and h=H-E_PH, elementary expansion gives

 Cov_sel(F,H)-Cov_P(F,H)
   = Cov_P(w,fh)/p
       - Cov_P(w,F)Cov_P(w,H)/p^2.                          (4.2)

The second term is a mean-shift term. An estimate only for w times
the centered product does not by itself dispose of it. Equation(4.2)
is an identity, not a proposal to bound its terms separately by 1/p.
Such separate bounds can be extremely poor when p is small.

6. FINITE COUNTEREXAMPLE WITH RARE BRIDGES AND REFLECTION POSITIVITY

This is a counterexample to an inference from the stated probabilistic
inputs, NOT to Wilson theory, locality of its action, or YM4 existence.

Fix 0<rho<1 and 0<epsilon_+<epsilon_-<1. Let Z=+1 or -1 with equal
probability. Given Z=z, independently generate two reflected halves.
Each half has:

  - an endpoint variable Y_+ or Y_- in {-1,+1} with mean rho z;
  - N bad flags B_i, each Bernoulli(epsilon_z).

All endpoint variables and flags are independent conditional on Z.
The two endpoints occupy disjoint local slots; their support does
not grow with N. The flags form a chain of 2N cells joining them.
One may regard Z as the retained coarse variable.

Reflection exchanges the two halves and reverses the flag chain.
For any complex function A of one half,

 E[(Theta A) A]=(1/2)sum_(z=+,-)|E[A|Z=z]|^2>=0.

Thus the law is reflection positive. Its finite marginal on the
endpoint variables and flags has full support. The all-good selection
also preserves this mixture-of-products reflection positivity.

Let w=product_i 1_(B_i=0), with all 2N flags included. Write

 p_z=1-epsilon_z,  r=p_-/p_+ in(0,1).

Then

 p=Ew=(p_+^(2N)+p_-^(2N))/2 ->0,
 P_sel(Z=-1)=r^(2N)/(1+r^(2N)),
 Var_sel(Z)=4r^(2N)/(1+r^(2N))^2.                           (6.1)

The endpoint means conditional on Z remain rho Z after selection.
Their conditional covariance is zero. Therefore

 Cov_full(Y_+,Y_-)=rho^2,
 Cov_sel(Y_+,Y_-)=rho^2 4r^(2N)/(1+r^(2N))^2 ->0.           (6.2)

This is exactly the coarse-mean term of(4.1), not a hidden
within-fiber covariance.
In this two-state example E_sel(Y_+Y_-)=rho^2 is unchanged;
the selected mean rho E_sel Z changes. The decay in(6.2) concerns
the CENTERED covariance, not its uncentered cross moment.

For EVERY prescribed set A of flags, conditional independence gives

 P(B_i=1 for all i in A)
  =(epsilon_+^|A|+epsilon_-^|A|)/2
  <=epsilon_-^|A|.                                        (6.3)

Thus the strongest elementary prescribed-set rare-event bound holds,
not just a one-point probability estimate. The chain has a bad bridge
only if all 2N flags are bad, whose probability is at most
epsilon_-^(2N). Bounded marks can be included:

 E[ |Y_+Y_-| 1_(bad bridge)]<=epsilon_-^(2N).

The endpoints have full-law mean zero. Hence their centered
bad-bridge product tends to zero exponentially while their full
covariance stays rho^2. The no-bridge channel carries the correlation.

For a cutoff illustration choose N proportional to a^-1 and an
explicit M_a=a^-Delta, Delta>0. Put F_a=M_a Y_+, H_a=M_a Y_-.
Their supports are unchanged, though their finite-regulator bounds
now grow. Then

 Cov_full(F_a,H_a)=rho^2 a^(-2Delta),
 Cov_sel(F_a,H_a)=rho^2 a^(-2Delta)Var_sel Z ->0,
 E[|F_a H_a|1_(bad bridge)]
                    <=a^(-2Delta)epsilon_-^(2N) ->0.       (6.4)

The selected covariance has a uniform upper bound and the marked
bad-bridge contribution vanishes, yet the full covariance diverges.
This arbitrary explicit scaling is not asserted to be a Yang--Mills
normalization. It tests a claimed comparison theorem allowing
polynomially normalized bounded local variables.

The missing susceptibility is visible exactly from(2.4):

 partial_st log p(s,t)|_0
       =M_a^2 rho^2(Var_sel Z-1).                          (6.5)

It is not suppressed by the bridge probability or by subtraction of
the extensive source-free log p. Every step of this example has a
positive normalized denominator.

The same obstruction can preserve both endpoint means exactly.
Replace Z by {-1,0,+1}, with probabilities 1/4,1/2,1/4. Keep the
conditionally independent endpoint means rho Z. Take bad rate
epsilon_0 at Z=0 and epsilon_1 at |Z|=1, with
0<epsilon_0<epsilon_1<1. Put

 r=(1-epsilon_1)/(1-epsilon_0)<1.

Both before and after all-good selection E Y_+=E Y_-=0 by the
Z->-Z symmetry. Direct summation gives

 Cov_full(Y_+,Y_-)=rho^2/2,
 Cov_sel(Y_+,Y_-)=rho^2 r^(2N)/(1+r^(2N)) ->0.                (6.6)

Indeed the selected probability of |Z|=1 is the last ratio and
the conditional cross moment is rho^2 Z^2. The same
mixture-of-products RP proof and every bad-set/bridge estimate
hold with epsilon_1. Thus controlling the global one-point means
would not repair the inference either: the covariance of the
coarse conditional means still changes.

7. A SOURCE-DEPENDENT NORMALIZER DIAGNOSTIC

All identities above freeze w when differentiating. If one instead
recomputes a family w_(s,t), this changes the insertion. For a smooth
positive weight on common support, put at zero

 A=partial_s log w_(s,t), B=partial_t log w_(s,t),
 C=partial_st log w_(s,t).

Differentiating its numerator directly gives

 partial_st log E[w_(s,t)exp(sF+tH)]|_0
             =Cov_sel(F+A,H+B)+E_sel C.                    (7.1)

Thus a source-retuned cutoff or baseline is not harmless. For hard
moving domains, appropriate boundary derivatives replace the smooth
calculation and cannot simply be omitted.

Even more simply, multiplying a raw source numerator by exp(lambda st)
leaves the zero-source probability law unchanged but changes its mixed
source derivative by lambda. Field independence alone does not justify
discarding this factor. Pure-s and pure-t factors do cancel from the
connected object(2.3); a mixed source vacuum does not.

8. PRECISE CONCLUSION FOR THE NEXT MATHEMATICAL STEP

At the original-weight identification already retained for the selected
sector, equation(2.4) is exact. It removes an unnecessarily strong
requirement: one need not prove the globally selected mass tends to one.

A useful missing theorem would instead bound the source-connected
selection correction L_w^c, or its mixed derivative, for the SAME
calibrated original observables. A local, summable estimate for the
chronological increments in(3.1) would suffice. It must include the
actual complementary/cemetery histories and their coarse mean
fluctuations, and retain all mixed source scalar coefficients.

TURN70 bounds the selected contribution. TURN68 bounds a particular
original-law large-action bridge contribution in its own admitted
family. Neither currently controls this selection susceptibility;
the finite counterexample proves that their probabilistic forms alone
cannot do so, even if a common-law identification were supplied.

This is a sharper location of the full-law comparison problem, not
its solution. No original continuum correlation, nonzero reflection
norm, or completed YM4 existence theorem is inferred.

SUBSEQUENTIAL SEPARATED CORRELATIONS AND A RESOLVED MARTINGALE LAW

RESULT AND STATUS

The TURN70 selected estimates do not require a cross-regulator Cauchy
theorem to yield subsequential separated correlation limits. There is a
stronger conclusion than extracting unrelated bounded scalar sequences:
one can extract a JOINT law of the centered conditional means at every
fixed physical resolution. This law is a consistent increasing-resolution
martingale. The fine-scale covariance tail then identifies the limits of
the literal original selected-source covariances with its separated
second-moment limits.

The construction uses ONE selected probability law at each regulator,
with one common terminal grid and coherent source interpolation. It does
not choose a different selected law for each observable. It does not
need a common analytic chart compactness assertion: the bounded resolved
observables themselves are included in the compact state.

Without an additional equicontinuity/strict-chart hypothesis the limiting
resolved observable need not be a deterministic function of the limiting
unaugmented coarse field. More importantly, the limiting martingale need
not have a terminal random variable, even for a single same-support

A separate conditional Gram-form theorem below states what subsequence

1. FIX ONE FAMILY, ITS NORMALIZATION, AND ITS PHYSICAL SOURCES

Fix the admitted periodic cubic terminal grid, macro ratio R>1, terminal
physical spacing ell_*>0, weak-coupling ceiling, window parameters, and
the finite terminal physical torus. Let V_* be the real vector space of
terminal vertex profiles. Its dimension m is finite. Let

              ell_r=ell_* R^(-r),       r=0,1,2,...

For ultraviolet depth n, the physical fine spacing is ell_n. Use the
actual normalized selected path probability P_n constructed in TURN70
Section1, with the complete raw densities, coarea factors, product
windows, and terminal restriction retained. Its coarse states are
U_0^(n),...,U_n^(n) in the original fine-to-coarse indexing.

For f in V_* write F for its continuous tensor multilinear interpolant.
At fine-to-coarse stage j the source profile is

                   f_j^(n)=I_(R^(n-j)) f.                 (1.1)

Thus for fixed resolution r the profile is f^[r]=I_(R^r)f,
independent of n. The original insertion A_n(f) is the literal weighted
original Wilson action of TURN70, using I_(R^n)f and its four-corner
plaquette average. No a-dependent multiplier or bare inverse coupling
is added. A_n is a linear function of f and is bounded at each finite
regulator. Its mean is allowed to diverge.

Write G_j^(n)=sigma(U_j^(n),...,U_n^(n)). The exact raw one-mark identity is

 X_j^(n)(f_j^(n),U_j^(n))=E_n[A_n(f)|G_j^(n)].          (1.2)

Raw scalar source terms have not been discarded in (1.2). Define the
CENTERED resolved observables, indexed from coarse to fine, by

 Y_(n,r)(f)
   =E_n[A_n(f)-E_n A_n(f)|G_(n-r)^(n)],  0<=r<=n.      (1.3)

The strong one-mark quotient norm supplies a representative Phi_(n,r),
normalized by subtraction at the reference field, such that

 sup|Phi_(n,r)(f)| <= 2 C_X ||f^[r]||_1,
 Y_(n,r)(f)=Phi_(n,r)(f)-E_n Phi_(n,r)(f).              (1.4)

Consequently

 |Y_(n,r)(f)| <= B_r(f):=4 C_X ||I_(R^r)f||_1
                    <=4 C_X R^(4r)||f||_1.            (1.5)

This estimate is valid on the actual admitted real states. It does not
need the representative to be chosen measurably across regulators:
(1.3) defines Y exactly, and any near-minimal representative proves its
bound. Deterministic additive constants in different representatives
cancel in (1.4).

There is also the martingale identity, for 0<=s<=r<=n,

 E_n[Y_(n,r)(f)|G_(n-s)^(n)]=Y_(n,s)(f).               (1.6)

Everything up to this point is already supplied by TURN70. Positivity is
that of the complete selected path measure; signed cluster coefficients
are not interpreted as probabilities.

2. THE RESOLVED JOINT LAW: NO MONTEL HYPOTHESIS IS NEEDED

Choose a basis e_1,...,e_m of V_*. For fixed r let

 Y_(n,r)=(Y_(n,r)(e_1),...,Y_(n,r)(e_m)).

It lies in the fixed compact box

              K_r=product_(a=1)^m[-B_r(e_a),B_r(e_a)].

For r>n put Y_(n,r)=0; this arbitrary extension has no effect on any
fixed finite set of resolutions once n is sufficiently large. The joint
laws of (Y_(n,r))_(r>=0) are probabilities on the countable compact
metrizable product K=product_r K_r.

Here is an elementary extraction argument. On each finite product
K_0 x ... x K_k, compactness gives a weakly convergent subsequence of
its probability laws. Taking nested subsequences and then a diagonal
gives convergence of every finite marginal. Their limits are consistent
because coordinate projection is continuous. They define a probability
P on K. Equivalently, compactness of K and approximation of continuous
functions by continuous cylinder functions gives the same weak limit.

Thus along one subsequence n_l -> infinity there are jointly defined
random vectors (Y_r)_(r>=0) with the following properties:

(i) Every finite collection of resolved mean vectors converges jointly
    in distribution. Since each such collection is uniformly bounded,
    all its polynomial moments converge too.

(ii) Put Y_r(f)=sum_a f_a Y_r(e_a), where f=sum_a f_a e_a.
     This is ONE linear random functional on V_* at each r. Linearity
     and every exact linear relation hold simultaneously for all real
     or complex coefficients. No separate subsequence is chosen for f.

(iii) With H_s=sigma(Y_0,...,Y_s),

          E[Y_r(f)|H_s]=Y_s(f),     r>=s.             (2.1)

Proof of (iii). For a bounded continuous function h of Y_0,...,Y_s,
the corresponding finite-n vectors are measurable with respect to
G_(n-s)^(n). Equation(1.6) gives

 E_n[(Y_(n,r)(f)-Y_(n,s)(f))h(Y_(n,0),...,Y_(n,s))]=0.

The integrand is continuous and bounded on a fixed compact product, so
the identity passes to the weak limit. Continuous functions determine
finite signed measures on that compact product; hence it holds for all
bounded Borel h. This is exactly (2.1). In particular E Y_r(f)=0.

Optional coarse-field coordinates can be included in this SAME
extraction whenever they lie in identified compact spaces. On a fixed
finite coarse lattice the absolute compact-group link tuple has this
property; normal dummy Gaussian variables need not be included. At
resolution r its finite number of links is fixed independently of n.
Adding those link tuples to K and to each H_s preserves the proof,
because they are measurable with respect to G_(n-s)^(n) for s-coarse
states. One must include their actual data/geometry if claiming more
than the link marginal. Deterministic coarse relations pass only when
their graphs are closed in the chosen coordinates; no continuity of
a discontinuous gauge section is assumed.

This is a jointly consistent resolved-observable probability law.
It is not merely a collection of scalar limits. It remains weaker than
a law of the original unresolved observables A_n: the bounds B_r grow
with r, and nothing here makes (Y_r(f)) uniformly integrable in r.

3. SEPARATED COVARIANCES CONVERGE ON THAT SAME SUBSEQUENCE

Suppose f,h in V_* have continuous interpolants F,H with actual periodic
support distance d>0. Let

 C_n(f,h)=Cov_(P_n)(A_n(f),A_n(h)),
 C_(n,r)(f,h)=E_n[Y_(n,r)(f)Y_(n,r)(h)].

Splitting the exact TURN70 martingale decomposition at stage n-r gives

 |C_n(f,h)-C_(n,r)(f,h)| <= tau_(f,h)(ell_r),          (3.1)

for n>=r and ell_r<=min(d,ell_*), where one may take

 tau_(f,h)(epsilon)
    =C_(F,H) C_X^2 g_*^2 epsilon^(-4)
                         exp[-c d/epsilon].           (3.2)

The constant includes the fixed R,J and physical support boxes and the
profile sup norms. The output spacings of all omitted conditional
births are <=ell_r, so this is precisely TURN70's proved fine tail.
There is no extra transport of a two-mark effective potential.

The same estimate holds for a finite interval of resolutions:
if n>=s>=r and ell_r is sufficiently small, then

 |C_(n,s)(f,h)-C_(n,r)(f,h)| <= tau_(f,h)(ell_r).       (3.3)

This follows either by summing those conditional covariance births,
or directly by orthogonality of the corresponding martingale increments.
The absolute value bound is essential: separated increments need not
have either sign.

Finite-resolution joint convergence from Section2 yields

 C_(n_l,r)(f,h) -> C_r(f,h):=E[Y_r(f)Y_r(h)]           (3.4)

for every fixed r and for ALL f,h in V_*, because these are finite
linear combinations of the same convergent matrix of moments.
Passing (3.3) to the limit shows

 |C_s(f,h)-C_r(f,h)| <= tau_(f,h)(ell_r),   s>=r.       (3.5)

Hence C_r(f,h) converges as r->infinity whenever the supports are
separated; call its limit C(f,h). Equations(3.1),(3.4),(3.5) prove

                  C_(n_l)(f,h) -> C(f,h),             (3.6)
 |C(f,h)-C_r(f,h)| <= tau_(f,h)(ell_r).                (3.7)

For example, first choose r with 2 tau(ell_r)<epsilon and then l large
enough for the finite-resolution moment difference in (3.4) to be
less than epsilon. This proves (3.6) without a cross-regulator Cauchy
estimate on either the selected densities or the effective actions.

The subsequence in Section2 was chosen before f,h. Therefore (3.6)
holds simultaneously for EVERY separated pair in the fixed finite
terminal profile space, not only for a preselected countable list.
There is no need to assert a hat-support decomposition of a pair:
finite-dimensional moment convergence at each r already handles every
linear combination, and the fine-tail bound is then applied to its
actual interpolants.

The limiting C is symmetric and real for real profiles. Bilinearity
holds on every linear configuration of profiles for which the relevant
separations hold, by taking limits of the finite-n identities.
It has the TURN70 separated upper bound. It need not extend as a finite
positive semidefinite covariance form to overlapping profiles.

The same argument works for a COUNTABLE vector space of source labels
that are all defined on the SAME sequence P_n and have the required
fixed-resolution bounds and coherent interpolation. Include all its
basis coordinates in the countable compact product. This statement
does not supply those hypotheses for arbitrary new source families.

In particular, refining the terminal grid or changing its terminal
restriction may change the selected law itself. Independent
subsequences of such different regulators cannot simply be combined
into the theorem above. At a fixed terminal grid its interpolated
source space is not dense in all smooth continuum test functions.
Extending the source algebra/calibration is a separate obligation.

4. WHAT A COMMON ANALYTIC CHART WOULD ADD, AND WHAT WAS NOT ASSUMED

The preceding theorem permits a limiting Y_r to carry randomness not
determined by an accompanying limiting coarse link tuple. Boundedness
alone does not preserve the graph of a varying measurable function.
For instance U_n uniform on the circle and Y_n=sin(n U_n) have subsequences
whose joint limit is U independent of a nondegenerate arcsine variable.
For trigonometric polynomial tests this follows by Fourier orthogonality,
and uniform approximation gives the assertion for continuous tests.
Every finite-n Y_n is a deterministic function of U_n.

A sufficient deterministic-function upgrade at each fixed r is:

  (A) the actual real state supports lie in a common compact K_r
      strictly inside a common finite-dimensional complex analytic
      domain D_r;
  (B) compatible normalized totals Phi_(n,r)(e_a) are holomorphic on
      D_r and uniformly bounded there;
  (C) gauge quotient/chart identifications used to express the physical
      totals have compatible continuous transitions on these supports.

On a finite cover of K_r by smaller complex polydiscs, Cauchy estimates
give a uniform derivative bound. Arzela--Ascoli and a diagonal
extraction give uniform convergence on K_r of all the finite source
basis totals. Locally uniform subsequential analytic convergence on
D_r is obtained in the same way on a compact exhaustion. Additive
constants have already been fixed by subtraction at the common
reference field. With simultaneous weak convergence of the coarse
law, the centered limits are then deterministic functions of that
coarse field, and their finite moments converge.

The actual TURN48 macro bridge equation(7) does establish

 ||log U_pre(w,gKxi)/i||_infinity
                   <=C_a b/R+C_R gT < b/2

on its declared output tube, in its stated smooth pre-gauge coordinates.
This is genuine strict incoming containment. It is not by itself a
verification of (A)--(C) for every terminal restriction, every subsequent
coordinate identification, and every gauge quotient in a limiting
hierarchy. TURN70 permits a specified terminal weak domain but does not
state that every such choice has compact closure strictly inside the
common complex tube. A smaller terminal domain can be specified as an
additional literal selected restriction, but must be kept in its law.

Accordingly the deterministic-function upgrade is conditional here.
The actual bounded augmented-state theorem in Sections2--3 uses none
of (A)--(C), so it does not leave this analytic premise hidden.

5. WHY A RESOLVED MARTINGALE NEED NOT GIVE AN ORDINARY COMPOSITE LAW

Here is an explicit probability example separating the conclusions.
It is an inference counterexample for the bounds, not a Wilson model.

Take a fixed physical torus with fine mesh a_n=R^(-n), independent
symmetric signs epsilon_z on its fine vertices, and gamma_n=1/sqrt(n).
For a fixed continuous profile F define

                A_n(F)=gamma_n sum_z F(z)epsilon_z.   (5.1)

A constant can be added to each summand to make the uncentered
observable positive, without changing any statement below.
All coarse variables can be deterministic, so every conditional mean
after the first integration is zero. The first conditional covariance is

       gamma_n^2 sum_z F(z)H(z).                     (5.2)

It has only local connected source carriers, with a rooted coefficient
norm O(gamma_n^2). Each fine summand is a bounded analytic linear
coordinate on a fixed chart if the coordinate takes values
+gamma_n and -gamma_n. Grouping its source roots onto the next fixed-R
grid only changes the local carrier constants. Thus the example has
the relevant uniform one-mark bound, a local small two-mark birth,
and zero separated covariance/fine tail for disjoint profiles.

For any nonzero smooth real F,

 V_n:=Var A_n(F)=gamma_n^2 sum_z F(z)^2
                      ~a_n^(-4)n^(-1) integral F^2 ->infinity.

Independence gives
 E A_n(F)^4=3 V_n^2-2 gamma_n^4 sum_z F(z)^4 <=3 V_n^2.
Applying Cauchy--Schwarz to A_n(F)^2 on the event
A_n(F)^2>=V_n/4, or the elementary Paley--Zygmund inequality, gives

             P(|A_n(F)|>=sqrt(V_n)/2) >=3/16.         (5.3)

Hence A_n(F) is not tight. No subsequence yields its ordinary real
random-variable law, despite exact convergence of every disjoint
covariance to zero and trivial compactness of all fixed coarse means.
The unresolved contact noise is invisible to those coarse means.

This example does not prove failure of tightness for the actual selected
action. It proves that the TURN70 estimates alone do not imply it.
The independent FREE Maxwell calculation in TURN63 Section3A gives the
physically relevant warning: centered regularized F^2 can have divergent
ordinary variance while its separated reflection forms have a nonzero
OS-Cauchy limit. Demanding an ordinary-L2 random composite as the only
route to a QFT would therefore be unnecessarily restrictive.

6. COMMON POSITIVE-TIME GRAM LIMITS: A DIFFERENT, CONDITIONAL ENDPOINT

Suppose one instead has actual cutoff probability laws mu_n with
reflection positivity, and a countable family of positive-half
observables B_(n,i), i>=1. Include in the index set all products and
linear combinations needed by the proposed local algebra, if bounds
for those objects are available. Define

 G_n(i,j)=E_mu_n[(Theta B_(n,i)) B_(n,j)],             (6.1)

with Theta the antilinear reflection. Centering may be imposed at the
cutoff if that is the physical source convention. Assume the diagonal
entries satisfy

                   0<=G_n(i,i)<=M_i<infinity          (6.2)

uniformly in n for each fixed i. These are separated REFLECTED
self-pairings when B_i is supported at positive distance from the
reflection plane, not ordinary same-support variances.

RP makes each finite G_n positive semidefinite. Its two-by-two minors
give |G_n(i,j)|<=sqrt(M_i M_j). Countable diagonal extraction therefore
gives one subsequence on which all entries converge to G(i,j).
For every finitely supported complex c,

       sum_(i,j) conjugate(c_i)G(i,j)c_j
          =lim_n sum_(i,j) conjugate(c_i)G_n(i,j)c_j >=0.       (6.3)

Thus the formal finite span of the labels has a positive semidefinite
sesquilinear form. Quotient by its null space and complete to obtain a
Hilbert space. Any fixed exact linear relation with zero cutoff norm
passes to the quotient. An asymptotically vanishing-norm relation does
too. No common cutoff Hilbert space and no full-sequence Cauchy theorem
were assumed.

This gives a compatible Gram form, not an automatic local operator
algebra or a reconstructed four-dimensional theory. Products require
their own labels and estimates; multiplication on equivalence classes,
domains of unbounded fields, locality, time-translation contraction and
continuity, spatial symmetries, and reconstruction need the corresponding
identities/bounds. A nonzero diagonal limit must also be shown, and even
that alone need not exclude a static zero-energy sector; the separate
root note turn71_spectral_subsequence_root gives a quantitative
positive-energy criterion.

The selected law in Sections1--3 has not been proved RP. Its ordinary
probability positivity proves its finite ordinary moment matrices are
positive, but not its reflected matrices. For example the exchange-
symmetric two-half law (Y_+,Y_-)=(xi,-xi), xi=+/-1 equally likely,
has positive probability and reflection invariance, while
E[Y_-Y_+]=-1. Its reflected one-vector form is negative.
Accordingly (6.2)--(6.3) cannot presently be attached to the selected
correlation limit just constructed.

7. NARROWING OF THE EXISTENCE OBLIGATIONS

What is now established, at the exact admitted TURN70 hypotheses:

  * A common subsequence for a JOINT resolved mean hierarchy under one
    selected regulator family, with its martingale consistency.
  * Simultaneous subsequential limits of all its separated covariances
    on the fixed terminal profile space.
  * Quantitative approximation of those limits by finite-resolution
    covariances, with the already proved ultraviolet tail.

Therefore uniqueness of the UV limit and a direct Cauchy comparison of
the varying selected coarse laws are not prerequisites for this scoped
existence result. The coarse means can be retained as coordinates when
a deterministic-function compactness theorem is unavailable.

What has NOT been established:

  * An unresolved random field or all its same-support moments;
  * a dense, coherently calibrated continuum source algebra under the
    same cutoff laws, or all its separated higher Schwinger functions;
  * RP of the selected family, continuum translations/rotations, or
    reconstruction of a nontrivial quantum theory;
  * a nonzero dynamic limiting observable;
  * any transfer of these selected results to the full original Wilson
    measure, whose omitted mass and coarse channels remain.

All physical covariance claims here concern the literal action source
normalization of TURN70. Multiplicative renormalizations depending on
cutoff must be checked in the bounds; divergent additive deterministic
constants cancel in the centered quantities. The finite terminal grid,
fixed physical torus, and absence of any thermodynamic-uniform
continuum-source conclusion are part of the result.

APPENDIX: Physical spectral subsequence and positive-energy criterion

ONE-OBSERVABLE SPECTRAL COMPACTNESS AND A DYNAMIC WITNESS
TURN71. Root derivation. 20 October 2026.

This is a conditional consumer theorem, not a Yang--Mills estimate.
Its purpose is to avoid requiring a full cross-cutoff Cauchy theorem
when a subsequential existence construction would suffice, and to
distinguish a surviving positive-energy state from a frozen mixture.

1. SETUP

For every cutoff a, let A_a be a nonnegative self-adjoint operator,
Omega_a a normalized ground vector, and v_a a centered physical
observable vector. No common Hilbert space is assumed.

Let rho_a(B)=||1_B(A_a)v_a||^2. At each fixed cutoff rho_a is finite.
The estimates below also work when this is only locally finite and
the stated positive-time transforms exist.

For a fixed time-window width delta>0 put

 b_delta(E)=(1-exp(-delta E))/E,   b_delta(0)=delta,

 R_a(t)=integral exp(-2tE)b_delta(E)^2 rho_a(dE).

This is the squared norm of exp(-t A_a)b_delta(A_a)v_a.
For the actual fixed-spatial Hamiltonian and a magnetic multiplication
observable it is also the reflected covariance of the centered time
window [t,t+delta]. Its finite-regulator source identity was derived
in TURN67 and attached to temporal Wilson limits in TURN68--69.
Nothing here extends those results uniformly in the spatial cutoff.

2. SUBSEQUENCE WITHOUT A CAUCHY ESTIMATE

Suppose for some t0>0 that R_a(t0)<=M<infinity uniformly along a
cutoff sequence a->0. Set

 nu_a(dE)=exp(-2t0 E)b_delta(E)^2 rho_a(dE).

These positive measures have total mass <=M. Regard them as measures
on the compact metrizable interval [0,infinity], including infinity.
They have a weakly convergent subsequence nu_a -> nu_hat. Let nu be
the restriction of nu_hat to finite energies.

For every t>t0 the function exp[-2(t-t0)E], assigned value zero at
infinity, is continuous on this compactification. Hence on the SAME
subsequence,

 R_a(t) -> R(t)
   := integral_[0,infinity) exp[-2(t-t0)E] nu(dE).

An atom of nu_hat at infinity is lost at these strictly later times.
Thus a uniform early-time upper bound alone already gives a nonnegative
Laplace-transform subsequential limit for this single observable.
It does not give convergence at the boundary t=t0.

For any k>=1 and t>=t0+eta,

 |partial_t^k R_a(t)|
   <= M sup_(E>=0) (2E)^k exp(-2eta E)
   = M [k/(e eta)]^k.

Weak convergence of the continuous derivative kernels and these
bounds give convergence of every derivative locally uniformly for
t>t0. This can also be proved by Arzela--Ascoli on compact time
intervals, with the pointwise Laplace limit fixing every subsequential
limit. No analyticity of a physical-source logarithm is required for
this implication.

This theorem does not produce the PREMISE R_a(t0)<=M. The available
selected-sector upper bound is not automatically such a bound for
the original Hamiltonian/Wilson physical source.

3. WHY A LATER POSITIVE NORM ALONE CAN LEAVE ONLY ZERO ENERGY

A later lower bound R_a(t1)>=m>0 prevents all weighted spectral mass
from escaping to infinity. It need not prevent collapse to zero.

For example take the two-state Markov Hamiltonian

 A_epsilon=(epsilon/2)[[1,-1],[-1,1]],
 Omega=(1,1)/sqrt(2),
 Q=diag(1,-1),  v=Q Omega.

It has a unique ground state at every epsilon>0, a positive
semigroup, and centered spectral measure delta_epsilon. Its stationary
reversible path law is reflection positive. Nevertheless

 R_epsilon(t)=exp(-2t epsilon)b_delta(epsilon)^2 -> delta^2

at every fixed t. The limiting centered state can lie in an enlarged
zero-energy sector. Finite-regulator uniqueness does not rule this out.

For a full QFT construction, vacuum clustering/ergodic-component
control is one way to address this. The following stronger finite-
cutoff criterion gives a directly dynamic witness instead. It is
sufficient, not asserted necessary for every observable choice.

4. A QUANTITATIVE FIXED POSITIVE-ENERGY BAND

In addition to R_a(t0)<=M, suppose for fixed t1>t0 and s>0,

 D_a:=R_a(t1)-R_a(t1+s)>=m>0.

Let alpha=2(t1-t0)>0 and beta=2s>0. Then

 D_a=integral q(E) nu_a(dE),
 q(E)=exp(-alpha E)(1-exp(-beta E)).

This q is nonnegative, bounded by one, and vanishes at BOTH zero
and infinity. Put

 epsilon=m/(4 beta M),
 Lambda=max(2 epsilon, alpha^-1 log(4M/m)).

The premises imply 0<m<=M. On E<epsilon, q(E)<=beta E, so that

 integral_(E<epsilon) q(E) nu_a(dE)<=m/4.

On E>Lambda, q(E)<=exp(-alpha Lambda), whence

 integral_(E>Lambda) q(E) nu_a(dE)<=m/4.

Therefore

 integral_[epsilon,Lambda] q(E) nu_a(dE)>=m/2,
 nu_a([epsilon,Lambda])>=m/2.

This is a FIXED physical energy band, independent of cutoff. Since
exp(-2t0 E)b_delta(E)^2<=delta^2, it also implies

 rho_a([epsilon,Lambda])>=m/(2 delta^2).

Passing to the weak subsequence from Section2, q is continuous on
[0,infinity], with q(0)=q(infinity)=0, so

 integral q(E)nu(dE)>=m.

The same low/high estimates give nu([epsilon,Lambda])>=m/2.
No choice of interval endpoints with zero atom is necessary for this
conclusion; it follows by the integral estimate.

The resulting single-observable spectral representation has a
nonzero positive-energy subspace. For example on L2(nu), multiplication
by E is a nonnegative self-adjoint generator and the vector 1 has
nonzero projection onto [epsilon,Lambda]. Its semigroup matrix
elements reproduce R(t0+u/2) for u>0.

This is an abstract one-observable limit, not a local four-dimensional
Yang--Mills theory: no common continuum algebra, spatial translations,
rotations, products, or gauge-theory identification follows.

5. EXACT ORIGINAL-SOURCE EXPRESSION FOR THIS CRITERION

For a fixed finite-spatial magnetic observable Q, define

 K_a(T,z)=log <Omega_a,
    exp[-T(H_a-z Q_a-E_a)] Omega_a>.

The endpoints are the UNSOURCED ground vector, and
K_a(T,0)=0. Write J_a(T)=partial_z^2 K_a(T,0).
TURN67's exact Duhamel/reflection identity is

 R_a(t)=(1/2)Delta_delta^2 J_a(2t),
 Delta_delta^2 J(T)=J(T+2delta)-2J(T+delta)+J(T).

Consequently the dynamic witness in Section4 is exactly

 D_a=(1/2)[Delta_delta^2 J_a(2t1)
                   -Delta_delta^2 J_a(2t1+2s)].

There are only finitely many required slab lengths. Any source scalar
affine in T cancels in each Delta_delta^2, provided the SAME
normalization is used at all these lengths. Source-dependent errors
not known to be affine cannot be discarded.

For the global logarithmic coupling source of TURN67, with magnetic
observable Q_gamma=4 gamma S_sp/a and gamma=g^-2, the factor 1/2
above is replaced by 1/8 for its source curvature. Local coupling
profiles have the separately retained energy-current correction;
this identity does not remove it.

These formulas specify the quantitative source information that
would suffice. No upper bound M or positive difference m uniform
in spatial cutoff has been proved for the actual required YM family.

6. ROLE IN THE EXISTENCE PROGRAM

Full-sequence uniqueness and cross-cutoff Cauchy estimates are not
logically necessary for a subsequential existence argument. One
can instead seek compatible limits and preserve positivity along
a common subsequence. The present theorem demonstrates this for
one physical spectral family and supplies a sufficient positive-
energy witness.

The full proof still needs estimates for the ORIGINAL law, a common
local observable algebra with the required products and continuum
symmetries, and an appropriate reconstruction. Neither a scalar
Laplace-transform limit nor compactness of unrelated correlations
performs those steps.
