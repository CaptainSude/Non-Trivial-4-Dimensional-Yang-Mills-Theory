The literal condition log(C_q N)<=b r^2/2 implies
A>=b r^2/2. It also implies Nq<=exp(-b r^2/2), so the
required Nq<=1/4 follows at a fixed weak floor. If no mask exists,
the original unmasked profile is used separately; the logarithm
with N=0 is not needed.

For any D above the stated lower bound,

 aD>=aK(r^2+s),

Thus Phi_A(D)>=c_K(r^2+s). Choosing

 Phi_A(D)/2>=k r^2+4s log L,

which proves exp[-Phi_A(D)/2]<=exp(-k r^2)L^(-4s).
There is no missing factor of two, logarithm, or power of r.

3. COMPLETE READ AND A UNIFORM EARLY WINDOW

Assume exactly the displayed complete-read input
n(D)<=C(1+D)^m, with C,m fixed independently of r and s. For
r>=2 and choice(A),

 1+D<=C_K(r+s)^2,
 n(D)L^(-s)<=C'(r+s)^(2m) exp(-s log L).                 (B)

This follows from (r^2+s)/r<=r+s. The fixed collar in(A) is
absorbed in C_K; a growing additional collar requires its own bound.

Here is an explicit uniform threshold argument. Put
s0=C_m log(2+r). Choose C_m so C_m log L>2m+1. At s=s0,
r+s0<=C_(m,L) r for all r>=2, and (B) is at most

 C'' r^(2m-C_m log L),

which is <=eta after a fixed weak floor (depending on eta and the
fixed constants). For all s>=s0, the logarithmic derivative of
the right side of(B) is

                  2m/(r+s)-log L.

It is negative once r+s>=2m/log L, again ensured by the floor.
Therefore the same small-image bound holds UNIFORMLY for every
s>=C_m log(2+r) for which the other actual box/census hypotheses
remain satisfied. This is stronger and more precise than a bare
eventual-dominance statement.

If the real consumer's n(D) contains an extra factor g0^(-c), the
threshold above does not follow: its logarithm contributes

assumes the fixed polynomial-in-D bound explicitly; this audit

For the complementary finite window 0<=s<=C_m log(2+r),
