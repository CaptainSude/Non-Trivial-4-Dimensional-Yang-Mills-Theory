AN EXACT LOCAL-COVER CRITERION FOR UV-UNIFORM TWO-REFERENCE PRESSURE
20 October 2026. TURN50. history47.

RESULT

An exact multiscale decomposition with the SAME universal-cover local
coefficients at both reference volumes has a cutoff-uniform pressure
defect if its rooted long-cluster tails are exponentially small in
the current torus period, up to fixed powers of that period and of
the scale index. The ultraviolet bulk terms may diverge; they cancel
EXACTLY in j^4 log Z_a(L)-log Z_a(jL).

The primitive sufficient hypotheses below concern rooted connected
objects with their complete spatial paths/reads declared. They are
stronger and more concrete than a bound on an already assembled
pressure remainder. No small total bad probability is assumed.
These full-law cluster/local-cover hypotheses have NOT been proved
for the present YM4 construction.

1. SCALES, SAME PARAMETERS, AND RAW IDENTITIES

Fix an integer ratio ell>=2 and a terminal physical scale a_*>0.
Fix theta in (0,1/4), used in the local-cover hypotheses below.
Let s_k=a_* ell^-k, k=0,...,K, and a=s_K. Let

         x=L/a_* >=x_min>=theta^-1, n_k=L/s_k=x ell^k

be compatible integer periods. Fix an integer cover j>=2. Both
reference volumes L and jL use the SAME bare parameters, counterterm
prescription, measure normalization and blocking conventions.
Scale coefficients are allowed to depend on a and on those common
parameters. They may not be tuned independently at the two volumes.

Suppose the full positive partition functions have the EXACT raw
decomposition

 log Z_a(L)
    =sum_(k=0)^K [n_k^4 v_(a,k)+F_(a,k)(n_k)]
                                  +T_a(x),                    (1.1)

and the identical formula at jL with n_k replaced by jn_k.
The numbers v_(a,k) are field-independent bulk normalization factors
per current cell and are the SAME at both volumes. All determinants,
gauge/constraint factors, cutoff masses, and extracted vacuum terms
must be assigned either here, to F, or explicitly to T. No
normalization may be discarded by working only in a field quotient.

For the simplest version assume

 T_a(x)=x^4 t_a+r_a(x),  |r_a(x)|<=C_terminal,           (1.2)

with the same t_a at both x and jx, uniformly in a and admitted
periods. Section6 gives alternatives derived from primitive terminal
models, including an explicit terminal cover bound without (1.2).

2. PRIMITIVE ROOTED LOCAL-COVER HYPOTHESES

For each scale (a,k), let Omega_infinity be a countable collection
of rooted, connected, finite spatial objects on Z4. They may contain
polymer supports, propagator paths, trees, graph decorations and
orientation labels. Their root is the origin. Give them a nonnegative
geometric length ell(omega), which controls every declared read and
path displacement. Finite torus objects form Omega_n.

The coefficient w_(a,k)(omega) includes its proper rooting/symmetry
factor. For example if

 F(n)=sum_(connected X in torus) Phi_n(X)

with translation-covariant coefficients, then

 F(n)/n^4=sum_(X contains 0) Phi_n(X)/|X|.

This is a standard way to obtain the rooted w's, not a further
normalization approximation.

Assume the following for the fixed theta above, A,c>0 and
nonnegative integers p,m, independent of a,k,n.

(LC1) Exact connected representation:

        F_(a,k)(n)=n^4 sum_(omega in Omega_n) w_(a,k,n)(omega),

with absolute convergence. There is a corresponding absolutely
convergent infinite-root sum

                 p_(a,k)=sum_(omega in Omega_infinity)
                                             w_(a,k)(omega).  (2.1)

(LC2) Common short-object coefficients:
Objects of length <theta n on the torus are in bijection with
infinite rooted objects of that length, and their coefficients agree
EXACTLY under that bijection. The identification is the canonical
lift fixing the root, and uses the same a and bare parameters.

(LC3) Uniform tails on BOTH catalogues:
For every t>=1,

 sum_(Omega_infinity, ell(omega)>=t) |w_(a,k)(omega)|
 +sup_n sum_(Omega_n, ell(omega)>=t) |w_(a,k,n)(omega)|
                  <=A (1+k)^m (1+t)^p exp(-ct).                (2.2)

The minimum-period assumption x>=theta^-1 ensures that every
threshold theta n_k used below is at least1. Extending the result
to smaller periods would require a separate cutoff-uniform bound
on tails at thresholds below1; LC3 alone does not supply it.

A simpler, stronger sufficient input for LC3 is the exponential
root norm

 sum_omega exp(c ell(omega))|w(omega)|<=A(1+k)^m

on each catalogue, in which case p=0. A fixed polynomial shell or
derivative loss gives the displayed version (2.2).

WHY LC2 IS A LOCAL ASSUMPTION.

For finite-range lattice vertices and fully declared connected paths,
a connected object of total length <theta n cannot wind around the
torus. Its root-fixed lift is unique, and evaluating the same local
vertex rules gives the same coefficient. Conversely every such
infinite object embeds in the torus. Expanding a periodized
propagator into its individual lifted paths likewise assigns every
nontrivial winding contribution length at least a fixed multiple
of n; it is then paid by LC3.

A bare assertion of exponential decay of a matrix entry does NOT
establish this property. Nor does the cardinality of two endpoint
reads detect a long or winding propagator path. The declared geometric
length must include those paths, or an equivalent distance/winding
certificate. Volume-dependent backgrounds or bare retuning can also
destroy LC2 even when each separate catalogue has exponential decay.

One may replace exact LC2 by a proved local comparison whose TOTAL
short-object rooted discrepancy is bounded by the right side of
(2.2) at t=theta n. The proof below then has one additional copy
of that bound. Such a comparison remains an obligation on local
coefficients, not something inferred from the eventual pressure
cancellation.

3. ONE SCALE: THE COMMON BULK COEFFICIENT AND ITS ERROR

Subtract the finite and infinite rooted sums in LC1. Their short
parts cancel term by term by LC2. Both remaining sums are controlled
by LC3. After changing fixed constants,

 |F_(a,k)(n)-n^4 p_(a,k)|
          <=C A n^4(1+k)^m(1+n)^p exp(-c_1 n),       (3.1)

where c_1=c theta and C is independent of a,k,n. Complex or signed
cluster coefficients cause no issue because the estimate is absolute.
Only the original partition function must have the fixed real log
in (1.1).

Define q_(a,k)=v_(a,k)+p_(a,k) for k>0 and
q_(a,0)=v_(a,0)+p_(a,0)+t_a.
Equations(1.1),(1.2),(3.1) give the exact common-bulk decomposition

 log Z_a(L)
       =L^4 sum_(k=0)^K s_k^-4 q_(a,k)+R_a(L),       (3.2)

with

 |R_a(L)|<=C A sum_(k=0)^K
       (L/s_k)^4 (1+k)^m (1+L/s_k)^p exp(-c_1 L/s_k)
                                                   +C_terminal. (3.3)

Since k=log(a_*/s_k)/log ell, this is the requested polynomial-in-
period-and-log-scale form. The coefficients q_(a,k) need not have
an ultraviolet limit and need not be bounded uniformly in k.
Their EXACT equality at the two volumes is what will be used.

4. THE SCALE SUM IS UNIFORMLY SMALL IN THE UV CUTOFF

For x>=1, n_k=x ell^k, and k>=0, one has

 n_k^4(1+n_k)^p(1+k)^m exp(-c_1 n_k)
 <=2^p x^(4+p) exp(-c_1 x/2)
       ell^(k(4+p))(1+k)^m exp[-(c_1/2)ell^k].

The last series in k converges. For example its successive ratio
tends to zero, because the exponential change
exp[-(c_1/2)(ell-1)ell^k] beats every fixed factor ell^(4+p)
and every polynomial in k. Consequently there is a finite
C_sum=C_sum(ell,p,m,c_1), fixed before K, such that

 sum_(k=0)^K n_k^4(1+n_k)^p(1+k)^m exp(-c_1 n_k)
                <=C_sum x^(4+p) exp(-c_1 x/2).        (4.1)

Thus

 |R_a(L)|<=C_bulk x^(4+p) exp(-c_2 x)+C_terminal,
                  c_2=c_1/2,                        (4.2)

uniformly as a goes to zero. Any fixed polynomial
P(n,log(a_*/s)) is covered by a finite combination of these bounds.

This is the substantive ultraviolet summability: at finer physical
scales, the SAME reference torus has geometrically increasing current
period, so winding costs decrease faster than exponentially in k.
A small but scale-independent error per volume would not suffice.

5. THE TWO-REFERENCE DEFECT

Let

            D_(a,j)(L)=j^4 log Z_a(L)-log Z_a(jL).

The bulk sums in (3.2) cancel exactly, with no asymptotic replacement:
j^4 L^4=(jL)^4 and the q_(a,k) are the same. Therefore

 D_(a,j)(L)=j^4 R_a(L)-R_a(jL),

and

 |D_(a,j)(L)|
 <=C_bulk[j^4 x^(4+p)e^(-c_2 x)
                  +(jx)^(4+p)e^(-c_2 jx)]
                         +(j^4+1)C_terminal.         (5.1)

The right side is independent of K and hence of a. It is also
bounded by C' j^4 x^(4+p)e^(-c_2 x)+(j^4+1)C_terminal, with
C' independent of j>=2, since
j^p exp[-c_2(j-1)x] is bounded uniformly for x>=1.

This criterion supplies an ABSOLUTE bound. If an independent exact
chessboard/trace argument proves D_(a,j)(L)>=0, one may combine it
with (5.1). The local-cover argument alone does not manufacture that
sign or a reflection-positive chessboard estimate.

If a normalized product-transfer theorem charges a known multiple
of D_(a,j)(L), insert (5.1) into that proved normalization formula.
No exponent of j or of the number of cells in such a transfer
formula is inferred from this pressure criterion.

6. PRIMITIVE WAYS TO HANDLE THE TERMINAL FACTOR

The terminal scale is fixed in physical units. It must not hide a
cutoff-dependent global normalization. There are several concrete
sufficient alternatives.

6A. Terminal connected expansion.
If its exact logarithm also has LC1--LC3 with k=0, include it in
F_(a,0). Then no separate terminal remainder is needed. A remaining
raw scalar factor with uniformly bounded logarithm contributes
the C_terminal in (1.2).

6B. Uniformly bounded terminal density.
Suppose

 exp T_a(x)=c_a(x) integral exp[V_(a,x)] dnu_x,

where nu_x is a probability measure, c_a(x)>0,
|log c_a(x)|<=C_t, and V_(a,x) is real-valued with
|V_(a,x)|<=B_t x^4 uniformly in a. Pointwise positivity gives
|T_a(x)|<=C_t+B_t x^4. One may set no terminal bulk term and obtain
the UV-uniform cover error

 |j^4 T_a(x)-T_a(jx)|
                    <=(j^4+1)C_t+2B_t j^4 x^4.       (6.1)

This replaces the last term in (5.1). It is weaker in large x but
already sufficient for a fixed physical two-reference comparison.
It follows from an actual terminal integral bound, not from a
postulated pressure remainder.

6C. Finite-range product-reference terminal interaction.
Suppose instead nu_x and nu_(jx) are products of the SAME local
probability law at the common bare parameters; this local law may
depend on a but may not depend on the outer side length. Suppose
V_(a,x) is real-valued and is the periodization of the SAME
finite-range translation-invariant interaction, of range r0 and bounded rooted absolute
norm B_t, with x>4r0. Tile the jx torus by j^4 x-tori. Compare
the large-torus interaction with the sum of the small periodic
ones on the same product probability space.

Only terms within distance r0 of the artificial tile faces can
change. There are at most C(r0)j^4 x^3 such rooted locations.
The exact interaction difference is bounded by
C(r0)B_t j^4 x^3. Since both positive integrals use the same
product reference measure,

 |log Z_terminal(jx)-j^4 log Z_terminal(x)|
                              <=C(r0)B_t j^4 x^3.    (6.2)

Include any separately bounded scalar normalizers. This is again
UV uniform and does not require a terminal cluster expansion.
For a quasilocal interaction the analogous proof follows by a
summable range decomposition, with the explicit boundary/root tails
retained. It is not automatic for a correlated reference measure,
global constraints, or an unbounded terminal action.

7. NON-TAUTOLOGICAL CONTENT AND WHAT WOULD HAVE TO BE VERIFIED

The criterion does not say "assume the pressure defect is bounded".
It asks for:

 (a) an exact raw multiscale identity for the SAME full partition
     function and bare parameters at both volumes;
 (b) actual connected local objects with declared paths/reads;
 (c) local equality under the covering lift for short objects;
 (d) an absolute rooted tail bound for long and winding objects;
 (e) a terminal factor controlled by one of the explicit mechanisms.

It then DERIVES both a common bulk coefficient at each scale and
the cutoff-uniform two-reference pressure defect. Common bulk
coefficients are not extracted separately from each finite torus
after the fact.

The current YM4 work has selected-sector local catalogues, exact
vacuum bookkeeping, finite tuned trajectories and conditional
weak-chart label tails. These do not yet provide (a)--(d) for
the full original measure. In particular:

* Good-conditioned joint bad-label bounds do not yield connected
  defect activities or their spatial/path decay.
* A finite quotient representative may be globally supported and
  depend on the regulator. Its small normalized field norm does
  not identify a common raw pressure catalogue across covers.
* A trajectory retuned separately for each outer volume violates
  the SAME-parameter premise unless an additional comparison
  explicitly repairs it.
* Scalar normalizations from zero modes, gauge stabilizers or flux
  sectors must be retained. An unaccounted log(1/a) term outside
  the common bulk would survive with coefficient j^4-1 and defeat
  UV uniformity.
* A finite-volume kernel's decay estimate alone is not its equality
  to the same universal-cover coefficient. Exact liftability or a
  quantitatively controlled comparison is required.

No claim that these remaining full-law hypotheses have been proved
is made here. The theorem isolates a precise sufficient pressure
input which can be pursued at the level of actual local clusters,
without requiring the total bad-event probability to be small.
