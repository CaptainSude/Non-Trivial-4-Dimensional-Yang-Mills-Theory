INDEPENDENT AUDIT OF THE POSITIVE-CHANNEL INNOVATION LEDGER
19 October 2026. Audit of positive_channel_innovation_ledger_turn14.txt only.

VERDICT

The covariance telescope, the random-geometry qualification, the product
refill oscillation inequality with constant1/4, and the dimension-four
translated-loop bound are correct under their stated hypotheses. They
do not themselves verify a native R kernel or backward influence estimate.

Three clarifications strengthen the exact scope:
 (i) bare-field transfer must not hide an auxiliary random seed in X_0;
 (ii) the integrated innovation budget gives an L1 product-defect bound on
      the specified finite reference span, not a uniform algebra embedding;
 (iii) geometric-mesh summability makes the EARLY ultraviolet tail small;
       all later stochastic dates still have to be paid, and any observable
       renormalization factor is squared in the variance estimate.

1. EXACT COVARIANCE TELESCOPE: PASS

For an actual finite Markov chain and F_N in L2(mu_N), write

 F_j=K_j F_(j+1),    H_j=sigma(X_0,...,X_j).

The Markov property gives E[F_N(X_N)|H_j]=F_j(X_j). Jensen makes every
F_j square integrable. The vector differences

 d_j=F_(j+1)(X_(j+1))-F_j(X_j)

satisfy E[d_j|H_j]=0. They are orthogonal to every earlier square-
integrable martingale increment and to the centered F_0(X_0). Therefore

 Cov(F_N(X_N))=Cov(F_0(X_0))+sum_j E[d_j d_j^T],
 E[d_j d_j^T]=E_mu_j Cov_(K_j(X_j,.))(F_(j+1)).        (1.1)

Every summand is positive semidefinite. The parent note's operator-norm
comparison with a terminal covariance floor consequently gives the claimed
bare-side covariance floor for F_0, subject to the initial-state clause
below. No independence of distinct replacement dates is required.

The same calculation works for complex references with Hermitian covariance
and conjugate transpose, or by taking real and imaginary parts. The note
correctly states the real-vector version.

1A. INITIAL AUXILIARY RANDOMNESS MUST BE PAID

For an interpretation as an observable of the original Wilson field U,
the initial sigma-field must be sigma(U), with only deterministic auxiliary
data. If the enlarged initial state is X_0=(U,A), where A is a random
partition, random frame, global seed or other auxiliary variable, set

 F_bare(U)=E[F_0(U,A)|U].

Then the true bare-field identity is

 Cov(F_N)=Cov(F_bare(U))
          +E_U Cov(F_0(U,A)|U)+sum_j D_j.             (1.2)

The extra positive matrix cannot be dropped. Equivalently begin the chain
at U and sample A in an initial transition, so that its innovation is
already in the ledger. Augmenting the state to make a process Markov is
legitimate; moving all randomness to a hidden seed at time0 and then
calling the remaining deterministic transitions innovation-free would
not prove bare nondegeneracy. This is the only additional exact initial-
state condition needed for the stated covariance argument.

2. RANDOM GEOMETRY AND CONDITIONAL PRODUCTS: PASS

If a transition first draws a geometry label A conditional on x and then
draws a refill Y conditional on(x,A), the exact decomposition is

 Var(f(A,Y)|x)
 =E_A Var(f(A,Y)|x,A)+Var_A(E[f(A,Y)|x,A]|x).          (2.1)

The second term remains even if the field refills are independent once A
is fixed. If A is itself determined by x, it vanishes. If A was sampled
earlier, it must already be in the conditioned state. The parent note
states precisely this requirement.

A pure deterministic averaging map has zero innovation. But a T-stage
implementation which additionally samples a partition, gauge choice or
other future-relevant label is not a deterministic transition on the
enlarged state. It should be split into pure averaging and its actual
auxiliary sampling substeps. Conditioning on the sampled choice does
not dispose of the second term in(2.1).

For a product refill law conditional on the COMPLETE input state, the
Efron--Stein/tensorization inequality reads

 Var(f)<=sum_i E[Var(f|Y_(not i))].

The conditional range inequality Var(Z)<=(sup Z-inf Z)^2/4 gives

 Var(f)<=1/4 sum_i osc_i(f)^2.                       (2.2)

The oscillation must cover the complete actual coordinate/read change
in the i-th refill, with the other output coordinates fixed. The factor
1/4 is correct. Applying(2.2) to each unit vector c.F and then taking
the supremum over c gives the parent note's matrix bound, provided v_j
is a measurable bound valid for every such c. Conditional product
structure is a genuine hypothesis: separated centers are insufficient
when the output laws share interior constraints or changed read collars.

3. PRODUCT DEFECT: EXACT FORM AND NORM OF THE CONCLUSION

Let P_(0,j)=K_0...K_(j-1), with P_(0,0)=I, and let

 C_j(x_j)=Cov_(K_j(x_j,.))(F_(j+1)).

There is a POINTWISE conditional covariance identity on the initial state:

 C_total(x_0)
 :=P_(0,N)(F_N F_N^T)(x_0)-F_0(x_0)F_0(x_0)^T
  =sum_j P_(0,j) C_j(x_0).                           (3.1)

It follows either by conditional variance or by telescoping
P_(0,j+1)(F_(j+1)F_(j+1)^T)-P_(0,j)(F_jF_j^T).
Integration gives E_mu0 C_total=sum_j D_j. All these matrices are
positive semidefinite pointwise where defined.

If ||sum_j D_j||_op<=epsilon, then for fixed c,d,

 || c^T C_total d ||_(L1(mu0))
 <= [E c^T C_total c]^(1/2)[E d^T C_total d]^(1/2)
 <=epsilon |c||d|.                                 (3.2)

Thus the backward channel is approximately multiplicative in L1 on
products of the specified finite linear combinations of terminal
references. An L-infinity estimate, a uniform operator product theorem,
or multiplicativity for the generated unbounded algebra does NOT follow
from the integrated budget alone. The parent phrase "approximately
multiplicatively" should be understood with this norm and finite-family
scope. Combined with the covariance lower bound, it does give a genuine
injective map of that finite centered reference space in L2.

The single-step identity K(fh)-(Kf)(Kh)=Cov_K(f,h) is exact. The forward
conditional source map Lf(y)=E[f(X)|Y=y] and the backward map Kh(x)=
E[h(Y)|X=x] satisfy

 int f Kh dmu_X = int (Lf) h dmu_Y.

They are the two Hilbert adjoints associated with the SAME actual joint
law. Neither formula permits replacing K by L without changing the
direction of the observable being transported. The parent distinction
is correct.

4. TRANSLATED LOOPS IN DIMENSION FOUR: THE COUNTING PASSES

Suppose the loop has n_gamma<=C(1+ell/a) edge occurrences at mesh a.
For each occurrence and one specified fine link, at most one lattice
translation places that occurrence on the link, up to the fixed
orientation conventions. Repetitions or periodic wrapping can reduce
the number of distinct affected translations; counting occurrences
still provides the stated upper bound.

Each changed real normalized character differs by at most2. Thus

 osc_e(O_a)<=2 a^d ||f||_infinity n_gamma
          <=C ||f||_infinity (ell+a) a^(d-1).         (4.1)

Changing v links sequentially gives osc_B<=v times(4.1). For at most
C_V a^-d independently refilled blocks, (2.2) gives

 Var_K(O_a)<=C_V ||f||_infinity^2 (ell+a)^2
                                      v^2 a^(d-2). (4.2)

For variable block sizes the sharper direct expression is

 C ||f||_infinity^2 (ell+a)^2 a^(2d-2) sum_i |B_i|^2, (4.3)

exactly as in the parent note. No small-field expansion or Gaussian
concentration is used. The constants retain the actual physical volume
and the weight norm. The count is for complete link changes, not merely
the geometric core if a refill modifies a larger collar.

If v(a)<=C(1+log(1/a))^p and a runs through geometric meshes below h,
then in d>2

 sum_(a_j<=h) a_j^(d-2) v(a_j)^2
       <=C h^(d-2)(1+log(1/h))^(2p).                 (4.4)

For d=4 the right side is O(h^2 times a fixed logarithmic power). This
proves a small EARLY ultraviolet tail as h->0. Summing all scales up to
a fixed terminal physical scale instead gives only a finite bound;
the later stochastic innovations need a separate quantitative budget.

For a renormalized observable Z_a O_a, multiply(4.2)--(4.3) by |Z_a|^2.
In particular an a-dependent renormalization may destroy summability.
The bounded-character estimate is not a free estimate for curvature
fields obtained by dividing shrinking-loop defects by powers of a.
The parent already warns that it has not supplied such a normalization
or a terminal continuum covariance witness; this arithmetic makes the
warning explicit.

5. BACKWARD FUNCTIONS VERSUS BARE LOOP FUNCTIONS: ESSENTIAL DISTINCTION

The innovation at date j applies(2.2) to the actual F_(j+1), which includes
ALL later backward transitions. The bare loop count(4.1) does not by
itself estimate that function. Even a deterministic positive channel
can amplify individual-coordinate influence while being an L-infinity
contraction: map one input spin u in{-1,1} to N identical output spins
(u,...,u). The terminal mean N^(-1)sum_i y_i has individual-output-spin
oscillation2/N, but its backward image is u and has input oscillation2.
No innovation is generated by this deterministic map; the example only
disproves automatic propagation of the small influence norm.

Therefore a native attachment needs a quantitative backward influence
estimate, including all source-support enlargements, large components,
geometry choices and exact conditional refills. The terminal coarse
density minorization controls none of those quantities by itself.
The parent note correctly keeps this as a separate target, and does not
replace it by an estimate on an arbitrarily chosen loop at date j.

6. AUDIT CONCLUSION

The main ledger and the d4 counting calculation are valid. No source
paper or full native-history assumption was required to check the
identities. The useful corrections are the time0 auxiliary term(1.2),
the L1 finite-span product norm(3.2), and the early-tail/normalization
qualification(4.4). Their incorporation leaves a precise possible
native target; it does not establish that the original R construction
satisfies the needed conditional factorization or backward influence
bound, nor identify F_bare with a prescribed Wilson/native-average
observable.
