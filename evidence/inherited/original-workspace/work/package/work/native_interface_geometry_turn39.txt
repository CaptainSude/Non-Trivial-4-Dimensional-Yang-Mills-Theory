RESULT AND EXACT SCOPE

Two pieces of the turn38 interface can be proved without assuming that
the printed B109 corridor pivot remains free at a mixed boundary.

(A) There is an exact bare conditional Wilson theorem when EVERY link
crossing the active block-union boundary is fixed. The free variables
are all links wholly inside that union, including its geometric boundary
links. Its gauge group, action, polynomial gap, and normalized tail are
specified below. Thus the incident-fine-exterior interpretation causes
no obstruction to a bare polylogarithmic-box tail.

(B) The ordinary first-L nonlinear average has a uniformly bounded
right inverse supported in strict interiors of the active owner blocks.
It fixes all crossing links, prescribes ALL affected coarse rows, and
gives a genuine holomorphic coarea formula in an UNQUOTIENTED weak-link
chart. The complete Wilson/Haar/coarea numerator in this chart has
logarithmic datum derivative O_L(R^4/g^2). No native gauge determinant
or corridor Jacobian is asserted equal to this new coordinate formula.

The remaining coverage issue is real. A forest window is invariant
under a large root-fixed fine gauge group, whereas a weak chart of every
fine link contains an exponentially small fraction of such an orbit.
Section7 gives an exact bound for that fraction. Consequently the new
analytic numerator is not yet a reference carrying almost all of the
conditional fiber mass. Neither a full native R kernel nor a continuum
construction is claimed.

2. AN EXACT CONDITIONAL GEOMETRY WITH FIXED CROSSINGS

Work in fine lattice units, G=SU(N_c) with N_c fixed, d=4. Let K be a
cube of vertices congruent to {0,...,R}^4 whose vertices form a union
of COMPLETE ordinary L-blocks. A translation and R=qL-1 realize this;
choose q compatible with the fixed native big-block alignment. L is
fixed, odd, and at least11. Retain an exterior collar of fixed width
C_L covering the native layer gap, all plaquettes used below, and all
ordinary-average two-block footprints. Constants may depend on L and
that fixed collar width, not R or the ambient lattice volume.

Define the free edge set and its fixed complement by

 E_K={e: BOTH endpoints of e lie in K},       e_fixed=E_K^c.   (2.1)

Condition on every fixed link. Let S_e be the sum of Wilson plaquette
actions h(U_p)=1-Re tau(U_p) over EVERY plaquette reading a free edge.
It includes outward plaquettes with one free boundary-tangential edge.
All omitted plaquettes are fixed constants and cancel from conditional
normalizations. The exact conditional law is

 dQ_e(U)=Z_e^-1 exp[-S_e(U)/g^2] product_(b in E_K) dU_b.     (2.2)

This is a disintegration of the original law, not a changed boundary
action. For separated cubes whose complete action reads are disjoint,
these interiors are conditionally independent given all fixed links.

Every geometric boundary vertex of K touches a fixed crossing link.
Therefore use gauge transformations equal to1 at ALL such vertices
and outside K. All strictly interior vertices are gauge active. Choose
the axial forest from each interior vertex along negative e1 to its
root on x1=0. Its edges have transverse coordinates in {1,...,R-1}
and x1-tail 0,...,R-2. Do not include the top edge connecting to the
second boundary anchor. Every forest component contains exactly one
anchor; all other boundary vertices are isolated anchors.

The triangular forest change of variables has product-Haar Jacobian1.
It fixes every prescribed crossing link. The gauge variables integrate
to1, leaving n<=C R^4 real chord coordinates. Boundary-tangential links
are free CHORDS here. They must not be discarded or treated as fixed.

3. LOOP FILLINGS, GAP, AND NORMALIZED TAIL

Assume the fixed links on the complete local collar obey
delta=max ||e_b-I||_tau. In forest gauge, the usual chord loop consists
of the chord, its two forest paths, and a path on the geometric boundary
of K. The first two paths contribute identity. Interior transverse
chords give a strip of area at most R; a side-reaching chord uses the
same strip and a side-face path. A top e1 chord is filled by an e1-e2
rectangle of area at most R^2. The boundary connector length is O(R).

Every edge of such a boundary connector is tangent to at least one
face. Replace it by the other three edges of the adjacent OUTWARD
plaquette. Those three edges are fixed: their endpoints lie outside K
except for the two crossing endpoints. A boundary-tangential chord
itself is treated by exactly this single outward plaquette. Attach the
outward strips to the interior rectangle. The resulting connector
uses only fixed links, has length O(R), and the filling has O(R^2)
plaquettes, each with bounded multiplicity. Its added outward
plaquettes all belong to S_e because each reads its free tangential
edge. There is no uncharged action outside the conditional action.

Nonabelian lattice Stokes, bi-invariance, and Cauchy--Schwarz give

 max_chord ||y_b-I|| <= C R sqrt(S_e(y))+C R delta.          (3.1)

The weaker CR^2 sqrt(S_e) version is enough below. At identity fixed
data, linearization gives the curl inequality

 ||x||_2^2 <= C R^6 ||d x||_2^2 <= C R^8 ||d x||_2^2,      (3.2)

with the outward plaquettes included and forest/fixed variations zero.
This proves positivity of the forest Hessian with lower bound cR^-8.
There are no surviving boundary gauge modes: those vertices are fixed
anchors. Conversely the largest Hessian eigenvalue is O(1).

Each plaquette reads at most four link coordinates, and each link is
in a bounded number of plaquettes. Thus on a fixed log chart

 ||D^2 S_e(x)-D^2 S_I(0)||_(2->2)
                         <=C(||x||_infty+delta).          (3.3)

Choose r_R=cR^-8, delta<=c'R^-24. Equation(3.1) at e=I gives action
at least cR^-20 outside ||x||_infty<r_R/4. Changing e changes S by at
most CR^4 delta, while S_e(I)<=CR^4 delta^2. With c' small the global
minimum lies in that chart. Strict convexity on ||x||_infty<2r_R makes
it unique there and globally; its real analytic dependence on the
fixed links follows from its nonsingular Hessian. Consequently

 cR^-8 |x-x_*|^2 <= S_e(x)-S_min <= C|x-x_*|^2             (3.4)

in the larger chart, and the complementary action gap is cR^-20.
The gradient at zero is bounded by Cdelta per edge, hence

                    ||x_*||_infty <= C R^10 delta.        (3.5)

For g and gT below a fixed multiple of r_R, let W be the forest event
||x-x_*||_infty<=gT. A Euclidean ball of radius g around x_* gives the
denominator bound e^-S_min/g^2 g^n exp[-CR^4 log(2+R)]. Gaussian
integration in the chart and compact Haar mass outside it give

 Q_e(W^c) <= exp[CR^4 log(2+R)-cR^-8 T^2]
          +exp[CR^4 log((2+R)/g)-cR^-20/g^2].             (3.6)

Thus the complete proof of turn37 survives in this fixed-crossing
geometry. Its conditional denominator is the ACTUAL Z_e. No vacuum
factor or external weight is altered.

4. THE COLLAR GAUGE ALSO USES ONLY FIXED LINKS

The fixed-link graph in a one-or-more-layer exterior shell, together
with its crossing edges into boundary vertices, is connected and has
diameter O(R+C_L). This remains true after deleting every free
boundary-tangential edge. An exterior vertex can travel along an
outside coordinate face to a fixed outer corner in O(R+C_L) steps;
a boundary vertex first takes one fixed outward crossing edge. A BFS
tree of this ACTUAL fixed graph therefore has depth O(R+C_L).

Its gauge depends only on conditioned links, so it is a legitimate
choice of boundary frame before the interior integration. All tree
links become identity; every remaining fixed-link value is a holonomy
around a loop of length O(R+C_L). In the complete enlarged cube, a
coordinate-tree filling bounds these loops by

 d_fixed <= C(R+C_L)^2 sqrt(S_big).                        (4.1)

Consequently d_fixed>delta implies S_big>=c delta^2 R^-4
for R>=C_L. Applying the already proved bare, fixed-cell chessboard
bound to this gauge-invariant fixed-link event gives

 Prob(bad collar) <= exp[CR^4(1+ell)-c g^-2 delta^2 R^-4],
                          ell=log(1/g).                  (4.2)

This uses the complete enlarged-cell action before conditionalizing;
it does not assume an arbitrary-boundary probability bound.
With delta=c g R^-10 T, (3.5) makes the minimum O(gT), and

 Prob(bad collar) <= exp[CR^4(1+ell)-cR^-24 T^2].          (4.3)

For R<=C ell^b, T=ell^a, a>14b+1/2, both (4.3) and (3.6) are
superpolynomially small in g. The collar cutoff satisfies delta<=cR^-24
for small g. On the good collar and W all links of the FOREST
representative are O(gT), and the fixed links are even smaller.

The ordinary intrinsic group-mean/log branch is then valid on each
complete first-L footprint. This does not make an original-frame
open transport small, fix the original coarse root frames, or certify
the printed gauge-fixing characteristic or a constrained-minimum chi0.

5. AN ALTERNATIVE LIFT WHICH NEVER USES A CROSSING PIVOT

Here no forest gauge is imposed. Work in a coherent weak fine-link
frame on all active blocks and the full averaging collar. Let J_K be
ALL ordinary coarse bonds with at least one active block endpoint.
All other coarse rows read only fixed data, by [S](0.12).

The flat derivative Q0 of the ordinary logarithmic first-L average
has a compact right inverse for a COMPLETE star at an owner block.
For clarity, the already audited turn16 proof is recalled. If
L=2m+1 and a_mu is supported strictly inside that block, the outgoing
and incoming straight-string weights for longitudinal tail j are

                  (j+m+1)/L,            (m-j)/L.          (5.1)

They sum to1. Two smooth nonnegative bumps with longitudinal support
in (1/8,3/8) and (-3/8,-1/8), normalized discrete masses, and a compact
transverse bump have a moment determinant bounded below. For every
L>=11 the supports contain fine sites; constants may depend on fixed
L. This prescribes the two incident rows per direction independently.
Equation(5.1) uses the physical-current normalization of the flat
average, with normalized fine-block counting. If Q0 instead maps fine
link angles to coarse link angles as in (5.3), multiply that flat
operator by L and divide its right inverse by L. L is fixed; this
does not change any claimed uniformity or support.

The ordinary derivative differs from this straight average by a
coarse endpoint gradient d_c chi, with chi supported at the owner root.
A compact fine gauge parameter equal to -chi at that root and zero
before every block boundary cancels it, using Q0 d lambda=d_c lambda.
Thus E_y prescribes all 2d oriented incident rows and vanishes in a
neighborhood of the owner's block boundary. It preserves every fixed
fine crossing link, even for a boundary owner block. This is the
ordinary average, not a replacement by straight path decimation.

Assign every row of J_K to ONE of its active endpoints. Feed its
desired value to that owner's whole-star inverse, giving zero to all
unassigned rows of the star. Sum the E_y. Their fine supports are
disjoint block interiors, so

 Q0 E=I on J_K,        ||E||_(infty->infty)<=C_L.          (5.2)

The column overlap is bounded as well. Every nonassigned coarse row
is zero, including outward rows not being varied. This is an exact
simultaneous inverse, not a succession of coordinate inversions with
a constant multiplied once per block.

Let U_* be any sufficiently weak coherent link field, with its fixed
exterior retained. Use relative logarithms

 F_*(a)= (1/i)log[M(exp(i a)U_*) M(U_*)^-1] on J_K.         (5.3)

Fixed finite-L products and the holomorphic group mean in [S] imply,
on a sufficiently small sup ball,

 ||D F_*(0)E-I||_infty <= C_L ||log U_*||_infty,
 ||D^2 F_*||_(infty,infty->infty) <= C_L.                  (5.4)

These are row-sum estimates independent of the number of blocks:
each output row reads only its two finite endpoint blocks. They also
hold for complex link fields on the indicated common branch. A
Neumann inverse gives a uniform right inverse of DF_*(0). More
concretely, for B in ker Q0, solve

             F_*(B+E t)=v                                (5.5)

by the contraction t=v-[F_*(B+Et)-t]. For sufficiently small fixed
||B|| and ||v|| it is holomorphic, unique in the small t ball, real on
real data, and ||D_v t||<=2. All links outside E_K remain EXACTLY
fixed. All coarse data outside J_K remain EXACTLY unchanged. Both
claims concern the original ordinary map and original link frames.

This proves the needed first-L constraint-coordinate statement even
when the printed corridor h cannot be used. It does not by itself
prove that a native graded minimum or its gauge-fixed integration
domain is the same coordinate chart.

6. CHECKED COAREA NUMERATOR IN THE UNQUOTIENTED CHART

This section is a new exact local calculation, not an assertion that
the B109 gauge/FP factors have been checked. Choose the ordinary
Lebesgue measure on ker Q0 and any fixed linear coordinates there.
The linear isomorphism a=B+Et has a positive, constant Jacobian c_E.
It can depend on R, but never on v, B, or the fine field.

Let j_f(a) be the product exponential-coordinate Haar densities on
the free fine links, and j_c(v) the corresponding product on J_K.
These functions have nonzero holomorphic continuations on a fixed
small Lie-algebra ball. The real constraint Haar delta in relative
coarse coordinates is delta(F_*(a)-v)/j_c(v). Changing t to F_* gives
the EXACT branch density on the fiber chart

 w(v,B;e)= c_E exp[-S_e(exp(i a(v,B))U_*)/g^2]
           j_f(a(v,B)) /
           {j_c(v) det[D_t F_*(B+Et(v,B))]}.              (6.1)

The determinant is positive at the real center with the chosen
orientation and nonzero on the whole small complex ball, since the
matrix differs from I by less than1/2 in operator infinity norm.
There is no Faddeev--Popov distribution here: these are the original
unquotiented fine Haar variables in a local link chart. Constant
linear determinant factors cancel from normalized branch ratios.

The Wilson action extends holomorphically by replacing Re tau U_p
with [tau U_p+tau U_p^-1]/2. With g<=1, direct differentiation gives

 sup_B |D_v log w(v,B;e)[h]| <= C_L R^4 g^-2 ||h||_infty.  (6.2)

Here B ranges over ANY fixed compact positive-volume subset of the
smaller kerQ0 ball, for example ||B||_infty<=gT' within that ball.
Proof: (5.5) gives ||D_v a||<=C_L. There are O(R^4) action and Haar
terms, each with bounded local derivative. For the determinant use
tr(A^-1 D_v A), with ||A^-1||_infty<=2, ||D_v A||_infty<=C_L, and
matrix dimension O(R^4). No trace estimate is obtained by summing an
uncontrolled number of inverse columns. For exterior derivatives use
the explicit reference U_*(e)=I on free links and e on fixed links,
or hold the reference fixed in a specified chart. With that explicit
choice, varying the complete weak fixed collar gives the same bound,
with a changed fixed C_L: the reference has derivative norm1 and each
average has fixed finite footprint. All such variations and their
boundary dependence are retained; D_e is not set to zero.
At fixed ORIGINAL Y one must also differentiate
v=(1/i)log[Y M(U_*(e))^-1]. This has the same fixed-L bound for this
reference and is included by the chain rule. For a DIFFERENT analytic
anchor U_*(e), require a stated bound on its complete derivative; the
constant then contains that bound. Arbitrary anchor dependence has
no uniform derivative estimate. Holding only the relative coordinate
fixed would also be a different derivative.

At a real center v0,e0, w is positive. Set A(v,e)=integral w(v,B;e)dB
over the SAME fixed B-domain. On radius r=c_L g^2/R^4, (6.2) gives

 |A(v0+z,e0)/A(v0,e0)-1| <= exp(1/4)-1 <1/2.             (6.3)

For a holomorphic test bounded by M on the complete chart, its
normalized branch integral has each fixed-order datum derivative
bounded by C_{p,L} M(R^4/g^2)^p. The proof uses (6.2) BEFORE integration
and the real positive center, so it does not presume that the full
coarse pushforward density is nonzero on a complex disk.

This removes the formerly unspecified coarea/log-derivative input for
THIS explicitly chosen numerator. It does not establish the same
formula for the source's axial/Landau chart, its hard selectors, or
the complete compact constrained fiber.

7. THE PRECISE REMAINING GAUGE-ORBIT COVERAGE OBSTRUCTION

The coordinates of Section6 preserve the original coarse frame.
The forest used in Sections2--4 generally moves coarse root frames.
One may not substitute its weak representative into (6.1) while
pretending that the retained datum Y is unchanged. A pure gauge with
large values at the coarse roots has S=0 and forest representative I,
but its original framed coarse average can be a large pure gauge.
It is therefore a good W configuration with no reason to lie in the
small-v chart of Section6.

Even for an intrinsically weak coarse datum there is a quantitative
orbit-volume problem. Consider the subgroup of fine gauges equal to1
on every boundary anchor AND every retained coarse root. It fixes
the original exterior and every ordinary coarse datum by endpoint
covariance. Choose a forest with one such anchor per component,
covering all remaining vertices. Its gauge variables factor as
normalized Haar, conditionally on all gauge-invariant quotient
coordinates and on the retained coarse datum. This is the same exact
triangular Haar argument as in Section2, with the larger anchor set.

Let N_g be its number of gauge-active vertices and
p_rho=Haar{V:||V-I||<rho}<1 for a fixed small rho. An event requiring
EVERY fine link to lie in this weak ball requires, in particular,
all N_g forest links to lie there. Their triangular gauge coordinates
are independent Haar after conditioning on the quotient. Hence

 Prob(all fine links weak | quotient, coarse datum)
                              <= p_rho^(N_g).            (7.1)

This is an almost-everywhere regular-conditional-probability assertion
for the original bare law on the intrinsic ordinary-average branch,
or with that gauge-invariant validity flag retained. It is not a
density assertion at an exceptional null fiber. An extra noninvariant
gauge-fixing weight would have to be included explicitly and would
not be covered by the unweighted Haar-orbit argument.

For a union of many fixed-L blocks N_g is of order R^4. Any branch
numerator supported on the unquotiented weak-link chart inherits this
bound relative to the full fiber mass wherever disintegration is
defined. This is a gauge-volume effect; a large action penalty cannot
repair it because the action and exact constraints are constant on
these gauge orbits.

Thus (6.3) is a normalized SMALL-CHART statement, not an almost-full
fiber statement. A next exact construction must either divide out
this precise root-fixed gauge subgroup with its complete measure,
or integrate a normalized family of gauge translates of the chart.
It must retain the coarse root frames and compare the resulting event
with W. No estimate for that new numerator's full-fiber mass or all
original hard selectors is proved here. The turn38 hard-window cusp
also remains: analytic coarea does not make the ambient posterior
P(W|Y) analytic.

8. CONSEQUENCES FOR THE FIRST-STEP MATCHING PROBLEM

The fixed-crossing geometric objection and lack of a boundary-compatible
ordinary-average pivot are removed by Sections2--5. The exact first-L
map, all exterior links, and every affected coarse row are retained.
The new analytic branch numerator in Section6 has its actual Haar and
constraint Jacobians and a polynomial normalized derivative bound.
These are concrete additional results beyond the turn38 conditional
chart criterion.

What is NOT removed is the passage from a gauge-invariant high-mass
forest event to this one weak-link chart, or identification of the
printed B109/B116 integration measure with it. Equation(7.1) prevents
inferring that passage from positivity or constraint analyticity.
The original law is preserved throughout: all extra fixed links are
conditioned variables with their original marginal, all coarse data
refer to the original frames, and no good-fiber denominator has been
replaced by an unnormalized numerator.
