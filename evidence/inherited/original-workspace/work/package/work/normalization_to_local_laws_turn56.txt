2. EXACT NORMALIZATION: WHICH SMALL QUANTITY IS NEEDED?
=======================================================

Let Q be a positive finite functional, I=Q(1)>0, and let E be the
COMPLETE extracted vacuum. An original partition identity, if
established, has the form

             Z_W=exp(E) I,       Z_W(F)=exp(E) Q(F_*).      (2.1)

Suppose a designated tagged contribution satisfies

             |Q_tag(F_*)|<=M_tag ||F_*||_infinity,
             I>=I_- >0.                                   (2.2)

Then its normalized contribution is bounded by M_tag/I_- times
the test norm. The same E must be used on both sides. No separate
lower bound on a tiny individual component mass is needed.

If the tag is a positive event and Q=Q_good+Q_tag, put
delta=Q_tag(1)/I. For any real |F|<=K,

 |Q(F)/I-Q_good(F)/Q_good(1)|<=2K delta                    (2.3)

whenever delta<1. This follows by writing the full law as the
convex mixture (1-delta) mu_good+delta mu_tag. A signed
localization/walk tag need not define a probability event; then
only the raw linear contribution bound is asserted.

For comparison with a signed perturbation Q'=Q+DeltaQ, the exact
identity is

 mu'(F)-mu(F)
 = [DeltaQ(F)-mu(F)DeltaQ(1)]/[I+DeltaQ(1)].                (2.4)

Hence ||DeltaQ||_TV<=delta I, delta<1, gives
|mu'(F)-mu(F)|<=2K delta/(1-delta). The norm here means the
supremum over measurable tests of norm at most one. This identifies
the relative error actually required by a division argument.

Why the vacuum convention matters: on {0,1}, let
Q_good=e^(-2n) delta_0 and Q_tag=e^(-n) delta_1.
The raw tagged mass tends exponentially to zero but its normalized
probability tends to one. Rescaling by the exact vacuum e^n makes
the partition of order one and the tagged mass of order one.
There is no contradiction; comparing numerator and denominator
in different scalar conventions was the invalid step.

4. BOUNDED ORIGINAL OBSERVABLES: EXACT POSTERIOR CRITERION
========================================================

This is a general measure-theoretic deduction, not the missing
identification of the native operation.

Suppose a COMPLETE positive joint history measure J_h(domega,dz)
has been identified, with omega the original physical field and
z all terminal data. Put nu_h=pi_z*J_h. For bounded original F,
the signed measure pi_z*(F J_h) is absolutely continuous with
respect to nu_h, and its Radon–Nikodym derivative f_h satisfies

               |f_h(z)|<=||F||_infinity,                  (4.1)
               integral F dJ_h=integral f_h dnu_h.

This follows immediately from
|pi_z*(F J_h)|(A)<=||F|| nu_h(A) on every measurable A.
The history may include the designated positive cut tag; the
same identity then holds on that history.

If the terminal operation is EXACTLY this complete positive
marginal, its mass estimate (test 1) already gives
|integral F dJ_h|<=||F|| nu_h(1). Thus (3.5) applies to the
original numerator after the global census/normalization.
One need not first prove that f_h belongs to an analytic terminal
test class. No locality, holomorphy, or small source norm of f_h
is needed for this particular positive-mass estimate.

This cannot be done with a signed Mayer term as a probability
history. Nor can it be done after eliminating a variable that
a later unperformed operation will reread: that would not be
the complete nu_h of (4.1). One may retain original fields in
the joint space until the last marginalization; existence of
this joint space does not itself establish that the actual
R operation preserves the original law.

Thus the lemma transfers bounded tests once the source identity
is established. It is not a proof of that identity, nor a proof
that the transported function remains local for cover comparison.

For signed decorations there is a stronger obstruction than lack
of a probabilistic name. A signed joint measure can have zero

Here pi_*J=0 but integral F dJ=2. One needs a bound on pi_*|J|,
or the corresponding inserted absolute expansion, to make this

not supply it for every signed walk/localization tag.

6. A CONCRETE OBSTRUCTION TO THE SHORT-COVER SHORTCUT
====================================================

Here is a compact gauge-invariant model showing that small long
tails, ultraviolet-stable partitions, and common analytic bounds
do not imply matching local laws without matching short data.
It is not asserted to be the Wilson model or Balaban's construction.

At each site/cell take an independent SU(2) variable U_x, with
normalized Haar measure, and f(U)=Tr(U)/2. It is real, invariant
under conjugation, |f|<=1, integral f=0, and integral f^2=1/4.
For 0<epsilon<1/2 and a global ancillary label sigma in {+1,-1},
define

             rho_(N,sigma)(U)=product_x [1+sigma epsilon f(U_x)].

Every partition is exactly one. Each logarithmic local activity
log[1+sigma epsilon f(U_x)] is supported at a single cell and is
uniformly analytic and O(epsilon) on a fixed sufficiently small
complex tube. All nonlocal or winding activities are zero.

Nevertheless

               mu_(N,sigma)(f(U_0))=sigma epsilon/4.

Let an ancillary convention select sigma=+1 on odd N and sigma=-1
on even N. A twofold cover of an odd side then has local expectation
difference epsilon/2, although its pressure defect is zero and
every declared long-support bound is exact with zero remainder.

The label has no charged deciding support. Normalizing its weight
choicewise does not repair the mismatch of its short coefficients.
One could also use probabilistic label weights depending on volume.
The example remains translation invariant at the cell level.

This disproves the proposed implication from NATIVE-TYPE SIZE
BOUNDS ALONE to two-volume local matching. It does not show that
the actual native G0,T0 choices cause such a mismatch; they may
cancel exactly after integration, but that cancellation needs
an identity. A proof of equality of fully averaged local kernels
would suffice; a label-by-label bijection is only one method.

Similarly, a single action-source family only determines the
distribution of that action. It does not determine conditional
distributions on its level sets. No estimate solely in the one
constant original-action source can establish comparison for a
general determining family of local observables.

7. EXACT LOCAL SENSITIVITY CRITERIA
==================================

7A. Bounded-observable log sources have a uniform zero-free strip.

For any probability mu and real |F|<=K, define
M_mu(t)=mu(exp[tF]). It is entire and

 Re M_mu(t)>=cos(K Im t) mu(exp[(Re t)F])>0
                     when |Im t|<pi/(2K).                 (7.1)

Thus its logarithm normalized by log M(0)=0 exists throughout
this strip, uniformly in volume and cutoff. No analytic field
extension of F is required for this statement about the ORIGINAL
finite probability law.

For two laws and the corresponding bounded local observable,
put

 D_F(t)=log[Z_1(t)/Z_1(0)]-log[Z_0(t)/Z_0(0)].

Then D_F'(0)=mu_1(F)-mu_0(F). If
sup_(|t|<=r)|D_F(t)|<=delta, r<pi/(2K), Cauchy gives

                |mu_1(F)-mu_0(F)|<=delta/r.               (7.2)

The vacuum drops out of this normalized source difference.
The unsourced pressure estimate at t=0 gives D_F(0)=0 and no
information about D_F'(0).

Even real source bounds suffice, without a complex native
construction: for real t,
(log M_mu)''(t)=Var_(mu_t)(F) lies in [0,K^2].
Consequently |D_F''(t)|<=K^2. If |D_F(plus/minus s)|<=delta,
Taylor's theorem yields

 |mu_1(F)-mu_0(F)|<=delta/s+K^2 s/2,   0<s<=r.             (7.3)

For s=sqrt(2delta)/K within the interval this becomes
K sqrt(2delta). This is a quantitative route from real local-source
pressure comparison to local expectations, but the required
source comparison is not supplied by uninserted stability.

7B. The correct exterior-integrated likelihood is also sufficient.

If two local marginal densities satisfy q_1=q_0 exp(L), and
osc L<=epsilon on their common support, normalization cancels
any additive constant in L. Their total variation distance is
at most tanh(epsilon/4); hence for |F|<=K,

             |mu_1(F)-mu_0(F)|<=2K tanh(epsilon/4).        (7.4)

Proof: let m<=exp(L)<=M, M/m<=exp(epsilon), and a=E_0 exp(L).
Convexity of |x-a| on [m,M] bounds the half L1 likelihood
distance by (M-a)(a-m)/[a(M-m)]. Maximizing over a gives
(sqrt(M)-sqrt(m))/(sqrt(M)+sqrt(m)). This proves (7.4).

This criterion concerns the exterior-INTEGRATED local density,
not the supremum of an unnormalized local activity before the
retained variables are integrated. It is another exact way to
state the missing normalized local sensitivity.
