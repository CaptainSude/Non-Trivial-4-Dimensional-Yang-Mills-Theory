1. THE SINGLE-COMPONENT BOUND IS REAL AND USEFUL

At a fixed admitted component X the native complex operation has the
form T_X(z)F=T_X^real(exp(sigma(z,xi)) F(z,xi)), with a positive real
reference functional and sup|sigma|<=epsilon<=1. Its reference mass
m_X=T_X^real 1 can be extremely small and must not be replaced by1.

The rendered equations state

 |T_X(z)F|<=m_X exp(epsilon) sup|F|,
 |T_X(z)1|>=m_X exp(-2epsilon),
 |(T_X(z)1)^(-1) T_X(z)F|<=exp(3epsilon) sup|F|.       (A)

The lower bound can be checked directly: for |sigma|<=1,
Re exp(sigma)>=exp(-|sigma|) cos|sigma|>=exp(-2|sigma|).
Integrate against the positive reference functional, then use
|integral exp(sigma)|>=Re integral exp(sigma). Thus the argument needs
the small TOTAL complex perturbation on this component, not merely a
small perturbation per bond without a component-size bound.

For first-class components use the UNNORMALIZED first inequality in(A)
when estimating the rare activity. The factor m_X carries the native
large-field suppression from(1.89). Dividing it away would erase exactly
the rarity needed in the subsequent sum. For second-class components,
the normalized estimate is appropriate, while their normalization masses
remain in the source-zero density exactly as in(1.103).

2. VALUE BOUNDEDNESS DOES NOT BY ITSELF PRODUCE HOLOMORPHY

For the holomorphic conclusion require, in the actual fixed private
coordinates, F(z,xi) holomorphic in ALL unintegrated native exterior/current
variables z on the full declared chart, measurable in real xi, and uniformly
bounded on a slightly larger admitted chart. Then dominated integration
and the nonzero denominator in(A) give a holomorphic output. The same
condition is needed for every scalar-localizer/source copy and its actual
own determining tower if such copies are used later.

A Borel mark independent of z is a valid special case. But a generic
bounded original observable after reconstruction can become F(z,xi),
and its real boundedness does not give this condition. Even a trivial
positive fixed kernel shows the issue: with xi uniform on[-1,1] and
original bounded mark 1_(u>1), the reconstructed mark F(t,xi)=1_(xi+t>1)
has normalized mean max(t,0)/2 near t=0. The kernel is entire and its
value norm is1; the marked output is not real analytic at0.

The native gauge-fixing removal on printed378 is an integrated
equivalence. A marked version needs a gauge-invariant original observable
or the full compensating gauge record/anchored gauge integration. Bounded
holomorphy alone does not license dropping its gauge dependence.

3. A FIRST CONNECTED EXPANSION EXISTS BEYOND THE PRIMITIVE

Printed387--390 supplies the actual Mayer and connected expansion over
first-class X components and interaction localization domains: (1.90)
--(1.99). Its positive estimates retain the first-class mass from(1.89),
the original interactions, normalization terms, and component history
reads. It is therefore possible to run a protected one-formal-mark
version, provided that the mark is an admitted complete local source.

The exact implementation is to insert one nilpotent variable z times
the mark, retaining its full old variable read as a distinguished
hyperedge. Enlarge that read by every touched selected component and the
required native collars, masks, determining sets, and gauge records.
In a degree-one expansion the mark is pulled out once at its uniform
bound. No exp(zF) on a regulator-independent disc is required. Clusters
which do not touch the mark cancel against the COMPLETE source-zero
denominator. The remaining connected carrier contains the marked read
and all actual connecting interactions/components.

This extension costs an exponential in the protected seed size plus the
ordinary connecting-carrier exponent. An amplitude bound alone does not
pay this cost. The source norm needs a complete cover and a reserved
exponential weight, including the original root/profile allocation. An
arbitrary disconnected read cannot be treated as a free zero-size vertex.
The seed extension must not charge a whole selected core once for every
potential occurrence inside an exponential; whole-core/history costs
belong once to the distinct protected component, with outside connectors
charged by their native activity estimates.

There is a quantitative distinction here: a relative weight which ignores
the interior of a fixed second-class Y cannot pay an exp(c|Y|) protected
seed price. One must either retain that price explicitly in the input/
output bound, provide a separate full-core debit, or prove a sharper
per-touched-core estimate. The latter may be possible using the native
small incident interaction norm and the finite selected-core size, but
does not follow just from the statement that no sum over Y is taken.
For instance, an asserted cost C alpha |Y| would be uniformly small only
after verifying both the actual alpha and the native size bound at the
same grade. No such improvement is silently used in this audit.

The resulting metric is the native boundary-relative metric. The first
expansion treats the fixed second-class Y collection as retained regions;
it does not by itself evaluate their interacting operations.

The first-stage marked ratio is not the final ratio after the remaining
second-class T'_Y operators. Products of their individual posterior

However, printed391(1.103) explicitly handles this: factor out each
T'_Y1, normalize the operators, and apply the SAME Mayer/connected
expansion to their COMMON interacting exp(sum B'). The paper retains
the whole bracket and its normalizer, not a product of independently
normalized interacting expectations. Its activities are(1.91) without
the first two sums; the operator cost is exp(3) per touched Y, and the
ordinary notion of connectedness is used. This gives a second genuine
protected one-mark stage by the same degree-one argument, if the source
input from stage one has its required complete support/reserve.

Thus a properly typed two-stage theorem can produce connected marked
coefficients after the first-class sum AND after integrating all the NEW
second-class operators at a FIXED retained Y collection. It need not
stop at a single-component bound. It must retain the common denominator
and cannot obtain this result by multiplying(A) for independent marks.

Printed391 says that the resulting estimate has no tree-decay factors
inside the Y domains and that this is acceptable because there is no sum
over those domains: they are FIXED in that expansion. Its linear size
is d_k(X minus union Y), with the corresponding native relative metric.

Consequently:

 (a) A connected coefficient in this theorem need not have decay in the

 (b) The outer sum over the Y collections/history choices is still an

 (c) The old never-selected T''(Z_k) operation outside the bracket in
     (1.72) remains external. It is not one of the new T'_Y operators
     eliminated by(1.103). Its inherited mixed-domain sources and their
     contacts need their own transport/control.

 (d) Source holomorphy through the old large-field private reconstructions
     is an input. A regular-pair source germ valid only on small fields
     cannot simply be evaluated at arbitrary real large-field interiors.

 (e) A one-mark bound does not prove the two-source contact renewal or
     permit replacing the transported original product by a product of

These are exact scope limits, not objections to the two-stage conditional
producer. The source-zero primary expansion contains the requisite
