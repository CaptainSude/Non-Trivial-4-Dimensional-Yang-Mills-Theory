RESULT AND SCOPE

The new readable Part-II formula supports a sharper conclusion than
"every potential is separately local." Within ONE prepared expression
(1.72), fix the new background/determining set and every retained outer
slot. For two admitted component families differing by removal of X,
the common noninterior coefficient V(C) is identical whenever C contains
no X. New allowed domain labels need not contain X, but their outside
part is pinned to a fixed enlargement of X. Combining this observation
with TURN61's ordinary-tree incidence bound gives

 |E_present-E_removed|
   <= alpha K [|partial_out X|+|N_1(Xplus)|].               (0.1)

Here E is the COMPLETE residual noninterior exponent in the curly
bracket, Xplus is the source's one-layer enlargement in the cell
convention in use, and K includes the stated continuous-tree/cube
conversion. Private internal variables are retained throughout.

This comparison does not remove the interior V(Xplus) from its raw
measure. Nor does it independently prove that every erased decorated
history is an allowed term of the same sum: that is the separate
catalogue/deletion question. It is not a comparison of two preparation
procedures or two different output backgrounds.

3. WHY NEW DOMAIN LABELS ARE NOT A REMOTE EFFECT

This finite-graph argument handles the potential domain-list change
explicitly. Use the actual native component convention for the
whole-component tests. B109 printed p257 defines localization domains
by FACE-connected chains of M-cubes. A continuous tree through their
cubes supplies only TOUCHING connectivity; these graphs must not be
identified. The incidence conversion below explicitly uses both.

In the following collar argument N_1 may be the larger touching
one-layer neighborhood, provided the actual enlargement contains it;
otherwise use exactly the adjacency used by that enlargement and
enlarge the final counting collar by a fixed number of layers.
The needed premise is the displayed N_1(Z_0) subset B_0. The argument
is a graph argument for the actual enlarged-component convention.

Write Z_1=Z_0 union X, where X is one connected component of Z_1.
Let B_b be the one-layer enlargement of Z_b in that convention. Retain
the actual union-preserving enlargement premise:

 B_1=B_0 union H,
 N_1(Z_0) subset B_0,    H=Xplus connected and containing X. (3.1)

In particular H is contained in B_1; merely requiring
B_1 minus B_0 subset H would not be sufficient.
Put A=N_1(H). Denote by D_b the connected domains C such that

 C intersects B_b in a nonempty union of WHOLE components of B_b,
                      and C minus B_b is nonempty.        (3.2)

The enlarged-component corollary in TURN61 implies that every such C
also includes every original Z_b component it meets whole.

CLAIM A. If C belongs to D_1 but not D_0, then X is contained in C.

Indeed whole-component inclusion for the larger B_1 implies it for B_0,
and C minus B_1 nonempty implies C minus B_0 nonempty. The only possible
failure is C intersect B_0 empty. Then a B_1 component included in C
meets B_1 minus B_0, hence Xplus, hence contains X. This proves Claim A.

CLAIM B. If C belongs to D_0 but not D_1, then

                    (C minus Z_0) intersects A.          (3.3)

If C meets D'=B_1 minus B_0, that intersection itself supplies a root:
D' lies in H and is disjoint from Z_0.

Otherwise C avoids D'. Its nonempty outside part C minus B_0 remains
outside B_1 too. It must therefore fail the whole-B_1-component rule.
Some B_0 component included in C is connected in B_1 to a point beyond
that component through D'. At the first transition into D', its
predecessor w belongs to that B_0 component and hence to C, and w is
adjacent to D'. The point w cannot be in Z_0: otherwise its adjacent
point of D' would be in N_1(Z_0), which is contained in B_0. Thus
w belongs to (C minus Z_0) intersect N_1(H), proving Claim B.

This argument also covers splitting a very large B_1 component when
X is removed. It does not charge the volume of that large component.
Every newly allowed branch includes an OUTSIDE root near the removed
set. A mere statement that C meets a collar would be weaker and could
incorrectly charge a huge surviving component.

In particular domains disjoint from A have identical admissibility.
The result uses adjacency and whole-component inclusion, not a free
contraction of retained regions.

4. THE QUANTITATIVE INCREMENT

Use the actual bound (1.68) for the noninterior coefficients on their
admitted variables. In graph notation write it as

         ||V_b(C)|| <= alpha exp[-kappa d_(Z_b)(C)].       (4.1)

For the actual continuous-tree metric (1.67), use the sharpened
conversion and two-graph count in native_incidence_budget_turn62.txt,
Sections2C--3. Let

 D_t=3^d-1, J=2^d, B=2^(J+1), A=4 D_t B, c_d=4J.

The native domain C is face-connected and contains every face-component
of Z it meets whole. Every included Z component has a FACE neighbor
in C minus Z, by taking a face path to that outside set. Thus at most
2d |C minus Z| possible components need encoding; 2d<=J. The spanning
tree supplied by a continuous tree is counted in the TOUCHING graph.
There are at most (4D_t)^m rooted embedded trees of m edges. Choosing
the outside subset of its vertices and its adjacent whole native
components gives the conservative count B A^m.

A continuous tree of length M ell meets a touching-connected set of
cubes containing all required outside cubes, with a spanning tree of
at most J-1+c_d ell edges. Consequently a valid incidence constant is

 K=exp[kappa(J-1)/c_d] B/(1-A exp[-kappa/c_d]),            (4.1a)

provided kappa>c_d log A. This threshold is about1123.3 in d=4.
The source bound (1.68) has the stronger exponent (1+2 beta)kappa;
discarding that extra reserve here is safe. The distinguished component
uses a face-boundary root, bounded also by its touching boundary count.

The parameter order is fixed kappa above both the native and incidence
thresholds, then the required fixed M and localization constants, then
the weak floor. This is supported by B109(1.18), Theorem3, and B119
after(2.31), as detailed in the budget note. Small alpha cannot replace
an insufficient decay exponent. No frozen TURN61 file is changed.

Let E_b be the sum of residual noninterior coefficients. By (2.1), common
domains not containing X cancel exactly. Every remaining present term
contains X, by Claim A or by definition of this difference. TURN61's
distinguished-component incidence bound gives

 sum_(present affected C) ||V_1(C)||
                             <= alpha K |partial_out X|. (4.2)

A remaining removed-side coefficient either is new, hence satisfies
Claim B, or has a common domain containing X. In the latter case X is
outside Z_0 and lies in A. Thus every such term has an outside root
in A. The outside-root sum yields

 sum_(removed affected C) ||V_0(C)||
                                      <= alpha K |A|.    (4.3)

Combining proves (0.1), uniformly in the retained private variables
and in the size or number of surviving components. The same proof
works for a zero-filled domain catalogue; absent terms are not silently
identified with nonzero common terms.

If X lies in the native cube of side100MR_k, and its source enlargement
has thickness bounded by CMR_k, then in M-cell units

             |A|+|partial_out X| <= C' R_k^d.             (4.4)

Consequently the insertion exponent is at most C' alpha K R_k^d.
The once-reserved p0(g_k) budget can pay this together with the actual
candidate census when its exponent dominates these R_k costs. The
interior history volume from (1.69) is NOT included in (4.4).

7. REALITY OF THE FIXED-RECORD EXPONENT

The positivity needed for insertion is pointwise in the real private
records, before the sum over families. It must not be inferred just

p380, that all integrand expressions in (1.71) are real at the real

positivity of each complete private measure. The residual potential
requires the following additional explanation of the native

Use the native real structure: complex conjugation in real local
Lie-algebra coordinates, and its induced antiholomorphic involution
on the complexified group. The real gauge group and real fluctuation
fields are its fixed locus. The native incoming localized actions are
Schwarz-real. This is the usual real input of the published inductive
representation, not a statement about arbitrary allowed complex

Here is its preservation through the specific operations used above.
Real Wilson actions, positive real Jacobian/normalization logarithms,
and real integration give Schwarz-real initial functions. The
reconstruction maps have real defining equations and their admitted
local branch is unique, so applying the involution gives the same
branch. In particular their Taylor coefficients in real variables
are real. Products, sums, differences and real Taylor subtractions
preserve this property. The decoupling parameters are scalar real
parameters before complexification; expansion in those parameters
and grouping coefficients by a geometric support commute with
conjugation. Cauchy contour integrals used to express or bound these
coefficients do not change their reality. Convergent connected-log
series have real combinatorial coefficients and the branch fixed
by the positive real integral. Absolutely convergent resummations
and the real finite coordinate averages preserve the same property.

These are precisely the operations in the source chain: the
prelocalization exponent (1.49), the differences (1.59)--(1.60), the
real reconstruction and decoupling localizations (1.61)--(1.64), and
