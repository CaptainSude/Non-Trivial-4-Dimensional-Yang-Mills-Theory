1. AN ELEMENTARY SIGNED-KERNEL LEMMA

Let K(y,z) be a linear-map-valued discrete kernel from a fixed source
component to a finite-dimensional quadratic output space. The measure
in the z sum includes its ACTUAL physical quadrature and any shifted
source lattice. Suppose the entire source set and its weights are
invariant under z->2y-z, and the complete kernel satisfies

 sum_z K(y,z)=0,
 K(y,2y-z)=K(y,z),
 sum_z ||K(y,z)|| |z-y|^2 <= M_y < infinity.             (1.1)

Also assume absolute summability, or a common symmetric summation with
the corresponding first and second moments absolutely summable.
Then for a scalar C^2 profile f on the convex hull of the reads,

 ||sum_z K(y,z) f(z)|| <= (M_y/2) ||D^2 f||_infty.       (1.2)

Proof. Evenness makes sum_z K(y,z)(z-y)=0 by pairing the terms.
The zero moment removes f(y). Taylor's integral identity gives

 f(z)-f(y)-Df(y)(z-y)
  =integral_0^1 (1-t) D^2f(y+t(z-y))[z-y,z-y] dt.

Insert that identity before taking absolute values and use (1.1).
This proves (1.2) for Banach-valued output as well. A centered integral
kernel has the identical proof. A finite periodic lattice can be used
inside a nonwrapping chart, or with a separately justified periodic
Taylor convention; arbitrary displacement representatives cannot be
substituted silently.

An analogous C^(1,theta) estimate costs the (1+theta)-moment. If only
the zero moment is known, the stronger second-order claim is false:
K=delta_(y+e)-delta_y has zero sum but acts nontrivially on linear f.
Evenness, or an independently proved first-moment identity, is needed.

2. WHY THE PROTECTED CUBIC IS A CANDIDATE

For distinct axes i,j,k,l use color vectors for SU(2) curvature and set

 X_ij;kl = det(F_ik,F_kl,F_lj)-det(F_il,F_lk,F_kj).       (2.1)

Its exact hypercubic properties are checked in the companion note:
it is odd under swapping i and j and has reflection character
epsilon_i epsilon_j. Every quadratic curvature polynomial with that
reflection character is a linear combination of
Tr(F_ik F_jk) and Tr(F_il F_jl), both even under that swap. Thus an
equivariant COMPLETE homogeneous linear transport has zero universal
quadratic extraction on this source sector.

If that transport has a translation-covariant pointed source kernel
K(y,z) for its extracted low response, the constant-profile conclusion
is precisely the first identity in (1.1). This requires the whole
coefficient, including its static/feed/reference companions. It does
not mean a single fixed-geometry polymer has a zero quadratic part.

Full coordinate inversion -I fixes every curvature two-form component
and hence both (2.1) and all quadratic curvature components. Therefore
an exactly same-centered inversion-covariant source kernel is even in
z-y. This gives the second identity in (1.1). A compatible centered
clover/read convention and the COMPLETE transformed pivot/geometry
catalogue are essential. If an individual near box or a surviving
large-field history breaks that covariance, the conclusion does not
apply to it without its missing/boundary comparison.

The existing exponential carrier estimates suggest that a second

does not infer a complete quadratic extraction kernel from a scalar

source points, physical masses, all current reads and the actual old

factor is MR_i r_i, not an invented r_i reach.

3. ENGINEERING MEANING AND THE REMAINING CHANNELS

Suppose such a regular one-leg kernel at physical scale r has a
positive second-moment bound

 sum_z ||K(y,z)|| (|z-y|/r)^2 <= B_r.                    (3.1)

Then (1.2) gives C r^2 B_r ||D^2 f||. A dimension-six cubic insertion
in four dimensions has an r^(-2) engineering source coefficient at
that scale. The product is consequently C B_r ||D^2 f||, rather than
an artificial r^(-1) loss from using only a first difference.

This is a cancellation of a power-counting loss. It is not convergence
of the remaining sum over RG dates. The bound B_r can contain coupling
powers, and even B_r proportional to g_r^2 need not be summable toward
the ultraviolet without the appropriate renormalization/calibration.
Second-derivative profile terms represent dimension-six descendants
of quadratic fields. They must be retained in the finite engineering-
dimension source frame and treated with their exact identities; they
are not declared zero because the constant-profile quadratic coefficient
vanishes. Possible total derivatives must likewise be handled as local
observable identities, not removed from a variable-profile source.

The symmetry statement is linear in the protected source. Products of
two such sources can transform in a quadratic sector and can generate
quadratic contacts. The explicit Gaussian contact in
loop_word_source_bridge_turn34 already demonstrates the general issue.
Separate-point joining may pay physical short-scale powers, but the
full original mixed contact and outer conditional mean still matter.

The algebraic tensor is also a nonzero Gaussian Wick observable, not

For example -partial_0^2 D-partial_i^2 D=-4/(4pi^2 t^4);

The explicit component is
