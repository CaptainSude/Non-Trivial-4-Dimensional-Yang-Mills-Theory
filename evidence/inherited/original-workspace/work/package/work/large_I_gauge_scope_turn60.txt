1. EXACT GEOMETRIC AND HISTORICAL ADMISSION

Printed p177 imposes TWO conditions on components selected for this basic
replacement:

 (i) the component is contained in a cube of size 100 M R_k;
 (ii) during the preceding N steps no new large-field regions were created
      inside it, and its predecessor regions satisfy (i) on their own scales.

The source says its region-construction rule then makes the relevant last-N
regions rectangular parallelepipeds. This is an admitted finite component
with its actual history, not an arbitrary subset of a periodic lattice.
The omitted components are left in the complementary T operation, (1.1).
An arbitrary winding component cannot be substituted for these rectangles.

The preparation on pp178--192 uses that chronology and several distinct
cutoffs. In particular p179 explains that the old factors (1.5)--(1.9) are
not gauge invariant for the integrations one wishes to perform. Their
preliminary treatment is therefore substantive, not an optional gauge
convention.

After these integrations, p192 defines Lambda in (1.73). It is a union of
M-cubes, is rectangular, and is contained in a current-scale cube of size
100 M. The reconstructed field U_(k,Z), (1.74), is defined separately in
each component of Z. It still has its fine-scale determining data; the
bounded size of Lambda does not make every background-field read finite
range automatically.

The function chi_(k,Lambda), (1.75), p193, depends only on the retained
field in Z intersect Lambda-complement. Its meaning is the existence of
an interior extension satisfying the stated regularity. The complement
is kept as a large-field branch. It is not the same function as the new
reference cut chi(Lambda), (1.101).

Thus the native reference is available only on an admitted real branch
and its proved complex extension. None of the inspected pages defines
the reconstructed fine action for every compact-group field without
the reference cut.

2. THE ACTUAL T0 GAUGE, INCLUDING ROOT AND BOUNDARY CONVENTIONS

Printed pp195--196 give the missing construction, rather than just naming
an unspecified maximal tree.

The old field is written V''=V' V0 on the determining set B0 in Lambda,
with V0 obtained from U0=U_(k,Z)(V_Lambda). Bonds crossing the boundary of
Lambda belong to B0; bonds crossing the boundary of the excluded innermost
domain do not. The second integral in (1.76) has the gauge invariance
needed for the stated Faddeev--Popov insertion. This assertion follows the
earlier preparation; it is not asserted for all the original cuts before
that preparation.

For two successive nested rectangular parallelepipeds

       P1=product_mu [a_mu,b_mu],
       P2=product_mu [a'_mu,b'_mu] subset P1,

the source rescales to unit lattice, takes

       y=(a_1+1/2,...,a_d+1/2),
       a in (a'_1,b'_1),

and joins each lattice point x in P1 minus P2 to y. For x_1<=a it uses the
coordinate-ordered axial contour. For x_1>a it routes through the outer
face x_1=b_1-1/2, as displayed on p196. Their union is the prescribed tree
T on the shell. These paths read the rectangular endpoints and the chosen
coordinate frame, not an ambient torus-wide graph search.

The construction has the stated restriction property when P2 is enlarged:
delete the paths for removed points and retain the original construction
parameter a. Recomputing an unrelated a after each restriction is not what
this statement says. The original a remains admissible when the inner
rectangle is enlarged.

Each shell tree leaves one gauge degree, removed by a specified external
bond. For intermediate inner domains the source gives the bond

 [(a'_1-1/2,a'_2+1/2,...,a'_d+1/2),
  (a'_1+1/2,a'_2+1/2,...,a'_d+1/2)].

For P1=Lambda it also adds [(y_1-1,y_2,...,y_d),y].
The external bonds on internal interfaces are at the appropriate larger
scale. For the last, excluded inner domain no gauge is fixed and no
external bonds crossing its boundary are added. Their union is T0,
described as a tree fixing the gauge completely in B0. The source then
inserts delta_(T0)(V') in (1.76).

The subsequent chi' in (1.82) is a genuine new partition of unity on each
component. Its complementary pieces are retained among the other
large-field terms. No assertion permits changing T0 after keeping the
same gauge-dependent chi' on the old variables.

The use of relative variables matters. For fixed V0, a gauge change of
V'' induces the corresponding twisted gauge action on V'. A tree can
fix V'=1 with this action as well, but this is not an excuse to drop the
background or its boundary data. Printed p197 expressly requires the
compensating V_h transformation and the adjoint transformation of V'
when U0 is changed to the axial/Landau gauge. The full integrand, with
those transformations, is invariant.

3. G_i, THE COMPONENT SUM, AND THE FINITE AVERAGING LAW

Printed p201 first factorizes the remaining integrations and history sums
over components X_i. It replaces global histories by the corresponding
localized sequences and relabels B0,T0,Lambda as B_i,T_i,Lambda_i.
In (1.100), the summation inside EACH component bracket includes its
localized history and BOTH G_i,T_i.

Here G_i is a graph fixing the axial gauge on Lambda_i. The source does
not enumerate a probability measure on every possible spanning tree.
Instead, the paragraph after (1.101) expressly permits implementing the
graph averages by the coordinate prescription acted on by

                   H_d = {signed coordinate permutations},
                   |H_d| = 2^d d!.

The safe implementation is a labelled frame average of the COMPLETE
component construction: the permitted coordinate prescription produces
its G_i,T_i and every associated frame-dependent factor. In particular
one must not average just the displayed reference density and leave
the old T_i/cut factors outside that average. The product of component
brackets supplies local component sums; it is not a single globally
shared frame label constrained across the entire torus.

The literal printed normalization is inconsistent:

  - (1.100) has 1/N_i outside the graph/history sum;
  - the following prose says N_i=1/(2^d d!).

Both facts were visually checked. If the sum over frames is an ordinary
sum, the asserted arithmetic average must instead have coefficient
1/(2^d d!), hence N_i=2^d d! in the displayed prefactor convention.
Using the literal prose value together with that displayed convention
would give total coordinate weight (2^d d!)^2 for one component.

The intended normalized average is supported by the source's explicit
word "averages" and normalization identity (1.102). We therefore record
the normalized interpretation as an explicit consistency correction,
not as a published erratum or a change to the PDF. Retain all 2^d d!
labels even if some produce the same physical graph; collapsing equal
graphs without their multiplicities changes the measure.

This resolves a major part of the former G0/T0-law question. One need
not reconstruct a unique measure on all abstract trees: the source
expressly permits this finite frame implementation. It does not say
that an arbitrary new forest, global random spanning-tree law, soft
orbit profile, or tree-dependent window has the same physical kernel.
The sentence mentioning alternative Landau-type gauges is not a
completed analytic construction of every such alternative.

4. WHAT SHORT-LIFT LOCALITY NOW FOLLOWS

Fix a permitted coordinate construction with all its choices, including
the shell parameters, boundary convention and component history. Let D
include the COMPLETE finite deciding geometry for that construction:
all nested rectangles, their scale labels, outer and excluded-inner
boundary bonds, roots, and the coordinate contours. Assume D has an
injective lattice lift preserving this data and the fields on it.

Then the explicit shell prescription is transported exactly: its endpoint
inequalities, coordinate segments, exterior bonds and restrictions are
identical after relabelling. The same is true of the ordinary axial
prescription for G_i in the lifted rectangle. A fixed deterministic
geometric choice of the initially free shell parameter can be used on
both lifts; its value is transported with the data during restrictions.
No new random choice with volume-dependent weights is needed.

Apply the SAME label r in H_d to the two copies of the complete component
construction. This is a bijection of labelled choices with identical
weight 1/|H_d|. Haar factors, normalized tree delta functions and the
number of constrained variables are likewise matched on those complete
finite graphs. This proves locality of the permitted ANCILLARY CHOICE
LAW and of these coordinate factors on complete short lifted data.

The condition on D is essential. A coordinate seam on a periodic display
causes no difficulty when D lifts; a genuinely noncontractible deciding
component is not replaced by a local rectangle under this argument.
The source's bounded-component criteria specify the class being treated.

This is not a whole-cover theorem. Analytic reconstruction may have
separately localized walk reads; raw history factors, global determining
data and scalar normalizers must still be compared on their own complete
supports. Equality of the finite gauge-choice law does not establish
equality of all field-dependent kernels on two different tori. It also
does not establish equality between two different choices of construction
tree on the SAME torus.

5. THE NORMALIZED REFERENCE AND REAL POSITIVITY

The reference factor in (1.100), p201, is literally

 q_e,dG(v) proportional to
   delta_G(v) chi(Lambda)(v)
   exp[-g_k^(-2) A(zeta,U_(k,X)(v V_Lambda(e)))] d v_Lambda,

with the denominator the integral of EXACTLY this expression in the
same relative field, with the same gauge graph, reference background,
action and cut. Equation (1.101) is

        chi(Lambda)
        = chi({ |(1/i) log v(b)| < M0 epsilon_k : b in Lambda }).

This is the reconstructed localized fine action, not the effective full
old action or a freely substituted coarse Wilson action. It is evaluated
only on the admitted reference window.

Printed p176 states positivity of the densities and nonemptiness of the
integration domains as the reason the denominators are positive. The
detailed minimum and domain facts are attached to the weak regularity
conditions on pp193--197: Proposition1 on p194 states a unique critical
orbit/minimum and analytic extension for sufficiently regular exterior
fields, and p195 connects that background with the integration and
effective-action analytic domains. The proof of Proposition1 is expressly
deferred. Thus accepting the published native minimum/domain module is
still an input, not a new proof furnished by merely reading (1.100).

On an admitted real branch, the gauge delta means Haar integration on
its slice, chi is nonnegative, and the Wilson factor is positive. The
nonempty weak neighborhood on the slice gives a strictly positive
reference denominator (with the usual zero-dimensional interpretation).
All cuts and background data must be those of this actual branch.
This conclusion is not a uniform complex lower bound on an arbitrary
larger domain or an unrestricted full-group fine-action theorem.

6. EXACT INTEGRATED PRESERVATION, AND ITS INSERTION LIMIT

The chronology is unusually explicit:

  p193: all preceding preparations preserve the density itself;
        the next operation is only an equivalence of integrals.
  p194: the old field over Lambda is integrated out, and the result
        is regarded as independent of the current field in that slot.
  p200: the transformed density is stated to have the same integral
        as rho_k, while generally being a different density.
  p201: the matching normalized reference is inserted in (1.100);
        (1.102) states integral R' rho_k = integral rho_k.

Consequently the old integral and new reference quotient are not being
identified from a heuristic similarity of two different denominators.
The actual formula supplies their variable slots and the normalization.

For a resolved positive old branch, one can retain its old variables as
passive records while sampling this normalized reference. The resulting
positive joint measure has the original old branch as its old marginal.
An old, resolved count is preserved by integrating the new reference.
This is a consequence of the literal source formula, not an assertion
that every old observable evaluated on the NEW reference has its old
expectation.

The chosen gauge representation requires care even for retained records:
an original gauge-noninvariant observable needs its removed gauge
variables or the corresponding inverse reconstruction. Gauge-invariant
observables can be transported through the stated compensating changes,
but they still need the ORIGINAL field record after the reference
resampling if they depend on that replaced interior. Gauge invariance
alone does not make the old and new current reference laws equal.

For a new event defined after the redraw, its indicator remains inside
the new reference expectation. A later complex source cannot simply be
substituted into a frozen real cut or reference background without the
appropriate marked-domain estimates. The equality of total integrals
does not supply such estimates.

This closes the specific missing source identification of the basic
positive replacement. It does not, by itself, prove an arbitrary-source
renormalization theorem or transport every original observable through
all subsequent localization and exponentiation steps.

7. CONSEQUENCES FOR THE OPTIONAL FULL-ACTION REFERENCE ROUTE

The native reference already contains the matched reconstructed action,
hard weak window and exact reference normalizer. There is no longer a
source-access reason to replace it merely to obtain a normalized positive
basic-R step. The optional TURN59 soft-reference comparison remains a
separate proved local perturbation at its own hypotheses.

The supplied pages do not eliminate its limitations:

 - the action is only known on the admitted reconstructed-field domain;
 - the relative gauge and exterior/background chronology are real data;
 - changing the construction gauge can change the current reference law;
 - replacing the window/profile requires new recursive admission bounds;
 - analytic continuation must respect the full specified native domains.

In particular, the old theta-graph counterexample to automatic equality
of different CURRENT tree kernels is unaffected. It never contradicted
choicewise normalization or equality of retained ORIGINAL marginals.
