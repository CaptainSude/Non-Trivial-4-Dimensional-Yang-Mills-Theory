 A_0 A=Q_M^T a_pen B,

3. UNIFORM FINE-CURVATURE BLOCH SYMBOL

Index each fine plaquette by its containing unit cell z, a relative

             F_M(z,u,alpha)=(d_eta H_M B)_(z,u,alpha).

Its Fourier symbol F_M(p;u) from the coarse midpoint components B is

 F_M(0;u)=0,
 F_M(p;u)=i C(p)+O(|p|^2),
 [C(p)v]_(mu nu)=p_mu v_nu-p_nu v_mu.                    (3.1)

The remainder and its differentiated bounds are uniform. Midpoint
phases do not change the first coefficient because the zeroth one

the total action symbol alone would not have identified it.

Coarse gradient inputs have zero lifted curl. Indeed a feasible fine

independent of the choice of coarse gauge fixing.

4. THE FULL COARSE GAUSSIAN AND ITS LONG-DISTANCE CURVATURE

Choose for convenience the positive coarse gauge-fixed covariance

       C_M(p)=[q_M(p)+d_c(p)d_c(p)^*]^-1, p!=0.           (4.1)

In the midpoint basis the denominator is
|p|^2 I+O(|p|^4). Equation(1.4) bounds it below by
|d_c(p)|^2 I, and the uniform analytic q_M bounds its derivatives.
Consequently, for every fixed multiindex beta and 0<|p|<p_*,

 ||partial^beta C_M(p)|| <= C_beta |p|^(-2-|beta|),
 ||partial^beta[C_M(p)-|p|^-2 I]|| <= C_beta |p|^(-|beta|).
                                                               (4.2)

For example the second bound follows from
C_M-|p|^-2 I=-|p|^-2 [q_M+d_c d_c^*-|p|^2I] C_M
and differentiated inverse identities. Away from zero all derivatives
are uniformly bounded. In four dimensions C_M is locally integrable,
so it defines the infinite-volume Gaussian. Constants on finite tori
may be fixed to zero; lifted curl annihilates the harmonic sector.

The covariance between two lifted fine curvatures has Bloch symbol

 K_M(p;u,v)=F_M(p;u) C_M(p) F_M(-p;v)^T.                 (4.3)

This is the actual low-pass minimizing-field covariance. It is not
the full fine Feynman covariance. The same object can be expressed
by the alias formula: with P_r, Omega_r and q_M as in the flat
covariance note, its fine alias blocks are

 d_r P_r^-1 Omega_r^* q_M Omega_s P_s^-1 d_s^*.           (4.4)

The q_M in (4.4), rather than W_M^-1 alone, removes the coarse
gradient direction. Using only the unprojected Feynman conditional
mean would give the wrong fine-curvature covariance in general.

Equations(3.1),(4.2) imply

 K_M(p;u,v)=C(p) C(p)^T/|p|^2+R_M(p;u,v),
 ||partial^beta R_M(p;u,v)||<=C_beta |p|^(1-|beta|).        (4.5)

The first term is the continuum Maxwell curvature covariance symbol
in the normalization(1.1). Matrix/color signs from the two factors i
cancel. The full symbol(4.3) is periodic in the fixed cell labels;
any midpoint quasiperiodic phases cancel in it.

Here is the Fourier decay argument, including its uniform remainder.
Take a fixed smooth cutoff near p=0 and a dyadic partition there. On
an annulus |p|~r, (4.5) gives the L1 derivative bound C_N r^(5-N).
For |z|=R>=1, the pieces with r<=R^-1 sum directly to C R^-5.
On r>R^-1 integrate by parts N>5 times in a coordinate with
|z_mu|>=R/2; the bound is C_N R^-N r^(5-N), whose dyadic sum is
C R^-5. The part away from zero is a smooth periodic symbol and
has faster than any fixed power decay. Finally the inverse transform
of the cutoff homogeneous leading symbol differs, away from z=0,
from the continuum homogeneous distribution by a rapidly decreasing
function: every derivative of order N>4 of its high-frequency
complement is integrable, and integration by parts applies there.
Thus

 K_M(z;u,v)=K_Maxwell(z)+O((1+|z|)^-5), |z|>=1,           (4.6)
 |K_M(z;u,v)|<=C(1+|z|)^-4,

uniformly M,u,v. If actual fine positions x=z+u, y=z'+v are used,
replace the leading K_Maxwell(z-z') by K_Maxwell(x-y): their
arguments differ by O(1), and its derivative is O(|z-z'|^-5).
The same error remains. The contact part of the homogeneous Fourier
distribution is irrelevant because the two packets are separated.

5. FINE ENERGY, WICK CONTRACTION, AND UNIFORM QUADRATURE

For a coarse Gaussian B of covariance C_M define the quadratic
fine energy smeared by f_rho(x)=f(x/rho) as

 E_M(f_rho)=(1/2) eta^4 sum_(fine x) f(x/rho)
                           sum_(alpha,color) F_M(x,alpha,color)^2.
                                                               (5.1)

The same formula with subtraction of the mean defines the centered
energy; its cross covariance is unchanged. Fine plaquette centers
or tails can be used consistently, with an O(eta/rho) error in the
test function. Wick's identity gives

 Cov(E_M(f_rho),E_M(h_rho))
  =(dim g/2) eta^8 sum_(x,y) f(x/rho)h(y/rho)
                         sum_(alpha,beta) K_M(x,y)_(alpha beta)^2.
                                                               (5.2)

The trace metric determines dim g=3 for the present SU(2) convention.
Because the supports of f,h are separated, all contributing distances
are at least c rho and at most C rho. Equation(4.6) therefore changes
each squared curvature factor by O(rho^-9). The weighted number of
fine pairs is O(rho^8), uniformly eta<=1. Hence the integrated error
is O(rho^-1).

The leading continuum kernel has homogeneity -8. Substitute x=rho X,
y=rho Y in the continuum double integral; the rho^8 volume factor
cancels. In the rescaled variables, the fine Riemann-sum mesh is
eta/rho<=rho^-1. The integrand is smooth and supported a fixed
positive distance from its diagonal, so standard cellwise first
derivative estimates bound the quadrature error by C eta/rho.
Combining the two errors proves(0.1).

If the actual source replaces f(p/rho) by a nearby coarse anchor
sum_z h_z(p)f(z/rho), with sum_z h_z(p)=1, uniformly bounded absolute
overlap, and |p-z|<=C on each support, the test-function error is
O(rho^-1). The same separated-kernel bound in(5.2) gives another
O(rho^-1) integrated covariance error. Thus either actual fine
plaquette weighting or its admitted local anchoring has the same
limit. The exact source convention is the Wilson term

for extraction; it is not an arbitrary total-action localization.
