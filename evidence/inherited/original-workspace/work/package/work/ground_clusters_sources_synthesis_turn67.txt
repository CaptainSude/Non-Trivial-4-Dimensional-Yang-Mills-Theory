1. THE LAW IS THE ACTUAL HAMILTONIAN GROUND LAW

On a finite periodic spatial SU2 lattice, use the Pauli/2 metric,
n sites, K=-Delta, and

 H_a=g_a^2 K/(2a)+4 S/(g_a^2 a),
 S=sum_(spatial p) h_p, h_p=1-Tr U_p/2.

Let Omega_a>0 be its normalized ground state, E0(a) its ground
energy, and mu_a=Omega_a^2 dHaar. TURN66 constructed this state
from actual anisotropic Wilson measures by taking the temporal
ground limit and then the continuous-time limit at fixed spatial
lattice. That proof retained the complete normalizer.

Its direct trial estimate gives E0(a)<=9n/a. The new multiplier
identity and the pointwise bound |grad S|^2<=8S imply

 log E_mu_a exp(t S/g_a^2)
                  <=(a E0(a)/2)artanh(t/2)
                  <=(9n/2)artanh(t/2), 0<=t<2.         (1.1)

This uses the actual ground equation. It is stronger than the
unmarked first moment but remains an extensive action bound.

2. JOINT LOCAL CONTROL, INCLUDING TILE BOUNDARIES

Take even spatial periods and rectangular tiles of fixed lattice
side lengths l_i>=2, with N_i/l_i powers of2. Put V=l_1l_2l_3.
Spatial site-reflection positivity follows by conditioning on
the two fixed planes in the finite Wilson model, then passing
to the fixed-spatial ground limits.

The multipoint estimate is proved explicitly. Normalize each
block function by its full-board dissemination norm, maximize
over the finite set of label patterns, and apply RP
Cauchy--Schwarz. Either reflected half of a maximizing pattern
is still maximizing. Dyadic reflections then disseminate one
label throughout the board. Its normalized expectation is1.
This gives the full mixed-pattern chessboard inequality.

For strict-interior tile actions S_b^int, the copies are disjoint:

 log E exp(sum_b t_b S_b^int/g_a^2)
          <=(9V/2)sum_b artanh(t_b/2), 0<=t_b<2.        (2.1)

For closed tiles every plaquette is covered and appears at most
twice. Shared boundary links are permitted by the site-RP
pairing. Consequently

 log E exp(sum_b t_b S_b^cl/(2g_a^2))
          <=(9V/2)sum_b artanh(t_b/2), 0<=t_b<2.        (2.2)

For the closed-tile bad events

 A_b={S_b^cl>=g_a^2 L^2},
 q=min{1,3^(9V/4)exp(-L^2/2)},

and EVERY prescribed finite set A of distinct tiles,

                 mu_a(intersection_(b in A) A_b)<=q^|A|. (2.3)

This is a simultaneous probability estimate under the original
ground law. It assumes no independence of block variables.

Give the tile board a specified adjacency of maximum degree D.
For the bad component C_b containing a fixed tile b,

 mu_a(|C_b|>=k)<=q(D^2q)^(k-1).                         (2.4)

The proof counts rooted connected k-sets by their canonical
depth-first spanning-tree walks. Taking the union over the
whole board costs its number K_board of tiles:

 mu_a(some bad component has size>=k)
                    <=K_board q(D^2q)^(k-1).           (2.5)

If D^2q<=exp(-c0)<1 and the physical volume is fixed, then
K_board=O(a^-3). Taking k>=4log(1/a)/c0+1 makes (2.5) O(a),
in fixed physical units. Outside this exceptional event every
component has physical diameter O(a log(1/a)), which tends
to zero. Touching adjacency (D<=26) covers all geometric contacts.
Face adjacency (D<=6) gives the corresponding face-connected result.

These events measure unflowed plaquette action at one fixed tile
resolution. They do not include Bałaban's complete reconstruction,
minimizer, gauge, or history predicates. Shrinking component
diameter does not by itself bound its effect on the flow.

4. AN ALTERNATIVE PHYSICAL CONSUMER: SEPARATED COMPOSITES

Let Q_a be a physically calibrated real gauge-invariant local
insertion, bounded at each regulator but not necessarily bounded
uniformly as a->0. Its renormalization is part of the work still
required. In the actual ground state set

 A_a=H_a-E0(a), q_a=Q_a-EQ_a,
 C_a(t)=<q_a Omega_a,exp(-t A_a)q_a Omega_a>.

For t>0, the vector exp(-t A_a)q_a Omega_a has squared norm
C_a(2t), and its energy form is bounded by C_a(t)/(e t).
Thus a separated lower bound C_a(2t)>=c>0 together with
C_a(t)<=C would avoid the equal-time variance and nonlinear
spatial-flow derivative requirements for this vector.

The new exact formulation uses an insertion integrated over a
positive-time interval[t,t+delta]. Its reflected norm is

 R_a(t,delta)
   =int exp(-2tE)[(1-exp(-delta E))/E]^2 rho_a(dE),       (4.1)

where rho_a is the positive centered spectral measure of Q_a.
For0<s<t its energy is at most R_a(t-s,delta)/(2e s).
An earlier-window upper bound and later-window lower bound
are therefore sufficient for a nonzero bounded-energy vector.

This is an exact consumer, not a proved interacting estimate.
A full continuum local algebra still requires compatible mixed
insertions, physical renormalization, symmetry and reconstruction.

5. FINITE-TIME SOURCE DATA EXTRACTS EXACTLY THAT NORM
