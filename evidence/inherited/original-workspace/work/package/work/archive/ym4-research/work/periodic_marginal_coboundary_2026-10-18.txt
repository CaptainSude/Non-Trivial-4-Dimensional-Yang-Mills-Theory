1. A BOUNDED PERIODIC DISCRETE PRIMITIVE

Let a:Z^4 -> V be p-periodic in every coordinate, with values in any normed
finite-dimensional tensor or coefficient algebra, and mean zero on
{0,...,p-1}^4. The argument also works for rectangular periods, replacing
p by their maximum. Write A_mu for averaging in coordinate mu, and set

 f_mu=A_1...A_(mu-1)a - A_1...A_mu a,  mu=1,...,4.

Then a=sum_mu f_mu, every f_mu has zero sum along its mu-line, and
||f_mu||_infinity<=2||a||_infinity. For each line define

 b_mu(z)=sum_(t=0)^(z_mu) f_mu(z_1,...,t,...,z_4),

using representatives 0<=z_mu<p and then periodic extension. The zero
line sum gives, including the wrap at zero,

 a(z)=sum_mu D^-_mu b_mu(z),
 D^-_mu b(z)=b(z)-b(z-e_mu),
 ||b_mu||_infinity<=2p||a||_infinity.                (1)

Subtracting the line mean of b is optional. All these operations are
linear contractions except the displayed factor p. They therefore act
coefficientwise in the copy-label l1 algebra, with that sum inside the
spatial norm. No factor equal to the number of copies is introduced.

For any finitely supported, or compatible periodic, root family F_z,

 sum_z a(z)F_z = -sum_(mu,z) b_mu(z)D^+_mu F_z.     (2)

This is summation by parts on the BIRTH ROOT lattice. It is not a bare
mesh derivative. Taking a primitive on mesh eta instead would give a
factor p r_i/eta and would be inappropriate for a Holder estimate.
