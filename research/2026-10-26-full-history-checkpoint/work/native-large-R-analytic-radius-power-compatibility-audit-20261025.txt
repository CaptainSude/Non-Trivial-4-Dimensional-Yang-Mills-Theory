1. NOTATION AND A FEASIBLE FIXED CHOICE

Let x=g^-2, ell=log x, R_sp the native smallest power of L no smaller than
ell^r, r>=2. Thus ell^r<=R_sp<L ell^r. Distinguish the real-cut functions
p0(g)=A0 ell^p0 and p1(g)=A1 ell^p1 from their exponents p0,p1. The analytic
caps are alpha0=g C0 ell^q and alpha1=g C1 ell^q, with a fixed sufficiently
large C1/C0. Equal exponents avoid any unregistered ordering of q0,q1.

For the real large-field lifetime hierarchy, one sufficient existing choice
in dimension d is

 p0=(d+9)r+8,   p1=p0-r-2.                           (1)

It gives

 p0>=5r,
 p0-(d+2)r=7r+8>0,
 2p1-(d+5)r-p0=2r+4>0,
 p0-p1-1=r+1.                                       (2)

After fixing these real cut exponents, choose an integer

 q>p_cut+4r+1,                                      (3)

where p_cut is the largest actual real-noise exponent in the source column
under consideration. For the full ordinary cutoff p_cut=p0, the explicit
choice q=p0+4r+2 suffices. No replacement of the smaller preparatory cutoff
p1 by p0 is made in the native estimates.

The homogeneous/native near-field requirements C_geo M alpha0<alpha1/2
and absolute smallness of M alpha1,C M^2 alpha0 are met by first choosing
C1/C0 and then the weak floor. Every fixed finite q has g ell^q->0.
The relative current/field ratios and the fixed fresh pre-R collar>=3
are included in these constants, not obtained by enlarging historical
rows after the function has been constructed.

2. THE REFERENCE DENOMINATOR MUST BE CANCELED BEFORE ESTIMATING

Large-II358(1.10) writes the inverse ORIGINAL reference denominator as

 D_x(U)^-1=exp[+x A(zeta0,U0)+I(U0)-E_k(Lambda)],     (4)

with |I(U0)|<=C log M |Lambda|<=C M^5. The preceding paragraph extends
the integral to the native complex U domain and states that replacing
epsilon_k by C(alpha0+alpha1) requires only a stronger weak-coupling
restriction. Its compact-gauge quadratic gap and axial coercivity are
kept, not reconstructed from a presumed scalar normalization.

The last sentence of printed358 expressly says that the first term in(4)
has to cancel. The actual Wilson transformations(1.17)--(1.48) perform this
cancellation against the numerator before an absolute estimate is taken.
Thus the reference computation does NOT leave a positive cost x alpha^2
which would grow as ell^(2q) and have to be paid by ell^p0. Bounding numerator
and denominator separately at this step would create a false restriction.

All genuine vacuum, gauge-volume and I(U0) scalar terms in(4) remain in
the exact ledger. The cancellation is of the matching Wilson function,
not a declaration that the denominator or its scalar equals one.

For a NEW source with Gamma=x+deltaGamma and the ORIGINAL reference
denominator frozen, there is instead the exact residual

             deltaGamma [S_new-S_old].                (5)

On real old private variables, the S_old part is paid by a once-reserved
fraction C|deltaGamma|/x of the native coercive action. On the complex
regular new background, the other part is at most
C|deltaGamma| alpha^2 per normalized current cell. For bounded absolute
|deltaGamma|<=r_G and any fixed q this tends to zero like
r_G x^-1 ell^(2q). On a component of volume C M^d R_sp^d(d'+1), its ratio
to the native chronological cost M^d R_sp^(d+1)(d'+1) is at most
C r_G x^-1 ell^(2q)/R_sp. The exact reconstruction/outer read remains
component-tagged. This observation is only its numerical compatibility;
the tagged source boundary constructor must still be proved.

3. THE ACTUAL LOWER AND UPPER N REQUIREMENTS

The potentially dangerous analytic Wilson term in Large-II368(1.47) is

       C A0 C1 B3^3 B5 M^10 p0(g) q1(g) R_sp^7 L^-N. (6)

The power is R_sp^7, verified in the printed image. The R_sp power in the
older parameter audit was understated; its conclusion is unchanged.
For fixed exponents, (6) is small if

 N log L>(p0+q+7r+1) log ell+C.                       (7)

Printed363 separately gives L^-N<=ell^-v with v>=2p0+d r. These are
logarithmic-logarithmic lower bounds on N; they impose no upper q.

Large-I198, after(1.94), requires

 N<=C ell^v,  with a positive integer v satisfying
                    v/2<=p0-p1-1.                   (8)

The first contribution in(1.94) is C N^(1/2) p1(g)/p0(g), and the second
is C L^-N N^(1/2). Equation(8) is exactly the printed reason they can both
be made small. With(1), the choice v=r is allowed with strict room.

More concretely, the later native lifetime construction at Large-II384
uses N=R_j. Its original R_j is within fixed factors of ell_j^r, and hence
this native choice satisfies(8). It also satisfies(7) for EVERY fixed q
at a sufficiently weak floor. Since beta0<=1, the intermediate factor
N^(1+beta0) in(1.47) is at most R_sp^2 when N=R_sp, exactly as used in its
last displayed bound. Thus no change to the native selection or preparatory
step count is needed to accommodate(3); only the floor may increase.

If an implementation fixes a different admissible N, the necessary explicit
condition is(7) together with(8), not the assertion that arbitrary fixed N
will work. The frozen-history argument must use its ACTUAL chosen N.

4. THE REMAINING ANALYTIC PERTURBATION HAS A POWER OF g

Large-II379, immediately after(1.72), bounds the first-order background/
current perturbation of the completed component quadratic form by

       C g R_sp^(d+2) p1(g)^2 q0(g).                  (9)

The resummed boundary expression V(X~) has the same bound, after the
connection created by the derivative is retained. For equal analytic
powers, (9) is at most C x^-1/2 ell^[(d+2)r+2p1+q], which tends to zero
for every fixed q. This controls the complex comparison by the real
component integral without deforming the real sharp cut.

The other radius appearances in the analytic minimum construction
(1.38)--(1.45) are small absolute alpha0+alpha1 perturbations of a fixed
compact-gauge inverse; (1.41) is linear in the relative field with fixed
native constants. The coupling-difference term after(1.37) is bounded by
a fixed polynomial in R_sp,M times epsilon^2 (or alpha^2 after analytic
extension), not x alpha^2. It is therefore also paid by the weak floor.

Once(9) is small, the real component estimate(1.79) and the fresh lifetime
reserve induction(1.80)--(1.88) involve the real p0,p1,R_sp hierarchy and
fixed chronological constants. They do not acquire a new ell^q cost from
the already estimated analytic perturbation. In particular, any fixed
enlargement of that chronological constant is paid by
R_sp^(d+2)=o(p0(g)), using(2). It must be replayed from primitive deposits,
not spent again on each occurrence of an exponentiated core factor.

The fine-M young column has the specific factor

 #young<=C(1+log ell).                               (10)

Equations(3) and(10) make theta_y #young->0. The R_sp^4 is an actual

the actual noise because g ell^p_cut/(g ell^q)->0. Those improvements are
compatible with (4)--(9), the native reference-minimum test ratios and
the strict absolute small-field requirements.

Parameter order is therefore: fix real cut exponents and native geometric/
carrier constants; fix the analytic power q and coefficient hierarchy,
all source/localizer margins and desired fixed cost-tilt coefficient;
then choose a common weak floor Y. Keep that Y and all these choices
fixed before the ultraviolet horizon, ambient period and source packing.
If the available history does not satisfy the resulting floor, it cannot
be reused by fiat: the admitted weak-window construction must be rerun.

No q-dependent obstruction remains in the explicit large-R primitive
estimates checked above. This narrow conclusion does not certify a new
