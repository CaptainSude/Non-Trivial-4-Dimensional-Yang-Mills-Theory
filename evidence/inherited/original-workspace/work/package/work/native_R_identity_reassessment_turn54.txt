TURN54 -- REASSESSMENT OF THE NATIVE R IDENTITY
20 October 2026. gauge47.

RESULT

The earlier normalization gate should be split into three assertions.
The exact weighted G0,T0 law is NOT logically necessary for a total
partition identity if the pre-averaged identity is already choicewise.
It is a separate issue for matching the chosen localized expansion
between covers. Likewise, preserving total mass is not the assertion
that a gauge-invariant observable can be left unchanged through R.

The inspected primary text gives two actual integrated identities:
the ordinary T pushforward and the terminal reverse-Faddeev--Popov
step on the first class of components in B122II. Both are established
below with their insertion scope. The inspected B119/B122II statements
do not explicitly give the remaining equality between the complete
R input and its pre-averaged output. B122II starts after that step,
by reference to Large-I(1.99)/(1.100).

The Wilson-only reference denominator is consistent with a normalized
auxiliary insertion, but the matching numerator and its full domain
are absent from the usable display of B122II(1.1). Thus this pass does
not certify that insertion choicewise. It also does not assert it is
false or absent from the published Large-I proof.

No prior frozen file is changed.

2. THREE DIFFERENT ASSERTIONS

For a fixed original bare density rho_0 and the actual full operations,
write Z(rho)=integral rho against all specified physical measures.

A. Total partition identity:

    Z(rho_n)=Z(rho_0),       rho_(j+1)=R_j T_j rho_j.       (2.1)

All original vacuum factors must be included in rho_0. For the convention
rho_0=exp[-g_0^(-2)A-E], equation (2.1) would give

    Z_Wilson = exp(E) Z(rho_n).                            (2.2)

It would not permit discarding E or replacing a finite-torus E by an
infinite-volume density without a separately proved comparison.

B. Inserted observable identity:

    integral F rho_0 = integral F_n rho_n.                 (2.3)

This requires the actual transported insertion F_n or an explicit
inserted-density construction. Gauge invariance alone does not say
F_n=F, especially when physical interior variables are resampled.
If (2.3) holds for a class including F=1, it implies A by substitution.
The converse fails: probability measures with different distributions
of an observable can have the same mass.

C. Cover-local weighted-choice law:

The complete labelled G0,T0/history choices over short injectively
lifted deciding data must have a bijection preserving weights and
their corresponding localized integrands. This is the precise version
used by the present common-cover catalogue strategy.

C alone does not prove A: multiplying both matched local kernels by
the same factor two preserves their cover matching and changes mass.
A and even equality of every physical inserted expectation do not
determine C: auxiliary labels can be redistributed without affecting
their integrated physical kernel. Conversely, if one directly proves
equality of the fully averaged local kernel, a labelled bijection is
only one sufficient method, not a necessary physical axiom.

Thus the large-field weighting gate must not be bundled into A unless
the only available proof of A actually uses those weights.

3. THE ACTUAL ORDINARY T IDENTITY IS COMPLETE

Let M(U) denote the source's averaging operation. With the normalized
product Haar measure and group delta functions, B119(0.1) reads

    (T rho)(V)=integral dU delta(M(U)V^(-1)) rho(U).

Integrating first in V gives exactly

    integral dV (T rho)(V)=integral dU rho(U),               (3.1)

since the delta kernel has integral one for every U. This is valid
for every integrable rho for which this actual kernel is defined,
without a weak-field restriction or a small-error replacement.

For every bounded measurable coarse observable f,

    integral dV f(V)(T rho)(V)
      = integral dU f(M(U)) rho(U).                        (3.2)

Gauge covariance of M maps gauge-invariant f to gauge-invariant
f composed with M. General fine observables require insertion before
integration: the pushforward numerator is T(F rho), not in general
F times T rho. Whenever desired, its conditional observable is the
Radon--Nikodym ratio T(F rho)/(T rho) on the latter measure's support.

The exact partitions of unity, legitimate changes of variables and
convergent expansions used to evaluate this T integral do not alter
(3.1). Every scalar Jacobian and vacuum extraction must remain in the
representation. This observation supplies T's part of (2.1), not R's.

4. THE TERMINAL GAUGE EQUIVALENCE HAS A REAL INSERTION EXTENSION

B122II p.378 separates components into two classes. On its first class
the remaining cuts and effective action are invariant under the
applicable gauge transformations. The source then removes their gauge
delta functions by reversed Faddeev--Popov integration, provided the
corresponding physical variables are also integrated.

Here is the exact extension of this source step. Let H_X be its gauge
group, with the prescribed roots/boundary values fixed, and let
Delta_X(U) be the normalized gauge-fixing factor used in that step.
Its Faddeev--Popov identity is

    integral_(H_X) Delta_X(U^h) dh = 1.

Let q(U) be the complete remaining density, including these retained
cuts, and F(U) a bounded insertion invariant under H_X. Haar change
of variables, followed by the identity above, gives

    integral dU F(U) q(U) Delta_X(U)
      = integral dU F(U) q(U).                             (4.1)

The same proof works with fixed external data whenever these are fixed
by H_X and the source step is admitted for those data. Full physical
gauge invariance implies invariance under this smaller group. If F
transforms nontrivially, its group average or its explicitly transported
version remains; it cannot be deleted from the calculation.

This proves more than a source-free statement for this particular
operation, and it does not need a weighted enumeration of possible
trees once the actual normalized gauge identity is fixed. It does NOT
prove that every earlier R operation is only a gauge transformation.
Nor does it permit removing the gauge factors on the source's second,
retained large-field class.

The text says this is the same integrated sense used for Large-I(1.99).
That identifies the meaning of the word equivalence there. It does
not reproduce the two sides of the earlier identity or certify every
normalization preceding it.

5. TEST OF THE NORMALIZED AUXILIARY-KERNEL ANGLE

There is genuine supporting evidence for this angle. The denominator
analyzed in B122II(1.2) is a reference Wilson integral with its own
cutoff and gauge-fixing factors. Its subsequent evaluation retains
Gaussian, Haar and gauge-coordinate contributions. It is not described
as an arbitrary normalization of the incoming quantum activity.

For this to supply the requested choicewise attachment, one must
identify the numerator factor as the SAME reference integrand over
the SAME variables/domain, with the denominator evaluated at precisely
the same retained exterior data. One must also determine whether its
insertion is made before any change of physical variables, and retain
the Jacobian of that change.

The following actual source identifications are still missing:

  -- which numerator variables correspond to the variables integrated
     in reference denominator (1.2);
  -- equality of their gauge factors, cut domains and fixed boundary
     data at the insertion stage;
  -- the place of the complete old density and any source inside the
     subsequent change of variables;
  -- any omitted scalar or determinant factor before arriving at
     Large-I(1.99), and positivity/nonvanishing of the used denominator.

These are not demands to reprove every estimate of Large-I. A readable
choicewise normalized insertion identity, or an explicit published
integrated identity with its input and output identified, would suffice
for A. One does not need to reconstruct the full convergent expansion
again in order to use such an identity.

The denominator alone is insufficient. Two numerators can use the same
positive reference denominator and differ by a scalar factor or by an
extra cutoff. Only one, or neither, need integrate to one. The missing
matching is therefore substantive even though the expected insertion
mechanism is elementary.

If the choicewise identity holds for arbitrary incoming integrable
densities at fixed reference data, it also holds with F rho or
exp(zeta F)rho in place of rho. The transported insertion then follows
the numerator through the actual operation. This would close the
source-preservation statement without demanding that F be unchanged
as a function of the newly introduced variables.

If every choice has that identity, any normalized choice law preserves
total mass, even a law depending on the ambient geometry. If the law
depends on an integrated input variable, the normalization must hold
pointwise in that variable before it is integrated. This is the
important simplification relative to the combined TURN52 gate.
The precise cover-local law C would still be a separate question.

6. WHAT THE PUBLISHED THEOREM CURRENTLY LICENSES

The accepted B122II Theorem 1 licenses its stated density class and
bounds for the actual construction under its coupling premise.
B119 Corollary 3 and the completion at B122II p.391 license the
corresponding stability conclusion. They should not be demoted merely
because our project has not reproduced every intermediate estimate.

However, the displayed theorem statement does not explicitly give
equation (2.1) or its inserted version. Calling rho_k an effective
density and calling the conclusion ultraviolet stability do not,
as mathematical statements, identify the missing input/output
normalization. Uniform bounds alone cannot determine that equality.

In particular, the identities and equivalences visible inside Part II
begin with its post-basic-step expression (1.1). Following them through
(1.49), (1.70), the terminal gauge equivalence, and exponentiation gives
the final representation of that expression. This proves an exact
chain AFTER the missing endpoint has been supplied; it does not
identify that endpoint with the incoming original density.

The optional normalization (1.103) does not bridge this endpoint.
Writing T_Y=m_Y times a mass-one operation retains m_Y explicitly.
It is an algebraic refactoring of the existing output, not a proof
that the entire R operation had mass one relative to its input.

This distinction is a limit of the statements inspected here, not
an accusation of a defect in Bałaban's construction. An exact identity
from Large-I could settle A immediately at theorem-level use.
