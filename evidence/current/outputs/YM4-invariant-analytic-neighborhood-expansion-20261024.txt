3. DIMENSION-FREE REAL-TO-COMPLEX TRANSFER

Let h be holomorphic on a neighborhood of the upper half-disc
D={t:|t|<2, Im t>0}, bounded there by M. Suppose |h(t)|<=epsilon
on its real diameter [-2,2], with 0<epsilon<=M. The harmonic measure
of that diameter at i is

 omega=1-(4/pi) arctan(1/2)>0.                          (7)

Indeed (2+t)/(2-t) sends D to the first quadrant, the diameter to
its positive real side and the semicircle to its positive imaginary
side. Their harmonic measures are 1-(2/pi)arg((2+t)/(2-t)) and its
complement. Apply the maximum principle to subharmonic log|h|, using
standard limiting arguments for zeros and the two corner points:

                 |h(i)|<=M^(1-omega) epsilon^omega.     (8)

For a target complex field beta=a+i v in B_n, both a and v have REAL
components. Hold v fixed and set h(t)=a_n(a+t v). Require
||v||_infinity<=rho_n/4, and that a has at least rho_n/2 spare margin
inside the real box where (5) holds. For |t|<=2 the real part remains
admitted and the imaginary part stays in the sparse complex tube.
Thus h is holomorphic on a neighborhood of the half-disc, (6) gives
M=18 eta, and (5) applies on its ENTIRE real diameter. If its right
side exceeds M, use the already available real bound M instead.
The construction uses one complex variable t regardless of |B_n|.
For v=0 the real estimate suffices directly.

Consequently, on this smaller coarse-parameter tube,

 |a_n|<=18 eta min{1, [C_1(1+n)^d exp(-mu n)/18]^omega}

with the trivial cap 18 eta retained when useful. After spending a
fixed fraction of the exponential, the polynomial shell factor is

4. CONSEQUENCES AND EXACT LIMITS

Equation (9) is an analytic local-carrier decomposition in the actual
physical coarse coordinates, with coefficients indexed by expanding
source neighborhoods. At fixed derivative order k, Cauchy on a further
smaller tube gives a price rho_n^-k. If the registered margins and
rho_n^-1 grow only polynomially in n and inverse g, this adds only
a polynomial carrier/birth price; the exponential spatial sum survives.
Any nonpolynomial margin loss must instead be retained and checked.

This establishes finite-volume coefficient estimates uniform in the
ambient volume, wherever the inputs (1),(2) have that uniform scope.
It does not by itself prove convergence of coefficients as the torus
grows; that requires a boundary-condition comparison. Nor does the
decreasing sequence rho_n imply a positive common tube for the entire
infinite sum. The finite identities and summable real bounds remain
valid without making such a global holomorphy claim.

Finally the operation pi_n is generally not equivariant under local
gauge transformations. Individual a_n therefore do NOT inherit a
gauge Ward identity merely because a separate invariant version of
A exists. Deterministic tree canonicalization has an exact finite
gauge law, but applying different gauges to different coefficients
requires a matched reconstruction/admission argument to preserve
telescoping. An independent native background/current-pair extension
is also not supplied by beta-holomorphy. These are the remaining
attachment requirements; they cannot be replaced by (9).
