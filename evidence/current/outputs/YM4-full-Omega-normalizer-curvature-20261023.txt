RESULT AND LIMIT

The negative curvature of the exact full-Omega normalizer can be bounded
without a second boundary-covariance formula and without smoothing away
its intersections. The proof uses the exact SU(2) constraints, an O(h^2)
enlargement of those SAME intersections solely as a comparison, the
registered normalized radial pressure, and the finite-dimensional
Prekopa--Leindler integral inequality. It gives a distributional lower
Hessian bound for -log Z, not a C2 assertion.

For the literal first near-flat family, the native minimum's analytic
first response supplies the second-center jet by one Cauchy derivative
with an explicit spare coarse radius a_min. The complete bare action
supplies the bulk Schur curvature with its full coefficient dependence.
The resulting bound is polynomial in the actual noise radius, plus the
displayed cutoff-tail term. This is not yet the weighted coercivity or
law match needed to replace the previous two-step success-only action.
The true outer stationary action, determinant/scalar prefactors and
other Omega records are all still present.

1. EXACT FINITE OBJECTS AND UNIFORM RESERVES

physical coarse-angle chart. Coupling g=g0 and old threshold rho are

For each bond b and actual old center label D define

 v_bD(t)=-i log[U_(1,D)(beta0+t w) U1(beta0+t w)^(-1)],

 Z(t)=integral_(K(t)) exp[-H(t,A)] dA,       F(t)=-log Z(t).    (1)

All sets in (1) are the real principal-log intersections registered in
first_full_omega_mixture_domain. In particular current failure shells
remain. No individual ordinary-branch normalizer is substituted.

Fix the EXACT zero-noise extraction H0(0;beta)=0 in B119(1.15):

The comparison enlargement is taken so small that the strict outer

2. ALMOST JOINT CONVEXITY OF THE ACTUAL CONSTRAINTS

All derivatives below are along the same straight physical beta path.
On the containing coordinate ball |a|<=R, the explicit SU(2) formula
gives constants c,C>0, independent of g,r and the number of bonds, with

 phi_aa >= c g^2 I,

Indeed -F_xx>=c I in the small common chart as proved in the domain
note. The mixed derivative F_xv is bounded. Also F_vv is bounded and
|F_v|<=|x|+|v|<=C rho on this containing ball, since gR is a fixed
multiple of rho. Applying the chain rule proves the last two bounds.
These are bounds for the exact group-ball condition, not its quadratic
approximation.

For a small interval around a chosen midpoint set

 M_b=C_phi sup_t max_D[|v_bD'(t)|^2+rho |v_bD''(t)|],            (4)

where C_phi is chosen large enough in terms of the constants in (3).
Young's inequality, or the scalar Schur complement using phi_aa,
shows that phi_bD(t,a)+(M_b/2)t^2 is jointly convex in (t,a).
This assertion is about EACH original constraint. It does not require
a differentiable active-center selector for their intersection.

Take a_minus in K_b(-h), a_plus in K_b(h), and a_mid=(a_minus+a_plus)/2.
Joint convexity gives

                   phi_bD(0,a_mid)<=M_b h^2/2 for every D.      (5)

Define rho_b^+(h) by

               cos rho_b^+=cos rho-M_b h^2/2,                  (6)

and let K_b^+ be the exact intersection at t=0 with these SAME centers
v_bD(0), but common radius rho_b^+ for that bond. For sufficiently
small h it is within the fixed spare outer margin and is convex.
Equation (5) proves the exact Minkowski inclusion

       (K_b(-h)+K_b(h))/2 subset K_b^+,
       (K(-h)+K(h))/2 subset K^+=product_b K_b^+.               (7)

No sign is assigned to an unproved second surface derivative. The
min-of-centers corners remain in both K(0) and K^+.

3. EXACT COST OF THE O(h^2) COMPARISON ENLARGEMENT

Interpolate only the comparison radii at the MIDPOINT background:

 cos rho_b(s)=cos rho-s M_b h^2/2, 0<=s<=1.

Hold H(0,A) fixed throughout. The exact positive radial boundary of
one center has derivative

 partial_rho r_v(n)=sin rho/sqrt(h_v(n)^2-cos^2 rho)<=C_theta,

using the reserve |v|<=theta rho. Therefore the actual independent-A
radial boundary speed of the intersection obeys, a.e. in s,n,

        0<=partial_s q_b(s,n)<=C_theta M_b h^2/(g rho).          (8)

The minimum of the one-center boundaries has this same Lipschitz bound
at switching centers. Integrating the unnormalized radial Leibniz
identity and dividing by the actual current normalizer gives

 log[Z^+(0)/Z(0)]
  =integral_0^1 sum_b integral_(radial boundary) density_s
                                                partial_s q_b ds
  <= C R epsilon_r h^2/(g rho) sum_b M_b.                      (9)

The density_s is normalized by the full Z_s, including every other
moving factor. Its radial pressure is (2) because ALL these products
stay between B_r and B_R. Thus (9) neither divides by the probability
of all original cuts nor assumes conditional tails at fixed exterior
coordinates. The enlarged Z^+ is solely a comparison quantity. The
target throughout remains the exact Z in (1).

4. COMPLETE SMOOTH-ACTION CURVATURE AND PREKOPA--LEINDLER

Require a uniform scalar A_H>=0 along the path and containing product
such that the complete Hamiltonian satisfies

 H_AA>=c0 I,
 H_tt-H_tA(H_AA)^(-1)H_At>=-A_H.                              (10)

A convenient sufficient choice is

 H_tt>=-B_H,  ||H_At||_2<=C_cross,
                    A_H=B_H+C_cross^2/c0.                     (11)

These are pointwise bounds for the COMPLETE physical H, including its
coefficient/minimum response. They are not just an estimate of the
moving cutoff. The function H(t,A)+(A_H/2)t^2 is jointly convex, so

 H(0,(A_minus+A_plus)/2)
       <=[H(-h,A_minus)+H(h,A_plus)]/2+A_H h^2/2.              (12)

Use the finite-dimensional integral inequality: if nonnegative
functions f_minus,f_plus,k obey k((x+y)/2)>=sqrt(f_minus(x)f_plus(y)),
then integral k>=sqrt(integral f_minus integral f_plus). This is
Prekopa--Leindler, used here only at the midpoint. Apply it to the
two exact endpoint densities and

              k(A)=exp(A_H h^2/2) 1_(K^+)(A) exp[-H(0,A)].

The pointwise premise follows from (7),(12). Hence

 Z^+(0)>=exp(-A_H h^2/2) sqrt[Z(-h)Z(h)].                      (13)

Combining (9),(13), with the signs displayed explicitly, gives

 F(0)<=[F(-h)+F(h)]/2
           +[A_H/2+C R epsilon_r/(g rho) sum_b M_b]h^2.        (14)

Equivalently,

 F(-h)+F(h)-2F(0)
       >=-[A_H+C R epsilon_r/(g rho) sum_b M_b]h^2,            (15)

where C in (15) includes the harmless factor two. This is the desired
one-sided curvature inequality. With locally uniform constants and the
already established continuity of Z, midpoint semiconvexity implies
the corresponding distributional lower second-derivative bound.
Thus the nonsmooth part of the directional second derivative cannot
have an uncontrolled negative singular measure. Smoothness is not
claimed or required.

Primary external reference checked: A. Prekopa, "On logarithmic
concave measures and functions," Acta Sci. Math. 34 (1973), 335--343,
Theorem3, integral inequality (2.3). Public primary copy:
https://rutcor.rutgers.edu/Prekopa/pdf/SCIENT2.pdf
Only that integral inequality is imported; (3)--(15) are the present
domain/pressure argument.

5. NATIVE SECOND CENTER JET: WHY FIRST RESPONSE ALONE IS NOT ENOUGH

The needed quantity is the QUADRATIC tangent census sum_b M_b. A first
derivative bound for v, or smallness of v itself, does not bound it.
Here is a native way to obtain the missing second derivative at the
literal first two-grade scope.

Keep a common spare coarse complex-coordinate radius a_min>0 explicitly.
Assume the physical minimum and the actual local comparison minimum
are analytic on a polydisc with this spare radius and that their first
response kernels retain the uniform complex bound there. CMP102
Proposition9/(190) provides that combination on its admitted tube.
Composing the fixed-L copying/ordinary-return maps of B119(1.2),(1.3)
and the common one-step axial frames gives, for the actual center,

 ||D_q v_bD||<=C exp[-mu d1(b,q)]                              (16)

uniformly on that SPARE COMPLEX polydisc, not only its real slice.
All group inverses/logarithms use their analytic small branches. Both
minima remain in the center; neither is frozen.

One coordinate-block Cauchy derivative in q' gives

 ||D_q D_q' v_bD||<=C a_min^-1 exp[-mu d1(b,q)].

Interchanging q and q' gives the second bound. Since the path uses
ordinary fixed coarse coordinates, mixed derivatives commute. Taking
the smaller estimate yields

 ||D_q D_q' v_bD||
  <=C a_min^-1 exp[-mu max(d1(b,q),d1(b,q'))]
  <=C a_min^-1 exp[-(mu/2)(d1(b,q)+d1(b,q'))].                 (17)

This min-of-two step is crucial. A single Cauchy bound with an arbitrary
global direction would instead give an unhelpful l1 times linfinity
price. Let k_bq=exp[-(mu/2)d1(b,q)]. For a straight tangent w,

 max_D |v_bD'[w]|^2 <=C (sum_q k_bq |w_q|)^2,
 max_D |v_bD''[w,w]|<=C a_min^-1 (sum_q k_bq |w_q|)^2.

The first-lattice Schur/Young bound therefore proves

             sum_b M_b(w)<=C(1+rho/a_min)||w||_2^2.           (18)

Constants include the fixed-L/color/return conventions. The number of
centers per b is uniformly bounded, but (17),(18) need only their
uniform supremum. There is no growing comparison-cube volume factor.

8. WHAT (21) DOES NOT SAY ABOUT THE NEXT LAW

The exact outer full-Omega density is still

 J_Omega(beta) exp[-g^-2 Action(U1(beta))] Z_Omega(beta).

Its negative log contains g^-2 Action(U1), -log J_Omega and the
normalizer term studied here. Outer scalar/determinant terms did not
cancel from this density merely because they cancel in an inner ratio.
Their signs, curvature and pullback under the next current constraint
map must remain in any next conditional action.

The result supplies a lower DISTRIBUTIONAL curvature bound in straight
coarse angle coordinates. It gives neither C2 smoothness nor the full
weighted accretivity/row bounds of the next ordinary action. A nonlinear
next-noise substitution also has its own second-chain-rule term. Other
Omega sectors, old histories and source genealogy remain outside this
first-family statement. Thus the previous two-step law cannot yet be
replaced just by citing (21).

In particular, favorable positive singular curvature at an active-mask
switch can be a NONDIAGONAL rank-one form in coarse coordinates. A
spatial scalar-block weight W need not commute with that form. Positivity
in the ordinary real quadratic form does NOT imply positivity of the
real part after W conjugation. Therefore even an eventual unweighted
next-law coercivity statement would not by itself justify the weighted
spatial covariance consumer. One needs a separate weighted treatment
of the nonsmooth part, or another spatial comparison mechanism. No such
claim is included in (21).

The substantive finite advance is that the genuine intersection corners
are no longer an obstruction to one-sided normalizer curvature: their
cost is rigorously handled by (5)--(15) with the existing normalized
tail pressure and without a boundary-trace covariance assumption.
