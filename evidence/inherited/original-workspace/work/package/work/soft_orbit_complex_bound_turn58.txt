FINITE-GRAPH COMPLEX BOUND FOR A SOFT SU(2) ORBIT PROFILE -- TURN58
20 October 2026

Status: frozen after focused independent audit PASS in
soft_orbit_complex_audit_turn58.txt; root also checked the constants.
The finite-dimensional estimate below is proved directly. It supplies
a soft orbit factor, not a full native R replacement or a control of
the full native action outside its admitted chart.

1. GRAPH, TWISTED ACTION AND NORMALIZATION

Let (V,E) be a finite graph with one independently oriented copy of
each edge. Let B be its anchored vertices and g_b=I for b in B.
Every connected component must meet B. Put m=|V\B|.
Choose a rooted spanning forest F with exactly one anchor per forest
component, so |F|=m. All anchored vertices, including any fixed
exterior-incident vertices, are roots, possibly singleton roots.

Integrate the remaining g_v in SU(2) with normalized Haar measure.
Use the Hilbert--Schmidt norm |.| and the operator norm ||.||op.
For U in SL(2,C), set

                         q(U)=1-Tr(U)/2.

For U in SU(2), q(U) is real, nonnegative, at most 2, and

                         |U-I|^2=4q(U).                 (1.1)

Fix arbitrary real background links t0_e in SU(2), and let

 t_e=exp(T_e)t0_e,       T_e in sl(2,C),
 Q_(A,t)(g)=sum_(e=(u,v)) q(g_u exp(A_e)t_e g_v^(-1)t_e^(-1)).
                                                        (1.2)

The A_e are complex traceless matrices. For the quantitative
estimate require |A_e|<=r_A, with r_A a fixed finite number.
The physical chart uses r_A<=1. Write

 ||A||_2^2=sum_e |A_e|^2,      tau=max_e ||T_e||op,
 Q0=Q_(0,t0).

The placement in (1.2) is the correct twisted relative action:
if the physical link is U_e=exp(A_e)t_e, ordinary gauge
transformation of U followed by right division by t_e gives
g_u exp(A_e)t_e g_v^(-1)t_e^(-1).

For real lambda>=1 define

 w_lambda = int_SU(2) exp[-lambda q(g)] dg,
 Psi_lambda(A,t)=int dg exp[-lambda Q_(A,t)(g)],
 phi_lambda(A,t)=Psi_lambda(A,t)/w_lambda^m.              (1.3)

The denominator depends on lambda and m only. It is not
Psi_lambda(0,t), and is not adjusted as A or t changes.
In a reference quotient using the same profile, this
parameter-independent factor cancels exactly.

2. ANCHORED FOREST ESTIMATES

Let d_v be the number of forest edges from v to its root,
with d_b=0 at anchors, and define

 S_F=sum_(e=(u,v) in E)(d_u+d_v),
 C_F=max(1,2S_F).                                       (2.1)

Thus C_F<=max(1,4|E|D_max), where D_max=max_v d_v.
No sharp constant is needed. These constants are independent
of t0 and lambda.

Put a_v=|g_v-I| and

 b_e=|g_u-t0_e g_v t0_e^(-1)|.

Unitary invariance gives b_e^2=4q(g_u t0_e g_v^(-1)t0_e^(-1)).
Along a forest path, conjugation by its real background links
preserves norms and the root value is I. Hence

 a_v <= sum_(e in path_v) b_e,
 a_v^2 <= 4 d_v Q_F,
 Q_F=sum_(e in F) q(g_u t0_e g_v^(-1)t0_e^(-1)).          (2.2)

For every graph edge, b_e<=a_u+a_v, so

                  Q_F <= Q0 <= 2S_F Q_F <= C_F Q_F.      (2.3)

Also, for either selected endpoint of each oriented edge,

                  sum_e a_v^2 <= 2 C_F Q_F.              (2.4)

If m=0 then Q0=Q_F=0, and these statements still hold.
Anchoring is essential: an unanchored constant gauge mode
would invalidate the later w_lambda^m estimate.

For each forest edge orient the proof from parent to child,
and use its relative variable
h_e=g_parent t0_e g_child^(-1)t0_e^(-1).
Solving recursively gives
g_child=t0_e^(-1) h_e^(-1) g_parent t0_e.
Inversion, left/right multiplication, and unitary conjugation
preserve Haar measure. Thus the triangular change of variables
g_(V\B) <-> h_F preserves product Haar measure. Reversing a
forest orientation does not change q, by conjugation and
q(h^(-1))=q(h).

Consequently, for every s>0,

              int dg exp[-s Q_F]=w_s^m.                 (2.5)

Combining (2.3) and (2.5) gives the exact real bounds

        w_(C_F lambda)^m <= Psi_lambda(0,t0)
                                      <= w_lambda^m.    (2.6)

3. COMPLEX BACKGROUND PERTURBATION HAS NO ADDITIVE VOLUME COST

For one edge put B_e=t0_e g_v^(-1)t0_e^(-1), which is unitary,
and B_(e,T)=exp(T_e) B_e exp(-T_e). For tau<=1,

 |B_(e,T)-B_e| <= 2 e^2 tau |B_e-I| = C0 tau a_v,
                         C0=2e^2,                      (3.1)

by integrating exp(sT)[T,B_e-I]exp(-sT) from s=0 to1.
Trace is unchanged by this conjugation. Therefore the
linear-looking perturbation has a second vanishing factor:

 Tr[g_u(B_(e,T)-B_e)]
                 =Tr[(g_u-I)(B_(e,T)-B_e)].              (3.2)

Using |Tr XY|<=|X||Y|, (2.2), and (2.1), one obtains

 |Q_(0,t)-Q0|
   <= (C0 tau/2) sum_e a_u a_v
   <= (C0 tau/4) sum_e(a_u^2+a_v^2)
   <= (C0/2) C_F tau Q0.                               (3.3)

There is no term lambda tau |E| in the exponent. The
error is relative to the real anchored energy Q0.
At g_v=I for every vertex, both sides vanish exactly.

4. THE FIELD PERTURBATION AND A GRAPH-INDEPENDENT UPPER CONSTANT

Fix 0<delta<1 and assume

              tau <= tau_delta
                    :=min(1,delta/(2 C0 C_F)).           (4.1)

The allowed complex-background radius is graph dependent.
By (3.3) the background error is at most (delta/4)Q0.

For the A-linear term use cyclicity of trace and set
Y_(e,T)=B_(e,T)g_u. The real Y_(e,0) is unitary and
|Y_(e,0)-I|^2=4q(g_u B_e). From (3.1),(2.4),

 sum_e |Y_(e,T)-I|^2
   <= 8Q0+4 C0^2 tau^2 C_F Q0
   <= 9 Q0.                                           (4.2)

The last inequality follows from (4.1), C_F>=1 and delta<1.
This is the useful refinement: once the graph-dependent
T radius is chosen, the coefficient in (4.2) is universal.

Expand exp(A)=I+A+R(A). Tr A=0 implies

 |sum_e Tr(A_e Y_(e,T))/2|
   =|sum_e Tr(A_e(Y_(e,T)-I))/2|
   <= (3/2)||A||_2 sqrt(Q0).                            (4.3)

For |A_e|<=r_A,
|R(A_e)|<=(e^(r_A)/2)|A_e|^2.
Also |Y_(e,T)|<=sqrt(2)e^2. Thus

 |Q_(A,t)-Q_(0,t)|
   <= (3/2)||A||_2 sqrt(Q0)+C_R ||A||_2^2,
             C_R=sqrt(2)e^(r_A+2)/4.                   (4.4)

Young's inequality with epsilon=delta/2 gives

 (3/2)||A||_2 sqrt(Q0)
           <= (delta/2)Q0 + 9/(8delta)||A||_2^2.

Combining this with (3.3) yields the slightly weakened,
convenient form

 Re Q_(A,t) >= (1-delta)Q0-C_delta ||A||_2^2,
              C_delta=C_R+9/(8delta).                  (4.5)

C_delta is independent of graph size, forest depth, t0,
and lambda. The graph dependence is in the admitted
background radius (4.1). A less sharp argument could
retain a graph-dependent constant in (4.5), but it is
unnecessary here.

5. ELEMENTARY HAAR-INTEGRAL RATIOS

An SU(2) element has eigenvalues exp(+-i theta).
Normalized Haar integration of a class function gives

 w_s=(2/pi) int_0^pi sin^2(theta)
                          exp[-s(1-cos theta)] dtheta.  (5.1)

There are absolute 0<c<=C<infinity such that

             c s^(-3/2)<=w_s<=C s^(-3/2),   s>=1.       (5.2)

Indeed, for the lower bound restrict theta to
[0,s^(-1/2)], use sin theta>=2theta/pi and
1-cos theta<=theta^2/2. One may take
c=8 exp(-1/2)/(3 pi^3).
For the upper bound use sin theta<=theta and
1-cos theta>=2theta^2/pi^2, then extend the Gaussian
integral to infinity. One may take C=pi^(5/2)/2^(5/2).
The latter upper bound is valid for every s>0.

It follows, for lambda>=1, that

 w_((1-delta)lambda)/w_lambda
                   <= (C/c)(1-delta)^(-3/2)=K_delta,    (5.3)

and from (2.6)

   c_F^m <= phi_lambda(0,t0)<=1,
                   c_F=(c/C) C_F^(-3/2)>0.              (5.4)

The lower bound is for REAL t0 and A=0. It is not
a lower bound on the modulus for arbitrary complex A,t.

6. NORMALIZED COMPLEX PROFILE

Taking the modulus inside the compact Haar integral,
then applying (4.5),(2.5) and (5.3), proves

 |phi_lambda(A,t)|
   <= [w_((1-delta)lambda)/w_lambda]^m
                       exp[C_delta lambda ||A||_2^2]
   <= K_delta^m exp[C_delta lambda ||A||_2^2].           (6.1)

This holds for |A_e|<=r_A and (4.1), uniformly for every
real background t0_e in SU(2) and every lambda>=1.
Its upper-bound constants are independent of the finite
graph; the complex t neighborhood shrinks according to
its explicitly stated C_F.

The function Psi, and hence phi, is jointly holomorphic
in all complex entries A_e,T_e restricted to sl(2,C).
In fact it is entire in these finite collections of
coordinates: matrix exponentials are entire, inverses
are exp(-T), and compact Haar integration is uniformly
dominated on every compact parameter set. No complex
gauge integration or analytic continuation of a sharp
cutoff is involved.

For real A in su(2) and real t, the profile is strictly
positive. Holomorphy and (6.1) alone do NOT give a
zero-free complex neighborhood of a uniform radius
for log phi. No logarithm of phi is used in this proof.
Nor is phi(0,t) reset to one by dividing by its own
possibly complex background-dependent value.

7. PHYSICAL-SCALE CONSEQUENCE AND ITS LIMITS

Let g>0, let W>0 be the fluctuation width, and put

              lambda=(g W)^(-2)>=1.

Suppose each |A_e|<=C_A g h(g), where h(g)<=W and
C_A g h(g)<=r_A. For example h may be a fixed power
of log(1/g), with W at least that large. Then

                  lambda ||A||_2^2<=C_A^2 |E|.

Since m=|F|<=|E|, (6.1) gives

             |phi_lambda(A,t)|
                <= exp[(log K_delta+C_delta C_A^2)|E|]. (7.1)

Thus the SOFT PROFILE costs at most a fixed exponential
per edge on this complex A chart. There is no negative
power of g in its exponent. The admissible complex
background radius remains delta/(2 C0 C_F).

If the finite graph itself grows, its real lower
bound (5.4) has the explicit graph-dependent cost
m log(1/c_F). This is not silently replaced by a
graph-uniform lower pressure estimate. Likewise,
using a complex T radius independent of graph size
would require a stronger anchored estimate than
the one proved here.

This result keeps the normalized soft orbit factor
as part of the actual reference profile, with all
of its physical field dependence retained. It does
not justify discarding a generated quadratic or any
other allowed low-order term. At a nonflat background
no vanishing linear jet is asserted.

Still required for a full replacement:
an actual normalized positive basic operation with
this profile and its correct old/new chronology;
the native action/background on the entire proposed
real integration region; full locality and typed
source estimates; scalar/low-order bookkeeping and
the corresponding original-law identity. None is
inferred from the finite-graph estimate alone.
