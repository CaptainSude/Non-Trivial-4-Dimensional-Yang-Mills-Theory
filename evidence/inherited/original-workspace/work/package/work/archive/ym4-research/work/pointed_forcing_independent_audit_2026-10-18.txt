2. WHY THE EXTENSION CHOICE MATTERS

A direct pullback of a general original-tube activity need not be irrelevant
even when its Maxwell coefficient vanishes. For example, use a local
gauge-invariant squared difference between neighboring plaquette curvatures,
with parallel transport in the difference. Its quadratic symbol starts at
fourth momentum order and its Maxwell coefficient is zero. A target tube
permits a Cartan-valued potential with amplitude c a_k eta_k alternating
between neighboring fine sites: the potential C1 norm and curvature are
O(a_k), and independent J may be set to zero. The common-radius inclusion
places this pair in the target tube for fixed small c.

After dilation, neighboring old curvatures differ by O(h^2 a_k). Normalize
the old activity by E0/a_i^2. Its value is then of order
E0 h^4(a_k/a_i)^2, rather than h^(5-beta). This example refutes the direct
extension, not the reconstructed source extension. Reconstruction suppresses
the fine oscillation and supplies the regularity used in the source Ward
and spatial-replacement calculation.

Thus the old-V neutral direct-transport proof and the homogeneous pointed
irrelevant proof use DIFFERENT, explicitly specified analytic extensions.
Their values agree with their respective original expressions on the
physical minimizing graph. One must not silently exchange their bounds.
