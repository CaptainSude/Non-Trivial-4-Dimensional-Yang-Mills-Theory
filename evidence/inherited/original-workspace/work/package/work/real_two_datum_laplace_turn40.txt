REAL TWO-DATUM LAPLACE EXPANSION AND THE ACTUAL COARSE HESSIAN
19 October 2026. admission_proof. Turn40 finite-lattice theorem.

RESULT

The positive saturated numerator of constrained_effective_action_turn40
has a real C2 Laplace expansion in any two specified coarse FOREST CHORD
coordinates, uniformly on its weak q,e family and on the SAME fixed
enlarged fluctuation window. The proof differentiates the integral with a
smooth centered cutoff and estimates the differentiated tail in the original
B coordinates. It does not differentiate a previously proved value-only
error. Combined with local_saddle_and_prefactor_turn40, this proves an actual
exponentially local correction to the classical coarse Hessian on
sublogarithmic, or sufficiently small logarithmic, boxes.

This is the density of the explicit positive saturated quotient reference,
normalized only as specified below. It is not a theorem for the full coarse
marginal, a selected RCP at an exceptional fiber, arbitrary framed coarse
coordinates, or repeated RG kernels.

1. EXACT OBJECT AND THE PARAMETER FAMILY

Retain the fixed-crossing geometry, block-local G0 forest, all ordinary
coarse rows, fixed exterior strip, and complete coarea density from
constrained_effective_action_turn40. Write

 a_e(q)=integral_D exp[-f(B;q,e)/g^2] J(B;q,e) dB,
 f(B;q,e)=S_e(x(B;q,e)),     ell(B;q,e)=log J(B;q,e).     (1.1)

Here B belongs to the fixed linear space kerQ with induced Euclidean
measure. D is the fixed sup window enlarged once in that note. q denotes
the coarse chord angles in the FIXED forest section Y_s(q): it inserts
identity on forest rows and exp(iq) on chord rows. The original relative
datum v(q,e) and all its derivatives are retained. No datum-dependent long
coarse gauge restoration is performed in (1.1).

Let N<=C_LR^4 bound the total fine/coarse coordinate count and d=dim kerQ.
Fix the family

 ||q||_infinity, ||log e||_infinity <=gZ,
 Z>=1,   R,Z,T bounded by fixed powers of ell_g=log(1/g). (1.2)

Choose D using a slightly enlarged family, say the bound 2gZ, with the same
fixed margins as before. In particular D has half-width

 b_g=C_LgR^2(1+Z+RT+sqrt(ell_g)),

with its constant chosen once. The previous requirement gR^3V_g small
holds for sufficiently small g. Every saddle has a uniform interior margin
and D contains all centered truncation balls used below. If complete old-W
coverage is claimed, also impose Z>=C_LRT so the prescribed q family covers
the earlier coarse-quotient window. Otherwise the analytic statement is
restricted to (1.2) without discarding a probability outside it.

All q derivatives below are real derivatives in one or two fixed unit
coarse chord/color coordinates, including a repeated coordinate if desired.
The constants are uniform over their locations, the remaining weak data,
the enclosing volume, and g in the stated regime. Exterior e is held fixed
in these derivatives. For d=0 the corresponding Laplace ratio is exactly1.

2. MIXED CHART AND SADDLE DERIVATIVES

Let B_*(q,e) be the actual constrained minimum from the prior theorem and
H_*=f_BB(B_*;q,e). On the complete small chart,

              c_L I<=H_*<=C_L I.                       (2.1)

The finite-read action and ordinary map, the bounded row/column Neumann
inverse of the constraint chart, and finite implicit differentiation give
uniform Euclidean multilinear bounds on f derivatives of total orders
2 through6. Its first derivative can instead cost C_L sqrt(N) epsilon;
the original-coordinate tail estimate below permits that factor.
For terms containing the action gradient, use its per-link sup bound paired
with the l1 chart-derivative bound. This is the same estimate already used
for the constraint-curvature term in the Hessian. Higher local products and
implicit derivative partitions at these fixed orders add fixed constants,
not a factor per block. The relative-coordinate map v(q,e) has bounded
local derivatives and is included.

The corresponding log-density derivatives through total order4 are bounded
by C_LN in unit Euclidean directions. They include the trace derivatives of
the constraint determinant and all fine/coarse Haar factors. A separate
operator or per-link bound is used when available; C_LN suffices here.

Differentiating f_B(B_*;q,e)=0 gives

 B_*,u=-H_*^-1 f_Bu,
 B_*,uv=-H_*^-1{f_Buv+f_BBu[B_*,v]+f_BBv[B_*,u]
                              +f_BBB[B_*,u,B_*,v]}.    (2.2)

Thus ||B_*,u||_2+||B_*,uv||_2<=C_L. These bounds do not require dividing
by g or by the size of the datum ball. The first two datum derivatives of
H_* have uniformly bounded operator norms. The first two total derivatives
of the centered cubic action coefficient are uniformly bounded, and those
of the centered linear/quadratic log-density coefficients cost at most C_LN.

These statements also follow from the stronger anchored nonlinear KKT
response in local_saddle_and_prefactor_turn40. Only their Euclidean bounds
are needed for the Laplace error; its spatial conclusion is used separately
in Section6.

3. CENTERED CORE, WITH DERIVATIVES BEFORE ESTIMATION

Set S_min=f(B_*;q,e), J_*=J(B_*;q,e),

 Z_H=(2pi)^(d/2)/sqrt(det H_*),
 R_g(q)=exp[S_min/g^2] a_e(q)/(g^d J_* Z_H).            (3.1)

Choose a smooth even cutoff chi_A(xi), equal to1 for |xi|<=A and0 for
|xi|>=2A, with first two Euclidean derivatives bounded by C/A and C/A^2.
For example use a fixed smooth function of |xi|^2/A^2. Take

                  A=C_*sqrt(N+ell_g+1),                (3.2)

where C_* is fixed after derivative order2 has been selected. Its doubled
ball fits inside D after B=B_*+gxi for EVERY datum in (1.2). The cutoff is
independent of q in xi coordinates; there is no loss of window margin per
derivative.

Expand the CENTERED differences, rather than their large separate factors:

 [f(B_*+gxi;q)-S_min]/g^2
     = (1/2)xi^tH_*xi + (g/6)f_BBB(B_*;q)[xi^3]
                              + O_C2(g^2|xi|^4),
 ell(B_*+gxi;q)-ell(B_*;q)
     = g ell_B(B_*;q)[xi] + O_C2(g^2N|xi|^2).           (3.3)

The C2 symbol here means the two selected q derivatives with xi fixed.
It follows from Section2 and Taylor's integral remainder. Thus relative
to the normalized Gaussian G_q with covariance H_*^-1 the core integrand
is

 1+g L_q(xi)+E_g(q,xi),
 L_q=ell_B(B_*;q)[xi]-f_BBB(B_*;q)[xi^3]/6.             (3.4)

On |xi|<=2A, g(A^3+NA) tends to zero in the polylogarithmic regime, and
the remainder and its first two q derivatives are bounded by

 C_Lg^2{ |xi|^4+N|xi|^2+(|xi|^3+N|xi|)^2 }.           (3.5)

Enlarging the fixed numerical constant or adding lower-degree terms gives
the same estimates. L_q and both its datum derivatives are ODD. For every
q, the cutoff and G_q are even. Hence E_Gq[chi_A L_q]=0 identically as a
function of q, so its first two q derivatives also vanish. No assumption
that the full density or its nonzero-background logarithm is even is used.

For completeness, differentiating the normalized Gaussian contributes

 score_u(xi)=-1/2{xi^t H_*,u xi-Tr(H_*^-1 H_*,u)}.

It is bounded by C_L(N+|xi|^2). The second derivative contributes an even
polynomial bounded by C_L(1+N+|xi|^2)^2. These are the actual normalization
derivatives; they cannot simply be omitted. Gaussian moments in (3.5),
with these at most two scores, cost at most a fixed polynomial in N, for
example C_L(1+N)^5. The Gaussian cutoff deficit and its derivatives are
exponentially small at (3.2).

4. THE TAIL IS DIFFERENTIATED ON THE ORIGINAL FIXED DOMAIN

The remaining part of (3.1) is integrated over the ORIGINAL fixed D with
the factor

          1-chi_A((B-B_*(q))/g).

There is no derivative of a hard moving domain. A cutoff derivative costs
C_L/(gA), and a second derivative costs C_L[(gA)^-2+(gA)^-1], by (2.2).
They are supported in the annulus A<=|B-B_*|/g<=2A. Derivatives of the
centered phase at fixed B can cost g^-2, and two can cost g^-4. The finite
chart bounds give at most an additional fixed polynomial in N. Derivatives
of the prefactor ratio, J_*, and Z_H have the same fixed polynomial cost.

The action is strongly convex throughout D:

            f(B;q)-S_min(q)>=c_L|B-B_*|^2.

The complete prefactor ratio and determinant normalization cost at most
exp(C_LN), as in the previous Laplace proof. Consequently the tail and its
first two q derivatives have the safe bound

        C_Lg^-4(1+N)^C exp[C_LN-c_L A^2].               (4.1)

Choose C_* once so (4.1)<=g^4; the polynomial in N can be absorbed in an
additional fixed multiple of N in the exponent. The omitted Gaussian core
tails with their scores satisfy the same bound. This keeps the original
normalizer and window while paying all recentering derivatives. It neither
uses a full-fiber probability estimate nor differentiates an RCP.

5. REAL C2 RELATIVE FORMULA AND THE COMPLETE EFFECTIVE ACTION

Combining the core and tail proves

 a_e(q)=exp[-S_min/g^2] J_* (2pi g^2)^(d/2)
                       (det H_*)^-1/2 [1+r_g(q)],       (5.1)

with, for a deliberately conservative explicit polynomial,

 max_(|alpha|<=2)|partial_q^alpha r_g|
       <= C_Lg^2 P_g + C_Lg^4,
 P_g=(1+R^2+sqrt(ell_g))^40.                            (5.2)

The sharper moment count in Section3 is unnecessary. Constants may depend
on the fixed polylogarithmic growth exponents and the fixed window margins.
Z,T enter those margins and the small-chart threshold; the polynomial in
(5.2) is already sufficient once (1.2) and the chart condition hold.
In particular g^2P_g tends to zero. This is a derivative theorem proved
from the integral, not a derivative of a scalar bound on an unspecified
Err function.

Set Gamma_g(q,e)=-g^2 log a_e(q), with its real positive logarithm. Define

                  W=(1/2)log det H_* -log J_* .         (5.3)

The exact determinant identity of local_saddle_and_prefactor_turn40 gives
the equivalent coordinate-invariant expression on this specified quotient:

 W=(1/2)log[(-1)^m det M_*]-log j_C(x_*)+log j_J(v),
 M_*=[ S_e''+sum lambda_a F_e,a''   DF_e^t;
                            DF_e                 0 ],
 m=dim Y.                                             (5.4)

Here stationarity is grad S_e+DF_e^t lambda=0. The constraint curvature
is retained. The real determinant sign is fixed by inertia; c_E and the
constraint-coordinate determinant cancel only by the exact congruence
identity, not by dropping a measure factor. The Gaussian volume constant
-(g^2d/2)log(2pi g^2) is retained in Gamma_g and is q-independent.

Since g^2P_g is small, (5.2) controls the real C2 logarithm as well. For
the two chosen coarse coordinates u,v,

 |Gamma_g,uv-S_min,uv-g^2W_uv| <= C_L g^4 P_g.           (5.5)

All lower-order signed vacuum terms are represented by the actual J_*,
H_* and S_min in this expression. No source-dependent normalization is
silently replaced by a constant.

6. ACTUAL SPATIAL COROLLARY IN THE DECLARED COORDINATES

The independently proved nonlinear KKT companion establishes, uniformly on
this small-data branch,

 |S_min,uv|+|W_uv| <= C_L exp[-m_L dist(u,v)].           (6.1)

It retains the local contact from v(q,e) and the complete trace/Haar
derivatives. Its proof uses the exponential inverse of the local nonlinear
saddle matrix and anchored response kernels before summing trace vertices;
it is not a dimension bound on the determinant. In the weak/polylog regime
the local saddle is the actual global fiber minimum by the earlier coverage
argument, so it is the same S_min as in (5.1).

Equations(5.5)--(6.1) imply the actual positive-numerator estimate

 |Gamma_g,uv-S_min,uv|
       <=C_Lg^2 exp[-m_L dist(u,v)]+C_Lg^4P_g.           (6.2)

Restrict R<=C ell_g^b for a fixed b<1. Every pair of the indicated indices
has distance<=C_dR. For any fixed 0<m'<m_L,

 g^4P_g/[g^2exp(-m'dist(u,v))]
      <=exp[-2ell_g+log P_g+m'C_dR] ->0.               (6.3)

Thus, uniformly over the weak data and all specified coordinate pairs,

 |Gamma_g,uv-S_min,uv|<=C_Lg^2exp[-m'dist(u,v)].         (6.4)

The same holds for R<=c ell_g when c is sufficiently small, for example
m'C_dc<1. The polynomial factors from fixed polylogarithmic Z,T do not
alter this absorption. The estimate does not extend by this proof to an
arbitrary logarithmic coefficient, superlogarithmic boxes, or a fixed
physical box under UV refinement.

LOCALITY SCOPE IS ESSENTIAL. u,v are fixed coarse FOREST CHORD indices and
Y_s(q) is the local coordinate insertion used throughout. Transforming an
arbitrary framed coarse field y to q(y) uses long forest paths. Neither
(6.1) nor (6.4) proves locality after that transformation, or a native
gauge-invariant polymer norm. They concern the exact saturated quotient
density in the specified coordinates. The theorem also does not identify
the full coarse marginal or a later effective action, estimate omitted
compact branches on every fiber, or control a renormalized physical
observable. The actual exterior is retained throughout.
