at one exterior coordinate j differentiates the exact conditional
ratio by the complete score H'_j. Convexity of the exterior factor
permits the entire straight segment, and the containing-product
hypotheses cover that segment.

For ANY bounded test of the joint S-marginal, the bounded-local
projected-conditional-mean inequality bounds its score covariance.
The absolute weighted Hessian COLUMN sum supplies
C exp(-nu d(j,S)) without a cardinality |S|. This follows from
d(j,S)<=d(j,i)+d(i,S), and the weighted full column bound; using its
l1 bound also controls the required l2 norm. Taking the supremum
over 0<=F<=1 therefore gives TV of the ENTIRE S-marginal, as required
for the one protected-region maximal coupling. It is not a claim
deduced by summing single-coordinate total variations.

The conditional mean response for x_i follows from the same weighted

2. BLOCK TILTS AND THEIR EXACT NORMALIZERS

The conditional perturbation is the sum of all terms meeting B,
including terms crossing its boundary. Its oscillation is at most
delta|B|, since summing the per-site norm over B can only overcount
these terms. After constants are subtracted, the finite-volume sum
converges absolutely and uniformly even for a countable interaction.
Thus its normalized density ratio relative to the reference is
between exp(-a_B) and exp(a_B), a_B=delta|B|.

The mean-change estimate uses
E_nu[(x_i-m_i)(density ratio-1)]; its magnitude is bounded by the
reference centered second moment times O(a_B). The perturbed variance
is bounded by using the reference mean as a trial center. Neither
step compares an uncentered variable with a uniform spin supremum.

When the exterior changes only at j, only interactions containing j
can change, and an interaction also meeting B has diameter at least
d(j,B). The difference of its two evaluated conditional potentials
has oscillation at most twice the original oscillation. This proves
the exponentially small far tilt-difference (8).

The far TV comparison first applies the SAME bounded tilt to the two
reference laws and then changes the tilt. For a common positive tilt
f, the elementary normalized-ratio estimate
TV(T_f P,T_f Q)<=2 exp(osc log f) TV(P,Q) is valid. Hence (9)
retains the original conditional normalizers and gains the required
exp(-kappa d(j,B)) perturbation term. The intermediate common tilt
need not be a separately named physical law.

For a protected subregion S, using triangle inequalities through the

The proof correctly restricts this nondecaying a_B cost to NEAR

3. HYBRID COUPLING AND THE REMOVAL OF THE CUTOFF COST

For one update, maximally couple the entire chosen S-marginals, then
extend each side by its conditional complement. This produces one
joint block coupling with P(S differs)=p. No incompatible collection
of separate maximal couplings is requested.

On the event that S agrees, every protected coordinate agrees. On
its complement, subtract each law's own UNCONDITIONAL conditional-
block mean, use Cauchy--Schwarz and its variance bound. This yields

 E[1_(X_i!=Y_i)+|X_i-Y_i|]
 <=p(1+|m_i-m'_i|)+2C_sigma sqrt(p).

It is important that no variance conditional on coupling failure is
assumed. The unprotected bound follows by the same triangle estimate
without the event. The two means may be far apart, but their
difference is at most C s exp(-nu d(i,j))+C a_B, where s is the
input exterior displacement.

Dividing by the input hybrid cost 1+s bounds sqrt(s)/(1+s) and
s/(1+s) uniformly. This gives (15), with only O((r+1)^d)
unprotected coordinates, and no cutoff radius. For a far block,
using p/(1+s)<=C[exp(-nu k)+delta exp(-kappa k)] in the a_B p
term removes a spurious a_B floor. Together with a_B<=1 it gives
(16). Both details are needed for the infinite-volume uniform count.

The cost d_i=1_(x_i!=y_i)+|x_i-y_i| is a genuine triangle-inequality
