TURN82 -- QUADRATIC CURRENT BUDGETS FOR EXACT SU(2) CONDITIONAL SOURCES
20 October 2026. history47. Independent-current kernel theorem.

RESULT
The exact bounded conditional source from TURN81 has a substantially
larger quantitative domain than its total-l1 estimate suggested. Absorb
every real current change into the positive base. The normalized complex
kernel then has total variation bounded by exp(C sum_e v_e), where
v_e is the EXACT variance of the imaginary linear current on link e,
provided each v_e stays below a fixed number less than2. On S3,
  v_e <= |b_e,perp|^2/(1+r_e)
                       +4|b_e,parallel|^2/(1+r_e)^2,
and also v_e<=|b_e|^2/4. This gives dimension-independent bounds on
weighted complex l2 balls and on every fixed derivative order there.

A Bessel-product refinement proves the SHARP uniform one-link zero-free
strip |Im current|<j_(1,1), the first positive zero of J_1. More generally,
if |b_e|^2<=eta(r_e^2+j_(1,1)^2), eta<1, the total variation is at most
  exp{[2(1-eta)]^-1 sum_e |b_e|^2/(1+r_e)}.
The first bound can be stronger for longitudinal perturbations.

These are exact estimates for the independent tilted-Haar kernels
conditioned on the REAL two-HS auxiliary variables. They do not make
the interacting zero-link marginal nu a Gaussian law, supply a uniform
complex domain in all auxiliary coordinates, or prove a continuum
source bound. The literal original source/denominator and overlapping
read contacts remain those of TURN81.

1. EXACT CONDITIONAL SETUP AND REAL SHIFTS

Read work/turn81_bounded_probe_audit_tail.txt Section4A and
work/turn81_external_source_history.txt Sections3--6. The actual two-HS
law gives independent original SU(2) links conditioned on real (G,R):
  k_a(dq)=product_e exp(a_e dot q_e)/Z(a_e) dω(q_e),
  q_e in S3, a_e in R4, Z(a)=integral_S3 exp(a dot q)dω(q). (1.1)
The real four-vector a is the quaternion-linear part of
Re Tr J_e^dagger U_e. Complexification below is of this REAL-LINEAR
coordinate function, not a new antiholomorphic Re/conjugation operation.
The complementary real components of J do not couple to SU(2).

Fix the finite ORIGINAL link set S read by all probes. Links outside S
integrate to one. For a complex perturbation zeta_e=u_e+i b_e, u_e,b_e
real, set c_e=a_e+u_e, r_e=|c_e|. There is no smallness requirement on u:
it is absorbed exactly into the real positive product base k_c. The
normalized complex kernel is
  k_(c+ib)(dq)=k_c(dq) exp(i sum_e b_e dot q_e)/D_c(b),
  D_c(b)=product_e phi_(c_e)(b_e),
  phi_c(b)=E_(k_c) exp(i b dot q).                         (1.2)
Where D is nonzero, this is a mass-one complex measure, and exactly
  ||k_(c+ib)||_TV=1/|D_c(b)|.                              (1.3)
For every bounded measurable original B, its normalized conditional
mean m_B(c+ib)=integral B dk_(c+ib) therefore has absolute value at most
||B||infinity/|D|. B need not have an analytic extension in q.

2. THE EXACT S3 COVARIANCE AND ITS UNIFORM BOUNDS

For r>=0 write Z(r)=Z(r n), |n|=1, and
  c(r)=d log Z(r)/dr=E_r(n dot q).
We distinguish this scalar c(r) from the vector c_e. Direct spherical
integration or the even moment series gives
  Z(r)=2 I_1(r)/r, Z(0)=1,
  c(r)=I_2(r)/I_1(r) for r>0,
  c(r)=r/4-r^3/96+O(r^5) at zero.                         (2.1)
All subsequent covariance facts have the following elementary proof.
Put x=n dot q. Its density is proportional to e^(r x)sqrt(1-x^2)
on[-1,1]. Integrating the derivative of
e^(r x)(1-x^2)^(3/2) yields
  r E_r(1-x^2)=3 E_r x=3c(r).
The exact longitudinal/transverse decomposition is consequently
  Cov_r(q)=c'(r) n n^T + w(r)(Id-n n^T),
  w(r)=c(r)/r, w(0)=c'(0)=1/4,
  c'(r)=1-c(r)^2-3c(r)/r>0.                              (2.2)
The strict positivity is also the variance of x under a positive density.

We prove c'(r)<=w(r) without an asymptotic claim. Define
  b(r)=r/(sqrt(r^2+4)+2).
It solves b^2+4b/r=1. Its series is r/4-r^3/64+O(r^5), so c>b
near zero. At any positive crossing c=b the Riccati equation gives
  c'=b/r, b'=2/[sqrt(r^2+4)(sqrt(r^2+4)+2)],
and c'-b'>0. Hence c cannot first cross downward through b. Therefore
  c'(r)-w(r)=1-c(r)^2-4c(r)/r<=0.                         (2.3)
This also proves w'(r)<=0 and w(r)<=w(0)=1/4.
Since c'>0, the Riccati equation gives
  c(r)<r/[3/2+sqrt(r^2+9/4)],
  w(r)<=1/(1+r).                                         (2.4)
The last inequality uses sqrt(r^2+9/4)>=r and 3/2>=1.
Thus Cov_r(q)<=w(r)Id<=min(1/4,1/(1+r))Id.

For a useful sharper longitudinal estimate, let y=1-x. Its density is
a Gamma(shape3/2,rate r) density tilted by the decreasing nonnegative
function sqrt(2-y)1_(y<2). An increasing function and a decreasing
function have nonpositive covariance under any probability law (use
two independent copies). Therefore E_r y<=3/(2r), and
c(r)>=1-3/(2r). For r>=3/2 insert this lower bound in the decreasing
right side of the Riccati equation to obtain c'(r)<=9/(4r^2).
For r<3/2 the already proved1/4 bound suffices. In particular, globally,
  c'(r)<=min(1/4,9/(4r^2))<=4/(1+r)^2.                   (2.5)
The last comparison splits at r=3. The r=0 statement is by continuity.
No uniform covariance lower bound is inferred from these upper bounds.

For b=b_parallel n+b_perp, the exact scalar variance is
  v_c(b):=Var_c(b dot q)
      =c'(r)b_parallel^2+w(r)|b_perp|^2.                  (2.6)
It is at most |b|^2/4 and at most
  4 b_parallel^2/(1+r)^2+|b_perp|^2/(1+r).
At r=0 use v=|b|^2/4 without choosing a preferred direction.

3. ELEMENTARY VARIANCE TUBE AND NORMALIZED SOURCE LOGS

For a real random X with EX=0,
  |E exp(iX)-1|<=E X^2/2,
because |e^(ix)-1-ix|<=x^2/2. Applying this to
X=b dot(q-Eq) gives
  |phi_c(b)|>=1-v_c(b)/2.                                 (3.1)
Let 0<v_*<2 be fixed and assume v_e:=v_(c_e)(b_e)<=v_*.
Set V=sum_e v_e and
  C_*= -log(1-v_*/2)/v_*.
Since -log(1-v/2)/v is increasing on(0,2), equations(1.2)--(3.1) imply
  D_c(b) ne 0,
  ||k_(c+ib)||_TV<=exp(C_* V).                            (3.2)
This bound uses the sum of squares, with the exact anisotropic
covariance weights(2.6). For example |b_e|<sqrt(8) for each e already
gives a uniform one-link zero-free strip; Section4 improves its width.
The existence of the normalized kernel is independent of how large V
is, although its uniform bounded-observable norm grows with V.

Let B_i be arbitrary original measurable probes with 0<=B_i<=1,
each reading S. Define R=sum_i|z_i| and the COMPLETE conditional source
  C(z;c+ib)=integral exp(sum_i z_i B_i(q)) dk_(c+ib)(q).
Then
  |C(z;c+ib)|<=exp(R+C_*V),
  |C(z;c+ib)-1|<=exp(C_*V)(e^R-1).                        (3.3)
The second estimate uses the exact normalization C(0)=1 before taking
absolute values. Whenever exp(C_*V)(e^R-1)<1 there is the canonical
source logarithm, with
  |log C|<=-log[1-exp(C_*V)(e^R-1)].                      (3.4)
The logarithm is zero at z=0 and holomorphic jointly wherever this
strict bound holds. The defining integrals are entire before division;
their nonzero denominator and the disk around1 justify that assertion.
For V bounded by a fixed constant this supplies a fixed source radius,
regardless of the number of read links. It does not replace the complete
conditional covariance by a product of conditional means when probes
share original links.

4. SHARP UNIFORM STRIP AND A BESSEL-PRODUCT QUADRATIC BOUND

Here we use one explicitly identified classical special-function input:
the Bessel product J_1(z)=(z/2)product_(m>=1)(1-z^2/j_(1,m)^2),
and the reality of its nonzero zeros. Both are recorded in NIST DLMF
10.21(i),(iii), especially10.21.15:
  https://dlmf.nist.gov/10.21#i
  https://dlmf.nist.gov/10.21.E15
The modified-Bessel series is DLMF10.25.2:
  https://dlmf.nist.gov/10.25.E2
No lattice or source estimate is imported from these identities.

For a complex four-vector z, analytic continuation of the spherical
moment series gives the single-valued entire formula
  Z(z)=sum_(k>=0)(z dot z/4)^k/[k!(k+1)!]
      =product_(m>=1)(1+(z dot z)/j_(1,m)^2).             (4.1)
The dot product is bilinear, not Hermitian. Thus all zeros satisfy
  z dot z=-j_(1,m)^2.
For z=c+ib, this means simultaneously
  c dot b=0, |b|^2=|c|^2+j_(1,m)^2.                      (4.2)
Hence Z has NO zero when |Im z|<j_(1,1), uniformly over all real
parts. This uniform strip is sharp: c=0 and |b|=j_(1,1) give a zero.
An arbitrary normalized bounded-probe integral can have a genuine pole
there; for example B=(1+n dot q)/2 has a nonzero numerator at the first
zero along z=i j_(1,1)n, because the nonzero J_1 zero is simple.

For quantitative bounds assume, link by link,
  |b|^2<=eta(r^2+j_(1,1)^2), 0<=eta<1.                   (4.3)
Each factor of the normalized product in(4.1) obeys
  |1+(-|b|^2+2i c dot b)/(r^2+j_(1,m)^2)|
       >=1-|b|^2/(r^2+j_(1,m)^2)>0.
Taking logarithms, using -log(1-x)<=x/(1-eta), and differentiating
the positive real product yields
  sum_m 1/(r^2+j_(1,m)^2)=c(r)/(2r)=w(r)/2.
All products and derivatives converge locally; at r=0 use the limit.
Therefore
  1/|phi_c(b)|<=exp[|b|^2 w(r)/(2(1-eta))].              (4.4)
For the full conditional product, let
  Q_w=sum_e w(r_e)|b_e|^2,
  Q=sum_e |b_e|^2/(1+r_e).
Then
  ||k_(c+ib)||_TV<=exp[Q_w/(2(1-eta))]
                  <=exp[Q/(2(1-eta))].                  (4.5)
The exact mean-phase is irrelevant to total variation. Equations
(3.3)--(3.4) hold with the exponent in(4.5) in place of C_*V.

For any fixed rho<j_(1,1), the uniform per-link strip |b_e|<=rho
has eta<=rho^2/j_(1,1)^2 and thus the desired bound
exp(C_rho sum_e |b_e|^2/(1+|a_e+Re zeta_e|)). There is also the weaker
but immediate exp(C_rho sum_e |Im zeta_e|^2) estimate. The variance
bound(3.2) remains preferable when b is mostly longitudinal and r large.
These alternatives should not be conflated: (4.5) permits larger
transverse imaginary values, whereas(3.2) can spend a much smaller
longitudinal budget.

5. A FIXED REAL-CENTER WEIGHTED HILBERT BALL AND DERIVATIVES

The previous bounds depend on the absorbed real parts. A useful fixed
complex neighborhood of a prescribed real a is obtained by defining
  ||zeta||_a^2=sum_e |zeta_e|^2/(1+|a_e|).
Here |zeta_e| is the Hermitian complex Euclidean norm. If
||zeta||_a<=rho<=1/2, then individually
  |Re zeta_e|<=rho sqrt(1+|a_e|)<=rho(1+|a_e|),
  1+|a_e+Re zeta_e|>=(1-rho)(1+|a_e|).
Consequently the total exact variance obeys
  V<=rho^2/(1-rho)<=2rho^2<=1/2.                         (5.1)
Choose v_*=1/2 in(3.2); its constant is2 log(4/3)<2/3. Hence
  ||k_(a+zeta)||_TV<=exp[2rho^2/(3(1-rho))]
                       <=exp(4rho^2/3).                 (5.2)
In particular every bounded original m_B is holomorphic on this
weighted complex l2 ball and has norm at most exp(4rho^2/3)||B||infinity.
For bounded probes, a common source condition such as
  exp(1/3)(e^R-1)<1
provides a zero-free source logarithm on the entire rho<=1/2 ball.

These are dimension-independent Banach-space Cauchy inputs. For a unit
direction v in ||.||_a, ordinary one-variable Cauchy gives
  |D^m m_B(a)[v,...,v]|<=m! rho^-m exp(4rho^2/3)||B||infinity.
Polarization gives a corresponding multilinear operator bound with
the fixed-order factor m^m replacing m!. Radius may be decreased
strictly before applying Cauchy. This is a fixed-order directional/
operator-jet estimate; it does NOT assert an unweighted coefficient-sum
bound over arbitrarily many separate current labels. Mixed source jets
are controlled on the simultaneous current/source domain by the same
argument, with all conditional mixed scalars retained.

5A. EXACT FIRST AND SECOND REAL-CURRENT JETS

For 0<=B<=1 and real current directions h,k, put
X_h=sum_e h_e dot(q_e-E_a q_e). Independence gives
  V_a(h)=E_a X_h^2=sum_e v_(a_e)(h_e).
Differentiating the exact normalized product integral yields
  D_h m_B=Cov_a(B,X_h),
  D_h D_k m_B=E_a[(B-E_a B)X_h X_k].                      (5.3)
The second line is the complete mixed cumulant, including derivatives
of the normalizer. Consequently
  |D_h m_B|<=sqrt(V_a(h))/2,
  |D_h D_k m_B|<=sqrt[V_a(h)V_a(k)].                      (5.4)
Use Var(B)<=1/4 in the first inequality and |B-E B|<=1 plus
Cauchy--Schwarz in the second. These bounds are independent of the
number of read links and do not use Cauchy estimates. They control
directions in the exact current covariance metric. They do not give
a sum over arbitrary current labels for free, or control derivatives
of the interacting nu marginal.

6. THE ACTUAL GAIN AND THE REMAINING SCALING

For N comparable read links with real current size r and imaginary
perturbation size epsilon, (4.5) costs order N epsilon^2/(1+r),
instead of the TURN81 l1 cost N epsilon. A bounded norm therefore allows
epsilon of order sqrt((1+r)/N), subject to the stated per-link domain;
at weak real currents this is N^-1/2 rather than N^-1. At strong
currents r comparable to beta it is sqrt(beta/N). Longitudinal
perturbations can have the better variance budget of order
N epsilon^2/(1+r)^2 by(2.6). None of this gives a bounded norm for
arbitrary fixed perturbations on an increasing number of weak-current
links. Already at a=0, identical imaginary shifts t n produce the
denominator [2J_1(t)/t]^N, whose inverse grows exponentially in N for
fixed small nonzero t. This is the exact complex-measure operator norm.

In the actual HS representation the currents are
  J_e=sum_(first incidences) sqrt(beta)R_h
                 +sum_(second incidences)2R_h^dagger G_p.
Thus a complex change of auxiliaries has current increments bounded by
the local sums of sqrt(beta)|delta R|, |G||delta R|,
|R||delta G| and |delta R||delta G|, with fixed quaternion-projection
constants. Complexification here again means continuation of their
real coordinate polynomials. The auxiliaries are unbounded. A uniform
current-weighted ball is therefore not a uniform unweighted auxiliary
ball on all real records. Actual lower-current and Gaussian-moment
estimates can be relevant to averaging this consumer, but must be
proved for the interacting nu and on the intended read family.

The source identity for an original bounded probe remains
  E_mu exp(sum z_i B_i)=E_nu C(z;J(G,R)).
The full normalizer of nu is unchanged. Current-tube control of the
conditional integrand does not prove that the deformed interacting
nu integral has the same complex locality, or identify a new native
RG map. Covariance between distant probes can reside entirely in the
conditional means as functions of nu. Neither their bounded norm nor
the sharp one-link strip supplies a nonzero reflected signal, a spatial
decay estimate for nu, or a continuum limit.
