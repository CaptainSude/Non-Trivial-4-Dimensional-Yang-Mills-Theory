ORIGINAL CONDITIONAL MEANS, ADDITIVE SOURCE ERRORS, AND REFLECTION
TURN74. tail47. 20 October 2026.

RESULT

There is a new quantitative ORIGINAL-law consequence of the actual
TURN37 bare-box replacement and TURN64 Wilson energy theorem. For an
additive canonically normalized plaquette source, its coarse conditional-
mean error has an orthogonal fiber part and an exterior-dependent drift.
The all-order original energy moments control these parts respectively
by sqrt(alpha) log(1/alpha) and alpha log(1/alpha), with no exponential
factor in the number of read boxes. For sources in disjoint read boxes,
the fiber parts cancel from the covariance comparison EXACTLY. Only
the exterior drifts remain. Sections 1--4 prove this result.

This avoids the large local chi-square product in TURN73. It does not
provide decay with the physical distance between the sources. The
remaining physical weight count defeats the available bare alpha at
asymptotically free scaling. It is not a full-law continuum bound.

Ordinary conditional L2 contraction does avoid analytic-norm losses
when applied to the SAME original probability law. It cannot create
the needed localization or replace actual source conditional means
by selected ones. Section 5 spells out the exact chronology and cost.

There is also an exact full-Wilson positive-time route: site reflection
identifies the centered reflected covariance with the variance of an
original conditional mean on the two boundary slices. Any further
coarse projection of those boundary data gives a lower bound. This
is useful because it does not require ordinary L2 convergence of an
unsmeared composite. It still needs an actual nonzero original-law
coarse conditional mean; current estimates do not supply one.

All statements below are at a finite regulator unless a limit is
explicitly stated. No prior frozen note is changed.

1. THE EXACT BARE CONDITIONAL PRODUCT

Use the SAME notation and actual bare-box hypotheses as TURN73:

 mu(domega,du)=mu_X(domega) product_i Q_i^omega(du_i),
 Q_i^omega=integral nu_i^omega(dy_i) R_i^(omega,y_i)(du_i),
 Lambda(domega,dy)=mu_X(domega) product_i nu_i^omega(dy_i).

Every boundary link is in omega, no plaquette reads two active core
interiors, and Y_i only reads omega and core i. Set Z=(omega,Y).
The original conditional law at Z is product R_i.

Let W_i be the frozen original weak window and B_i an exterior bad-
boundary event. Assume Q_i^omega(W_i^c)<=alpha on B_i^c. With
p_i=R_i(W_i^c), 0<theta<1, let

 G_i={B_i^c,p_i<=theta},
 M_i=1_(G_i^c)+1_(G_i)1_(W_i)/(1-p_i),
 P_i=M_i R_i.

The replacement nu=Lambda product P_i preserves Lambda exactly.
It also preserves omega. All definitions are frozen at zero source.
There is no global all-good conditioning or its denominator.

Write L_i=1-M_i and

 c_theta=max(1,theta/(1-theta)).

At fixed omega, viewing Y_i as its actual function of U_i, direct
integration of the two values on W_i and W_i^c gives

 Q_i L_i=0,
 |L_i|<=c_theta,
 Q_i |L_i|=2 integral 1_(G_i) p_i dnu_i^omega <=2alpha,
 Q_i L_i^2=integral 1_(G_i) p_i/(1-p_i) dnu_i^omega
                                            <=alpha/(1-theta). (1.1)

On a bad boundary the left sides in the last two lines are zero.
Thus these are conditional bounds uniform in omega, despite the lack
of a small rejection probability at every individual coarse fiber.
Boundary events need not be independent.

A frozen soft weight w_i in[0,1] has the same conclusion after adding
an independent local uniform flag and setting W_i={flag<=w_i}. Original
sources do not read the flag; forgetting it leaves the same source
means. The same product/coarse-read hypotheses must still hold.

2. TWO DIFFERENT CONDITIONAL-MEAN ERRORS

Take an original additive source

 F=F_ext(omega)+sum_(i in A) F_i(omega,U_i).

The actual plaquette assignment has this form: assign a plaquette
reading an interior to its unique core, and assign an entirely
exterior plaquette to F_ext. In this section F_i is not allowed an
extra read of another core's Y_j. Let

 m_F(Z)=E_mu[F|Z],       m_F^nu(Z)=E_nu[F|Z],
 delta_i(Z)=R_i F_i-P_i F_i=R_i(L_i F_i),
 delta_F=m_F-m_F^nu=sum_i delta_i,
 b_i(omega)=E_Lambda[delta_i|omega]=Q_i(L_i F_i),
 xi_i=delta_i-b_i.

The xi_i are independent and mean zero CONDITIONAL ON omega, since
each is a function of omega,Y_i. Their cross terms and their cross
terms against any exterior-measurable function vanish. Consequently

 ||delta_F||_2^2
   =sum_i E Var(delta_i|omega)+||sum_i b_i||_2^2,           (2.1)

and

 Var(delta_F)
   =sum_i E Var(delta_i|omega)+Var(sum_i b_i).             (2.2)

The b_i are not independent. No square-sum estimate is asserted for
their sum. They represent precisely the coherent exterior response.

Here are useful general estimates, before specializing the source.
For any integer r>=2, conditional Jensen followed by Holder and(1.1)
gives

 ||delta_i||_2
 <= c_theta^(1/r) [alpha/(1-theta)]^((1-1/r)/2)
                                         ||F_i||_(2r,mu). (2.3)

Indeed ||E[L_i F_i|Z]||_2^2<=E[L_i^2 F_i^2]. Use Holder with
exponents q=r/(r-1),r and the bound
E|L_i|^(2q)<=c_theta^(2q-2)alpha/(1-theta).

The exterior drift has a better estimate:

 ||b_i||_2
 <=c_theta^(1/r)(2alpha)^(1-1/r)||F_i||_(r,mu).            (2.4)

To prove it apply Holder first at each omega:
|Q_i(L_i F_i)|<=[Q_i|L_i|^q]^(1/q)[Q_i|F_i|^r]^(1/r).
Use Q_i|L_i|^q<=c_theta^(q-1)2alpha. Then the L2(mu_X)
norm of [Q_i|F_i|^r]^(1/r) is at most ||F_i||_(r,mu), since
r>=2. No arbitrary-exterior Wilson moment estimate was used.

Any exterior-measurable constant can be subtracted from F_i in
(2.3)--(2.4), because R_i L_i=0. Below it is convenient simply to
bound the unshifted canonical original plaquette source. Subsequent
deterministic vacuum centering does not change delta_i or b_i.

3. ACTUAL WILSON ACTION MOMENTS REMOVE THE BARE BETA FACTOR

For the original periodic SU(2) Wilson probability at homogeneous
beta>0, put h_p=1-Tr(U_p)/2. The actual TURN64 theorem gives

 E_mu exp(beta h_p/4)<=2^(3/4).

Tail integration, valid for every positive integer s, yields

 ||beta h_p||_s<=C_s,
 C_s=4[2^(3/4)s!]^(1/s)<=4*2^(3/(4s))*s.                 (3.1)

The last inequality uses s!<=s^s. We are not differentiating an
inequality of generating functions. The explicit bound on ALL s is
important: the order r below grows as alpha decreases. This is not
an application of a native fixed-jet constant at an increasing order.

For deterministic real plaquette weights let

 F_i=beta sum_(p assigned to i) f_p h_p,
 W_i=sum_(p assigned to i)|f_p|.

Minkowski gives ||F_i||_s<=C_s W_i under the SAME full original
law, with its exact normalizer. Put

 L_alpha=2+log(1/alpha),
 r=max(2,ceil(log(1/alpha))).

Then r<=L_alpha and alpha^(-1/r)<=e. Inserting(3.1) into
(2.3)--(2.4) proves, for a constant depending only on theta,

 ||delta_i||_2<=C_theta sqrt(alpha) L_alpha W_i,
 ||b_i||_2<=C_theta alpha L_alpha W_i.                    (3.2)

For example any sufficiently large fixed multiple of
e max(1,sqrt(c_theta))/sqrt(1-theta) is a valid C_theta.
The exact order-dependent formulas(2.3)--(2.4),(3.1) specify
the constants if needed. There is no remaining beta factor.

Combining(2.1) with Minkowski only for the b_i sum gives

 ||delta_F||_2^2
 <=C_theta^2 L_alpha^2
       [alpha sum_i W_i^2+alpha^2(sum_i W_i)^2].          (3.3)

The same upper bound applies to Var(delta_F). This holds for any
finite number of cores; no condition |A|alpha<=1 is needed.
It improves the exponential read-count estimate for these additive
sources. It does not replace the square of the sum in the drift term
by the sum of squares.

For an unscaled action sum f_p h_p, divide the right sides of
(3.2) by beta and(3.3) by beta^2. For any other calibration the
observable's actual factors must similarly be retained.

4. DISJOINT SOURCE CORES: ONLY EXTERIOR DRIFTS SURVIVE

Let F,H be original additive sources as in Section2 with disjoint
interior read-core sets A,B. Their physical supports must have enough
separation to ensure this after the actual plaquette/core collars.
Their exterior pieces may read any preserved exterior links.

Set

 a_F(omega)=E_mu[F|omega],   a_H(omega)=E_mu[H|omega],
 b_F=sum_(i in A)b_i,       b_H=sum_(j in B)b_j.

Given omega the interiors, and after replacement the pairs(Y_i,V_i),
are independent across cores. Therefore exactly

 Cov_mu(F,H)=Cov_(mu_X)(a_F,a_H),
 Cov_nu(F,H)=Cov_(mu_X)(a_F-b_F,a_H-b_H).                  (4.1)

In particular

 Cov_mu(F,H)-Cov_nu(F,H)
 =Cov(b_F,a_H)+Cov(a_F,b_H)-Cov(b_F,b_H).                 (4.2)

This is stronger than merely applying Cauchy--Schwarz to delta_F.
The centered fiber pieces xi_i do not occur at all: at fixed omega
they have zero covariance with the disjoint source's coarse means.
There is still no independence assumption on the exterior drifts.

For canonical action sources let W_F,W_H include all their weights,
including exterior plaquettes, and W_F,int,W_H,int include only their
respective core weights. Conditional L2 contraction and(3.1) give

 ||a_F-E F||_2<=2 C_2 W_F,
 ||a_H-E H||_2<=2 C_2 W_H.

Together with(3.2), this proves the actual original-source comparison

 |Cov_mu(F,H)-Cov_nu(F,H)|
 <=2 C_2 C_theta alpha L_alpha
                [W_F,int W_H+W_F W_H,int]
    +C_theta^2 alpha^2 L_alpha^2 W_F,int W_H,int.          (4.3)

The normalizer is the full original Wilson normalizer. Replacement
preserves the exact coarse and exterior marginals. This theorem has
no global no-bad probability, no growing product of fiber normalizers,
and no exponentially large read-core factor. It is an actual bare-
Wilson two-source result, not a statement about selected RG kernels.

It does not contain exp[-c distance/scale]. Disjointness is used only
to obtain(4.1). It gives no decay as two nonintersecting source regions
are moved farther apart. All their correlations may reside in omega.

5. WHAT FULL-LAW MARTINGALE CONTRACTION REALLY SAVES

At a finite regulator, retain an ACTUAL positive augmentation P of
the original Wilson law, with every needed old read and future
auxiliary draw. Choose decreasing sigma-fields G_0 superset ...
superset G_n from that SAME joint law, with F,H measurable at G_0.
For example retaining complete future records after each actual
elimination defines such sigma-fields; the current coarse field
alone need not give the conditional law if old records remain read.

Define

 X_j(F)=E_P[F|G_j],       D_j(F)=X_j(F)-X_(j+1)(F).

Then exactly

 Cov_P(F,H)=sum_(j<n) E_P[D_j(F)D_j(H)]
                   +Cov_P(X_n(F),X_n(H)),                 (5.1)
 sum_(j<n)||D_j(F)||_2^2
           =Var_P(F)-Var_P(X_n(F))<=Var_P(F).             (5.2)

Proof: E[D_j(F)|G_(j+1)]=0, which gives orthogonality of
different dates and of each increment with the terminal variable.
Expand F=X_n(F)+sum D_j(F), with its original mean retained.
The finite action source is bounded at each finite regulator,
so no integrability assumption is being guessed here.

If an actual inherited source error Z has been identified in L2(P),
then ||E_P[Z|G_j]||_2<=||Z||_2 at every later date. This removes
repeated analytic-norm losses for that SAME error. But errors born
at different dates are not orthogonal just because they are called
births. Without their identification as the D_j above, Minkowski
only gives the sum of their norms, not a square-sum.

The retained native private theorem controls only a specified part
of an original source at a fixed complete outer datum. Its regular,
healed and mixed-source pieces are not thereby identified with D_j.
The original regular conditional mean X_j is also not the one-mark
map on the TURN70 selected path. That path has the original density
multiplied by the declared windows and a different normalizer.

Finally L2 contraction has no spatial localization conclusion.
For separated original sources, (5.2) and Cauchy--Schwarz give only
sqrt(Var(F)Var(H)), not a decay factor joining their supports.
The known energy moments bound Var(F) by C W_F^2, with W_F of
order a^-4 for an unscaled fixed-physical canonical action profile.
This does not provide a useful continuum bound on(5.1).

6. PHYSICAL SCALING AND THE CONTACT ISSUE

Consider bounded nonzero profiles on fixed separated four-dimensional
physical regions, with canonical original source

 F_a=beta(a) sum_p f(x_p) h_p.

There is no additional a^4 in this source: for a smooth classical
field, h_p has its a^4 factor already, and the sum approximates the
integrated action density in its stated field convention. Its
quantum vacuum and contact terms are a separate renormalization
question. The absolute weight W_F is of order a^-4.

For boxes of polylogarithmic fine diameter R, bounded profiles give
sum_i W_i^2=O(a^-4 R^4), while (sum_i W_i)^2=O(a^-8).
Thus(3.3) has terms of order

 L_alpha^2[alpha a^-4 R^4+alpha^2 a^-8].                  (6.1)

The separated covariance bound(4.3) is at best of order
alpha L_alpha a^-8 by these estimates. In the admitted bare
polylog regime alpha behaves as exp[-c(log(1/g))^p] for fixed
p, whereas beta(a) is of order log(1/a). Therefore log(1/g)
is of order log log(1/a). Such an alpha does not beat any fixed
power of a. The displayed bounds do not tend to zero or remain
bounded for these physical canonical sources. This is a failure
of the estimates, not a proof that the actual covariance diverges.

The damped source a^4 F_a has W=O(1), and(4.3) does give a
vanishing replacement error as alpha tends to zero. That is a
valid comparison for a differently normalized observable, not
a nontriviality result for the calibrated action-density source.

Nor is ordinary same-support L2 convergence a necessary condition
for the desired composite. TURN63 explicitly proves, in free Maxwell
theory, that compactly mollified centered action squares have divergent
ordinary variance while their positive-time reflected mixed norms
converge to a nonzero OS vector. Replacing every separated estimate
by the diagonal bound in(5.2) can lose this distinction. The present
analysis does not impose ordinary random-distribution tightness on
the unsmoothed composite as a necessary route to YM4.

7. AN EXACT FULL-WILSON REFLECTION TARGET

There is a positive original-law formulation of the remaining mean
problem. Take a finite periodic Wilson lattice with even temporal
period N>=4. Reflect t to -t. Condition on B, the spatial link
variables in the two fixed slices t=0 and t=N/2. The remaining link
variables split into two reflected open half-cylinders. Every Wilson
plaquette belongs to one half or to a fixed slice; no plaquette reads
interior link variables from both halves. Conditional on B, the two
halves are independent and have reflected identical laws.

Let F be a real gauge-invariant original observable whose COMPLETE
read is in the positive half and its boundary, and let theta F be
its reflected copy. The original conditional densities and their
normalizers therefore give

 C_OS(F):=Cov_mu(theta F,F)
       =Var_mu(E_mu[F|B])>=0.                            (7.1)

Indeed the conditional expectation of the reflected copy is the
same function m(B), and E[theta F F|B]=m(B)^2. The two boundary
slices are both retained; replacing this finite periodic geometry
by only one conditioning slice would require a separate argument.

For ANY sub-sigma-field C of B, conditional orthogonal projection
gives the exact further decomposition

 C_OS(F)=Var(E[F|C])+E Var(E[F|B]|C)
                                          >=Var(E[F|C]). (7.2)

Thus an original coarse conditional mean at the reflection boundary
can certify a nonzero full reflected norm from below. For a real
square-integrable C-measurable test G of nonzero variance,

 C_OS(F)>=Cov_mu(F,G)^2/Var_mu(G).                        (7.3)

These statements are derived directly from the finite Wilson
conditional product; no selected law or replacement reference
appears. They apply to the literal finite-regulator action sources
as well as bounded gauge-invariant positive-time probes.

They do not furnish a cutoff-uniform lower bound on the right side.
The available energy estimates are upper bounds, and neither the
selected source theorem nor the private raw-source theorem computes
this full original boundary conditional mean. A coarse-preserving
replacement preserves the boundary distribution but not the function
E[F|B], exactly as the drift calculation above shows. Therefore its
preserved marginal is not already a nontriviality certificate.

Even a uniform nonzero bound in(7.2) alone would not establish positive
energy in a continuum limit or interacting Yang--Mills dynamics. Its
time dependence and spectral content would still have to be controlled.

8. PRECISE NEXT ATTACHMENT

The new useful estimate is(4.3), with the stronger error structure
(3.3), for actual original bare conditional boxes. It controls the
outer means directly and improves their source/count dependence.
It does not close the physical support count or repeated full-law
conditional-source problem.

To use full-law martingale contraction constructively, a mark must
first be identified with the SAME original conditional source and
given a physical L2 or separated/OS bound. Then later conditional
expectations cost no additional L2 factor. What remains unproved is
that first localized bound, its complete scalar and cross terms,
and an actual reflection-compatible nonzero mean such as(7.2).

The current native/selected estimates do not permit an original
full-source closure by L2 contraction alone. No completed continuum
existence or nontriviality proof is asserted.
