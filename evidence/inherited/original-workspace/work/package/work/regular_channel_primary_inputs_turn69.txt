3. THE PUBLISHED 2025 PLAQUETTE-SOURCE RESULT: EXACT NORMALIZATION CHECK

Primary paper:
 P. A. Faria da Veiga and M. O'Carroll,
 "On Yang-Mills Stability Bounds and Plaquette Field Generating Function",
 Reports on Mathematical Physics 95 (2025), 303--380.
 DOI 10.1016/S0034-4877(25)00035-7.
 Publisher:
 https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0034487725000357
 Full published institutional copy:
 https://repositorio.usp.br/directbitstream/ef6d3c36-bb3e-4609-acf3-305a632492f3/3265110.pdf

This full PDF was successfully retrieved, not inferred from its abstract.
Saved as work/remote69/FO_published2025.pdf (78 pages), with pypdf text.
Printed pp347--348 were visually checked in FO-pub-45.png and
FO-pub-46.png. The earlier preprint is arXiv:2205.07376v2.

The paper's equations (5)--(7) use

 U_b = exp(i a g A_b),
 A_p = 2 Re Tr(I-U_p) = ||I-U_p||_HS^2,
 density proportional to exp[-(a^(d-4)/g^2) sum_p A_p].

On printed p347, equation (82), the scaled observable used in Theorem 4 is

 F_p^s = (a^(d-4)/g^2)^(1/2) Im Tr(U_p-I).                 (3.1)

For defining SU(2), Tr(U) is real, so (3.1) vanishes identically. This
does not dispose of the paper's other observable: Remark 16, on the
same printed page, explicitly proposes the action field and says the
arguments carry over. However its displayed normalization is literally

 Tr H = A_p/(a^4 g),        Tr S = a^d Tr H,              (3.2)

and claims H approximately Tr(F^a)^2. With its own link convention,
U_p = I + i a^2 g F^a + higher terms, one instead gets

 A_p = a^4 g^2 Tr[(F^a)^2] + higher terms.                (3.3)

Thus (3.2) is g times the usual canonical action density at this
formal leading order. This is a printed normalization discrepancy.
It may be a typo in the claimed continuum interpretation, but we
must not replace the actual displayed field and import the same
source bounds without redoing their proof.

There is a second scope qualification: the displayed development concentrates
on U(N) (printed p308); the paper describes SU(N) modifications separately.
Remark 19, printed p350, explicitly qualifies the limiting source
analyticity domain for SU(N) as finite. We do not attribute an entire
limiting SU(2) theorem to the authors. The calculation below rules out
an attempted entire canonical-energy upgrade, not a fixed-disc theorem.

Theorem 4, printed pp348--349, keeps the complete finite-volume
normalizer and gives a factorized upper bound for r insertions,
then thermodynamic subsequential bounds. The finite-volume bound
contains z_u(r J_j), z_l, and the explicit retained/extra-bond counts.
Its estimates are uniform for fixed r in the cutoff-scaled fields;
they contain no separation-sensitive cross kernel or nonzero
separated connected lower bound. The r-dependent source amplification
must be retained when r grows like the number of physical cells.

Here is a direct actual-law test of the normalization upgrade.
Let U have normalized SU(2) Haar measure, h(U)=1-Tr(U)/2, and

 c(beta) = integral exp[-beta h(U)] dU
         = 2 exp(-beta) I_1(beta)/beta,   beta>0.

The normalized one-plaquette Wilson law has density
exp(-beta h)/c(beta). It is also realized by a single cycle after
tree gauge fixing. Under the paper's convention, A=4h and beta=4/g^2.
The canonical cell action is X=A/g^2=beta h. Exactly,

 E_beta exp(J X) = c((1-J)beta)/c(beta).                  (3.4)

For fixed real J<1, Laplace's method at U=I, or the standard positive
real I_1 asymptotic, gives

 c(beta) = sqrt(2/pi) beta^(-3/2)(1+O(beta^-1)),
 E_beta exp(J X) -> (1-J)^(-3/2).                         (3.5)

At J=1 the exact numerator is c(0)=1, and

 E_beta exp(X) ~ sqrt(pi/2) beta^(3/2) -> infinity.        (3.6)

All these finite-beta MGFs are entire. It is their cutoff-uniform
entire bound which fails at canonical normalization. In contrast
the paper's d=4 scaled action A/g is g X; its fixed moments tend
to zero, with

 E_beta[(A/g)^k] = g^k[(3/2)_k+o(1)]

for every fixed nonnegative integer k. This follows from (3.4) on
a fixed real source neighborhood below J=1, or directly by the
same rescaled Haar integral.

Consequently a uniform entire Gaussian source envelope for A/g
cannot be used as a uniform entire envelope for A/g^2. This exact
single-plaquette calculation does not contradict the theorem at
its stated scaling, nor does it assert a result for all correlated
four-dimensional plaquettes. It demonstrates the invalidity of
the proposed normalization-only shortcut.

Our separated source construction does not require an entire
spatially uniform log partition function. A renormalized mixed
remainder on an appropriate fixed disc could still be adequate.
Neither (3.4) nor Theorem 4 supplies that separated remainder.

4. WHAT THE CLASSICAL INFRARED BOUND ACTUALLY ASSUMES

Primary paper:
 J. Froehlich, B. Simon and T. Spencer,
 "Infrared Bounds, Phase Transitions and Continuous Symmetry Breaking",
 Communications in Mathematical Physics 50 (1976), 79--95.
 DOI 10.1007/BF01608557.
 Author-hosted full text:
 https://math.caltech.edu/SimonPapers/65.pdf

Saved work/remote69/FSS1976.pdf and text; printed p81 was visually
checked. Read the theorem and proof at printed pp81--84.

Section 2 assumes a product of identical single-site measures on
R^N, with the stated exponential integrability, and a nearest-neighbor
ferromagnetic bilinear energy

 H = -J sum_<x,y> sigma_x dot sigma_y.

Theorem 2.1 is a Gaussian domination estimate for a divergence source:

 E exp[sigma(sum_i partial_i h_i)]
       <= exp[(2J)^(-1) sum_(x,i) |h_i(x)|^2].             (4.1)

Theorems 2.2--2.3 and Corollary 2.5 extract covariance quadratic-form
bounds. The proof rewrites the interaction as Gaussian difference
kernels, shifts those kernels, and uses a positive-definite
kernel/transfer-operator inequality and Schatten Hoelder. It is not
an inference from reflection positivity alone. Printed p84 explicitly
cautions that the resulting quadratic-form estimate does not imply
a pointwise covariance upper bound by the lattice Green function.

This is closely related to the successful deterministic ambient
Gaussian-feature shift in TURN65. That analogue is already proved
for the actual Wilson temporal kernels. It does not turn a nonlinear
magnetic observable into a deterministic linear source.

The pure Wilson pushforward to plaquette actions does not have the
product-site/bilinear-spin structure above. Plaquettes share links,
and their products obey compatibility constraints. Neither merely
renaming plaquettes as spins nor taking a small-field chart supplies
the source-maximization theorem used in (4.1).

Non-Abelian symmetry by itself is not an obstruction to (4.1):
O(N) examples are included. The issue is the exact measure, energy,
and source deformation, and the distinction between linear and
composite observables.

Two elementary checks make this last distinction precise.

(a) Linear sub-Gaussian domination does not supply Wick's quadratic
cross identity. Let R be 0 or sqrt(2), each with probability 1/2,
and let independent fair signs e_1,e_2 be independent of R.
Put Y_i=R e_i. Then

 E Y_i=0, Cov(Y_1,Y_2)=0, E Y_i^2=1,
 E exp(t_1Y_1+t_2Y_2) <= exp(t_1^2+t_2^2),
 Cov(Y_1^2,Y_2^2)=1.                                    (4.2)

The MGF bound follows by conditional cosh bounds and R^2<=2.
Thus an upper Gaussian proxy with zero off-diagonal block does
not imply zero quadratic cross covariance. The TURN30 determinant
identity needs the law itself to be Gaussian or a separately
controlled connected four-point remainder.

(b) Even a Hessian lower bound cannot be polarized into a
separation-sensitive covariance formula. Consider the centered
two-dimensional Gaussian with precision

 H = [[2,1],[1,2]] >= I.

For F(x)=x_1 and G(x)=x_2, Cov(F,G)=-1/3, whereas
grad(F) dot I^(-1) grad(G)=0. A valid variance bound using a
Hessian lower bound can be combined with Cauchy--Schwarz, but
its right side cannot simply be polarized into an off-diagonal
upper bound. This is not a defect in TURN11; it rules out an
unproved additional step sometimes proposed after convexity.

new marked bridge bounds remove a particular large-action connection

  log Z(s,t)-log Z(s,0)-log Z(0,t)+log Z(0,0),
