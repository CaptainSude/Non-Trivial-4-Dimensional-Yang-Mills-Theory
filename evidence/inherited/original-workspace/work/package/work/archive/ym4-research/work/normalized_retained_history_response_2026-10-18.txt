1. WHICH CONDITIONAL LAW ACTUALLY TRANSPORTS THE MARK

At a fixed positive source-free history write the incoming measure as
  nu(dx,dy)=m(dx)P_x(dy).
The actual R replacement is m(dx)K_x(dy'), where K_x is its normalized
reference fill, including the original cutoff, gauge factors and real
denominator. Its support can lie wholly inside the wider analytic tube
a=g log^q(g^-2). This does not imply that P_x has that support.

Use nilpotent labels and write the incoming marked density as
  nu[1+e1 F1+e2 F2+e1 e2 V12],  V12=F12+F1 F2.
The exact output coefficients are
  F_a^+=P_x F_a,       V12^+=P_x V12,
  F12^+=P_x F12+Cov_(P_x)(F1,F2).                    (1)
They are independent of the new draw y'. The same formulas hold for a
whole frozen prefix by using its actual positive joint process once.
At zero output density the corresponding marked numerators vanish;
no division at such a point is needed.

Jensen therefore gives ||F_a^+||_p<=||F_a||_p and
||V12^+||_p<=||V12||_p for the respective normalized actual marginals.
In particular a GLOBAL incoming bound |F_a|<=M_a, |F12|<=M12 gives
  |F12^+|<=M12+2 M1 M2.                              (2)
This is one application to the full prefix, not a factor C per step.
It would preserve polynomial weak-coupling bounds if those bounds held
on all incoming histories. Our original-tube estimates presently hold
on regular bulk fields, not on the retained cores of every live history.

The distinction cannot be removed by the narrow reference support.
For example retain a hidden variable h=+/-1 with equal probabilities,
replace all current coordinates by a draw in [-a,a], and insert
F1=F2=L h. The exact output first marks remain L h and their global
connected covariance is L^2 for any L. The reference fill and its
normalization place no bound on L. This tests the proposed inference,
not the actual Yang--Mills activity bounds.

The local source-vacuum subtraction must also be kept in (1).
Positivity of the unrenormalized insertion does not make its subtracted
conditional response positive. In particular our separated S0 source
bound supplies no uniform L2 bound for a single F^2 packet: repeating
that packet introduces collision diagonals. Cauchy--Schwarz using that
unavailable repeated-source bound would not be a valid shortcut.

2. A LITERAL POSITIVE REWARD THAT DOES NOT CHANGE OLD BULK VACUA

Use the actual positive endpoint history before Mayer/connected-log
localization. For each LIVE terminal component C, open its unresolved
ancestry and retain its original own-scale action debit nu_C. One may
take the larger cost ledger used in CMP122-II (1.79), namely the sum of
fixed M^d R_j^(d+1) d'_j(Z_j) contributions over the unresolved component
ancestry. Each occurrence is counted exactly once in that ledger.
It dominates the associated own-scale volumes in (1.69).

Set D(h)=sum_(C live) nu_C. Do not charge already healed bulk R' births,
their vacuum constants, or all past bad events. D is nonnegative,
depends only on the real history labels, and is a genuine random
variable under the positive endpoint law. It is not a signed polymer
activity or the mass of the majorant gas.

Choose a fixed t0>0 within an UNUSED lifetime/action reserve. Concretely,
reserve the coefficient t0 of the above cost ledger when making the
strict source inequalities (1.79)--(1.88). Equivalently the O(1)
coefficient of that ledger in the real upper bound may be enlarged by
t0 while preserving the original conclusions. This is a fixed source
constant choice, independent of UV depth, torus volume and history.
No assertion is made for a charge larger than this actual ledger.

For 0<=t<=t0 multiply the REAL terminal history weight by exp(tD(h)).
All old reference inverses, regular bulk coefficients and their vacuum
constants remain unchanged. On each opened live component this merely
adds t nu_C to the same cost bound. Source CMP122-II pp379--387 first
bounds non-Gaussian terms by an own-scale volume cost (1.69), bounds
the normalization cost by O(M^d R_j^(d+1)d'_j(Z_j)), then pays these
costs by the lifetime induction (1.79)--(1.88). With the reserve just
specified, the final (1.89) bound, including its spatial decay, remains
valid with the reserved constants. This works before any signed
exponentiation and therefore bounds the marked positive history sum.

The absolute last-scale proof CMP119 (2.43)--(2.50) uses precisely those
component bounds and the unchanged real action bounds. Apply that same
proof, without running a new RG map, to obtain
  Z_live(t)=sum_h integral exp(tD(h)) w_h
               <=exp(E_+^* V_stop),  0<=t<=t0.        (3)
The original positive lower floor is
  Z_live(0)=Z(0)>=exp(-E_- V_stop).                     (4)
E_+^*,E_- are independent of UV depth and ambient torus volume.
They may depend on the endpoint coupling and fixed source constants.
The printed Corollary 3 does not by itself supply a stated polynomial
weak-coupling dependence of these constants; none is inferred here.

This is a finite endpoint insertion in the existing positive
representation. It introduces no new ultraviolet vacuum subtraction.
Rewarding every historical bad birth would be a different construction:
then healed bulk pressures and their vacuum constants change, and
(3) with unchanged normalization is not automatic.

3. NORMALIZED FIRST MOMENT AND WHAT TRANSLATION COVARIANCE GIVES

Let mu be the normalized endpoint history law. Convexity, or Jensen,
gives
  t0 E_mu D <= log E_mu exp(t0 D)
             =log Z_live(t0)-log Z_live(0)
             <=(E_+^*+E_-)V_stop.                     (5)
This is a normalized conclusion; there is no factor exp(CV_stop).

Assign each primitive own-scale debit to an endpoint cell containing
its location, using the actual compatible scaffold, and write
D=sum_z d_z with d_z>=0. Use translations preserving that scaffold;
these suffice. On a torus tiled by its common cells, covariance yields
  E_mu d_z <= C_cell (E_+^*+E_-)/t0.                  (6)
Here d_z denotes the charge in one common cell and C_cell is its
fixed endpoint volume. Consequently for deterministic nonnegative
cell weights f,
  E_mu sum_z f(z)d_z
       <=C_cell(E_+^*+E_-)/t0 sum_z f(z).             (7)
This proves a useful local FIRST moment for precisely this debit.
It does not say that the whole charge of a component intersecting a
cell equals that cell's assigned charge. Such an enlargement needs
its actual carrier/diameter weight and cannot be made silently.

4. THE PRECISE SECOND-RESPONSE INTERFACE STILL MISSING

Equations (3)--(7) do not imply a local second moment. On a torus with
V cells take one uniformly random cell Z and d_z=V 1_(z=Z). Then
D=V deterministically and log E exp(tD)=tV, while
E d_z=1 and E d_z^2=V. Thus global pressure and translation invariance
alone permit arbitrarily large local second moments.

For a live component with positive operator T_C and marked action
L_C(z), the exact real connected response is
  partial_12 log[T_C exp L_C(z)] at 0
   = E_(T_C)[partial_12 L_C]
      +Cov_(T_C)(partial_1 L_C,partial_2 L_C).        (8)
The denominator in this formula is T_C 1. A pointwise score bound
|partial_a L_C|<=C nu_C gives only a covariance bound C nu_C^2.
Small current coordinates and real positivity do not improve that
quadratic debit to a linear one.

A useful inner-component next interface is a normalized
connected estimate for the actual opened live operator,
  |partial_12 log[T_C exp L_C]| <= P(g^-1) nu_C
times the two actual endpoint profile/localization weights, with all
frozen reference factors and original source-vacuum terms present.
Alternatively one needs a volume-uniform LOCAL second debit moment
or component-tail bound strong enough for the nu_C^2 term. The bound
must use the actual normalized law; absolute T majorants alone do not
supply it. For a component-by-component assertion the same exterior
and same T_C denominator must be used.

This component estimate alone is NOT sufficient. After all component
integrations write the exact remaining density as
  w_h exp[z1 A1(h)+z2 A2(h)+z1 z2 A12(h)+...].
The complete normalized mixed logarithmic derivative is
  E_mu A12 + Cov_mu(A1,A2).                           (9)
The first term contains the inner-component connected responses in
(8). The second also contains correlations of first responses in
DISTINCT components, mediated by the outer current/history law.
Equation (7) integrates a linear-debit bound for the first term only.
It supplies no bound for that outer covariance.

The sufficient next interface must therefore bound the COMPLETE
connected normalized response in (9), including its outer covariance,
by P(g^-1) times a source-local FIRST debit functional whose weights
have total mass O(R_N^4). Equivalently, supplement the inner-component
estimate with a normalized outer correlation estimate for the first
marks, or with local second-moment/tail bounds strong enough for their
products. Only then can (7) remove the global-volume loss and the
endpoint R_N^4 factor pay a fixed polynomial weak-coupling cost.

The present argument establishes the exact contraction and the live-
debit first moment. It establishes neither the complete connected
second-response bound nor the LF nontriviality contribution.
