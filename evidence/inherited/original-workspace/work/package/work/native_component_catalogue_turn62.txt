4. ERASING A COMPLETE NATIVE RAW COMPONENT

Take one actual term of (2.1) with a first-class component X.
Erase its COMPLETE private raw record. At each old date remove
the restricted raw pieces belonging to that current component,
and reconstruct the old complementary domains as unions of the
remaining pieces and the fixed outer records. Do not modify the
fixed E/R coefficient functions already produced by earlier healed
regions.

This is an operation on the ALREADY INTEGRATED native representation.
It is not a map which fills a new microscopic field into X and then
tests that field against all deleted past small-field predicates.
Former regular integrations and their tests have already produced
the fixed regular coefficients. In the displayed representation their
completed contribution is in A'_k and the residual coefficient
functions; there is no additional product of unintegrated old

The geometric checks are as follows.

4A. No surviving old component is cut.

The native raw operations are organized by current connected
components. B119(2.19)--(2.22) factor them into commuting current
component operations and then into ordered date operations
restricted to the same component. Large-I(1.1) uses that exact
factorization when selecting the components on which R acts.

A still-raw ancestral component cannot straddle two distinct
current components. Successive covering/layer operations contain
their input and merging joins complete components. If a prior R
step healed a component, it removed the whole corresponding raw
lineage and filed its coefficient in the regular action; that
already-filed coefficient is not part of the current erased raw
record. A retained component likewise remains complete.

Thus deleting X removes complete ancestral raw pieces. The
restriction of every surviving history to every surviving
current component is unchanged.

4B. Union/layer reconstruction remains admissible.

The native no-new-field operation S is the smallest cover by
the next prescribed cells followed by ten layers. Large-II p384
expressly uses S(union D_i)=union S(D_i). Both the cover and
fixed-layer operation have this property. Therefore removing
one entire current lineage does not change the reconstructed
regions of the other lineages.

Fresh domains and their complete deciding records in the erased
lineage are removed together. Other fresh domains remain as
before. Any merger which joined two retained raw pieces into
one current component belonged to a SINGLE current lineage;
it cannot be half-erased by removing a different current
component. Componentwise small hull/rectangle conventions are
likewise unchanged on every surviving component.

Nestedness and the prescribed cell alignment persist under
this restriction. Boundary collars surrounding surviving
components are unchanged; boundaries belonging only to the
erased lineage disappear. This is deletion of a whole final
raw component, not deletion of an arbitrary subset of its
fine cells or of one of its pre-merger ancestors.

4C. Eligibility is unchanged on surviving components.

Large-I p177 selects R components by their own size (containment

fields during the prescribed preceding N steps, with the
corresponding earlier size conditions. Both are unchanged for

In the source term X was already first-class and had already

6. WHY FIXING THE OLD TOTAL REGION WOULD BE WRONG

If one fixed the OLD eligible set Z, its components would already
be present. The subsequent first/second-class choice is a binary
partition of their weights. Small total raw mass would not imply
that first-class status is conditionally rare. For instance weights
epsilon and epsilon^2 for two branches give first-class probability
1/(1+epsilon), although the first branch's unnormalized mass is
epsilon.

That diagnostic is not a counterexample to the native bound. It
explains why the fixed datum must be the NEW outer datum in (1.72),
and why erasure must be allowed to change the old large-field
catalogue. The representation inspected above has exactly that
ordering.
