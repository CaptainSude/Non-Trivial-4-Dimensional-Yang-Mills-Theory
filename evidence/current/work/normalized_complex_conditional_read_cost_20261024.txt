RESULT

There is a direct finite normalized-expectation bound which avoids an
exponential seed price on a sufficiently small, read-dependent complex
tube. It requires a bound on the TOTAL phase-gradient energy under the
positive real-part tilt. With a Schur-bounded score-gradient kernel and
h changed parameters, the sufficient radius is c/(K sqrt(h)), subject
to the original action and source-image margins. Fixed-order Cauchy
prices are then polynomial in h. This is a smaller tube, not a bound
on the old fixed native pair domain.

Real positivity, strong convexity, finite-range dependence, even cuts,
and a small complex Hessian perturbation do NOT themselves bound the
complex normalized functional on bounded tests polynomially in h.
An exact independent centered Gaussian example gives exp(c h rho^2).
For holomorphic tests the available analytic domain matters: that
bounded-test example alone does not refute an improved bound for the
native wide occupied-noise source ball. A separate narrow-strip example
does refute an unrestricted positivity-to-holomorphic-norm inference.

The remaining native question is precise: can the actual complex
R-marginal be represented, or bounded on holomorphic source functions,
inside their full source domain with polynomial read cost on the
DESIRED output radius? Existing occupied Gaussian cancellation gives a
small exponential body-volume price; it does not by itself prove this
stronger statement. No second-step source-image inclusion is supplied
by the present note.

1. EXACT PHASE IDENTITY AND THE SOURCE MARGINAL

Let K be a fixed finite real product cut, dxi its fixed reference
measure, and H_z its complete holomorphic exponent, real for real z.
All Haar/pivot/coarea densities must be included, or equivalently kept
as a holomorphic density with a fixed nonzero branch on this chart.
Assume the finite integrals exist. No source-dependent normalizer or
cut is introduced. Put

 Z(z)=integral_K exp(-H_z) dxi,
 Q_z F=Z(z)^-1 integral_K F_z exp(-H_z) dxi.

At a fixed complex z define the POSITIVE probability

 nu_z(dxi)=exp(-Re H_z(xi)) dxi / Z_abs(z),
 Theta_z(xi)=Im H_z(xi),
 c_z=nu_z exp(-i Theta_z).

Then Z(z)=Z_abs(z)c_z, and, whenever c_z is nonzero,

 Q_z F=nu_z[F_z exp(-i Theta_z)]/c_z.                    (1)

A field-independent complex scalar cancels from (1) exactly. Its
original outer-law factor must still be retained in the full ledger.

The norm on ALL bounded measurable complex tests is EXACTLY

              ||Q_z||_(Linf -> C)=1/|c_z|.              (2)

The upper bound is immediate; F=exp(i Theta_z) attains it. There is
no positivity assertion about Q_z itself.

If tests read only independent coordinates R, disintegrate nu_z over
xi_R and put b_z(xi_R)=nu_z[exp(-i Theta_z)|xi_R]. Then the exact norm
on arbitrary bounded R-tests is

 C_R(z)=nu_(z,R)|b_z| / |nu_(z,R)b_z|.                 (3)

In particular C_R<=1/|c_z|. Phases independent of R can cancel from
this ratio; one must not replace an ambient volume by |R| without
proving such cancellation or a bound on the actual marginal b_z.
Formula(3) does not require b_z to have a zero-free logarithm.

For a holomorphic source F on its own domain D, with sup_D|F|<=M,
these inequalities apply whenever its COMPLETE actual image Psi_z(K)
lies in D. They only use the resulting pointwise cap on K. The norm
on this smaller holomorphic class can be strictly less than (2)/(3).

2. THREE SUFFICIENT FINITE CRITERIA

(a) Phase oscillation. If some scalar theta has

       |Theta_z(xi)-theta|<=a<pi/2 on K,

then |c_z|>=cos(a) and |Q_z F|<=M/cos(a). More generally an interval
of phase length 2a suffices. This costs total phase oscillation, not
the supremum of the source alone.

(b) Complete log-density oscillation. Relative to a positive real
center z0, suppose after subtracting one scalar that

             sup_K |H_z-H_z0-c(z)|<=delta<log(2).

Then |Z(z)e^{c(z)}/Z(z0)|>=2-exp(delta) and

             |Q_z F|<=M exp(delta)/(2-exp(delta)).       (4)

The proof is |exp(-w)-1|<=exp(delta)-1 and the triangle inequality.
This is the previously used direct finite density-ratio argument.
If one complete physical boundary score has cap C beta, h independently
changed boundary coordinates give delta<=C beta h rho and hence a
sufficient radius O((beta h)^-1). No inverse global cut mass appears.

(c) Phase variance. Center Theta_z by its nu_z mean. Since
cos(u)>=1-u^2/2,

 |c_z|>=1-Var_(nu_z)(Theta_z)/2.

Consequently, for v<2,

 Var_(nu_z)(Theta_z)<=v
    implies Z(z)!=0 and |Q_z F|<=M/(1-v/2).             (5)

This is an exact finite statement, with no cumulant truncation. The
positive law in (5) is nu_z, not the original law at Re z. Its actual
real-part exponent and cutoff must be used.

Suppose additionally nu_z has Poincare constant c0^-1, uniformly on
the complex parameter set being tested. Then

 Var_(nu_z)(Theta_z)<=c0^-1 nu_z |grad_xi Theta_z|^2.     (6)

For convex product balls a uniform lower bound on the full real-part
action Hessian is one sufficient source of this Poincare inequality.
No complex normalized probability is used in its proof. A theorem
only for the positive law at REAL parameters is not automatically a
theorem for nu_z; that extension must be checked separately.

3. A POLYNOMIAL READ-PRICE COROLLARY ON A SMALLER TUBE

Fix a real parameter center a and a finite changed parameter set T,
|T|=h. Leave all other parameters fixed at their actual real values.
Let z=a+Delta, with max_q|Delta_q|<=rho. Assume throughout that tube:

 * the same K and complete density chart are valid;
 * the source's complete actual image lies in its own bounded domain;
 * nu_z has the Poincare constant in(6);
 * for a nonnegative matrix K_iq,

   ||partial_(xi_i) Theta_z||_(L2(nu_z))
                    <=sum_(q in T) K_iq |Delta_q|,
   ||K||_(ell2 -> ell2)<=K_* .                         (7)

An exponentially decaying mixed-score kernel with uniform row and
column sums is sufficient for the last operator bound. Equation(7)
can be proved by integrating the COMPLETE mixed action derivatives
along the parameter segment, provided the L2 bounds are uniform for
the final nu_z (or have a proved uniform comparison to that law).
Minkowski does not by itself change the integration measure from one
point of the segment to another.

It follows directly that

 nu_z |grad Theta_z|^2<=K_*^2 ||Delta||_2^2
                         <=K_*^2 h rho^2.

Thus rho<=sqrt(c0)/(K_* sqrt(h)) gives v<=1 in(5) and

                         |Q_z F|<=2M.                  (8)

Intersect this radius with every source-image, real/complex chart,
minimum-response and actual action margin. Those margins may be the
smaller restriction. The case h=0 is trivial.

Holomorphy and Cauchy on a fixed smaller fraction of this tube now give
for each fixed total derivative order k

 |partial^k Q_z F|<=C_k M [K_* sqrt(h)/sqrt(c0)]^k,       (9)

with the other inverse margins included when they dominate rho.
This is a genuine polynomial-in-read FIXED-JET estimate, not an
analytic bound on an h-independent radius. The source can itself
depend holomorphically on z; (8) uses its uniform cap, so no missing
direct-source derivative is dropped in (9).

Coordinate-scaled versions are immediate: replace h rho^2 by the
actual squared ell2 parameter displacement. This can be considerably
smaller than a box bound for a localized perturbation.

The analogous result for R-local tests without an ambient parameter
count needs (7) for an ACTUAL reduced marginal phase, or another bound
on(3). Defining -log b_z would require a zero-free conditional b_z;
this is an extra hypothesis, not a consequence of global Z(z)!=0.

4. BETA, NOISE UNITS AND WHAT THE RADIUS MEANS

Two scalings must not be conflated.

For a physical-coordinate strongly convex law with Hessian of size
beta, a physical linear score of size beta rho gives phase-gradient
energy beta^2 h rho^2. Division by the Poincare gap beta gives a
sufficient phase variance O(beta h rho^2). Thus the variance criterion
allows rho=O(beta^-1/2 h^-1/2)=O(g h^-1/2), rather than the conservative
oscillation radius O(beta^-1 h^-1). The same calculation in normalized
noise x, where physical noise is g x, has gap O(1) and phase force
O(rho/g); the answer is unchanged.

After the WHOLE stationary linear cancellation, a parameter may enter
only the precision and higher primitive. If the complete normalized
mixed score instead has L2 size O(rho) per coordinate, the sufficient
radius is O(h^-1/2), with no inverse g. This stronger scaling requires
that actual mixed-score estimate. One must not assign it to an
uncanceled physical current, boundary term or change of coordinates.

The registered first primitive small-mean/centered-moment bounds are
useful inputs to such L2 estimates; they do not remove the sum over h
independent phase contributions. Nor do they alone prove (7) for the
positive real-part law at every complex output pair.

5. EXACT FAILURE OF A GENERIC POSITIVITY IMPLICATION

Take the normalized centered Gaussian on the ORIGINAL symmetric cut
[-r,r], with r>0 fixed, and its n-fold product. Write X for one scalar
coordinate. For a sufficiently small fixed nonzero epsilon put

 H_epsilon(x)=sum_i x_i^2/2 - i epsilon sum_i x_i^2.

Its real-part Hessian is exactly the identity, the cut is even, the
range is zero, the complex Hessian perturbation is as small as desired,
and there is no linear primitive. Let

                 m(epsilon)=E exp(i epsilon X^2).

Since Var(X^2)>0,

 log |m(epsilon)|=-epsilon^2 Var(X^2)/2+O(epsilon^4).

For small fixed epsilon, m is nonzero and has modulus below1. The
normalized complex functional on bounded tests therefore has norm

                |m(epsilon)|^-n=exp(c_epsilon n).       (10)

The phase-canceling bounded test attains it. The source noise read is
the n coordinates themselves, so this is not an ambient-volume error.
It proves that real positivity, centered cuts, convexity and small
complex quadratic perturbation do not imply a polynomial bounded-test
norm. Even exact unmarked Gaussian normalization does not erase(10).

CAUTION: this does NOT prove the same lower bound on the native bounded
HOLomorphic source class. The phase-canceling entire function has a
large supremum on a wide complex noise ball; normalizing by that sup
may erase the growth. This distinction is essential here.

There is nevertheless a simple holomorphic counterexample if no width
compatibility is imposed. Use instead H_t=sum x_i^2/2-i t sum x_i and
m1(t)=E exp(i t X). For small fixed t>0, m1(t)>0 and

                 -log m1(t)=sigma_r^2 t^2/2+O(t^4).

On the product strip |Im z_i|<a, define

                 F_n(z)=exp(-i t sum z_i-n t a).

It is entire and has modulus at most1 on that strip (also on its
intersection with any larger real-coordinate box). But

                 Q_t F_n=exp[-n t a] m1(t)^-n.

Choose a=sigma_r^2 t/4 and then t small. The result grows exponentially
in n. All denominators are nonzero, the real law is strongly convex,
and the source is bounded holomorphic on its declared domain.

This does not model the verified first cavity entry, whose source has
a much wider occupied-noise ball than its real cut. It identifies the
missing logical implication: source holomorphy without a quantitative
contour/domain compatibility condition is not enough.

6. WHAT EXACT CONTOUR CANCELLATION COULD ADD

A sufficient analytic-source criterion stronger than(5) is an exact
positive representation supported inside the source domain: if

 Q_z F = integral F(Phi_z(y)) dnu(y) + R_z(F),

where nu is a probability, Phi_z(y) lies in the source's holomorphic
domain, and |R_z(F)|<=epsilon M, then |Q_z F|<=(1+epsilon)M. This
criterion concerns the action, contour, source image and boundary
terms together. It must be proved for the SAME original cut/normalizer.

For a full Gaussian, an admissible contour translation/rotation can
give an exact such representation. A hard product cut adds surface
terms. Existing occupied-body/contour estimates bound a collection of
marked surfaces and determinant/primitive losses by small factors
PER occupied block. Summing them gives, in general, an upper bound of
the form exp(c_small |S|), or (1+epsilon_site)^|S|. This is polynomial
only in a range such as c_small |S|<=C log(2+|S|). That upper bound is
not asserted sharp; it simply does not prove a better global claim.

In particular one may not first drop the active source cut, use a full
Gaussian cancellation, and then omit the contour boundary terms. The
source must remain on all images actually used by that deformation.
For the verified first column, B116 keeps f(B) on protected REAL B
with chi_Y0; its unrestricted whitened exterior X never enters f.
This fact proves source admissibility for that construction, but is
not itself a new positive representation of its complex normalized
R-marginal on an arbitrary large carrier.
