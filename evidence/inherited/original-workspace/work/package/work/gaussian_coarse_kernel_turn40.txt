RESULT

For the explicit fixed-crossing box and block-local G0 forest of turn39,
the Gaussian fluctuation at fixed COMPLETE ordinary coarse datum has a
spectral gap and exponential covariance decay independent of the box side
R. L is fixed throughout. The exact minimum lift and coarse precision have
exponentially decaying kernels as well. The coarse precision is gauge
invariant and remains massless; its inverse is NOT asserted to have such
decay. These statements follow from a local cochain estimate and a finite-
range indefinite saddle matrix, rather than the polynomial gap of a global
forest or an imported covariance-decay claim.

The result is for the exact Gaussian tangent at the identity with actual
ordinary averaging. It supplies a concrete one-step spatial ingredient for
the next nonlinear calculation. It does not identify the non-Gaussian
effective action, supply its counterterms, or close an RG iteration.

1. PRECISE SPACES, NORMALIZATIONS AND GAUGE SCOPE

edges have both endpoints in K; all crossing/outside edges are fixed.
Every plaquette reading a free edge occurs in the quadratic Wilson action.
The exterior strip includes the complete two-block reads of every retained
ordinary coarse row J. At the flat tangent, variations of fixed links are
zero. Constants below depend on L, dimension and fixed color normalization,
but NOT R or the number of blocks.

Use the block-local forest F rooted at all active coarse centers AND all
geometric fine boundary vertices, exactly as in
gauge_saturated_coarea_turn39. Its paths have length <=C_L. Let X be the
real chord-coordinate space: extend a in X by zero on F and on fixed links.
Use counting l2 norms, with one fixed orthonormal Lie-algebra basis. All
operators below tensor with the color identity. Write d for the fine curl
including the complete conditional plaquette set, and choose the action
normalization so its quadratic form is

                  S_2(a)=1/2 <a,H a>,       H=d* d.       (1.1)

A fixed positive color/action constant can instead be retained in H.
Let Q:X->Y=R^(J times color) be the literal angle-to-angle derivative of
the original ordinary first-L mean. Its read range and l2 operator norm
are bounded by C_L. It is not replaced by straight-path decimation.

The compact whole-star inverse from native_interface_geometry_turn39 §5,

             QE=I,     ||E||_(2->2)<=C_L.                (1.2)

Both row and column sums are bounded: each owner-block star has fixed

2. A LOCAL RECONSTRUCTION ESTIMATE, WITH BOUNDARY LINKS RETAINED

There are finite-range linear maps R_d,R_Q, of range and row/column norms
bounded by C_L, such that on X

                      a=R_d d a+R_Q Q a.                (2.1)

In particular,

          ||a||_2^2 <= C_L (||da||_2^2+||Qa||_2^2).     (2.2)

Here is a proof which needs only the actual ordinary map's local read and
its differentiated endpoint covariance, Q d lambda=d_c lambda|roots.

Fix a fine chord e=(x,y). Its two local forest paths end at anchors A,B
within C_L of e. Choose a contractible cubical patch of diameter C_L which
contains these paths, the necessary one or two coarse-row reads, and any
required fixed exterior detour. Consider a field on this patch whose curl
is zero, whose relevant coarse Q rows are zero, and whose forest/fixed
edges are zero. On the patch it is d lambda. Forest edges imply
lambda(x)=lambda(A), lambda(y)=lambda(B).

If A=B the chord value vanishes. If A,B are centers in adjacent owner
blocks, the retained ordinary row gives lambda(B)-lambda(A)=0. If one
anchor is a geometric fine boundary point, join the other center to an
exterior coarse center by at most two retained rows. The fixed exterior
strip connects that center to the boundary anchor, and its variation is
zero. If both anchors are boundary points, the same fixed strip connects
them. Hence the chord value again vanishes. Outward plaquettes used to
close these short loops are in the ACTUAL action because they read a free
edge; wholly fixed plaquettes have zero curl automatically.

Thus the linear functional a_e vanishes on the kernel of the finite patch
map collecting curl, those Q rows, and forest/fixed edge values. Elementary
finite-dimensional row-space duality expresses it as a linear combination
of those data. Restrict to X and the last values vanish. There are only
finitely many patch/forest/boundary types for fixed L. Choose a coefficient
representation once for each type. Their norms are finite C_L. Each patch
has bounded overlap, so their aggregate maps have bounded row AND column
sums. This proves (2.1)--(2.2), uniformly for all box sizes, including the
finite small-box variants by enlarging C_L.

This proof is not a global Poincare inequality on a long tree. It does not
require a quantitative filling estimate growing with R. It does require
ALL the coarse rows, fixed edges, and exterior/action plaquettes just
specified; dropping a boundary row or strip would invalidate that argument.

On N=ker Q the constrained Hessian H_N=P_N H|N therefore satisfies

                  c_L I <= H_N <= C_L I.                (2.3)

Also H+Q*Q has the same uniform positive lower bound on X. The constrained
gap is compatible with a massless unconstrained field: its slowly varying
degrees of freedom have been retained in y.

3. EXACT CONSTRAINED GAUSSIAN AND SCHUR FORMULAS

Let G=(H+Q*Q)^(-1) and A=QGQ*. By (1.2), QQ*>=c_L I. Together with the
uniform bounds for H+Q*Q this gives c_L I<=A<=C_L I. Define

    E_min=GQ*A^(-1),
    C=G-GQ*A^(-1)QG,
    K_c=A^(-1)-I.                                      (3.1)

Then QE_min=I, QC=0, C Q*=0, and C on N is H_N^(-1). The unique minimum
on Qa=y is a_min=E_min y and

  1/2<a,H a>=1/2<y,K_c y>+1/2<xi,H_N xi>,
  a=E_min y+xi, xi in N.                               (3.2)

Indeed H E_min=Q*(A^(-1)-I), so the cross term against xi vanishes;
E_min*H E_min=K_c. In particular K_c is positive semidefinite. These
identities do not invert H on its gauge null space.

For the exact normalized fiber law with density proportional to
exp[-<a,H a>/(2g^2)] subject to Qa=y,

             E[a|y]=E_min y,
             Cov(a|y)=g^2 C.                           (3.3)

With Euclidean chord/data measures and the ordinary delta constraint,
the COMPLETE fiber integral equals

 (2pi g^2)^((dim X-dim Y)/2)
 [det(QQ*)]^(-1/2) [det H_N]^(-1/2)
                 exp[-<y,K_c y>/(2g^2)].               (3.4)

This follows from Euclidean coarea and translation by E_min y. Different
fixed linear coordinate measures multiply (3.4) by their fixed Jacobians.
The constant exponential-Haar values at the identity are likewise retained
when identifying a compact-law tangent. The determinant term is a real
vacuum/normalization term; it is not set equal to one. At this Gaussian
level it is independent of y. Nonlinear field-dependent determinants are
not covered by (3.4).

4. UNIFORM SPATIAL DECAY FROM THE ACTUAL SADDLE MATRIX

The most direct locality proof uses

                  M=[ H  Q* ; Q  0 ].                   (4.1)

It acts on fine chord and coarse-row indices with their actual positions.
It has finite range C_L and norm <=C_L. It is uniformly invertible:
given M(a,lambda)=(f,h), write a=Eh+v, v in N. Projection of the first
equation to N and (2.3) give
||v||<=C_L(||f||+||h||). Then Q*lambda=f-Ha and QQ*>=c_L I give
||lambda||<=C_L(||f||+||h||). No box-volume constant appears. Its inverse is

                   M^(-1)=[ C  E_min ; E_min*  -K_c ].   (4.2)

Choose M_0>=||M|| and sigma<=||M^(-1)||^(-1), both uniform. The convergent
operator-norm identity

 M^(-1)=M_0^(-2) sum_(n>=0) M(I-M^2/M_0^2)^n            (4.3)

has tail bounded C_L (1-sigma^2/M_0^2)^n. Each nth polynomial has range
at most (2n+1)C_L. For any two sets of fine/coarse indices A,B,

 ||1_A M^(-1) 1_B||_(2->2)
           <= C_L exp[-c_L dist(A,B)/L].                (4.4)

Absorb the fixed difference between C_L and L into c_L. The same bound
holds for C,E_min,K_c, with their respective fine/coarse locations. In
particular K_c is an exponentially local COARSE PRECISION, including its
boundary rows. Equation(4.4) is an operator bound between arbitrary sets,
not a volume-multiplied pointwise estimate. Pointwise bounds and the fixed
lattice growth then imply uniform exponentially weighted row/column sums
at a smaller exponent. Consequently E_min also maps bounded coarse data
to bounded fine means with a uniform C_L norm.

The proof tolerates real local plaquette weights bounded above and below
by positive constants: replace H by d*Wd. The same gap, saddle proof and
constants work with the ellipticity bounds included. This is a genuine
Gaussian inhomogeneous scalar-action extension, with Q unchanged. It does
not assert the nonlinear constrained Hessian equals d*Wd.

5. COARSE GAUGE INVARIANCE AND THE SECOND FOREST QUOTIENT

Let lambda_c vanish at exterior coarse anchors. Extend it to a fine gauge
parameter supported at the active coarse centers, then project d lambda
to the local G0 forest. Denote the resulting chord field by V lambda_c.
Endpoint covariance and exactness give

             d V lambda_c=0,
             Q V lambda_c=d_c lambda_c.                 (5.1)

It follows from the minimum characterization that

 K_c d_c=0,   E_min d_c lambda_c=V lambda_c.              (5.2)

Conversely zero coarse energy produces a flat fine minimizer. On the
contractible box with the complete fixed exterior strip, it is a gradient
whose potential is constant on that strip; after fixing that constant its
coarse datum is d_c lambda_c. Thus ker K_c is exactly the residual coarse
gauge space in this geometry. A torus would additionally require an explicit
harmonic/holonomy sector and is not silently included.

Choose the coarse rooted forest F_c used in turn39. Let J_c insert a
coarse chord vector q with zero on F_c. The quadratic action on the TRUE
fine-plus-coarse quotient is

                    1/2 <q,J_c* K_c J_c q>.             (5.3)

This precision is positive definite for the declared anchored box. Its
entries retain the exponential bound (4.4), because J_c is just a coordinate
insertion. The transformation from framed data to forest chord plus coarse
gradient coordinates is triangular with constant Jacobian one in the
standard oriented-edge normalization. At the original compact level both
gauge Haar integrals were exactly one. One must not instead integrate an
infinite Gaussian gauge volume and call it normalized.

The change from an arbitrary framed y to its q may involve long forest
paths. Accordingly the covariance of q, the covariance of unconstrained
fine fields, and sensitivities to arbitrary noninvariant coarse frames do
NOT follow from (4.4). The inverse of (5.3) need not have a uniform gap or
exponential decay. The coarse action retains long-wavelength physical
modes. Locality of its precision is the useful statement for iteration.

6. AN ACTUAL SPATIAL TWO-SOURCE ESTIMATE ON THE GAUSSIAN FIBER

Let K_A,K_B be real symmetric matrices supported on finite fine-edge sets
A,B, and let O_A=a^t K_A a, O_B=a^t K_B a. For the normalized Gaussian fiber
with mean mu=E_min y, Wick's identity gives EXACTLY

 Cov(O_A,O_B|y)
 =2g^4 Tr(K_A C K_B C)+4g^2 mu^t K_A C K_B mu.           (6.1)

Using (4.4),

 |Cov(O_A,O_B|y)|
 <=C_L g^4 ||K_A||_HS ||K_B||_HS exp[-2c_L dist(A,B)/L]
  +C_L g^2 ||K_A mu_A||_2 ||K_B mu_B||_2
                                  exp[-c_L dist(A,B)/L]. (6.2)

The mean term is essential and has not been replaced by a vacuum estimate.
For canonical g^-2 quadratic marks, divide (6.1)--(6.2) by g^4. If y=gz
with bounded z, mu/g=E_min z is uniformly bounded; each fixed-footprint
source then has a uniform exponential spatial bound. Curvature sources
are included by K=d*Kd with their actual finite stencil and normalization.

After writing a=E_min y+g xi, the covariance of xi is C. Any fixed finite
collection of local polynomial vertices with uniformly bounded coefficients
and bounded mu/g therefore admits the usual connected Gaussian Wick bound
with an exponential tree factor: each propagator is now an ACTUALLY proved
exponentially decaying kernel. Summation of finitely many internal vertices
retains exponential separation at a smaller exponent, by the weighted row
sums following (4.4). This statement is at each fixed polynomial order;
it does not sum the interacting series or control the actual compact
complement. Unlike the earlier unconstrained one-loop forest formula, its
spatial input has been established here rather than inferred from connected
graph combinatorics.

7. WHAT THIS DOES NOT FOLLOW FROM, AND WHAT IS STILL NEEDED

A polynomial global forest bound cR^-8<=H is insufficient to obtain (4.4).
A long axial tree can represent a link coordinate as a sum of curvature
increments along a path; even independent increments then give covariance
min(s,t), or min(s,t)-st/R with an endpoint constraint. Long forest gauge
coordinates can thus have long correlations despite local curvature input.
The uniform constrained gap here relies instead on the local G0 forest,
all fixed-L ordinary rows, and the exact boundary strip.

Likewise a small additive compact/Gaussian pressure error from turn39 does
not inherit (6.2). To pass to the actual coarse action one still needs the
nonlinear constraint graph, its Hessian and complete coarea density, the
signed interacting numerator with its normalizer, and the original compact
complement under the relevant exterior/coarse law. The determinant in (3.4)
is part of the Gaussian vacuum, and its nonlinear analogue cannot be omitted.
No renormalized physical-scale or later-history assertion is made.
