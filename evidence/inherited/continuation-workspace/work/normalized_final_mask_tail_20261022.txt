1. THE REFERENCE HAS A UNIFORM NORMALIZED DISPLACEMENT TAIL

Fix all source-free reference-deciding data r exactly as in the two-reset
ownership note. Fix one component and one gauge-tree/coordinate-average
label. The reference is

 q_r(dV') = Z_r^{-1} dV'|Lambda delta_G(V') chi(Lambda)
      exp[-g_k^{-2} A(zeta_0,U_{k,H}(V' V_Lambda(r)))].          (1)

Use the SAME actual V_Lambda as in LI Proposition1; it is not replaced by
a flat-boundary minimum. Write V'=exp(i g_k b) on the free bonds after the
tree deltas. Here b is the fresh reference coordinate, not the old B field
inside T'_k. LI(1.101) gives the physical logarithmic cut

          ||b||_infinity < T_out = M0 epsilon_k/g_k.           (2)

This is already a growing Gaussian-coordinate cut in the literal reference
definition; no correction of the disputed ordinary/preliminary tests is
needed to justify (2). In the native notation epsilon_k/g_k=A0 p0(g_k).
The number n of real free coordinates is bounded by a constant n_M, because
Lambda has side at most100M. All constants below may depend on the fixed
M, M0, group, and other native constants, but not on k, r, cutoff depth,
or the eligible component's outer size R_k.

On this cut, LII(1.2) expands the exponent as

 -g_k^{-2} A(zeta_0,U0) - (1/2)<b,D_r b> + E_r(b),            (3)

with the sign of the bounded linear/cubic terms absorbed into E_r. LII
(1.5)--(1.6) bound the residual linear term uniformly on the whole cut,
using the actual stationary U0 and separation of zeta_0's transition.
The cubic remainder is uniformly small for sufficiently weak g_k: its
bound is a fixed power of log(g_k^{-2}) times a positive power of g_k,
times the bounded size of Lambda. In particular one can arrange

                  sup_cut |E_r| <= C_M.                      (4)

LII(1.7)--(1.9) give, on the actual real gauge slice,

 <b,D_r b> >= lambda_M |b|_2^2,
 lambda_M = gamma0/[2d(100M)^5] > 0.                         (5)

The boundedness of D_r used immediately after(1.9) also supplies an upper
bound C_M on the unit ball, uniformly for the same admitted r. Only that
local upper bound is needed below.

The gauge deltas fix tree bond variables to the identity; they leave
product Haar measure on the remaining independent group variables. In
the logarithmic chart, its density is g_k^n times a product of sigma(g_k b).
It is strictly positive. Since |g_k b|<=M0 epsilon_k tends to zero, continuity
at the identity bounds the density relative to its value at zero between
two positive constants depending only on n_M. This checks positivity and
does not introduce an unaccounted gauge Jacobian.

For sufficiently weak g_k the Euclidean unit ball in b is inside (2),
because T_out=M0 A0 p0(g_k) tends to infinity. The unit ball therefore gives
a strictly positive lower bound c_M for the scaled integral in the SAME
denominator, after extracting exp[-A(zeta_0,U0)/g_k^2] and g_k^n sigma(0)^n.
The extracted factors cancel between numerator and denominator. The numerator
on ||b||_infinity>=T is bounded by C_M times the corresponding full Gaussian
tail, using (4)--(5). Consequently

       q_r{||b||_infinity >= T} <= C_M exp[-c_M T^2]            (6)

for T>=1. When T exceeds the actual cut the event is empty. A zero-dimensional
fiber is also harmless. The finite gauge/coordinate average preserves(6).

This proves a normalized estimate with the denominator, cutoff and real
gauge measure all retained. The large boundary action A(zeta_0,U0)/g_k^2
does not have to be bounded: it cancels exactly. The estimate is about
DISPLACEMENT from U0, so it says nothing by itself about whether U0 passes
the final small-field tests.

2. THE EXACT FINAL TEST AND THE SOFT DICHOTOMY

For every factor a=(square,p) in the actual final chi_k(H^{~-6}), put

 F_a(v) = eta^{-2} [U_{k,square}(v,partial p)-1],

where U_{k,square} is exactly B119(2.16), with determining set
B_k(square^{~4}) and data M'(Q_k^*v); p ranges over square^~ as in(2.17).
The test requires ||F_a(v)||<epsilon_k for every a. This keeps the complete
determining arrays, endpoint reads and collars. It is NOT a direct test
of individual v plaquettes.

Let v0(r) equal V_Lambda(r) on Lambda and the actual frozen v outside.
Separate tests only by their COMPLETE raw-data read:

 I_fix = tests whose determining-data read is disjoint from Lambda;
 I_hit = all other tests.

This is a definition, not a claim that I_fix is nonempty. Even if raw v
plaquettes outside Lambda are fixed, their reconstructed F_a may depend
on Lambda; such a test belongs to I_hit. Depending on the exact geometry,
old chi_k may already force every I_fix test to pass. No contrary claim is
made here. In this notation there is the exact factorization

 pi_bad(r)=1-chi_fix(r) E_{q_r}[chi_hit].                       (7)

For the hit tests, take any certified physical radius a_*(r)>0, not exceeding
M0 epsilon_k, on which all their actual local reconstructions stay on their
admitted native branches under v|Lambda=exp(i h)V_Lambda, ||h||_infinity<=a_*.
Define the actual sensitivity

 K(r,a_*)=sup_{a in I_hit, ||h||_infinity<=a_*}
            ||D_h F_a(exp(i h)V_Lambda,r)||_(infinity->matrix).

Use a slightly smaller closed radius than the open admitted domain, so K is
finite. The norm is for physical h, not for b. Let

 m_hit(r)=min_{a in I_hit}[epsilon_k-||F_a(v0(r))||].

If I_hit is empty set m_hit=+infinity and K=0. For 1<=T and g_k T<=a_*,
the real mean-value estimate and(6) prove

 pi_bad(r) <= 1{chi_fix(r)=0 or m_hit(r)<=K(r,a_*) g_k T}
                       + C_M exp[-c_M T^2].                  (8)

Equation(8) is valid without an a priori strict minimum margin. It gives a
useful logarithmic tail on any subevent where m_hit>=c epsilon_k and
a_*>=c epsilon_k and K has the needed uniform bound. Such a uniform K is
NOT proved in this note. The ordinary padded-minimum result does not
automatically control every complete raw-coordinate read of these final
local tests, and the smooth homogeneous-inset theorem must not be applied
to a discontinuous Q_k^* fluctuation without its own input registration.
