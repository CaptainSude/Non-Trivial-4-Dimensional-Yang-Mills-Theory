GAUGE-COVARIANT FINITE-STEP REFERENCE AND THE REMAINING CARRIER PROBLEM
19 October 2026. TURN46. gauge46 research note.

SCOPE AND OUTCOME

The present weak-chart assumptions do not justify averaging an arbitrary
carrier over the full vertex gauge group. Nor is there a support-preserving
bounded projection of the needed kind with size-independent bounds. An
explicit SU(2) cycle example below quantifies the latter obstruction.
These are obstructions to particular deductions, not to gauge localization.

There is a useful constructive repair of the AUXILIARY REFERENCE itself.
Use relative link coordinates at the constrained saddle, its MOVING
orthogonal tangent space, the canonical right inverse of the ordinary
constraint, and independent Gaussian COLOR BALLS. Every object then
transforms by local adjoint rotations. The entire auxiliary integral and
the actual-versus-auxiliary tail are separately gauge invariant on their
common weak domains. The existing any-leg analytic catalogue is preserved
by the explicit analytic operations used below. The local cluster terms
obtained by the old Taylor/tree assignment need not be individually gauge
invariant. That last fact is kept separate throughout.

An additional norm-contracting global-color average does make every
carrier globally color invariant, with zero linear datum jet at identity,
without changing any source bound or the exact summed identity. This is
not a local Ward identity.

1. THE PRECISE RESIDUAL GAUGE ACTION ON THE FOREST SECTION

Use the actual G0 forest of intrinsic_energy_framed_reference_turn41:
its roots are the active coarse centers and the geometric boundary
anchors. G0 is the fine gauge group fixed at those roots, and its unique
forest representative has every forest link equal to I. The remaining

Assign a coarse gauge h(r) to each active root and I to every fixed
boundary root. Extend h to a fine vertex v by h(r(v)), where r(v) is
the root of its forest component. This is constant on each forest
component. Hence a forest link (v,w), whose endpoint roots coincide,
still equals I after the transformation. A chord link transforms as

 U_b -> h(r(b_-)) U_b h(r(b_+))^-1.                    (1.1)

This is the literal section-preserving lift; a block-constant lift
would be sufficient only when its blocks are these forest components.
No unstated identification of two different forests is used.

The complete ordinary coarse map transforms at its coarse endpoints.
The conditional action, its physical plaquette source, and the original
energy cutoff are invariant. Fixed exterior links remain fixed when
the lift fixes their endpoints; otherwise include the transformed exterior
as a datum and state the identity jointly in (Y,e). All statements below
use this latter convention whenever required by the geometry.

Let U_*(u) be the already established constrained saddle in this section.
For real h for which both the original and transformed candidates remain
in the common uniqueness neighborhood, (1.1) maps its constrained
stationarity equation to that for u^h. Uniqueness of the LOCAL KKT branch
then gives U_*(u^h)=U_*(u)^h. In the actual covered finite-box regime one
can equivalently transport the complete physical fiber minimum. No

It is enough for the local identities below to take h in a neighborhood

2. AN EXPLICIT EQUIVARIANT ORTHOGONAL CONSTRAINT CHART

The fine chord space is a direct sum of fixed-dimensional Lie-algebra
color spaces. Write a nearby field in LEFT relative coordinates,

                  U_b=exp(c_b) U_{*,b}(u).

The relative coordinate transforms exactly as c -> R_h c, where
R_h is block diagonal with block Ad(h(r(b_-))) on chord b. For real
compact gauges R_h is orthogonal in the invariant color inner product.
The coarse relative constraint is

 F_u(c)=log[M(exp(c)U_*(u))Y^-1],     Q_u=DF_u(0).

On the actual fiber F_u(c)=0. Its output action is another block diagonal
adjoint S_h, and the exact covariance identity is

 F_(u^h)(R_h c)=S_h F_u(c),    Q_(u^h) R_h=S_h Q_u.     (2.1)

The complete ordinary-row right inverse already proved in TURN39--42
implies a uniform lower bound for Q_u Q_u^t on a smaller real weak
domain. Its analytic inverse persists on a fixed smaller complex domain.
Define

 E_u=Q_u^t(Q_u Q_u^t)^-1,
 P_u=I-E_u Q_u,             T_u=ker Q_u.               (2.2)

For real u, P_u is the orthogonal projection onto T_u. For complex u,
transpose is the holomorphic BILINEAR transpose; P_u is an analytic
idempotent, not a Hermitian projection. The ranks remain fixed. Equation
(2.1) gives, for real gauges and hence as a local holomorphic identity,

 E_(u^h)=R_h E_u S_h^-1,       P_(u^h)=R_h P_u R_h^-1.  (2.3)

For b in T_u solve the literal constraint by

 c_u(b)=b+E_u tau_u(b),       F_u(c_u(b))=0.

The derivative of F_u in tau at (0,0) is I; its derivative in b is zero.
The analytic implicit equation therefore gives tau_u(b)=O(b^2), on a
fixed small sup domain. Its ambient extension replaces b by P_u b.
Uniqueness and (2.1)--(2.3) give

 tau_(u^h)(R_h b)=S_h tau_u(b),
 c_(u^h)(R_h b)=R_h c_u(b).                             (2.4)

All analytic operations here are quantitatively controlled by the
already used any-leg tree algebra. Q_u is the finite-read derivative
of the original ordinary map, composed with the local KKT branch. It
therefore has the full datum catalogue, including its two operator legs.
The inverse of Q_u Q_u^t is uniformly exponentially local by its gap;
its marked inverse series gives the same any-leg bounds. Multiplication,
the implicit equation for tau, and the entire local group exponential
then give a fixed-radius catalogue for c_u and its derivatives. This
uses the all-arity TURN41--42 inputs, not merely a physical C2 Hessian.

3. COMPLETE COAREA FACTOR AND MOVING COVARIANCE

The measure is unchanged by using this new chart. For real u, let
A_u(b):T_u -> fine chord tangent space be the derivative of
b -> exp(c_u(b))U_*(u), written in invariant orthonormal tangent frames.
Let Q_phi be the derivative of the complete ordinary map at that point,
also in invariant fine/coarse tangent frames. The exact coarea formula
relative to full coarse Haar is

 a_E(u)=int_(T_u) exp[-f_u(b)/g^2] J_u(b)
                         psi(f_u(b)/E0) db,

 f_u(b)=S_e(exp(c_u(b))U_*(u)),
 J_u(b)=c_geo [det_(T_u)(A_u(b)^t A_u(b))]^(1/2)
                           [det(Q_phi Q_phi^t)]^(-1/2).          (3.1)

c_geo retains the fixed normalized-Haar volume conventions. The chart
integral is over the complete supported fiber in the covered weak regime;
the previous coverage result still applies since this is an analytic
coordinate change about the same saddle with uniformly invertible tangent
map. The smooth cutoff is the same fixed real function of total energy.

At b=0, A_u(0) is the inclusion T_u -> ambient, so

 J_*(u)=c_geo det(Q_u Q_u^t)^(-1/2).                  (3.2)

Formula (3.1) is also an explicit analytic formula: embed the numerator
determinant as det[A^t A+I-P_u], and use the square-root branches fixed
by positivity on the real domain. At zero b this embedded matrix is I.
The denominator has its uniform gap. Its logarithm and the numerator
logarithm have local trace-series catalogues by the same marked-cycle
argument as the earlier complete Jacobian. Thus the full log J catalogue
is retained. A naked constant Euclidean fiber measure is NOT substituted
for (3.1).

Let H_u=D_b^2 f_u(0) restricted to T_u. The first derivative vanishes
by constrained stationarity. Its positive gap follows from the already
proved constrained Hessian and the bounded tangent coordinate change.
Extend H_u by zero on T_u^perp and set

 Aprec_u=H_u+(I-P_u),
 C_u=P_u Aprec_u^-1 P_u,
 K_u=P_u Aprec_u^(-1/2) P_u.                          (3.3)

The branch is the positive one on real data. This avoids taking a square
root across the moving zero eigenvalues. The established resolvent and
any-leg composition arguments give fixed-radius local analytic catalogues
for P_u,C_u,K_u, with the same type of volume-independent constants and
some fixed remaining localization reserve. On real data K_u maps a
standard ambient Gaussian to the intrinsic Gaussian on T_u, and

 K_u^t H_u K_u=P_u.

Every factor in (3.1)--(3.3) is equivariant under R_h; determinants are
invariant. In particular J_(u^h)(R_h b)=J_u(b), H_(u^h)=R_h H_u R_h^-1,
and K_(u^h)=R_h K_u R_h^-1. The leading Gaussian density is gauge invariant
separately. Its value agrees with that in the old chart by ordinary
Gaussian change of variables; its coordinate factors need not separately
look the same before their Jacobians are multiplied.

4. PRODUCT COLOR BALLS GIVE A SEPARATELY INVARIANT AUXILIARY INTEGRAL

Let q=dim g and let n_ch be the number of ambient fine chord color blocks,
so m=q n_ch. Replace the old scalar cube by

             C_T={xi: |xi_b|_g <=T for every chord b}.

Its reference law mu_T is the product of normalized q-dimensional Gaussian
balls, and c_(q,T)=Pr{|Z_q|<=T}. Its COMPLETE cutoff normalization is
c_(q,T)^(n_ch), not a power of dim T_u and not one. Redundant Gaussian
directions have still been retained.

Define, exactly as in TURN45 but in the new chart,

 V_z(xi,u)=-[f_u(zK_u xi)-s(u)-z^2 xi^t P_u xi/2]/z^2
                    +log[J_u(zK_u xi)/J_*(u)].

The apparent z=0 singularity is removable. Real compact gauges preserve
each color ball and its Gaussian measure, and (2.4), (3.3) give

              V_z(R_h xi,u^h)=V_z(xi,u).               (4.1)

The physical source sum sum_p w_p h_p is invariant under the same map.
Hence a change of the FIXED real Gaussian variables proves exact gauge
invariance of the full auxiliary density and its normalized source log.
No sharp complex cutoff has been moved. Local complex gauge identities
follow from holomorphic uniqueness, initially proved for real h near I.

The entire bounded-potential cluster proof still applies: cells now carry
q coordinates, where q is fixed; each scalar coordinate is bounded by T;
disjoint cells are independent; and simultaneous xi -> -xi preserves the
product law. The any-leg catalogues above therefore give a local analytic
logarithm F(z,u)=sum_X F_X(z,u), with F_X even in z and

                         ||F(z,.)||_kappa <=C|z|^2 T^6.

The whole F is gauge invariant. The Taylor/tree assignment used to obtain
the F_X is not itself equivariant under local coarse gauges, so individual
F_X are NOT claimed gauge invariant.

The pure-tail comparison of TURN45 survives this replacement. The Gaussian
union bound is now

 Pr(C_T^c)<=C n_ch (1+T)^q exp(-T^2/2),

and C_T lies in the same linear ball if n_ch T^2<<Lambda^2. The linear
and Morse comparison requires an additional step because the tangent
space moves. In particular, the indicator |P_u xi|<=R must NOT be used
as a complex-parameter integration domain. Here is a fixed-domain repair.

Let P_0=P_(u=0). On a smaller common datum domain set

 W_u=P_u P_0+(I-P_u)(I-P_0),
 O_u=W_u [I-(P_u-P_0)^2]^(-1/2).

The convergent branch near I satisfies O_u^t O_u=I, det O_u=1,
P_u O_u=O_u P_0, and ||O_u-I||<=C||u||. These algebraic identities
hold for symmetric analytic idempotents with the bilinear transpose;
on real u this is a real orthogonal matrix. The functions are analytic
and bounded by the same resolvent/power-series argument. O_u is used
only to justify a contour comparison, not as a new covariant carrier.

First compare the product-color-ball integral with the integral on the
FIXED FULL AMBIENT real ball |xi|<=R, R=c Lambda. Their common integrand
is e^{-|xi|^2/2+V_z(xi,u)}, and their difference is bounded by the color-
ball Gaussian union tail, exactly as in TURN45. The full ambient ball
contains the product of balls once n_ch T^2<R^2/4.

Next apply holomorphic Stokes in ambient dimension m to the two chains
xi in B_R and O_u xi, xi in B_R, using

 omega=(2pi)^(-m/2) exp[-xi^t xi/2+V_z(xi,u)] dxi_1...dxi_m.

The interpolating side is xi_t=[I+t(O_u-I)]xi with |xi|=R. On this
side Re(xi_t^t xi_t)>=R^2/2 after a fixed datum shrink, while
|V_z(xi_t,u)|<=C|z|R^3+CN|z|R=o(1). The homotopy stays in the entire
analytic fluctuation chart. Its volume and sphere factors are bounded
by exp(Cm) times a polynomial in R, so the side contribution is
O(exp(CN-cR^2)). The determinant at the final chain is exactly one;
no absolute-value substitute for a complex Jacobian is made.

In the final integral use the FIXED orthogonal decomposition
xi=eta+nu with eta in T_0 and nu in T_0^perp. The identities above give
K_u O_u nu=0 and (O_u xi)^t(O_u xi)=|eta|^2+|nu|^2. Thus V_z(O_u xi,u)
is independent of nu. Replace the full ball by the cylinder
|eta|<=R/2, nu arbitrary. The symmetric difference is bounded by a
Gaussian tail: on the ball part outside the cylinder, |eta|>R/2;
on the cylinder part outside the ball, |nu|>sqrt(3)R/2. The nonlinear
factor is uniformly bounded since it reads only eta, with |eta|<=R
on both parts. Hence this replacement also costs exp(CN-cR^2).
Integrating nu gives one, exactly. The remaining integral is the
linear whitened physical-tangent ball with fixed coordinate eta in T_0.

Apply the TURN45 fiber Stokes comparison to that last d-dimensional
ball, expressed in b=O_u b_0 coordinates. Its quadratic form is exactly
|eta|^2/2, its linear and Morse chains stay in the analytic chart, and
its side estimate is exp(CN-cR^2). The final radial comparison is the

energy t; the redundant normal variables never enter this cutoff.
The common energy interval cancels exactly, leaving the same Gamma

Combining these fixed-domain comparisons gives

 |R_E-R_aux|<=C n_ch(1+T)^q exp(-T^2/2)

with the previously stated exp(CN) division cost on the larger arbitrary-

Since the actual density and the new auxiliary density are separately

It is still a single finite-step statement, with the same energy window.

5. A SAFE CARRIERWISE GLOBAL-COLOR PROJECTION

Use color-ball datum domains, with invariant Euclidean norms on every Lie
algebra factor. They lie inside a fixed slightly larger scalar polydisc
after at most fixed color-dependent radius changes. Include all exterior
datum slots if they are not identity. For a local analytic carrier define

        P_col F_X(u,w)=int_G F_X(Ad_h u,w) dh.          (5.1)

This compact average stays INSIDE the declared domain. It is analytic,
does not enlarge the read set X, commutes with source and z derivatives,
and contracts its supremum norm. Therefore it contracts every rooted
exponential carrier norm. Apply it also to the tiny whole-box carrier.

For any total function already invariant under simultaneous conjugation,
sum_X P_col F_X=P_col(sum_X F_X)=sum_X F_X. Absolute convergence justifies
the interchange. Thus (5.1) preserves the exact source identity and all
established local/tail powers. At the all-identity datum, each projected
carrier has zero linear datum jet: every linear covector would be invariant
under the adjoint representation, which has no trivial subrepresentation
for a semisimple Lie algebra. This holds at each fixed scalar source w.

For a fixed nonidentity exterior that is NOT included as a transformed
slot, full simultaneous-conjugation invariance need not hold. In that
case (5.1) cannot silently replace the original total. Also (5.1) gives
no Ward identity for gauges varying from vertex to vertex.

6. WHY NAIVE LOCAL REYNOLDS AVERAGING DOES NOT FOLLOW

A single weak link U=I is sent by independent endpoint gauges to h_-h_+^-1.
For SU(2), choose h_-=I and h_+=-I. The transformed link is -I, outside
every sufficiently small logarithmic chart about I. Thus the integral
over all endpoint gauges asks for values that our analytic carriers do
not possess on their stated domain. Knowing that their SUM has a global
physical definition does not extend each summand there.

There is a quantitative obstruction even to a uniformly bounded,
support-preserving projection applied carrier by carrier. Consider a
single simple cycle of m edges with free vertex gauges, SU(2) links, and
the weak domain ||log U_e||<r, where 0<r is fixed and er<1. Put

             W(U)=1-(1/2)Tr(U_1...U_m).

This holomorphic class function is real on SU(2) and gauge invariant.
Expand each U_e=I+(U_e-I). The expansion of W is a finite sum W_S over
edge subsets S; the full-support term is

             W_E=-(1/2)Tr product_(e in E)(U_e-I),
                    ||W_E|| <=(e r)^m.              (6.1)

Every proper subset can be assigned to a proper connected path carrier
containing it (omit one missing cycle edge). Such a carrier is a forest.
A gauge-invariant analytic function supported only on a forest is
constant: its infinitesimal vertex gauge fields span all its link tangent
directions near identity. This local argument does not require taking a
large gauge transformation outside the chart.

Suppose a linear support-preserving family P_X produces gauge-invariant
carriers and preserves the sum of every gauge-invariant decomposition,
allowing a constant vacuum adjustment. All projected proper-path terms
are constant, so P_E W_E must reproduce all variation of W. Take all
U_e=exp(i theta sigma_3/m) with fixed 0<theta<1 and m>theta/r. This field
and identity are in the weak domain, and W changes by 1-cos(theta)>0.
Consequently

     ||P_E|| >= [1-cos(theta)]/[2(e r)^m].             (6.2)

Hence no size-independent norm bound is possible for this proposed
support-preserving projection. Its unavoidable exponential cost cannot
be paid from an unspecified small localization reserve. The example
does not rule out controlled support enlargement, a different covariant
construction, or the specialized Bałaban localization scheme.

7. INDEPENDENT BACKGROUNDS AND THE PUBLISHED LOCALIZATION THEOREM

With an independent background V, the relative decoupling

 U_e^s=V_e exp[s_e log(V_e^-1 U_e)]

is equivariant under simultaneous endpoint transformations of U,V, on
its common branch. Any alternating difference or anchored derivative
constructed from a gauge-invariant total then remains jointly invariant.
However a coefficient may still read V everywhere. Already the zero-
increment term of F(U)=sum_p f_p(U) is F(V), which retains all background
reads. A derivative coefficient of a general quasi-local F has the same
issue. Thus this operation is not a strict-support producer unless the
background dependence is separately localized. The independent current
variables in the native construction are not manufactured by this fact.

The relevant published result is Bałaban, CMP116 (1988), Lemma 2,
pp.10--11, (1.41)--(1.43), and its integration Lemma 3, p.20, (2.38),
with logarithmic estimate (2.41). It treats its original localized
exponent on the CMP109 orbit/current domains, including the restricted
reconstructions and its own operator walk deformations. The archived
primary-source interface audit is cmp116_interaction_bridge_research.md.
The primary publication metadata and abstract were rechecked this turn:
https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01239022
https://scholarship.libraries.rutgers.edu/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/Renormalization-group-approach-to-lattice-gauge/991031665969904646

That theorem is a genuine route to carrierwise gauge invariance in its
own setting. This note has not proved that our newly defined covariance,
energy window, independent domains, or finite-step source match all its
hypotheses. In particular a convergent polynomial expansion of a local
covariant positive operator about scalar cI preserves covariance term by
term, but saddle-dependent matrix coefficients can still read the datum
globally. Its covariance does not by itself localize those datum reads.

8. EXACT ADVANCE AND REMAINING OBLIGATION

The moving orthogonal chart and color-ball reference remove a concrete
finite-step cutoff/gauge mismatch. They provide separately gauge-invariant
TOTAL local correction and tail, with the same local source powers and
tail estimates, given the previously recorded all-arity geometric inputs.
Global-color averaging additionally gives carrierwise color invariance
and vanishing linear datum jets at identity.

Neither result is the native carrierwise local-gauge theorem. An exact
bounded localization of the saddle/background dependence on the required
independent current/orbit domains is still missing. No all-scale Wilson
history closure, large-field recurrence, physical nontriviality witness,
or continuum existence is inferred.
