LARGE FIELD I: USEFUL ESTIMATES AND THEIR PHYSICAL SCOPE -- TURN60
20 October 2026. Independent primary-source review by tail47.

RESULT

The newly supplied paper gives concrete local estimates and the literal
basic-R normalization mechanism which previously had to be imported
without readable primary pages. In particular, it explicitly defines
the enlarged independent-(U,J) analytic domains, bounds localized
boundary coefficients, constructs the finite current reference region,
and explains how failed restrictions remain in other large-field
branches. These are useful attachments to the unchanged native route.

It does not provide continuum correlation or nontriviality estimates.
Even ultraviolet stability is a stated objective completed in Part II,
not a self-contained theorem proved in Part I. The source now makes
several estimation modules readable; it does not convert coefficient
locality into physical correlation decay.

2. WHAT ITS OPENING SMALL FACTOR ACTUALLY CONTROLS

Printed p175, (0.1), considers a plaquette satisfying

             |U(partial p)-1| >= g0 p0(g0),
             p0(g0)=A0 (log g0^(-2))^p0.

It retains a Wilson factor bounded by exp[-p0(g0)]. This is a
pointwise action penalty. The printed rewriting as a power of g0
has a factor-two inconsistency with the displayed definition of
p0(g0); the bound exp[-p0(g0)] and its qualitative consequences
are sufficient here and avoid that harmless constant typo.

The source itself explains the crucial dimension-four limitation:
for the proposed logarithmic bare-coupling behavior, this penalty
does not give any positive power of lattice spacing. It supports
many renormalization steps but cannot alone support all steps.
Re-renormalizing older large-field expressions is the stated
reason for introducing R.

Consequences and limits:

 - This is useful input to a dated-history census with denominators
   and all branches retained.
 - It is not a normalized probability estimate for a Wilson event.
 - It is not a proof that the full original measure is globally in
   a weak chart.
 - It is not a physical mass gap or a continuum correlation bound.
 - The introductory logarithmic coupling behavior is motivation
   here, not an independent proof of the tuning construction.

The abstract explicitly announces that the ultraviolet-stability
proof will be completed in the second part. Part I ends with
further localization and exponentiation still to be performed.

3. THE OPERATION ACTS ON A PARTICULAR MATURE COMPONENT CLASS

Printed p177 specifies the components on which this basic step
acts. At scale k each is contained in a cube of side100 M R_k.
For the preceding N steps, no new large-field region has been
created inside, and the earlier regions obey the analogous size
condition. Their recent domains are consequently rectangular
parallelepipeds. Equation(1.1) factors the corresponding component
operators; the operators for the other components are retained.

This geometry is much narrower than all original configurations.
The component classes rejected during the subsequent construction
are not thrown away. For example:

The final construction is therefore organized around a complete
branch decomposition, not a measure conditioned to stay all-good.
Any quantitative use must keep the appropriate component/history
scope and the normalizations of the complete expression.

4. CLASSICAL LOCALITY AND CUTOFF ADMISSION

The proof on pp183--187 establishes the cutoff redundancy
identity(1.29). Its ingredients are the localized background maps,
their earlier classical decay estimates, and explicitly reserved
cutoff margins.

One useful displayed form is (1.45), p186. The localized difference
field H_j^(n+1), including its scaled first derivative, is bounded
by a constant times delta'_j times an exponential of minus the
distance from the changed region. The following estimates convert
this to a decay in the scale separation j-i. By choosing the
fixed geometric constant M sufficiently large, the error is paid
by summable geometric margin terms. Equations(1.48)--(1.52)
then verify the earlier characteristic functions equal1 on the
new admitted domain.

This proves deterministic stability of the classical backgrounds
under the specified substitutions. It does not say that two
random physical observables separated by that distance have
exponentially small covariance.

Printed p188, (1.57)--(1.59), also addresses a relevant hard-cut
issue. The difference between the localized and full backgrounds
contains an exp(-R_j) factor. A change of fluctuation variables
using [III] makes the actual rejected fluctuation gate independent
of the analytic background variable. The resulting action
corrections are retained. Thus background analyticity is not
inferred by differentiating a moving indicator.

This is an attachment for the source's actual substitution and
cut catalogue. It does not authorize arbitrary additional hard
tests or arbitrary complex physical source insertions.

5. AN EXPLICIT PRIMARY INDEPENDENT-(U,J) DOMAIN

Printed pp190--191, (1.64)--(1.69), explicitly define enlarged
complex spaces of PAIRS (mathsf U,mathsf J). They are not merely
the graph of one physical minimizing map.

The definition writes mathsf U=exp(i eta A') U, with U real-group
valued and A' complex, and requires a local real gauge in which
U=exp(i eta A). Its bounds simultaneously control:

 - plaquettes of mathsf U and of the derived local background
   U_(p,X)(M'(mathsf U));
 - mathsf J and the corresponding derived J_(p,X);
 - real plaquettes of U;
 - A' and its covariant first derivative;
 - local real-gauge A and its first derivative.

The bounds depend on the dated annuli, powers of L0, and explicitly
reserved geometric margin sums. They are not a single fixed
Euclidean ball independent of g and of the declared native type.

Printed p191 states:

 - real gauge invariance of these spaces;
 - recovery of the older space at n=0;
 - nesting as n increases, under beta<=1/4 and L0^2<=L/3;
 - the stronger inclusion of the actual fluctuation-substitution
   pullback in the preceding space, including its J component.

The last inclusion is not proved line by line again. The source
refers to the nearly identical argument in(1.44)--(1.52).

This genuinely improves the documentation of previously retained
native domain imports, notably the actual independent-current
admission used in the TURN53 marked-preparation discussion.
It does not prove an unrestricted all-arity source theorem, or a
full original-law observable estimate. Those conclusions require
their own input norm, admitted substitutions and proof.

6. THE LOCALIZED BOUNDARY COEFFICIENT ESTIMATE

The fluctuation integrations are organized on pp188--192.
Equations(1.61)--(1.63) express the new boundary terms as localized
coefficients. The connected expansion uses [III]; this paper
examines its enlarged domains and accumulated constants.

Printed p192, (1.70), gives the concrete estimate

 |C_k^(n)(X,(mathsf U,mathsf J))|
       <= C0 exp[-(1+3 beta) kappa d_m(X)],   X in D_m,

on the spaces just defined. It explicitly discusses the number
of possible overlapping terms and the logarithmic losses caused
by the relevant lookback N0. The small fluctuation factors pay
these losses within the stated weak regime.

This is useful analytic locality of an effective boundary
coefficient, with its native size metric and domain. It can feed
a marked/localized expansion when that additional argument is
actually proved. It is not any of the following:

 - a bound on a normalized Wilson correlation function;
 - an exponential physical clustering theorem;
 - an estimate for arbitrary local physical sources;
 - a nonzero lower bound on a separated reflection norm.

The distinction remains even though the coefficient bound has
an exponential spatial factor.

7. THE ACTUAL REFERENCE GEOMETRY AND A PHYSICAL FAILURE TRIGGER

Printed p192, (1.73), constructs the final current reference
region Lambda from the preliminary integrations. It consists of
the current Z''_k plus one layer of M-cubes, is rectangular, and
lies in a cube of side100 M. This is the current reference
geometry. It is distinct from the original raw component size
100 M R_k and from the number of reconstructed fine links.

Thus estimates whose constants depend on this current reference
graph can be independent of earlier refinement when M is fixed.
This verifies the geometric premise used in the narrow TURN58
reference-profile discussion. It does not give the same bounded
dimension for every fine-background or history-variable family.

Printed p193, (1.75), defines chi_(k,Lambda) by existence of an
interior extension with a specified small reconstructed curl.
The paragraph below states that this condition only concerns
the retained exterior current field. An axial boundary
construction gives an explicit sufficient exterior condition.
Consequently, failure forces an exterior coarse plaquette with
size at least

                    (O(1) B3 M^2)^(-1) epsilon_k.

The Wilson term then supplies a small exponential factor, and
the failed component remains in a large-field branch.

This is a concrete deterministic bridge from this particular
branch failure to a physical coarse plaquette. To turn it into a
full-law probability estimate one must still allocate the action
penalty without double spending, retain the full denominator and
sum the appropriate history census. The displayed implication
alone does not do those tasks.

8. THE CRITICAL ORBIT AND ITS SMALL REPRESENTATIVE

Proposition1, printed p194, concerns the function

             V_k|Lambda -> A(U_(k,Z)(V_k))              (1.77)

in its declared mild-regularity class, with the exterior fixed.
It states uniqueness of the critical gauge orbit, its minimizing
property, an analytic orbit-valued continuation, and

       |V_Lambda(partial p')-1| < B5 M^5 epsilon.       (1.78)

The text says the proposition will be proved later. It is a
source statement in Part I, not a complete proof there; the
actual proof belongs to the companion reference analysis.
It is not a global minimum/uniqueness theorem on all group-valued
configurations.

Printed p195, (1.79)--(1.80), constructs U0 from this exterior-
dependent minimum and records its curl bound. The error away
from Lambda is controlled by

          O(1) B3 B5 M^5 exp[-delta dist(p,Lambda)]
                          epsilon_k eta^2,

in addition to the stated base bound2 epsilon_k eta^2.
The discussion links its justification to Proposition1 and to
the earlier locality inputs.

Printed pp197--200 then puts this background into its mixed
axial/Landau representative, retains compensating changes of the
older fields, and verifies the stronger admitted-domain/cutoff
claims. The representative in(1.87) has bounds of order a fixed
geometric constant times M^6 epsilon_k for the potential and

small background domain, not on the full compact coarse field
space.

These results help ensure that the reference action and the
actual analytic coefficients can be evaluated where the
operation needs them. They give no quantum fluctuation
variance or continuum lower bound by themselves.

9. NORMALIZATION: A NEW PRIMARY ATTACHMENT, WITH TWO PRECAUTIONS

The introduction gives schematic formulas(0.3)--(0.6), p176.
Printed p177 explicitly warns that the actual denominators use
localized parts of the small-field density rather than the whole
reference density. The literal final formula is therefore the
proper source.

Printed pp193--194 distinguishes:
  (a) earlier transformations preserving the current density;
  (b) integration of the current Lambda variables, changing the
      density while retaining its integrated mass.

Printed pp200--201, (1.99)--(1.100), retains the old integration
operators and inserts, at fixed exterior and component/graph
choice, a normalized new reference. The numerator and denominator
have the same axial delta, relative-link cut(1.101), negative
action exponential, and reconstructed reference action.
Equation(1.102) explicitly states equality of the total
integrals before and after the basic R' operation.

This is substantially more information than an unsupported
partition-function equality: it exposes the actual old integral
slot and the normalized new slot. The detailed independent
attachment and passive-record consequence are reviewed in
work/large_I_branch_attachment_turn60.txt and its audit.

Two qualifications matter:

 - This does not preserve the redrawn current marginal pointwise,
   or identify an old physical observable with the same expression
   evaluated at the new reference field. Original observables
   require the old record and the inverse gauge/relative maps.

 - Printed p201 has an averaging-normalization inconsistency:
   (1.100) displays1/N_i before the graph-choice sum, whereas the
   prose states N_i=1/(2^d d!). The intended normalized positive
   symmetry average is explicit, as is(1.102), but both reciprocal
   conventions cannot be literal for an ordinary unweighted sum.
   Use explicit probabilities summing to1 and record this
   discrepancy; do not silently transcribe an incorrect scalar.

The equality also does not imply an estimate for a source-modified
native action. A passive bounded old observable or an already
born positive count uses linearity of the displayed old-integrand
slot; it does not acquire the analytic class of the action.

10. CONSEQUENCES AFTER THE TURN59 ORIGINAL-LAW SIMPLIFICATION

TURN59 already separates the common-parameter lattice problem
from the continuum estimates. At each fixed spacing, an original
Wilson law and its bare parameter can be extracted jointly from
finite volumes in a compact parameter interval. No equality of
redrawn RG kernels across volumes is needed merely to identify
that limiting DLR law.

This paper is useful for using the unchanged native construction
as an estimation tool on that original law:

 - the actual basic replacement and old-variable slot are now
   readable and can be retained in the positive chronology;
 - the independent analytic domains and local boundary estimate
   have precise primary locators;
 - the geometry and cut-failure implications are concrete;
 - failed branches remain available for the full census.

The following physical claims are still absent from Part I:

 - ultraviolet-uniform local bounds for a correctly normalized
   continuum observable family, with sufficient tightness or
   uniform integrability;
 - uniform mixed separated-source estimates for that family;
 - a nonzero centered physical reflection norm on the same
   sequence;
 - a continuum nontriviality theorem;
 - continuous Euclidean symmetry and reconstruction of the
   desired theory from those observables.

Compact raw link variables at a fixed lattice spacing do not
supply these rescaled-field estimates. Locality of a coefficient
does not supply them either. The fixed-stopping-volume factors
in earlier normalized history estimates still cannot be treated
as uniform in physical volume.

The concrete change in the next mathematical action is to retain
the unchanged native operation, attach its now-readable positive
old/new formula, and ask for an estimate on an identified original
physical observable. Replacing the reference solely to remove
current tree dependence is not forced by this source review.
No new all-scale or continuum proof is asserted here.
