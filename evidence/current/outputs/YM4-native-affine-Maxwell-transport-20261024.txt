2. The actual primitive average on affine data
--------------------------------------------
Work in unit fine-lattice coordinates. Fix one commuting Hermitian color
H with tr(H^2)=1. Write a_mu(x)=c_mu+sum_nu B_munu x_nu, with B_mumu=0,
and U_mu(x;t)=exp(i t a_mu(x) H). The oriented mu,nu plaquette has angle
t F_munu H, where F_munu=B_numu-B_munu. The one-plane probe used below
is a_mu(x)=x_nu, mu!=nu; its plaquette sign is immaterial to the quadratic
extractor. A symmetric-gauge representative gives the same curvature.

For x=y+r in a primitive centered L-block, each actual endpoint contour
has angle r.a(y)+q_Gamma(B,r). The second term depends on the offset and
the translated contour, not on y. The first derivative of the native
mean is its arithmetic mean, by B109(0.8). Thus its endpoint-contour
linearization is

  p(y,y+r)=r.a(y)+qbar(B,r).                            (1)

In fact endpoint equivariance on commuting data factors the nonlinear
mean as exp(i t r.a(y)H) p_t(B,r), but first order already suffices.
The two endpoint factors p_t(B,r) cancel in the translated full contour
from y through y+r and y+r+L e_mu to y+L e_mu. Its linear angle is

  L a_mu(y)+L sum_nu (B_munu-B_numu) r_nu.               (2)

The reference string in(0.12) has angle L a_mu(y). The relative log
averages the second term of(2), and sum_r r_nu=0 in the ACTUAL centered
block. Therefore, for Q=DM(1),

  (Q a)_mu(y,y+L e_mu)=L a_mu(y).                       (3)

In output unit coordinates Y=y/L this is an affine potential with
curvature L^2 F. If a translated block convention has a nonzero mean
offset, the additional term is a CONSTANT one-form and hence a
polynomial gradient; it does not change the conclusion below. For the
registered centered convention it is exactly zero.

Iterating(3) means composing the literal primitive maps. After p steps,
L is replaced by L^p. No independently chosen large-block averaging
operation is substituted for that composition.

3. Why the native classical lift has this first jet
--------------------------------------------------
Let U_k be the actual bare Wilson constrained minimum at k homogeneous
steps, with its native based gauge. Fix k before taking any infinite
lattice limit. Its flat stationary precision in constrained, gauge-fixed
coordinates has an exponentially summable inverse. The inverse identities
hold first on finite-support inputs; this is the flat instance of the
native constrained inverse used in CMP102(174)--(188). The constraint and
gauge chart and the linear inhomogeneous response also have exponential
kernels. Returning from the intermediate gauge to the native averaged
axial representative uses the actual finite-block endpoint contours.

Here is the only extension of those bounded-field identities required.
If A and G=A^-1 have exponentially summable kernels, their convolution
identities extend to every polynomial-growth sequence. Indeed

 (1+|j|)^s <= (1+|i|)^s (1+|i-j|)^s

makes both convolutions absolutely convergent, and also justifies the
double-convolution rearrangement. Hence A has no nonzero polynomial-
growth nullvector. The statement is about a fixed finite k; it asserts
no k-uniform bound on G or on polynomial moments of G.

For the affine potential a, d*a's curvature equation is

                 d^* d a=0.                            (4)

More explicitly, d a is constant, so the two oriented plaquettes
incident to any varied link cancel. Thus a is a stationary Maxwell
field against every compact variation, not only constrained variations.
Its actual coarse datum is(3) iterated k times. Put it in the actual
based gauge. The required gauge parameter is polynomial-growth at
fixed k, since the within-block contour offsets form a finite set.
It leaves the final coarse datum unchanged and adds only a gradient.
This gives a polynomial-growth solution of the SAME linearized
constraint, gauge and stationarity equations as DU_k(1) acting on that
coarse affine datum.

The difference between this candidate and DU_k(1) is in the homogeneous
constrained tangent and solves its zero-source precision equation.
The exponential inverse argument makes that difference zero. Therefore

 DU_k(1)[B_affine] = a_fine + d omega,
        curvature(a_fine)=L^(-2k) curvature(B_affine).   (5)

The gauge gradient in(5) is harmless but is not silently discarded in
the stationary equation. No assertion that an unbounded nonzero affine
field belongs to the original small-field domain is needed: (5) is an
absolutely defined polynomial test of the Taylor kernel at flat.

Now let P_(m,p) denote the actual p-step classical substitution from
grade m+p to grade m. The primary identity is

                  P_(m,p)=M^m U_(m+p).                 (6)

Apply(5) at endpoint m+p and then(3) m times. Gauge covariance transports
the possible based gauge to a polynomial-growth gauge at grade m. Its
coefficients may be piecewise polynomial with the fixed block-periodic
pattern; no single global polynomial gauge is required. If h=L^-p,
the output affine probe t Y_nu e_mu is lifted into old grade-m unit
coordinates as

 DP_(m,p)(1)[t Y_nu e_mu]
             =h^2 t x_nu e_mu + d omega_t.              (7)

Equation(7), rather than merely a value H(0), controls the first momentum
jet and the possible alias components. An oscillatory alias with
nonzero curvature would contradict the same constrained polynomial
uniqueness. A pure gauge alias is removed only when testing an invariant
scalar, as in the next section.

4. The exact infinite extractor and its density hypothesis
---------------------------------------------------------
Let E=sum_x rho_x be a scalar on an infinite unit lattice. Assume:

(i) rho_x are translation-covariant scalar germs, each invariant under
    local gauge transformations and constant semisimple color conjugation;
(ii) their quadratic kernels k_x(b,c)=D^2 rho_x(1) are exponentially
     summable with the density root x and both differentiated bonds;
(iii) the composed scalar uses the ACTUAL P_(m,p), with its first
      derivative from Section3. Fixed finite-depth kernel composition
      preserves the polynomial moments needed below.

For the Maxwell component choose one normalized color and mu!=nu. Bond
positions below use the same component mu, so midpoint versus tail
coordinates do not affect the transverse coordinate nu. Let
K(0,r)=sum_x k_x(0,r) be the total same-component Hessian. Define

             beta(E)=-(1/2) sum_r r_nu^2 K(0,r).         (8)

If the scalar has the registered spatial symmetries, this is its Maxwell
coefficient; without them it is this specified quadratic tensor entry.
The corresponding polarized statement transports the whole curvature
quadratic form and does not replace an anisotropic tensor by Wilson.
This is exactly the native normalization, not an unverified convention.
The printed264 pixels give Pi^{ab}=D^2 E^{ab} at flat in(1.20), with
NO extra minus or factor1/2; (1.21) selects the coefficient of delta_ab.
Choose the same trace-orthonormal color direction tr(H^2)=1. Equations
(1.22) and(5.42) define the native beta by

 beta=-partial_pmu partial_pnu Pi_munu(0)
      =sum_r r_mu r_nu Pi_munu(r),             mu!=nu.

Printed297(5.37) gives the exact transverse decomposition

 Pi_munu(p)=beta[delta_munu Delta(p)-dbar_mu(p)d_nu(p)]
                                               +O(|p|^3).

Consequently (1/2)partial_pnu^2 Pi_mumu(0)=beta for mu!=nu,
which is precisely(8) under the same Fourier convention. Printed297
(5.43) uses the corresponding normalized trace pairing. A single
positive orientation per independent plaquette gives the Wilson check
(13)--(14); the antisymmetric sum over both direction orders in(5.43)
carries its stated compensating factor1/2.

For completeness, constant semisimple color invariance makes each
D rho_x(1) zero in every link component. Its local gauge Ward identity
then makes k_x annihilate compact gradient fields in either slot.
Exponential moments extend this identity to polynomial gradients. In
particular a linear gauge parameter supplies a constant one-form, so the
selected same-component kernel has zero row and column sums. Hence

 [t^2]rho_0(t x_nu e_mu)
  =(1/2)sum_bc k_0(b,c)b_nu c_nu
  =-(1/4)sum_bc k_0(b,c)(c_nu-b_nu)^2
  =-(1/4)sum_r r_nu^2 K(0,r).

The last change of variables is b=-x, c=r-x. Every rearrangement is
absolute by(ii). Thus

             beta(E)=2[t^2]rho_0(t x_nu e_mu).           (9)

This is NOT true for an arbitrary pointwise representative of a total
action. For example (1/2)a.d*d a vanishes on affine a, whereas a
curvature-square density does not. Individual local gauge invariance
of the chosen densities prevents this integration-by-parts ambiguity.

5. Classical transport: both the h^4 and the actual cell census
-------------------------------------------------------------
Group the original old-grade density roots in the L^p-by-...-by-L^p
block assigned to one new-grade site Y. Define its transported density

  rho'_Y(V)=sum_{x in that old block} rho_x(P_(m,p)(V)).  (10)

These are individually invariant under new coarse gauge changes by the
actual covariance of the minimum map. They are exponentially rooted at
fixed m,p and translation-covariant in the new unit lattice. Crucially,
there are h^-4 ORIGINAL roots in(10), rather than one newly relabeled root.

The quadratic composition chain rule has only

  D^2(rho_x o P)=D^2 rho_x[DP,DP],                      (11)

because D rho_x(1)=0. In particular no second derivative of the minimum,
and no flat-Hessian assumption away from flat, is required. Inserting(7),
the gauge Ward identity removes d omega. Each old root has the same
affine coefficient: moving its root only adds a constant pure-gradient
one-form. Each contribution to(10) is therefore h^4 times the old
coefficient in(9). In four dimensions h^-4 h^4=1, so

                 beta(E o P_(m,p))=beta(E).             (12)

Here E o P means the actual sum and regrouping(10), including exact
scalars. Flat scalar subtraction has no effect on beta. No original
source calibration is changed. A non-translation-invariant source
insertion does not automatically have the homogeneous extractor(8);
its original rooted positions and source weights must instead be kept.

7A. Native Gaussian invariant densities, without a changed operator
------------------------------------------------------------------
This subsection is an addition after the initial proof. It verifies the
density hypothesis for the native Gaussian, rather than assuming a
formal archive determinant represents it.

At one ordinary step, write the exact native Gaussian from B109(1.4) as

 Z(V)=int dB' delta(Qtilde(V) B')
                   exp[-(1/2)<B',Delta(V)B'>].           (15)

Delta includes the actual classical/current and constraint-curvature
quadratic terms and the actual averaged-gauge quadratic. None is
dropped. B109 printed267 defines the private pivot b0(c) for each coarse
row c. Denote its invertible color coefficient by M_c(V); scale factors
such as a fixed L in the row are included consistently or left in the
background-independent normalization. Solving the delta gives
B'=C(V)B on the fixed set of remaining FREE bonds, with precision

                    D(V)=C(V)^* Delta(V) C(V).          (16)

For real V in the admitted germ, D is positive and the pivot determinants
have their continuous nonzero real branch. Thus exactly, with the
background-independent Gaussian coordinate constant left in const,

 log Z(V)=const -sum_c log|det M_c(V)|
                         -(1/2) Tr log D(V).           (17)

This is the original delta/coarea determinant, not a determinant inferred
from a covariance and then added a second time.

For an actual coarse gauge change and its native block lift, B109
printed269(2.16) gives B'_b -> R_b B'_b, R_b=Ad u(b_-). Each R_b is
orthogonal and det R_b=1 for SU(2), or the identity component of a
compact semisimple group. Absolute determinant one already suffices
for the real absolute pivot determinant in(17). Gauge covariance of
the literal averaging equation gives Qtilde(V^u) R_full=R_out Qtilde(V).
The pivot sets are fixed geometric bond sets, so they are preserved by
this pointwise transformation. Therefore

 M_c(V^u)=R_out,c M_c(V) R_pivot,c^-1,
 C(V^u) R_free=R_full C(V),
 D(V^u)=R_free D(V) R_free^-1.                           (18)

The second identity follows from uniqueness of solving the same linear
constraint with prescribed free coordinates. The third uses covariance
of the COMPLETE quadratic form, including its gauge term. This is the
native permitted block lift, not an arbitrary fine gauge transformation
of an original positive-coupling sharp window.

It follows from(18) that every log|det M_c| is individually gauge
invariant and that every diagonal color trace tr_g(log D)_bb is
individually invariant. Functional calculus commutes with the block-
diagonal orthogonal conjugation; the diagonal block is conjugated by
R_b alone. Their analytic germs inherit these identities. A fixed

Translation covariance here is on that ACTUAL OUTPUT lattice. The
