GAUSSIAN ITERATION WITH THE LITERAL ORDINARY AVERAGES
19 October 2026. balaban_inputs. Turn41 bounded constructive result.

RESULT AND SCOPE

There is a genuine Gaussian iteration input here. On compatible periodic
full-block lattices, the effective Maxwell quadratic form after ANY number
of ordinary averaging steps satisfies

       ||d y||^2 <= q_M(y) <= C_d ||d y||^2,
       C_d=(pi/2)^(2d+4),                 M=L^k.          (0.1)

The constants are independent of M and volume. Successive Gaussian energy
minimizations equal the direct total-M minimization. Thus (0.1) is NOT
obtained by multiplying a one-step constant. It supplies a uniform gap on
each next constrained, block-local forest fluctuation space.

For the actual fixed-fine-crossing cube of turns39--40 there is also a
direct-total-M bound

       ||d y||^2 <= q_M^K(y) <= C_d' ||y||^2,           (0.2)

with fixed L>=11 and constants independent of M and box size, on the full
incident coarse-row set. The upper bound uses the already proved compact
owner-block complete-star inverse; unlike an older incident-bond lift it
does preserve frozen fine crossings. This weaker bound, combined with the
lower bound and fixed-L local reconstruction, suffices for uniform spectral
bounds on the next constrained Gaussian and its KKT matrix. It does not
prove the curvature-relative upper bound in this boundary geometry.

The inherited next state consists of ALL incident coarse rows, including
variable crossing coarse rows. Freezing these rows afresh is a different
conditional operation. Neither (0.1) nor (0.2) identifies a later interacting
action, proves a uniform nonzero-background radius, or discards Gaussian
determinants. Uniform spatial decay of the boundary hierarchy is also not
deduced from spectral comparison alone.

2. NORMALIZATION AND THE TWO DIFFERENT LINEAR AVERAGES

Use final spacing one, fine spacing eta=1/M, M odd. A fine potential A
means U_b=exp(i eta A_b), while y is the final physical potential. Norms
use eta^d on fine sites and counting measure on the final lattice. The
fine differential d_eta includes division by eta. In d=4 this is exactly
the quadratic Wilson action normalization under rescaling; colors simply
tensor the following real scalar matrices.

The normalized straight average is

 (Q_M^st A)_mu(z)=M^(-d-1) sum_{u in centered M-block}
                                  sum_{r=0}^{M-1} A_mu(Mz+u+r e_mu).

Its two-form counterpart has two independent longitudinal sums and
coefficient M^(-d-2). Discrete Stokes and column counting give EXACTLY

 d_1 Q_M^st=Q_M^(2) d_eta,
 ||Q_M^(2)F||_1 <= ||F||_eta.                           (2.1)

The literal rooted ordinary derivative R_L differs from Q_L^st by a
coarse gradient. Differentiated endpoint covariance gives

 R_L d lambda=d_c(lambda restricted to coarse centers),
 Q_L^st d lambda=d_c(block average of lambda).

After k actual steps the chain-rule derivative R_M is the COMPOSITION of
these one-step R_L maps, with physical scaling retained. Straight averages
compose exactly: centered start digits and longitudinal string digits each
have a unique base-L expansion. Induction therefore gives

                   R_M A=Q_M^st A+d_1 Psi_M(A).         (2.2)

One is not claiming R_M=Q_M^st. On a full periodic hierarchy their energy
infima nevertheless agree: correct a straight-feasible field by a fine
gradient with specified coarse-center values -Psi_M(A); conversely use
the onto scalar block average to correct a rooted-feasible field. Fine
curl is unchanged. No bound on that particular correcting potential or
its support is needed for this equality of infima. Section6 uses a
different, compact correction when boundary support matters.

Define

 q_M(y)=inf_{R_M A=y} ||d_eta A||_eta^2
       =inf_{Q_M^st A=y} ||d_eta A||_eta^2.              (2.3)

Equation(2.1) proves the lower half of (0.1). It also applies to R_M,
either through (2.2) or by successive curvature contraction.

3. CURVATURE-RELATIVE UPPER BOUND: THE MISSING SHARPENING

On the coarse torus select k_nu in [-pi,pi] for each frequency. Embed
this same physical frequency into the fine torus. With physical volume
normalization its normalized Fourier mode has the same norm on the two
lattices. The straight-average component multiplier on this lowest alias is

 t_mu(k)=exp[i(M-1)k_mu/(2M)] r_M(k_mu) product_nu r_M(k_nu),
 r_M(t)=sin(t/2)/(M sin(t/(2M))), r_M(0)=1.

For every M and |t|<=pi,

                  2/pi <= r_M(t) <=1.                 (3.1)

Write delta_c,nu=e^(ik_nu)-1 and lambda_c=|delta_c|^2.
For k!=0 split y=y_T+delta_c phi with delta_c^* y_T=0.
The exterior-product identity gives

              |d_c y|^2=lambda_c |y_T|^2.             (3.2)

Lift ONLY y_T on the lowest alias by A_mu=y_T,mu/t_mu(k).
Then Q_M^st A=y_T. Its fine derivative multiplier is
delta_f,nu=M(e^(ik_nu/M)-1). Consequently

 |d_eta A|^2 <= |delta_f|^2 |A|^2
 <= |k|^2 (pi/2)^(2d+2)|y_T|^2
 <= (pi/2)^(2d+4) lambda_c |y_T|^2.                    (3.3)

Restore the discarded coarse gradient by a fine gradient whose scalar
block average is phi. This costs zero curl energy. At k=0 use a constant
fine potential for the harmonic coarse datum, again with zero energy.
Thus harmonics are retained, not incorrectly called exact gradients.

Sum(3.3) by Parseval. For a real input, taking the real part of a complex
feasible lift preserves its real target and cannot increase energy; this
also handles a chosen representative of a Nyquist frequency. Equations
(2.3),(3.2),(3.3) prove(0.1) on every compatible finite torus. The argument
also gives the corresponding infinite-lattice quadratic-form statement.

This lift is global, not compact. The result is an energy comparison, not
a local C1 lift or a boundary-domain claim. The earlier archive bound by
C||y||^2 did not first remove the coarse gradient; that simple additional
step is precisely what yields the curvature-relative upper bound.

4. EXACT NESTING AND THE NEXT CONSTRAINED GAP

For compatible successive full ordinary averages, elementary minimization
over the intermediate datum gives

 inf_{R_L y=z} q_M(y)
   =inf_{R_L R_M A=z} ||d A||^2
   =q_(ML)(z),                                        (4.1)

after the usual physical relabeling. In d=4 there is no residual energy
power under this relabeling. The first equality uses the ACTUAL composition
and is valid before invoking its gradient relation to Q_(ML)^st.

Fix the block-local forest for the NEXT fixed-L step. Let X_L be its chord
space (forest entries zero), and Q_next the actual next ordinary derivative.
The finite-patch reconstruction already proved for this geometry gives

 ||a||^2 <= C_L (||d a||^2+||Q_next a||^2).             (4.2)

It is a local exactness/endpoint-covariance statement. It holds on a torus
with periodic multiplicities, and on the declared anchored boundary version
when all exterior rows and outward plaquettes are retained. On ker Q_next,
(0.1) and(4.2) imply

                 q_M(a)>=c_L ||a||^2.                 (4.3)

The upper bound is q_M<=C_d d^*d<=C_d' I. The next constraint has a fixed-L
right inverse with bounded row and column sums. Hence the saddle matrix

             [ J_L^* K_M J_L     Q_next^* ]
             [ Q_next                0   ]             (4.4)

is uniformly invertible in l2, by the same decomposition into ker Q_next
and its right-inverse complement as turn40. J_L is simple chord insertion;
no long global forest is applied to the local fluctuation coordinates.
All constants in(4.3)--(4.4) are independent of M, step number and volume.

This is a reusable Gaussian gap, with its exact total-refinement reason.
It is stronger than repeatedly proving q_(j+1)>=c q_j and multiplying c.
The coarse precision K_M itself remains massless: (0.1) has precisely the
coarse gradient and harmonic null spaces on a torus. No uniform gap for
K_M or exponential decay of its unconstrained inverse is asserted.

5. GAUSSIAN NORMALIZATION AND QUOTIENT CONVENTIONS

At each finite step, after quotienting gauges that fix the next coarse
roots, ordinary Euclidean coarea gives a constraint factor

    det(Q_next Q_next^*)^(-1/2)

and the constrained fluctuation integral gives

    (2pi g^2)^(dim ker Q_next/2)
                  det(H_restricted)^(-1/2).

These are independent of the prescribed datum in the FLAT Gaussian theory,
but they are not one and must remain in the vacuum ledger. Exact Fubini
with these measures gives the nested Gaussian pushforward; an action-only
comparison does not justify deleting the constants.

The full framed coarse variables have residual gauge directions. On a
torus there are also harmonic modes. To call a Gaussian a probability,
divide by the declared coarse gauge subgroup and fix or separately retain
the harmonic sector. At finite compact level the normalized gauge Haar
integrals were exactly one; an infinite linear gauge volume cannot stand
in for that identity. Equations(0.1),(4.1) are quadratic-form statements
on full framed variables and descend to any correctly specified quotient.

6. THE FIXED-CROSSING CUBE: DIRECT TOTAL-M BOUNDS

Take the ORIGINAL fine cube K, with crossing/outside edges fixed to zero
at the tangent, and every plaquette reading a free edge in the action.
Assume it is a union of complete total-M blocks. Retain J_M, ALL coarse
rows with at least one active endpoint, including crossing coarse rows.
Extend fine fields and coarse data by zero outside their declared sets.
The two-endpoint-block footprint implies that rows outside J_M vanish.
The complete global fine curl of the zero extension is exactly the actual
conditional curl: any nonzero plaquette reads a free edge. Thus the global
curvature contraction proves

                    ||d y||^2 <= q_M^K(y).              (6.1)

For the upper bound assign each incident row to one active endpoint block.
The turn16 construction supplies, inside that owner block, a lift of its
whole incident star which sets every other star entry to zero. Recall the
actual ingredients, because support is decisive here:

 * For N=M=2m+1 the incoming/outgoing longitudinal weights on a compact
   owner-block field are (m-j)/M and (j+m+1)/M. Two smooth interior bumps
   with separated normalized first moments give a uniformly invertible
   2 by2 matrix. Transverse compact bumps give all 2d star directions.
 * The bumps have uniform physical C2 norms for M=L^k>=L>=11 and vanish
   before the block boundary. Thus all original fixed fine crossings stay
   zero, even when the target coarse row is a crossing row.
 * The iterated ordinary endpoint correction is d Psi_M with Psi_M
   supported at that owner center for such a bump. A compact fine gauge
   parameter taking the opposite center value cancels it; its derivative
   has uniform physical C2 norm and the same strict interior support.

There are at most 2d assigned directions per owner. Their physical block
volumes are one, and their supports have uniformly bounded overlap. The
sum E_M therefore obeys

 R_M E_M=I,   ||d_eta E_M y||_eta^2 <= C_d' sum_{c in J_M}|y_c|^2.
                                                               (6.2)

This is the actual ordinary whole-star lift; it does not use the older
one-sided incident-fine-edge lift which allowed forbidden crossings.
It is unnecessary to project E_M into a long global fine forest before
computing the variational energy. If a quotient representative is desired,
the allowed fine gauge projection preserves the constraint and curl.

Equations(6.1)--(6.2) prove(0.2). This argument has been written for M>=L;
M=1 is the original field space itself, not an assertion that a fixed fine
crossing is an independent variable at refinement one.

For an aligned nested sequence define the intermediate state to be J_M,
with its outside coarse rows zero, and apply the full next ordinary map.
Then EXACTLY

             inf_{R_L y=z} q_M^K(y)=q_(ML)^K(z).         (6.3)

The inherited intermediate field space has free incident crossing rows.
Using a next local forest rooted at the next centers and fixed exterior
anchors, the same finite-patch proof of(4.2) applies: curl zero implies a
local gradient, forest paths reduce its endpoint values to anchors, complete
ordinary rows compare neighboring center values, and the fixed exterior
strip compares boundary anchors. Only finitely many fixed-L patch types
occur; bounded overlap gives row and column estimates. The compact
next-star inverse is supported on a subset of these allowed variables.

Consequently(6.1),(6.2),(4.2) give a next constrained gap and KKT inverse
norm uniform in M and R, as in(4.3)--(4.4). The direct total-M upper bound
is what prevents an accumulating upper constant here.

This construction does NOT freeze the crossing rows at intermediate
scales. If those values are conditioned separately, they become additional
constraints and(6.3) must retain them. Dropping them or replacing the state
by a new fixed-crossing box is not an identity. Similarly, nonaligned holes,
partial coarse row sets, or independent fine boundary data added at later
levels are not covered by this hierarchy.

7. WHAT THE RESULT ADDS, AND THE REMAINING SPATIAL INPUT

The periodic comparison(0.1) and exact nesting give uniform Gaussian energy
ellipticity and next-fiber gaps for the literal successive ordinary maps.
For the explicit anchored cube, (0.2) gives the corresponding spectral
iteration input on the inherited incident-row state. These are genuine
flat reference statements at arbitrary depth, not an interacting RG theorem.

The archived homogeneous periodic symbol/locality proof additionally supplies
uniform exponentially weighted kernels for K_M. With that separate input,
the next KKT inverse has weighted decay by a small exponential conjugation
and the just-proved uniform spectral inverse. For the fixed-crossing total
hierarchy, (0.2) alone supplies no such weighted kernel estimate. The one-step
turn40 proof used a finite-range original H; the generated K_M is nonlocal.
Therefore its uniform boundary spatial estimates need an actual direct
total-M propagator proof, not a repetition of the finite-range argument with
an unrecorded growing interaction range.

No finite-g Laplace error or its derivatives have been absorbed into K_M.
Likewise the local one-loop saddle determinant and the exact Gaussian
vacuum factors remain separate from the classical quadratic action.
