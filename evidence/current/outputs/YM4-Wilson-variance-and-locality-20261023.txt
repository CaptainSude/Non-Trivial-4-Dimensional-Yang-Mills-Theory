MAIN FINDING

The inhomogeneous energy consumer is sound, but asking for a uniform
ordinary L2 norm of the project's complete smeared Wilson energy is
too strong. For the actual SU(2) Wilson measure, a self-contained
conditional-link argument below proves, for every nonzero fixed real
Lipschitz scalar profile f and all sufficiently fine lattices and weak
bare couplings,

 Var_mu[ c_a sum_p f_p h_p ] >= C_f |c_a|^2 a^(-4) t_a^(-2).  (A)

The profile is the project's coherent scalar profile; its plaquette
weights have NO extra a^4. Constants in (A) are independent of lattice
spacing, surrounding periodic volume and t_a once the fixed positive
profile box is contained. This is an actual Wilson-law lower bound,
not a Gaussian toy example or a lower bound on an individual RG sector.

On the registered bounded-step weak trajectory t_a=O(1+log(1/a)),
either declared endpoint calibration c_a=t_R or the bounded nonzero
selected-coordinate candidate makes the right side diverge. Subtracting
a deterministic first-source mean does not change this result.

Thus global source-variance absorption cannot supply the separated
energy-covariance target in its present form. The finite energy lemma
remains useful for suitably matched actual tails, bounded sectors, or
relative errors with sufficient cutoff dependence. This route needs
separation-sensitive estimates, such as the qualified near/near bound,
or equivalent cancellations or controlled coarse-source variances.

1. A DIRECT NONCIRCULAR ENERGY-ABSORPTION CRITERION

Use the notation of connected_energy_findings.txt. For an actual
source f, arbitrary reference sequence s and residual r=f-s, write

 F=||f||_E=sigma_b(f_b), S=||s||_E, R=||r||_E.

Minkowski gives exactly F<=S+R. Hence a sufficient noncircular uniform
variance condition is an independently proved uniform reference-energy
bound S<=S_0 and a uniform emitted-error budget R<=B_0. The latter can
be supplied by

 sigma_b(f_b-s_b)+sum_e sigma_(e+1)(F_e s_e-s_(e+1))<=B_0.

These conditions need not use pointwise source bounds. A weaker useful
relative version is R<=eta F+B with eta<1 and independently controlled
S,B. At finite cutoff this algebraically implies

                         F <= (S+B)/(1-eta).             (1)

No already established uniform F is needed to absorb it. However,
the relative inequality itself must be proved from model inputs,
not by declaring a normalized remainder small. The fixed eta<1 must
be uniform. Section 3 shows that (1), with S,B uniformly finite, is
impossible for the complete chosen Wilson observable on its weak
trajectory. Thus at least one of its hypotheses necessarily fails.

For a desired separated error it is unnecessary even to impose a
uniform F: the exact single-emission bound is

 sigma_(e+1)(delta_e) sigma_(e+1)(g_(e+1)).               (2)

One can seek summability of these products using ACTUAL tail variances
and additional source separation. Replacing every second factor in
(2) by sigma_b(g_b) is a convenient finite estimate, not a harmless
uniform-limit step. Keeping (2) is the weaker interface.

2. SELF-CONTAINED UNIFORM MEAN-ENERGY BOUND

Take a four-dimensional rectangular periodic lattice with spacing a,
at least four sites in every direction, and normalized SU(2) link
Haar measures. Let N be its number of sites, N_b=4N its unoriented
positive-coordinate links and N_p=6N its unoriented plaquettes. Set

 h_p=1-(1/2)Tr U_p in [0,2], H=sum_p h_p,
 dmu_t=Z(t)^(-1)exp[-tH] product_b dU_b,   t>=1.          (3)

Use the standard bi-invariant geodesic distance on SU(2). The Haar
mass of the identity ball of radius 1/(4 sqrt(t)) is at least
c_0 t^(-3/2), with c_0>0 independent of t. On the event that EVERY
link lies in that ball, the triangle inequality gives plaquette
distance at most 1/sqrt(t), hence h_p<=1/(2t). Therefore

 Z(t)>=exp[-N_p/2] (c_0 t^(-3/2))^(N_b),
             -log Z(t)<=C N_p log(2+t).                  (4)

The finite function log Z is convex, log Z(0)=0, and its derivative
is -E_t H. The derivative at t is at least the secant slope from 0:

                 E_t H <= -log Z(t)/t
                         <=C N_p log(2+t)/t.             (5)

For each orientation, translations make the N plaquette expectations
equal. Positivity of every h_p thus implies, without needing equal
physical side lengths or rotational symmetry,

                      E_t h_p <= C_1 log(2+t)/t.         (6)

Indeed the sum over one orientation is at most E_t H, and N_p/N=6.
All constants are independent of volume and a. The archived sharper
TURN64 energy bound is not required for this argument.

3. ORIGINAL-WILSON VARIANCE LOWER BOUND

3A. A positive-density family of conditionally independent links

Let f be a nonzero real Lipschitz scalar function in fixed physical
units. The source weights f_p may be samples, the project's coherent
four-corner averages, or any deterministic weights satisfying

 |f_p-f(x_l)| <= C a Lip(f)
        whenever p contains the link l with midpoint x_l.             (7)

The coherent tensor-multilinear interpolation of fixed endpoint
profile data is itself a fixed Lipschitz f and satisfies (7).
Replacing f by -f leaves its source variance unchanged. Hence choose
a fixed physical box B of positive volume and f_*>0 with f>=f_*
throughout B. Such a box exists by continuity after a possible sign
change. For sufficiently small a, B contains at least c_B a^(-4)
link midpoints of one fixed orientation, say direction 1.

Form a graph on those candidate links: join two when they occur in
one plaquette. Each candidate has at most six neighbors, the parallel
links displaced by +/-one step in each of the other three directions.
A greedy independent set S therefore has

                       |S| >= c_B a^(-4)/7.              (8)

No plaquette contains two selected links. There is no even-size or
dyadic assumption. Restrict B to an ordinary coordinate patch of the
torus; periodic identifications elsewhere cause no new degree beyond
the same six neighbors. The assumption of at least four sites per
direction excludes degenerate plaquette conventions.

Condition on all links outside S. Every plaquette either contains no
selected link or one. The Wilson action and the observable split into
sums of single-selected-link functions plus outside constants. Thus
the selected links are independent under this conditional measure,
even though their stars may share already conditioned links.

3B. The actual single-link conditional law

Represent SU(2) by unit quaternions u in S^3. For every plaquette p
containing a selected link l, cyclicity and conjugation of the trace
give a unit quaternion v_(l,p), determined by the outside links, with

                 (1/2)Tr U_p = u_l dot v_(l,p).

There are exactly six such plaquettes. Define

 w_l=sum_(p contains l)v_(l,p),
 w_(f,l)=sum_(p contains l)f_p v_(l,p),  |w_l|<=6.        (9)

The conditional density of u_l relative to uniform S^3 is proportional
to exp[t u_l dot w_l]. Its observable contribution, up to a conditional
constant, is -c_a u_l dot w_(f,l). These are exact formulas; no
small-field approximation, gauge fixing or boundary-law replacement
is involved.

Set S_l=sum_(p contains l)h_p=6-u_l dot w_l. If |w_l|<3 then S_l>3
for EVERY u_l. By (6) and Markov's inequality,

 P_t(|w_l|<3) <= P_t(S_l>3)
                     <= C_2 log(2+t)/t.                 (10)

Consequently there is a universal t_0 such that
P_t(|w_l|>=3)>=1/2 for all t>=t_0, uniformly in l, a and volume.
The staple event is outside-measurable, but (10) uses the original
unconditional energy estimate. No uniform conditional energy bound
under arbitrary fixed boundary links is being assumed.

3C. A uniform radial variance bound for tilted S^3

On |w_l|>=3 put n=w_l/|w_l|, z=u_l dot n and k=t|w_l|.
The exact density of z on [-1,1] is proportional to

                         exp(kz) sqrt(1-z^2).

For k>=6, the variable y=k(1-z) has density proportional to

 exp(-y) sqrt(y) sqrt(2-y/k),       0<=y<=2k.            (11)

Its normalization is at most sqrt(2) Gamma(3/2). On the two fixed
intervals I_1=[1/2,1] and I_2=[2,3], sqrt(2-y/k)>=sqrt(3/2).
Each interval therefore has probability at least a strictly positive
universal constant p_i, independent of k. For independent copies
y,y', Var(y)=(1/2)E(y-y')^2>=p_1 p_2, since the intervals have distance
one. Consequently

                         Var(z)>=c_rad/k^2.             (12)

This elementary argument supplies the needed uniform bound without
Bessel asymptotics. Increase t_0 if necessary so t_0>=2.

3D. The weighted physical profile retains the radial variance

Let alpha_l=w_(f,l) dot n. From (7),(9),

 |w_(f,l)-f(x_l)w_l|<=6 C a Lip(f).

Choose a small enough that C a Lip(f)<=f_*/4. On |w_l|>=3,

          alpha_l >= f_* |w_l|-6 C a Lip(f)
                         >=(f_*/2)|w_l|.                (13)

The tilted S^3 law is invariant under every rotation of the three
coordinates perpendicular to n. These coordinates have zero means,
and each has zero covariance with z. Hence, decomposing w_(f,l) into
its radial and perpendicular parts, its conditional variance satisfies

 Var(u_l dot w_(f,l) | outside)
      >=alpha_l^2 Var(z)
      >=c_rad f_*^2/(4t^2),     on |w_l|>=3.             (14)

The discarded perpendicular variance is nonnegative. This exact
orthogonality avoids an additional a t->0 assumption that a crude
triangle estimate of the profile error would introduce.

3E. Sum the actual independent conditional variances

The law of total variance and conditional independence from 3A give

 Var_mu[ c_a sum_p f_p h_p ]
  >= E_mu Var( c_a sum_p f_p h_p | outside S)
  = |c_a|^2 sum_(l in S) E_mu Var(u_l dot w_(f,l)|outside S)
  >= |c_a|^2 |S| c_rad f_*^2/(8t^2)
  >= C_f |c_a|^2 a^(-4)t^(-2).                           (15)

Equations (8),(10),(14) prove the claimed lower bound. The many
outside-dependent conditional means cannot cancel this positive
conditional-variance contribution. This is why the argument applies
to the full correlated Wilson law, rather than requiring independent
plaquettes or a sign theorem for plaquette covariances.

3F. EXACT LOCALITY: THE DIVERGENT VARIANCES NEED NOT ENTER A SEPARATED PAIR

Let Q_S denote the SAME conditional elimination of any deterministic
independent link set S: it averages the selected links and holds every
other link fixed. For two scalar profiles f,g, exact conditional
independence gives

 Cov_(Q_S)(H_f,H_g)
   =sum_(l in S) Cov_(single l)
                (u_l dot w_(f,l),u_l dot w_(g,l)).        (15a)

The signs from the two minus signs cancel. A term is zero whenever
one of its two weighted staples is zero. If the supports of the
actual plaquette weights f_p and g_p are physically separated by
more than a fixed star-diameter C a, no selected link belongs to a
plaquette with nonzero f weight AND a plaquette with nonzero g weight.
Consequently

                         Cov_(Q_S)(H_f,H_g)=0            (15b)

pointwise in the complete outside state. Include any original source
sampling/read collars in the supports used for this assertion.

The arriving means are also explicit local functions. Define

 C_f^S(outside)=sum_(p containing no selected link) f_p h_p
                    +sum_(l in S)sum_(p contains l) f_p,
 Z_1(k)=integral_(S^3) exp(k u dot n) dHaar(u),
 m(k)=d[log Z_1(k)]/dk.

Rotational invariance makes Z_1 independent of the unit vector n.
Then exactly

 Q_S H_f = C_f^S(outside)
       -sum_(l in S) w_(f,l) dot
              [m(t|w_l|) w_l/|w_l|].                    (15c)

The bracket is defined to be zero when w_l=0; this agrees with the
uniform S^3 conditional mean and the continuous extension. Each
changed summand reads only the outside links in that one plaquette
star. Thus (15c) keeps an explicit bounded geometric read at this
one elimination, with its EXACT local conditional normalizer.

By the law of total covariance, for the separated pair in (15b),

 Cov_mu(H_f,H_g)=Cov_(mu_out)(Q_S H_f,Q_S H_g).           (15d)

The microscopic conditional variances used to prove (15) can therefore
diverge while their cross conditional covariance is exactly zero.
Global Cauchy--Schwarz would discard this concrete separation. The
remaining outer covariance in (15d) is still the quantity to estimate.

This is one exact original-lattice conditional elimination. It proves
neither closure under repeated elimination nor locality for arbitrary
compiled native RG means, nor any continuum limit. It is a concrete
example of the locality information a suitable connected consumer
should preserve instead of bounding every pair by full self-variances.

4. CALIBRATION AND THE ACTUAL WEAK TRAJECTORY

The source is precisely the affine source in
original_energy_normalization_20261022 and
original_source_birth_catalogue_20261022:

 O_a(f)=c_a H_(a,f), H_(a,f)=sum_p f_p h_p,
 t_a=g_(native,a)^(-2)=4/g_(physical,a)^2.                (16)

For either c_a=t_R or the declared frozen-selected coordinate
c_a=t_R/alpha_N'(0), the supplied calibration is bounded above and
away from zero at fixed weak endpoint under its stated hypotheses.
The latter is not a full-native inverse-coupling theorem, and this
argument does not upgrade its scope.

The accepted bounded-step inverse-coupling bound gives
t_a<=t_R+C T on a=L^(-T) ell_T. Therefore for these calibrations

 sigma_mu(O_a(f)) >= c_f |c_a| a^(-2)/t_a -> infinity.   (17)

Even if t_a stays in a fixed sufficiently weak range, (17) diverges.
The weakest necessary normalization condition imposed by uniform
ordinary L2 would be |c_a|=O(a^2 t_a). It is not sufficient. It is
incompatible with the two chosen nonzero endpoint calibrations.

Multiplying by that vanishing normalization would change the target:
if the current candidate has the sought finite nonzero separated
covariance, multiplying it by O(a^2 t_a) makes that separated covariance
vanish on the stated trajectory. This is a conditional scaling
observation, not a proof of an already constructed Wilson limit.

Subtracting deterministic means or the universal first-source scalar
counterterms leaves (15) unchanged. A nonlinear source reparameterization
can subtract an additional mixed contact in its logarithmic derivative,
but the resulting number is no longer the positive ordinary variance
of O_a(f). It therefore cannot replace sigma_mu(O_a(f)) in the
positive-kernel energy proof without a new argument.

There is also a precise necessary scaling for using the GLOBAL
Cauchy--Schwarz error majorant itself. If its term B_f sigma_mu(O_a(g))
is to be <=epsilon for a fixed nonzero partner g, then (15) forces

                  B_f <= C_g epsilon a^2 t_a/|c_a|.     (17a)

This is a necessary condition on that proposed sufficient bound,
not a lower bound on the actual separated error. The current
fixed-record rarity caps, which are only stretched logarithmic in
the inverse coupling unless a genuine source age supplies more,
do not establish the exponentially small-in-depth scale in (17a).

5. POSITION CENSUS: WHY NEAR/NEAR AND CAPTURE DO NOT REPAIR GLOBAL L2

The actual near-source census retains

 sum_(R at m,i) A_R <=C N_m Mhat_i L^[-alpha(m-i)],
 N_m<=C(V+1)L^[4(T-m)], Mhat_i<=U(1+gamma_i)^D.           (18)

The separated conditional near/near theorem supplies a genuine
joining factor exp[-c dist_e(P_p,P_q)^(1/4)]. At fixed positive
physical separation d_0 and near-current m, it beats the two-arm
density and gives the established finite all-date bound after U^2
is retained. This argument has no positive d_0 for self-variance.
Even if a same-support near census is sharpened by summing the
distance kernel over the second position, a one-cell density remains;
there is no conclusion of bounded total energy from that theorem.

Similarly the capture energy result is per original record:

 B_R^cap<=C A_R exp[-p_*/32].                            (19)

Summing (19) by the available triangle/position census keeps (18)'s
current-cell density. At date e, even an arbitrarily strong valid
pre-capture m-to-e age can sum old m but leaves L^[4(T-e)]. The native
rarity exp[-c(log gamma_e)^p] does not beat that factor on
gamma_e=O(gamma_T+T-e). The energy lemma removes repeated payment
AFTER emission; it supplies no missing spatial source count.

Nor can one replace the sum of capture standard deviations by the
square root of their summed variances without orthogonality. The
coherent-emission example in connected_energy_findings establishes
the exact logical boundary. Even a hypothetical independence saving
that changed density a^-4 to a^-2 would not itself yield a uniform
full-source norm; (15) confirms actual divergence for that observable.

There is no contradiction with the near/near conditional theorem:
it estimates one qualified rare reset sector on fully held q,y.
The original source variance includes the regular quantum/contact
contributions, all other stages and the final covariance. A divergent
sum of ordinary conditional variances is exactly compatible with
the energy identity and finite separated covariances.

6. A WORTHWHILE REPAIR AND ITS PRECISE REMAINING INPUT

The all-order continuum note explicitly targets collision-flat
Schwartz tests, and the current four-entry witness uses separated
reflected supports. Neither requires an ordinary same-support L2
limit of the Euclidean Wilson energy random variable. Thus (15) is
not an obstruction to that chosen separated field-construction route.

Use the energy lemma on the appropriate portion of the exact actual
covariance decomposition, rather than demanding global (1):

* Keep early short-distance/contact contributions in a connected
  two-root calculation with their physical separation. A global
  Cauchy--Schwarz replacement loses exactly this information.
* At a fixed physical coarse scale b(a), retain the actual conditional
  means f_(b(a)),g_(b(a)). Their tail energy equals their actual
  variances, which may be finite even though (17) diverges. A uniform
  bound here is a new model estimate; it is not inherited from L2
  contraction of the divergent original source.
* For a bounded error emitted at e+1, use its exact actual tail and
  the local budget (2). Late fixed-window emissions can be useful
  once the other source's corresponding coarse variance is bounded.
  Early emissions still require sufficient joint spatial decay or
  a calibrated smoothing/conditional-variance estimate.
* The near/near joining theorem is already an appropriate producer
  for its qualified reference sector. Extend that kind of connected
  estimate to the surviving/recentered mixed terms, or prove a
  physically localized coarse-energy bound that can replace it.

This repair preserves the original affine observable, its calibration,
all independent contacts, and the exact terminal covariance. It does
not require a uniform ordinary L2 theorem that (15) rules out. It
also does not magically provide the missing mixed producer.
