2. CLASSICAL DEPTH DOES NOT COST M^4 IN THE DENSITY INPUT

Here is the direct source-density admission, rather than an assumption
that S_(M,f) has the same finite-range form as S_M.

In physical current units put eta=M^(-1). The imported ordinary
minimizer has a coherent complete-field C^(1,alpha) bound on a fixed
complex datum ball, independent of M. Its fine links are exp(i eta A).
For every fine plaquette p, the elementary product formula and
first-difference bound give

  ||U_p-I|| <= C eta^2 (||A||_(C^1)+||A||_(C^1)^2).

For SU2 (and for the fixed traced semisimple action convention), the
linear trace of the plaquette logarithm is zero. Thus, on a sufficiently
small fixed complete-field ball,

  |h_p(U)| <= C eta^4 ||A||_(C^1)^2.                  (2.1)

This is a complex analytic estimate as well as a real estimate.
For example write U_p=exp Z_p, Tr Z_p=0; the trace series begins with
Z_p^2, and ||Z_p||<=C eta^2||A||_(C^1). It holds on a fixed C^1 ball,
so its Banach Taylor coefficients inherit the eta^4 factor by Cauchy
on that fixed ball. The local plaquette evaluation reads a bounded
number of fine links in its physical current-cell collar.

Compose these local Banach coefficients with the imported complete
minimizer support family, keeping p and its current-cell root in every
carrier. The same geometric multilinear series as in the classical
support construction is convergent on one smaller fixed datum radius.
For any once-fixed admissible output reserves one obtains

  sup_x sum_(p rooted in x) sum_(X contains x)
          exp(a |X|) ||h_(M,p;X)||_b <= C b^2.        (2.2)

Indeed the per-p bound is C M^(-4)b^2, and at most C M^4 fine
plaquettes have roots in one current cell. The dimension-four factors
cancel. Rooted source labels count actual fine plaquette multiplicity;
they are not silently collapsed before this estimate.
For the output-rooted norm used below, retain a fixed spatial
exponential reserve joining the source root to every declared read.
Rerooting at an arbitrary output cell sums exp(-c distance) over
current cells, a volume-independent constant. It introduces no new
sum over the M^4 fine labels after their factor has already canceled.

The full read support is inherited from the complete-field family;
it includes the root of p and every background variable read.
Consequently multiplying each density by f(x_p) gives the same bound
times ||f||_infinity. Fixed relative derivatives and the subsequent
relative-chart composition are admitted with the same fixed margins.
This proof uses the actual homogeneous classical minimizer input. It
does not assert that an arbitrary quantum-renormalized effective action
has such a decomposition into positive microscopic densities.

The classical semigroup identity implies

  T_j S_(M_j,f) = S_(M_(j+1),f)                      (2.3)

as gauge-invariant scalar germs on their specified common domains:
both evaluate the same original weighted action on gauge-equivalent
ordinary minimizers. This identity is exact also for nonconstant f,
because each original plaquette term is gauge invariant. It is a
classical identity, not a semigroup identity for fluctuation integrals.

3. THE ACTUAL ORDER-g^2 COEFFICIENT, INCLUDING THE FIRST JET

On fixed sufficiently small product discs in (g,s,E), the marked
connected expansion for (1.3) is uniformly analytic and local.
Only a fixed s disc is needed. In fact J_f=O(g) in the occupied-support
norm, with constant C||f||, so this disc requires no independent-source
radius growing like g^(-2). In particular we do not use TURN47's large
source disc on an inadmissibly large background ball.

Every marked diagram retains its source label p and all its field,
fluctuation, chart and path reads. Product-ball reflection gives

  B_(-g,E)(f)=B_(g,E)(f),       B_(0,E)(f)=0.

Uniform analytic division on a fixed smaller g disc gives exact
source-rooted families

  B_g,E(f)=g^2 A_E(f)+g^4 C_g,E(f),                   (3.1)

with rooted bounds

  ||A_E(f)||_strong + ||C_g,E(f)||_strong
                       <= C ||f||_infinity.         (3.2)

Here and below source-only constants remain in their raw carriers.
This is an estimate of a linear one-mark response; it is not a
bound for arbitrary finite variable-profile sources.

The coefficient A_E is explicitly accessible. Write the ordinary
analytic Taylor expansions (coefficients include their factorials)

  J_f(g)=g J_(f,1)+g^2 J_(f,2)+O(g^3),
  V_g,E=g V_(1,E)+O(g^2).

Both first coefficients are odd in xi and hence have mean zero.
Expanding the SAME numerator and denominator in (1.3) gives

  A_E(f)=E_muT J_(f,2)+E_muT[J_(f,1)V_(1,E)].         (3.3)

The second term is essential. For a weighted action its constrained
saddle first derivative need not vanish. Coarea and inherited linear
increments in V_(1,E) are included, as is the cubic-action contribution.
Replacing A_E(f) by a weighted Gaussian Hessian trace alone would
generally give the wrong result.

For f=1, constrained stationarity of the actual FULL action gives
J_(1,1)=0. Its exact Hessian gives

  J_(1,2)=xi^t P_w xi/2,
  A_E(1)=v_T Tr P_w/2=v_T d/2.                       (3.4)

The rank d is fixed on the admitted branch. This is independent of w
and of E. Thus A_E(1) is exactly a vacuum term. No conditional Gaussian
approximation is used.

4. LOCALIZING A CONSTANT CANCELLATION: AN EXACT DERIVATIVE IDENTITY

We give the norm argument because cancellation of a total does not
mean cancellation of its original individual carriers.

Write A_E(f)=sum_(p,X) f(x_p) A_(p,X)(w|X), with complete source roots
in X and the bound (3.2) at radius b_s and exponent a_s. Let b_w<b_s,
a_w<a_s be fixed once. Each cell has at most D scalar coordinates.
For a field coordinate q, let x_q be its physical cell location and
write f_q=f(x_q), with no dependence on its color or link index.
The summed derivative of (3.4) is exactly zero:

                  sum_(p,X) partial_q A_(p,X)(w)=0.   (4.1)

Absolute normal convergence licenses differentiating and rearranging.
Integrating along the straight segment tw, 0<=t<=1, gives

 A_E(f)(w)-A_E(f)(0)
  =sum_(p,X) sum_(q in X) [f(x_p)-f_q]
                    int_0^1 w_q partial_q A_(p,X)(tw)dt.       (4.2)

To check the subtraction, sum its extra term first at fixed q and use
(4.1). This is an exact representative identity, not a choice of a
position-dependent vacuum subtraction. The true vacuum remains
sum_(p,X)f(x_p)A_(p,X)(0).

Cauchy at the fixed radius margin gives, for each coordinate,

 ||int_0^1 w_q partial_q A_(p,X)(tw)dt||_(b_w)
          <= [b_w/(b_s-b_w)] ||A_(p,X)||_(b_s).        (4.3)

A connected complete carrier in the current J-cell graph satisfies

 |x_p-x_q| <= C J ell |X|,

with shortest consistent physical distance on a compatible torus.
The fixed macrostep/grouping may make the output read-cell spacing
a fixed multiple of ell; that factor is included in C J, not treated
as a depth-independent equality of fine and coarse length units.
Thus for 0<alpha<=1, (4.2)--(4.3), D|X| coordinates, and
sup_(k>=1) k^(1+alpha) exp[-(a_s-a_w)k]<infinity imply

 ||A_E(f)-A_E(f)(0)||_(a_w,b_w)
                    <= C ell^alpha [f]_(C^alpha).    (4.4)

This proves (R1) after retaining the g^4 remainder from (3.1).
The strong baseline and its source-rooted birth catalogue are available
at b_s,a_s at EVERY step. b_w,a_w are chosen once for the marked output.
This argument does not spend b_s-b_w anew on an inherited marked input.
It therefore does not hide a succession of smaller source radii.

Individual summands in (4.2) need not be gauge invariant. Their exact
total is the gauge-invariant physical mark minus its scalar constant.
Any later argument requiring individually invariant representatives
must still supply those; (4.2) does not assert them.

5. SHARP SLABS AND THE ULTRAVIOLET SUM

For a bounded piecewise constant profile, a nonzero difference in
(4.2) requires a carrier joining two profile regions. If I_f is the
union of their interfaces, a carrier contributing to the rooted norm
at x must then have |X|>=c dist(x,I_f)/(J ell), up to a fixed cell
collar. A further fixed exponent reserve gives the rooted bound

 ||A_E(f)||_(weak;root x), modulo its vacuum
       <= C ||f||_infinity
                    exp[-c dist(x,I_f)/ell].          (5.1)

The coefficient is not literally zero away from the interface;
complete classical and covariance reads have exponential tails.
The conclusion applies to the order-g^2 field-dependent coefficient.
The order-g^4 bulk remainder is still present.

For a smooth profile suppose ell_j=ell_* L^(j-n), L>1, and
gamma_j=g_j^(-2)>=Gamma+b(n-j). Then

 sum_j g_j^4 <= Gamma^(-2)+(b Gamma)^(-1),
 sum_j g_j^2 ell_j^alpha
       <= ell_*^alpha/[Gamma(1-L^(-alpha))].           (5.2)

The first follows by the integral test; the second by discarding
the positive b(n-j) in the denominator and summing a geometric series.
For the actual q-block trajectory of TURN49, its stated
Gamma0+B_- floor((n-j)/q) lower bound gives the same conclusion
with fixed q-dependent constants. In particular

 sum_j ||B_j(f)||_weak
  <= C ||f|| [Gamma^(-2)+(b Gamma)^(-1)]
       +C ell_*^alpha [f]_(C^alpha)/Gamma.             (5.3)

These are sums of BIRTH norms. They do not omit the future transports
which appear in the exact sourced recursion in Section 8.

For a fixed positive distance from a sharp interface, the sum of
g_j^2 exp[-c dist/ell_j] is likewise ultraviolet summable and its
fine-scale tail tends to zero. At an interface root (5.1) only gives
O(g_j^2); no uniform sum over indefinitely many scales is claimed
there. A sharp slab requires its interface/source class to be kept
or its exact translated local terms canceled as in TURN68, with
periodic images or the stated alias-free restrictions retained.

7A. THE LEADING MIXED BIRTH HAS TWO PROFILE DIFFERENCES

The leading g^2 coefficient in Section 7 can be computed without
any external-field quotient. Write its linear first jet as

  J_(f,1)=sum_r xi_r . l_(f,r)(w),
  l_(f,r)=sum_p f(x_p)L_(p,r)(w).

Here r is an ambient color-block coordinate; L_(p,r) is the
literal first derivative of h_(M,p)(U_g) with respect to g xi_r
at zero. It contains the actual K and chart derivative. Constrained
stationarity of the FULL action implies

                  sum_p L_(p,r)(w)=0                 (7A.1)

for every r, not merely after integration. Choose a physical cell
location x_r for that block. Therefore

  l_(f,r)=sum_p [f(x_p)-f(x_r)] L_(p,r).

The source and fluctuation roots are retained in the same derivative
catalogue. A fixed length reserve and the profile modulus yield
an ell^alpha [f]_(C^alpha) factor in its rooted coefficient norm.
No external-field radius contraction or vacuum subtraction is
needed for (7A.1).

Since V_0=0 and both first jets have zero product-ball mean, the
mixed connected logarithm has the exact expansion

 d_s d_t L(g,0,0)
    =g^2 C_2(f,h)+g^4 C_4(g;f,h),
 C_2(f,h)=E_muT[J_(f,1)J_(h,1)]
          =v_T sum_r l_(f,r) . l_(h,r).               (7A.2)

The fixed one-mark catalogues and a fixed connected-product reserve
give, in the raw scalar/field carrier norm,

 ||C_2(f,h)|| <= C ell^(alpha+beta)
                         [f]_(C^alpha)[h]_(C^beta),
 ||C_4(g;f,h)|| <= C ||f||_infinity ||h||_infinity.    (7A.3)

When the two source supports have distance d, each connected
contraction still meets both supports. After the subtraction,
p itself need not lie in supp(f), but a nonzero f(x_p)-f(x_r)
requires p OR r to do so; similarly the other difference requires
its p-label OR r to lie in supp(h). The complete connected
carrier contains these points. Disjoint supports prevent r alone
from witnessing both. A further fixed reserve therefore keeps
exp(-c d/ell) in both bounds. If either profile is constant,
C_2 is identically zero. This is a cancellation of the ACTUAL
raw mixed coefficient, including its scalar part.

It does not eliminate all coincident-source vacuum divergences.
For instance a four-dimensional total root count ell^(-4) can
outgrow the ell^2 gain from two Lipschitz profiles when no positive
separation is available. Nor does (7A.2) control separately
propagated marks at their later common physical scale.
