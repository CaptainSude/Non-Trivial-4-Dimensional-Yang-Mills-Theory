1. THE SAME SOURCE OPERATORS AND THE REQUIRED NORMS

At a fixed finite regulator let C_j be the comparison-core normalized
source operator and A_j the actual normalized source operator. They use
the same source substitution, original cuts, and their respective full
source-zero normalizers. Put D_j=A_j-C_j. Empty ordered products are
the identity and C_[n,i)=C_(n-1)...C_i, similarly for A.

All formulas are on common admitted successive reads. They do not
equate the two densities or extend either one beyond its own domain.
Field-independent source terms are retained separately. Since D_j
annihilates constants, it also acts on the source quotient by constants;
that quotient does not delete the physical scalar ledger.

Let B_i be the strong norm for an actual running-domain source born at
date i, including its original roots, complete read, calibration and
profile coefficients. Let H_j be a current prepared source norm in which
both mature core components and the explicitly dated immature components
can be read. This is a REQUIRED native construction, not an assertion
that all components possess a common fixed analytic extension.

Suppose the core transports and difference insertions have the bounds

 ||C_[n,i) F||_(H_n) <= K_i W(n,i) ||F||_(B_i), n>=i,
 ||D_j F||_(B_(j+1)) <= epsilon_j ||F||_(H_j),              (1)

where K_i>=1 is the ONCE-BORN entry/read cost. It may include the finite
uniform-value replay, initial radius normalization, terminal unfinished
delay costs and the declared native source-density census. It may not
omit a future exponential positional count. The constants in (1) must
be uniform in the regulator. The same source profile convention and all
source/current reads are used on both sides.

Take u_j=Y+lambda(N-j), Y>0, and

 W(n,i)=(u_i/u_n)^nu,  nu>=0.                             (2)

Thus W is multiplicative and W(j+1,j)>=1. This weight may be obtained
from the already registered marginal/neutral Volterra core, including
its spatial census. A finite regulator error factor can be included
by replacing u_i^nu by a positive decreasing weight with the same
uniformly bounded comparisons. Merely bounding one classical source
value does not supply (1).

2. FINITE DYSON EXPANSION AND THE CORRECT ONCE-BORN PRICE

Repeatedly expand A_j=C_j+D_j. The term with insertions at
i<=j_1<...<j_m<n is

 C_[n,j_m+1) D_(j_m) C_[j_m,j_(m-1)+1) ...
                         D_(j_1) C_[j_1,i) F.             (3)

Each insertion creates a new B_(j_r+1) birth. Its subsequent core segment
therefore pays K_(j_r+1) ONCE, while the initial segment pays K_i.
For a zero-length segment, (1) includes the identity embedding. Its
bound can still be K>=1; there is no missing adjacent-insertion case.

The product of the weights in (3) is

 W(n,i) product_(r=1)^m [W(j_r+1,j_r)]^(-1)
 <= W(n,i).                                             (4)

Consequently the term is at most

 K_i W(n,i) ||F||_(B_i)
                    product_(r=1)^m [epsilon_(j_r) K_(j_r+1)].

The finite sum over all subsets of dates factorizes, giving

 ||A_[n,i)F||_(H_n)
 <= K_i W(n,i) product_(j=i)^(n-1)(1+kappa_j) ||F||_(B_i)
 <= K_i W(n,i) exp(sum_(j=i)^(n-1) kappa_j) ||F||_(B_i),
 kappa_j=epsilon_j K_(j+1).                              (5)

This bounds arbitrary repeated corrections, including corrections
before a previous source has completed its admission delay. It does
not require pretending that an immature correction is fixed-domain.
It DOES require the actual immature-source estimate in the second
line of (1); a bound only for already mature input is insufficient.

3. HIGH-POWER PREPARED ERRORS PAY THE ENTRY PRICE

Suppose K_i<=C(1+x_i)^d, where x_i=g_i^(-2) is comparable by fixed
constants to u_i. If epsilon_j<=C x_j^(-p), bounded adjacent ratios
give kappa_j<=C' u_j^(-p+d). Thus p-d>1 implies

 sum_(j<N) kappa_j <= C Y^(-p+d+1).                       (6)

This is the integral comparison on the tuned trajectory. In particular
the exponential in (5) is uniformly bounded and tends to one as the
weak terminal floor Y tends to infinity. If the prepared error is
instead polynomial times exp[-c(log x_j)^beta], beta>1, it pays every
fixed d and prescribed terminal power by decreasing c and increasing
the weak floor.

The relevant epsilon_j is the norm of the COMPLETE prepared discrepancy,
not a pointwise bare activity. Its old-date sum, roots, localization and
static/noisy composition costs must already be in it. If an unweighted
initial MR_j packet costs exp(c R_j^4), that cost is not a polynomial
unless an independent source norm estimate actually pays it.

4. COMPARISON WITH A UNIFORMLY BOUNDED REFERENCE CORE TRAJECTORY

The actual reference trajectory need not be estimated by applying the
generic polynomial W to its initial fine date. Suppose the specifically
matched core source X_j^C=C_[j,0)X_0 satisfies

                 ||X_j^C||_(H_j) <= M                   (7)

uniformly, after its original calibration is factored out explicitly.
The exact telescoping identity is

 X_n^A-X_n^C = sum_(j<n) A_[n,j+1) D_j X_j^C.            (8)

Using (5) gives

 ||X_n^A-X_n^C||_(H_n)
 <= M exp(sum kappa) sum_(j<n) kappa_j W(n,j+1).          (9)

At n=N, epsilon_j<=C x_j^(-p) and K_(j+1)<=C x_(j+1)^d
imply, if p-d>nu+1,

 ||X_N^A-X_N^C||_(H_N) <= C M Y^(-p+d+1).                (10)

Indeed factor Y^-nu and sum u_j^(-p+d+nu); the shift by one is
bounded uniformly. For super-polynomial logarithmic rarity, the
right side can be C_H M Y^-H for every fixed H. A fixed finite
scalar/tensor extractor on the mature norm inherits this bound;
any running-domain terminal extractor price must instead be included
in K or stated separately. This is not an original-law variance or
continuum nontriviality estimate.

The special core bound (7) can use the coherent reference's O(g^4)
marginal cancellation. Generic corrections need only the O(g^2)
feedback weight (2). It is incorrect to assign the special cancellation
to each arbitrary rare source.

This finite renewal lemma explains exactly how polynomial admission

6. A NONLINEAR UNMARKED BOOTSTRAP, IF ITS EXACT PRODUCER IS BUILT

The one-step normalized logarithm has a quadratic remainder, so the
following finite numerical consequence is useful. Suppose a genuinely
constructed unmarked difference, with d_0=0, satisfies

 d_n <= C sum_(j<n) u_(j+1)^d W(n,j+1)
                              [r_j+u_j^b d_j^2],
 r_j<=C_r u_j^-p, b>=0.                                 (12)

Here d_n is the SAME complete current prepared norm throughout. The
power u^b records any proved conversion from that norm to the quadratic
interaction estimate; it is not an omitted preparation cost. The forcing
r_j must be a registered actual map/outer-factor difference or initial
forcing. With identical exact maps, counterterms and initial action,
no new difference can simply be inserted under the name of an R birth.

Set s=p-d-1 and assume

                  s>max(nu,d+b+1).                       (13)

The forcing sum in (12) is bounded by C_F u_n^-s. If inductively
d_j<=A u_j^-s for j<n, the quadratic sum is at most

 C_Q A^2 u_n^-nu sum_(j<n) u_j^(d+nu+b-2s)
 <= C'_Q A^2 u_n^(-2s+d+b+1)
 <= C'_Q A^2 Y^[-(s-d-b-1)] u_n^-s.                     (14)

Adjacent u ratios are bounded; constants include those comparisons.
The integral comparison is valid since (13) implies
2s>d+nu+b+1. Choose A=2C_F (or a larger positive fixed constant),
then raise Y so C'_Q A^2 Y^[-(s-d-b-1)]<=A/2. Finite induction gives

                       d_n<=A u_n^-s                     (15)

uniformly in n,N. If a retained marginal/counterterm driver costs at
most C u_j^c d_j, c>=0, then s>c+1 additionally yields

 sum_(j<n) C u_j^c d_j <= C' A u_n^(-s+c+1).              (16)

Thus a sufficiently small complete rare forcing can control both a
quadratic correction recurrence and an explicitly priced driven marginal.
For a logarithmic rare tail of exponent greater than one, any fixed
power p in this numerical statement is available after all its costs
are fixed, provided the ACTUAL forcing has that stronger tail.

Equations (12)--(16) do not construct the required core or prepared norm.
They apply only after a same-coordinate unmarked evolution, actual

unknown complete Dprep bound has not been assumed as a conclusion:
(12) is the specific producer recurrence that would replace it.
