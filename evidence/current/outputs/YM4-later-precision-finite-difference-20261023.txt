RESULT AND LIMIT

A finite comparison of the COMPLETE CURRENT PREINTEGRATION quadratic
precision can be obtained from the native B99 operator construction.
The useful device is a small analytic modification of one endpoint
background, supported strictly inside a common homogeneous regular
buffer. It makes the two backgrounds agree on an inner buffer. Complete
localized tree words then cancel there; every other word visits its
complement. The actual physical current and the H/current branch are
retained. The auxiliary modified background need not be a minimum,
because the B99 operator family explicitly permits noncritical regular
backgrounds.

This proves the quadratic part of the finite-action score estimate on
the stated native buffer. It does NOT prove a complete reset score:
inherited E/R/B, old retained Schur precisions and direct source changes
still require their own actual endpoint read comparison. Nor does this
note exhibit a complete positive-mass eligible LII owner/source history
satisfying every one of those remaining matches.

1. THE ACTUAL QUADRATIC OBJECT, INCLUDING ITS CURRENT BRANCH

For one current ordinary occurrence, the B99 unit-lattice precision is

 D_sigma=C_sigma^* Delta_sigma C_sigma,
 <B,Delta_sigma B>
  =<B,(Q_sigma G1_sigma Q_sigma^*)^-1 B>-a_sigma(B,B)
                         -2<H1_sigma D_sigma^(2)(B),J_sigma>. (1)

This is (3.156)--(3.158), printed 428. The polynomial D^(2) in (1)
is the native nonlinear-average quadratic correction; it is NOT the
matrix D=C*Delta C. C is identity on the free bonds and fills the
selected pivots using their complete neighboring blocks. The current
is the physical Wilson current of the actual external fine background,

                   J(U)=D_U^* eta^-2 Im dU,                 (2)

as defined in B99 (3.11), printed 392. All maps in (1) depend on U.

In particular, expand G1 using (3.138), and expand the H occurring in
its Delta^(2) insertion. Printed 427 expressly describes the resulting
tree: a main chain and an additional H branch at the C^(2) insertion.
The terminal J read and its full current stencil belong to this tree.
Leaving that branch unexpanded would make a claimed local word false.
The outer C factors and their complete average/pivot reads also remain.

This object is NOT the old retained matrix
  1_E[D_j-D_j G_Lambda,j D_j]1_E
inside an inherited operation. The actual old reference read for the
latter is the separate issue isolated in TURN113. Nothing below replaces
that suppressed historical read by the current minimum.

2. WHAT IS LITERALLY AVAILABLE FOR NONCRITICAL BACKGROUNDS

B99 printed 390 expands the action around an arbitrary configuration U;
no critical-point hypothesis occurs. Printed 396 supplies the real
regularity conditions (3.35),(3.36), and the complex relative-field
conditions (3.37),(3.38). In matched grade-j units, these require bounds
on a_j A, a_j^2 nabla_U A and a_j^3 D_U^*D_U A. Printed 427, immediately
before (3.155), defines the unit-lattice operators for a sequence of
domains and ANY U satisfying those real regularity conditions.

Therefore (1) is an actual analytic operator expression on regular
external backgrounds, including backgrounds which are not constrained
minima. Its J must still be (2). One cannot change U and freeze J,
H1, Q, the embedding C, or the local coefficient D^(2).

The fixed complex allowance for this operator family is a strong
relative-field radius alpha_op>0, with native grade weights. It is the
B99 allowance, not a later inherited source's running analytic radius.
For physical endpoints with spare real regularity margin, one can choose
alpha_op smaller once, independently of coupling and cutoff.
In every complex formula, a printed adjoint/star means the holomorphic
continuation of its real-background expression, using inverse transports
where appropriate. Taking a new complex conjugate of U_z would destroy
the analyticity needed below and is not the native continuation.

For completeness, the uniform complex estimate for the full (1) follows
from the primary construction, rather than mere formal analyticity:

 * Theorem 3.4 extends the basic G', Q', R, G operators with their
   bounds on the strong relative tube.
 * The current in (2) is a local analytic expression with the strong
   bound supplied by (3.38). The additional Delta_pi and Delta^(2)
   insertions in (3.130),(3.138) therefore remain small on a smaller
   fixed tube. Their convergent Neumann series give G1,H1 and the
   inverse QG1Q* with the same strict spatial reserve.
 * Insert their native localized expansions, including the additional
   H/current branch. The estimates are absolute norm estimates and
   remain uniform on this smaller complex tube. Local average/pivot
   maps defining C are analytic and uniformly invertible there.

These steps give, in current unit coordinates, constants K,mu>0 such that

             ||D(U_z)_(ij)|| <= K exp[-mu d(i,j)]           (3)

throughout that tube, as well as the positive complete-read tree-word
majorant underlying (3). Fixed block/color constants are included.
B116's later quadratic-form/covariance analyticity statement uses this
same operator construction. No normalized complex probability law is
introduced in obtaining (3).

3. GEOMETRY AND PHYSICAL ENDPOINTS ACTUALLY NEEDED

Take two ACTUAL admitted physical minima U0,U1 at the same ordinary
date/coupling, on possibly different global determining arrays. Choose
a common current free-coordinate region I and the same local pivot
convention. Require that a complete buffered region around a finite
current read R lies in their common homogeneous top regular stratum.
The common old arrays, ordinary determining data, complete incident
rows and block/return conventions agree throughout this buffer.

There must be nested complete buffers B0 inside B1 inside B2 with R
inside B0, such that every separation between successive boundaries,
and the separation of R from B0^c, is at least d_*>0 in current units.
Fixed native pivot, current and word-localization collars are included
BEFORE measuring these distances. The buffer can be enlarged by a
fixed current-scale amount to supply them. Take d_* above this fixed
minimum; smaller distances can use the trivial uniform bound.

The homogeneous-stratum assumption is useful and substantive. It lets
one construct a scalar cutoff with first and second derivatives at the
same current scale. An arbitrary cutoff across mixed historical grades
need not satisfy (3.37),(3.38), and is not covered here.

The native common-shell theorem applies to this physical pair. Use its
permitted relative gauge of U1 and identify the free color coordinates
by the corresponding real adjoint transformation. On B1 write

                  U1=exp(i eta A) U0.

With complete current stencils retained, its field, covariant first
derivative and D*D/current comparison give

 ||A||_(strong, B1) <= C epsilon exp(-c d_*),             (4)

where the strong norm has the native grade weights. The gauge is part
of this identification; it is not a raw difference of arbitrary link
representatives. The same bound with a changed fixed constant holds
when the covariant derivatives in the strong norm use U1 instead of U0.
Their difference consists of the already small connection and lower
order products. Current and connection frames must not be mixed.

4. A SMALL ANALYTIC MODIFICATION THAT MAKES EXACT LOCAL AGREEMENT

Choose a real scalar cutoff chi=1 on B0, with its complete read collar,
and chi=0 before B1's boundary. It has bounded first and second
derivatives in current-scale units. Extend chi A by zero and put

                 U1*=exp(-i eta chi A) U1.                (5)

Then U1*=U0 on B0 and U1*=U1 off B1, exactly. The product rule for
D^*D(chi A) contains only chi D^*D A, first-cutoff times first-field
derivatives, and second-cutoff times A. Consequently

 ||chi A||_strong <= C epsilon exp(-c d_*) =: t_*.        (6)

Its support lies wholly in the common homogeneous stratum. The cutoff
and its transition do not touch unmatched rough exterior data.

The path exp(-i eta z chi A)U1 is within the endpoint-1 B99 operator
tube whenever |z| t_*<alpha_op. It need not be a physical minimum;
Section 2 explains why this is legitimate for (1). Recompute J by (2)
and all operators at every z. For t_*<alpha_op/4, directional Cauchy
applied to (3), followed by integration from z=0 to1, gives

 ||[D1(U1)-D1(U1*)]_(ij)||
       <=C (t_*/alpha_op) exp[-mu d(i,j)].               (7)

This is a Cauchy estimate for ONE actually defined operator family on
one fixed determining array. It does not require two global physical
backgrounds to share a complex domain or a physical interpolating path.
For bounded d_* use the uniform estimate and enlarge constants.

5. COMPLETE-READ WORD CANCELLATION FOR THE OTHER DIFFERENCE

Now compare D1(U1*) and D0(U0). Their global geometries and backgrounds
may differ. On B0 their geometry, external fields, local physical
currents, average/pivot maps and chosen free frames agree exactly.
Expand (1) completely by its native generalized tree construction.
Any word whose ENTIRE read stays in B0 is identical at the two endpoints
and cancels. This is the same literal local-word mechanism used in
B99 Theorem 3.14; a globally identical external field is unnecessary
once identity on every common word read has actually been established.

Every remaining word visits B0^c. The positive tree-length estimate
(3.108), including the additional H/current branch, pays that visit.
For i,j in B0, split a fixed part of the native rate between endpoint
separation and the visiting distance. A sufficient weaker estimate is

 ||[D1(U1*)-D0(U0)]_(ij)||
   <=C exp[-mu' d(i,j)] exp[-mu' dist(i,B0^c)].           (8)

For j outside B0, the ordinary endpoint decay already pays a visit.
Summing the complete positive words and then the current-unit endpoint
census, with fixed rate reserve, yields for every i in the common I

 sum_(j in I) ||[D1-D0]_(ij)||
 <=C[t_*/alpha_op+exp(-mu' dist(i,B0^c))].               (9)

Set dist(i,B0^c)=0 outside B0. The corresponding column estimate follows
by symmetry. The native mixed input density/measure and tree census are
those of B99; one does not insert a count of all fine links in a coarse
block. No retained island is contracted to a zero-length connection.

Thus the difference is not owner-supported as a matrix. It is small
near R because of (7) plus actual visiting words, not because min-close
was reinterpreted as action equality.

6. EXACT QUADRATIC ACTION DIFFERENCE ON THE COMMON CONDITIONAL FIBER

Let the two full current free vectors be f_sigma=(x,z_sigma), with x
in the SAME actual product cut on I and their respective exterior
coordinates held. Suppose their actual current amplitudes satisfy

                     |x_i|, |z_sigma,j| <= T_e.           (10)

This is a current-noise bound, not a bound on all old raw histories.
One may use the actual uniform physical prepartition range
T_e=C epsilon_e/g_e when that range has been matched at the occurrence:
B119 printed267 expands the current fluctuation on O(epsilon_e) before
the g_e rescaling. Keep the actual larger endpoint cap if necessary;
an arbitrary rough external coordinate cannot be assigned (10).

Modulo constants independent of x, the exact difference is

 Delta H_quad(x)
   =1/2 x^T(D1,II-D0,II)x + x^T(D1,IO z1-D0,IO z0).     (11)

The exterior index sets in the two force terms may differ. Each term
is estimated on its own actual set. Since the whole common buffer is
in I, their distances from R are bounded below by the protected width.
The separate exponential kernel bounds (3), (10) control these forces;
no exterior coordinates or their values are identified artificially.

Equation (11) is already an exact finite bounded-local-word expansion:
quadratic words read {i,j}, and linear force words read {i}. Their
oscillation caps are at most C T_e^2 ||(D1-D0)_(ij)|| and
C T_e |(D1,IO z1-D0,IO z0)_i|. Summing against the source response
weight exp[-nu dist(R,{i,j})], using (9), gives

 sum_w b_w exp[-nu dist(R,T_w)]
              <=C T_e^2 |R| exp(-c_* d_*).               (12)

Use exp[-nu min(d(R,i),d(R,j))] <= exp[-nu d(R,i)]
+exp[-nu d(R,j)] and the row AND column versions of (9) for the pair
words. Here c_*>0 spends fixed portions of the precision, shell and covariance
rates. To verify the last current-unit census, split i into distance
<=d_*/2 from R and its complement. On the first part the visiting
factor is small; on the second the response weight is small. The
four-dimensional shell polynomial is absorbed by one more fixed rate
loss. Sum j first using (9) and the ordinary kernel bound. This leaves
the displayed |R|, not ambient volume or an uncharged owner size.

The finite endpoint action-interpolation consumer therefore bounds
the QUADRATIC contribution to its exact normalized response by

                  C b_F T_e^2 |R| exp(-c_* d_*).         (13)

The factor T_e^2 is retained. This is a polynomial cutoff-radius price
at a matched native physical current window, not an artificial g_e^2
gain. Constants include the fixed covariance constants and color/read
conventions; complex tests use a fixed real/imaginary factor.

7. WHAT THIS DOES AND DOES NOT PROVE FOR A COMPLETE LAW

For actual complete endpoint Hamiltonians H_sigma=H_quad,sigma+V_sigma,
on a genuinely common fiber with the complete endpoint weighted-Hessian
premises, the exact identity is

 q1 F-q0 F=-integral_0^1 Cov_(q_tau)(F,Delta H_quad+Delta V) d_tau.

Thus (13) is one term of a bound for this identity. The remainder
integral involving Delta V remains. For different physical source
pullbacks also retain q1(F1-F0), exactly as in the finite-action note.
An original-source preintegration word with a registered complete read
inside R can use (13) with its actual cap and calibration. Neither the
whole later original trace nor an arbitrary compiled descendant is
declared local by this statement.

P has native joint U,J,noise local words, and the same split suggests
a next application to its finite difference. But inherited E/R/B may
have old real arguments, copied minima, historical radii and held costs.
Those arguments must be paired as ACTUAL endpoint words on the same
noise fiber. Separate size bounds do not make their difference remote.
In particular the old retained Schur precision identified in TURN113
cannot be replaced by (1) evaluated at the present minimum; its exact
inherited reference and conditional-domain read must be reconstructed.

for eligible same-date ordinary occurrences. Hence the comparison above

This is narrower than saying every compiled source meets the interface.

A flat common background by itself is not an honest example of a

outside a homogeneous buffer and explicitly list, at one matched

or identification of an exact sector in which some such channels are
