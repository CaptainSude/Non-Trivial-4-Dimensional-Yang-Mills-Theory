SUBSEQUENTIAL SEPARATED CORRELATIONS AND A RESOLVED MARTINGALE LAW
TURN71. tail47. 20 October 2026.

RESULT AND STATUS

The TURN70 selected estimates do not require a cross-regulator Cauchy
theorem to yield subsequential separated correlation limits. There is a
stronger conclusion than extracting unrelated bounded scalar sequences:
one can extract a JOINT law of the centered conditional means at every
fixed physical resolution. This law is a consistent increasing-resolution
martingale. The fine-scale covariance tail then identifies the limits of
the literal original selected-source covariances with its separated
second-moment limits.

The construction uses ONE selected probability law at each regulator,
with one common terminal grid and coherent source interpolation. It does
not choose a different selected law for each observable. It does not
need a common analytic chart compactness assertion: the bounded resolved
observables themselves are included in the compact state.

Without an additional equicontinuity/strict-chart hypothesis the limiting
resolved observable need not be a deterministic function of the limiting
unaugmented coarse field. More importantly, the limiting martingale need
not have a terminal random variable, even for a single same-support
profile. Only its separated second moments are controlled as resolution
increases. This distinction is appropriate for a composite with contact
singularities; it does not itself construct a continuum Schwinger algebra.

The result is for the literal selected family admitted in TURN70. There
is no full-Wilson comparison, reflection positivity, nonzero lower bound,
continuum operator calibration, or full Euclidean symmetry conclusion.
A separate conditional Gram-form theorem below states what subsequence
compactness would give if suitable ORIGINAL-law RP bounds were supplied.

1. FIX ONE FAMILY, ITS NORMALIZATION, AND ITS PHYSICAL SOURCES

Fix the admitted periodic cubic terminal grid, macro ratio R>1, terminal
physical spacing ell_*>0, weak-coupling ceiling, window parameters, and
the finite terminal physical torus. Let V_* be the real vector space of
terminal vertex profiles. Its dimension m is finite. Let

              ell_r=ell_* R^(-r),       r=0,1,2,...

For ultraviolet depth n, the physical fine spacing is ell_n. Use the
actual normalized selected path probability P_n constructed in TURN70
Section1, with the complete raw densities, coarea factors, product
windows, and terminal restriction retained. Its coarse states are
U_0^(n),...,U_n^(n) in the original fine-to-coarse indexing.

For f in V_* write F for its continuous tensor multilinear interpolant.
At fine-to-coarse stage j the source profile is

                   f_j^(n)=I_(R^(n-j)) f.                 (1.1)

Thus for fixed resolution r the profile is f^[r]=I_(R^r)f,
independent of n. The original insertion A_n(f) is the literal weighted
original Wilson action of TURN70, using I_(R^n)f and its four-corner
plaquette average. No a-dependent multiplier or bare inverse coupling
is added. A_n is a linear function of f and is bounded at each finite
regulator. Its mean is allowed to diverge.

Write G_j^(n)=sigma(U_j^(n),...,U_n^(n)). The exact raw one-mark identity is

 X_j^(n)(f_j^(n),U_j^(n))=E_n[A_n(f)|G_j^(n)].          (1.2)

Raw scalar source terms have not been discarded in (1.2). Define the
CENTERED resolved observables, indexed from coarse to fine, by

 Y_(n,r)(f)
   =E_n[A_n(f)-E_n A_n(f)|G_(n-r)^(n)],  0<=r<=n.      (1.3)

The strong one-mark quotient norm supplies a representative Phi_(n,r),
normalized by subtraction at the reference field, such that

 sup|Phi_(n,r)(f)| <= 2 C_X ||f^[r]||_1,
 Y_(n,r)(f)=Phi_(n,r)(f)-E_n Phi_(n,r)(f).              (1.4)

Consequently

 |Y_(n,r)(f)| <= B_r(f):=4 C_X ||I_(R^r)f||_1
                    <=4 C_X R^(4r)||f||_1.            (1.5)

This estimate is valid on the actual admitted real states. It does not
need the representative to be chosen measurably across regulators:
(1.3) defines Y exactly, and any near-minimal representative proves its
bound. Deterministic additive constants in different representatives
cancel in (1.4).

There is also the martingale identity, for 0<=s<=r<=n,

 E_n[Y_(n,r)(f)|G_(n-s)^(n)]=Y_(n,s)(f).               (1.6)

Everything up to this point is already supplied by TURN70. Positivity is
that of the complete selected path measure; signed cluster coefficients
are not interpreted as probabilities.

2. THE RESOLVED JOINT LAW: NO MONTEL HYPOTHESIS IS NEEDED

Choose a basis e_1,...,e_m of V_*. For fixed r let

 Y_(n,r)=(Y_(n,r)(e_1),...,Y_(n,r)(e_m)).

It lies in the fixed compact box

              K_r=product_(a=1)^m[-B_r(e_a),B_r(e_a)].

For r>n put Y_(n,r)=0; this arbitrary extension has no effect on any
fixed finite set of resolutions once n is sufficiently large. The joint
laws of (Y_(n,r))_(r>=0) are probabilities on the countable compact
metrizable product K=product_r K_r.

Here is an elementary extraction argument. On each finite product
K_0 x ... x K_k, compactness gives a weakly convergent subsequence of
its probability laws. Taking nested subsequences and then a diagonal
gives convergence of every finite marginal. Their limits are consistent
because coordinate projection is continuous. They define a probability
P on K. Equivalently, compactness of K and approximation of continuous
functions by continuous cylinder functions gives the same weak limit.

Thus along one subsequence n_l -> infinity there are jointly defined
random vectors (Y_r)_(r>=0) with the following properties:

(i) Every finite collection of resolved mean vectors converges jointly
    in distribution. Since each such collection is uniformly bounded,
    all its polynomial moments converge too.

(ii) Put Y_r(f)=sum_a f_a Y_r(e_a), where f=sum_a f_a e_a.
     This is ONE linear random functional on V_* at each r. Linearity
     and every exact linear relation hold simultaneously for all real
     or complex coefficients. No separate subsequence is chosen for f.

(iii) With H_s=sigma(Y_0,...,Y_s),

          E[Y_r(f)|H_s]=Y_s(f),     r>=s.             (2.1)

Proof of (iii). For a bounded continuous function h of Y_0,...,Y_s,
the corresponding finite-n vectors are measurable with respect to
G_(n-s)^(n). Equation(1.6) gives

 E_n[(Y_(n,r)(f)-Y_(n,s)(f))h(Y_(n,0),...,Y_(n,s))]=0.

The integrand is continuous and bounded on a fixed compact product, so
the identity passes to the weak limit. Continuous functions determine
finite signed measures on that compact product; hence it holds for all
bounded Borel h. This is exactly (2.1). In particular E Y_r(f)=0.

Optional coarse-field coordinates can be included in this SAME
extraction whenever they lie in identified compact spaces. On a fixed
finite coarse lattice the absolute compact-group link tuple has this
property; normal dummy Gaussian variables need not be included. At
resolution r its finite number of links is fixed independently of n.
Adding those link tuples to K and to each H_s preserves the proof,
because they are measurable with respect to G_(n-s)^(n) for s-coarse
states. One must include their actual data/geometry if claiming more
than the link marginal. Deterministic coarse relations pass only when
their graphs are closed in the chosen coordinates; no continuity of
a discontinuous gauge section is assumed.

This is a jointly consistent resolved-observable probability law.
It is not merely a collection of scalar limits. It remains weaker than
a law of the original unresolved observables A_n: the bounds B_r grow
with r, and nothing here makes (Y_r(f)) uniformly integrable in r.

3. SEPARATED COVARIANCES CONVERGE ON THAT SAME SUBSEQUENCE

Suppose f,h in V_* have continuous interpolants F,H with actual periodic
support distance d>0. Let

 C_n(f,h)=Cov_(P_n)(A_n(f),A_n(h)),
 C_(n,r)(f,h)=E_n[Y_(n,r)(f)Y_(n,r)(h)].

Splitting the exact TURN70 martingale decomposition at stage n-r gives

 |C_n(f,h)-C_(n,r)(f,h)| <= tau_(f,h)(ell_r),          (3.1)

for n>=r and ell_r<=min(d,ell_*), where one may take

 tau_(f,h)(epsilon)
    =C_(F,H) C_X^2 g_*^2 epsilon^(-4)
                         exp[-c d/epsilon].           (3.2)

The constant includes the fixed R,J and physical support boxes and the
profile sup norms. The output spacings of all omitted conditional
births are <=ell_r, so this is precisely TURN70's proved fine tail.
There is no extra transport of a two-mark effective potential.

The same estimate holds for a finite interval of resolutions:
if n>=s>=r and ell_r is sufficiently small, then

 |C_(n,s)(f,h)-C_(n,r)(f,h)| <= tau_(f,h)(ell_r).       (3.3)

This follows either by summing those conditional covariance births,
or directly by orthogonality of the corresponding martingale increments.
The absolute value bound is essential: separated increments need not
have either sign.

Finite-resolution joint convergence from Section2 yields

 C_(n_l,r)(f,h) -> C_r(f,h):=E[Y_r(f)Y_r(h)]           (3.4)

for every fixed r and for ALL f,h in V_*, because these are finite
linear combinations of the same convergent matrix of moments.
Passing (3.3) to the limit shows

 |C_s(f,h)-C_r(f,h)| <= tau_(f,h)(ell_r),   s>=r.       (3.5)

Hence C_r(f,h) converges as r->infinity whenever the supports are
separated; call its limit C(f,h). Equations(3.1),(3.4),(3.5) prove

                  C_(n_l)(f,h) -> C(f,h),             (3.6)
 |C(f,h)-C_r(f,h)| <= tau_(f,h)(ell_r).                (3.7)

For example, first choose r with 2 tau(ell_r)<epsilon and then l large
enough for the finite-resolution moment difference in (3.4) to be
less than epsilon. This proves (3.6) without a cross-regulator Cauchy
estimate on either the selected densities or the effective actions.

The subsequence in Section2 was chosen before f,h. Therefore (3.6)
holds simultaneously for EVERY separated pair in the fixed finite
terminal profile space, not only for a preselected countable list.
There is no need to assert a hat-support decomposition of a pair:
finite-dimensional moment convergence at each r already handles every
linear combination, and the fine-tail bound is then applied to its
actual interpolants.

The limiting C is symmetric and real for real profiles. Bilinearity
holds on every linear configuration of profiles for which the relevant
separations hold, by taking limits of the finite-n identities.
It has the TURN70 separated upper bound. It need not extend as a finite
positive semidefinite covariance form to overlapping profiles.

The same argument works for a COUNTABLE vector space of source labels
that are all defined on the SAME sequence P_n and have the required
fixed-resolution bounds and coherent interpolation. Include all its
basis coordinates in the countable compact product. This statement
does not supply those hypotheses for arbitrary new source families.

In particular, refining the terminal grid or changing its terminal
restriction may change the selected law itself. Independent
subsequences of such different regulators cannot simply be combined
into the theorem above. At a fixed terminal grid its interpolated
source space is not dense in all smooth continuum test functions.
Extending the source algebra/calibration is a separate obligation.

4. WHAT A COMMON ANALYTIC CHART WOULD ADD, AND WHAT WAS NOT ASSUMED

The preceding theorem permits a limiting Y_r to carry randomness not
determined by an accompanying limiting coarse link tuple. Boundedness
alone does not preserve the graph of a varying measurable function.
For instance U_n uniform on the circle and Y_n=sin(n U_n) have subsequences
whose joint limit is U independent of a nondegenerate arcsine variable.
For trigonometric polynomial tests this follows by Fourier orthogonality,
and uniform approximation gives the assertion for continuous tests.
Every finite-n Y_n is a deterministic function of U_n.

A sufficient deterministic-function upgrade at each fixed r is:

  (A) the actual real state supports lie in a common compact K_r
      strictly inside a common finite-dimensional complex analytic
      domain D_r;
  (B) compatible normalized totals Phi_(n,r)(e_a) are holomorphic on
      D_r and uniformly bounded there;
  (C) gauge quotient/chart identifications used to express the physical
      totals have compatible continuous transitions on these supports.

On a finite cover of K_r by smaller complex polydiscs, Cauchy estimates
give a uniform derivative bound. Arzela--Ascoli and a diagonal
extraction give uniform convergence on K_r of all the finite source
basis totals. Locally uniform subsequential analytic convergence on
D_r is obtained in the same way on a compact exhaustion. Additive
constants have already been fixed by subtraction at the common
reference field. With simultaneous weak convergence of the coarse
law, the centered limits are then deterministic functions of that
coarse field, and their finite moments converge.

The actual TURN48 macro bridge equation(7) does establish

 ||log U_pre(w,gKxi)/i||_infinity
                   <=C_a b/R+C_R gT < b/2

on its declared output tube, in its stated smooth pre-gauge coordinates.
This is genuine strict incoming containment. It is not by itself a
verification of (A)--(C) for every terminal restriction, every subsequent
coordinate identification, and every gauge quotient in a limiting
hierarchy. TURN70 permits a specified terminal weak domain but does not
state that every such choice has compact closure strictly inside the
common complex tube. A smaller terminal domain can be specified as an
additional literal selected restriction, but must be kept in its law.

Accordingly the deterministic-function upgrade is conditional here.
The actual bounded augmented-state theorem in Sections2--3 uses none
of (A)--(C), so it does not leave this analytic premise hidden.

5. WHY A RESOLVED MARTINGALE NEED NOT GIVE AN ORDINARY COMPOSITE LAW

Here is an explicit probability example separating the conclusions.
It is an inference counterexample for the bounds, not a Wilson model.

Take a fixed physical torus with fine mesh a_n=R^(-n), independent
symmetric signs epsilon_z on its fine vertices, and gamma_n=1/sqrt(n).
For a fixed continuous profile F define

                A_n(F)=gamma_n sum_z F(z)epsilon_z.   (5.1)

A constant can be added to each summand to make the uncentered
observable positive, without changing any statement below.
All coarse variables can be deterministic, so every conditional mean
after the first integration is zero. The first conditional covariance is

       gamma_n^2 sum_z F(z)H(z).                     (5.2)

It has only local connected source carriers, with a rooted coefficient
norm O(gamma_n^2). Each fine summand is a bounded analytic linear
coordinate on a fixed chart if the coordinate takes values
+gamma_n and -gamma_n. Grouping its source roots onto the next fixed-R
grid only changes the local carrier constants. Thus the example has
the relevant uniform one-mark bound, a local small two-mark birth,
and zero separated covariance/fine tail for disjoint profiles.

For any nonzero smooth real F,

 V_n:=Var A_n(F)=gamma_n^2 sum_z F(z)^2
                      ~a_n^(-4)n^(-1) integral F^2 ->infinity.

Independence gives
 E A_n(F)^4=3 V_n^2-2 gamma_n^4 sum_z F(z)^4 <=3 V_n^2.
Applying Cauchy--Schwarz to A_n(F)^2 on the event
A_n(F)^2>=V_n/4, or the elementary Paley--Zygmund inequality, gives

             P(|A_n(F)|>=sqrt(V_n)/2) >=3/16.         (5.3)

Hence A_n(F) is not tight. No subsequence yields its ordinary real
random-variable law, despite exact convergence of every disjoint
covariance to zero and trivial compactness of all fixed coarse means.
The unresolved contact noise is invisible to those coarse means.

This example does not prove failure of tightness for the actual selected
action. It proves that the TURN70 estimates alone do not imply it.
The independent FREE Maxwell calculation in TURN63 Section3A gives the
physically relevant warning: centered regularized F^2 can have divergent
ordinary variance while its separated reflection forms have a nonzero
OS-Cauchy limit. Demanding an ordinary-L2 random composite as the only
route to a QFT would therefore be unnecessarily restrictive.

6. COMMON POSITIVE-TIME GRAM LIMITS: A DIFFERENT, CONDITIONAL ENDPOINT

Suppose one instead has actual cutoff probability laws mu_n with
reflection positivity, and a countable family of positive-half
observables B_(n,i), i>=1. Include in the index set all products and
linear combinations needed by the proposed local algebra, if bounds
for those objects are available. Define

 G_n(i,j)=E_mu_n[(Theta B_(n,i)) B_(n,j)],             (6.1)

with Theta the antilinear reflection. Centering may be imposed at the
cutoff if that is the physical source convention. Assume the diagonal
entries satisfy

                   0<=G_n(i,i)<=M_i<infinity          (6.2)

uniformly in n for each fixed i. These are separated REFLECTED
self-pairings when B_i is supported at positive distance from the
reflection plane, not ordinary same-support variances.

RP makes each finite G_n positive semidefinite. Its two-by-two minors
give |G_n(i,j)|<=sqrt(M_i M_j). Countable diagonal extraction therefore
gives one subsequence on which all entries converge to G(i,j).
For every finitely supported complex c,

       sum_(i,j) conjugate(c_i)G(i,j)c_j
          =lim_n sum_(i,j) conjugate(c_i)G_n(i,j)c_j >=0.       (6.3)

Thus the formal finite span of the labels has a positive semidefinite
sesquilinear form. Quotient by its null space and complete to obtain a
Hilbert space. Any fixed exact linear relation with zero cutoff norm
passes to the quotient. An asymptotically vanishing-norm relation does
too. No common cutoff Hilbert space and no full-sequence Cauchy theorem
were assumed.

This gives a compatible Gram form, not an automatic local operator
algebra or a reconstructed four-dimensional theory. Products require
their own labels and estimates; multiplication on equivalence classes,
domains of unbounded fields, locality, time-translation contraction and
continuity, spatial symmetries, and reconstruction need the corresponding
identities/bounds. A nonzero diagonal limit must also be shown, and even
that alone need not exclude a static zero-energy sector; the separate
root note turn71_spectral_subsequence_root gives a quantitative
positive-energy criterion.

The selected law in Sections1--3 has not been proved RP. Its ordinary
probability positivity proves its finite ordinary moment matrices are
positive, but not its reflected matrices. For example the exchange-
symmetric two-half law (Y_+,Y_-)=(xi,-xi), xi=+/-1 equally likely,
has positive probability and reflection invariance, while
E[Y_-Y_+]=-1. Its reflected one-vector form is negative.
Accordingly (6.2)--(6.3) cannot presently be attached to the selected
correlation limit just constructed.

7. NARROWING OF THE EXISTENCE OBLIGATIONS

What is now established, at the exact admitted TURN70 hypotheses:

  * A common subsequence for a JOINT resolved mean hierarchy under one
    selected regulator family, with its martingale consistency.
  * Simultaneous subsequential limits of all its separated covariances
    on the fixed terminal profile space.
  * Quantitative approximation of those limits by finite-resolution
    covariances, with the already proved ultraviolet tail.

Therefore uniqueness of the UV limit and a direct Cauchy comparison of
the varying selected coarse laws are not prerequisites for this scoped
existence result. The coarse means can be retained as coordinates when
a deterministic-function compactness theorem is unavailable.

What has NOT been established:

  * An unresolved random field or all its same-support moments;
  * a dense, coherently calibrated continuum source algebra under the
    same cutoff laws, or all its separated higher Schwinger functions;
  * RP of the selected family, continuum translations/rotations, or
    reconstruction of a nontrivial quantum theory;
  * a nonzero dynamic limiting observable;
  * any transfer of these selected results to the full original Wilson
    measure, whose omitted mass and coarse channels remain.

All physical covariance claims here concern the literal action source
normalization of TURN70. Multiplicative renormalizations depending on
cutoff must be checked in the bounds; divergent additive deterministic
constants cancel in the centered quantities. The finite terminal grid,
fixed physical torus, and absence of any thermodynamic-uniform
continuum-source conclusion are part of the result.
