BOUNDED TRANSPORT AND A NONZERO QUANTUM WITNESS
20 October 2026. TURN56. Root derivation.

PURPOSE AND STATUS

The bounded-insertion transport reduction in TURN56 is useful for
discarded-history errors. It does not, by itself, construct physical
observables or a nonvacuum Osterwalder--Schrader (OS) state. This note
gives an exact quantitative test of that distinction and a sufficient
bounded-observable comparison criterion. No Yang--Mills hypothesis of
the criterion is asserted to hold.

The argument is elementary and proved here. The Gaussian examples are
counterexamples to proposed logical implications, not models of the
nonabelian Wilson law.

1. A BOUNDED PHASE CAN WITNESS A NONZERO OS STATE

Let a reflection-invariant probability law have a real observable X_+
in its positive-half algebra, with reflected observable X_-. Assume
reflection positivity on bounded functions in that algebra. Set

  m(t)=E exp(i t X_+),
  q(t)=E exp(i t X_+ - i t X_-).

The centered positive-half observable

  A_t=exp(i t X_+)-m(t)

has exactly the squared OS norm

  ||A_t||_OS^2 = q(t)-|m(t)|^2.                         (1.1)

Here the reflection operation includes complex conjugation. Reflection
invariance makes the reflected one-point expectation the conjugate
of m(t). Expanding the four terms proves (1.1); reflection positivity
ensures that its value is real and nonnegative.

For a centered Gaussian pair with equal variance v>0 and covariance
0<c<=v, the right-hand side is

  D(t)=exp[-t^2(v-c)]-exp[-t^2 v].                      (1.2)

This follows by evaluating the two real Gaussian characteristic
functions. In particular, at t^2=1/v, writing r=c/v,

  D(t)=e^(-1)(e^r-1) >= r/e.                           (1.3)

Now compare an actual reflection-positive law with this Gaussian pair
only at the two bounded tests needed in (1.1). If

  |m_actual(t)-m_Gauss(t)| <= epsilon,
  |q_actual(t)-q_Gauss(t)| <= epsilon,                  (1.4)

then, since both one-point characteristic functions have modulus <=1,

  ||A_t||_OS^2 >= D(t)-3 epsilon.                       (1.5)

Thus epsilon<=r/(6e) proves ||A_t||_OS^2>=r/(2e).
Neither differentiation of sources nor Euclidean second-moment
convergence is needed for this PARTICULAR sufficient criterion.

For use along cutoffs a, a fixed positive lower bound on r and a
strictly smaller error in (1.5) must survive. The observables must be
identified with elements of the intended limiting positive-half
algebra. A different arbitrary posterior at each cutoff, with no
support or limiting identification, is not such a construction.

2. LARGE SELF-VARIANCE CAN DEFEAT EVERY BOUNDED ONE-PROBE WITNESS

Let (X_+,X_-) be the above Gaussian pair and 0<=r=c/v<1.
For every complex square-integrable f of one real variable,

  E[conj(f(X_-)) f(X_+)]-|E f(X_+)|^2
       <= r Var(f(X_+)).                               (2.1)

Proof. Normalize Z_+=X_+/sqrt(v), Z_-=X_-/sqrt(v).
Let h_n denote the orthonormal Hermite polynomials for the standard
normal law. The generating function

  exp(t z-t^2/2) = sum_(n>=0) H_n(z) t^n/n!

and the joint Gaussian identity

  E exp(s Z_+-s^2/2) exp(t Z_--t^2/2)=exp(r s t)

give E[h_n(Z_+)h_m(Z_-)]=1_(n=m) r^n by coefficient
comparison. First expand a finite polynomial
f(sqrt(v) z)=sum a_n h_n(z). Its centered cross expectation is
sum_(n>=1)|a_n|^2 r^n, at most r sum_(n>=1)|a_n|^2.
Hermite completeness and Cauchy--Schwarz extend the equality and
inequality to every L2 function.

In particular, for all f with ||f||_infinity<=1,

  0<=||f(X_+)-E f(X_+)||_OS^2 <= c/v.                  (2.2)

If c stays fixed and positive while v tends to infinity, every
uniformly bounded one-probe witness tends to an OS-null vector,
including witnesses f depending on the cutoff. Yet the unbounded
linear observable X_+ has squared OS norm exactly c.

This is stronger than observing failure of one choice of phase.
Optimizing over bounded measurable functions of this one probe
cannot repair the vanishing correlation ratio.

3. THE EXAMPLE HAS AN EXACT REFLECTION-POSITIVE REALIZATION

The Gaussian-pair example is consistent with an entire finite lattice
reflection-positive law. On a finite reflection-symmetric set of sites
without fixed sites, set

  X_t=Y+epsilon_t,
  Y~N(0,c), epsilon_t~N(0,v-c),

with all epsilon_t mutually independent and independent of Y.
The two open halves are independent reflected copies conditional on Y.
Therefore, for every bounded positive-half F,

  E[(Theta F)F]=E |E[F|Y]|^2 >=0.                      (3.1)

The law is invariant under all permutations of the sites. In particular
it is reflection invariant. A single reflected pair has the covariance
used above. The case c=v is the degenerate common-variable limit.

Equation (2.2) concerns observables of one noisy probe. It does NOT
exclude averaging many probes to recover Y, and it does not establish
a general obstruction to continuum constructions. Its exact conclusion
is that positivity, bounded-test transport, and even a fixed positive
unbounded cross covariance do not alone give a uniformly bounded
nonzero quantum witness of that probe.

4. IMPLICATION FOR THE PRESENT PROOF ROUTE

The TURN56 posterior bound |E[F(original)|terminal,history]|<=||F||_inf
can transfer an actual positive-history discard estimate to bounded
characteristic functions. Separately tagged signed expansion terms
require the stronger joint absolute-majorant premise identified in
the transport note. This controls the corresponding discarded error.
It supplies neither:

  * a uniform modulus at zero for characteristic functionals;
  * a comparison between the posterior and a specified coarse physical
    observable;
  * a positive cutoff-uniform ratio c/v as in Section1;
  * the positive-half support of that coarse observable.

A possible bounded-observable route is to construct physically
identified probes for which (1.4) holds with a fixed positive margin.
Another route is to control the necessary unbounded renormalized
insertions directly, retaining all scalar and mixed source terms.
The Gaussian calculation explains why these routes are not
interchangeable just because bounded posterior transport is easy.

No conclusion about the actual size or divergence of a YM4
observable's variance is inferred from this example. Establishing
those facts, or a bounded witness satisfying (1.4), remains a
model-specific task.
