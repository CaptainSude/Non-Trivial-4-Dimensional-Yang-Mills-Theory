REAL LOCAL SECOND-BACKGROUND RESPONSE WITH A g^2 GAIN
18 October 2026. Research theorem; actual-source hypotheses isolated.

RESULT

We upgrade Notebook42's real scalar response estimate to a weighted
second-background derivative estimate. The theorem uses only finite-order
real local jets, uniform convexity, and the weighted Hessian hypothesis
already used for the real covariance lemma. It does not assume a complex
polymer norm or a complex probability measure. Derivatives of the
background-dependent quadratic Gaussian precision are included.

The new ingredients proved here are: (i) connected cumulant clustering
through order four from the weighted two-function covariance estimate;
(ii) rooted summability of these cumulants in a diameter-weighted jet
norm; (iii) an exact twice-differentiated interpolation formula in which
one interaction factor g remains in every connected term. Together they
give a volume-independent g^2 bound for the actual Hessian kernel and
its second spatial moment. Verifying the finite-order jet and convexity
hypotheses for all ACTUAL inherited source states is still required.

1. REAL MEASURES AND LOCAL JETS

Let I be a finite compatible lattice or metric regulator, with a fixed
number of real fluctuation components x_i at each site. The same metric
also locates the external background indices a,b; a fixed finite number
of color/link components per site only changes constants. Assume

  K_lambda=sup_i sum_j exp[-lambda d(i,j)]<infinity         (1.1)

uniformly in the regulator for the positive lambda used below. Polynomial
growth lattices have this property. For B in a fixed REAL neighborhood,
consider

  F_s(B,x)=onehalf <x,A(B)x>+sum_i b_i(x_i)+s V_g(B,x),
  dmu_(s,B)=Z_s(B)^-1 exp[-F_s(B,x)]dx,   0<=s<=1.          (1.2)

The barriers b_i are even, convex and independent of B and s. Hard
symmetric product cutoffs are allowed as limits of smooth barriers,
provided the interaction has smooth extensions on which all bounds
below hold uniformly, and the differentiated observables are uniformly
integrable during that limit. The cutoff is not differentiated in B.

Assume uniformly in s,B,x and the regulator that

  Hess_x F_s>=m I,
  sup_i sum_(j!=i)||H_ij||(exp[kappa d(i,j)]-1)<=m/2,       (1.3)

and the same column bound, where H=Hess_x F_s. No upper bound on its
diagonal blocks is needed. Notebook42's weighted resolvent proof gives,
for observables f,h supported in sets X,Y,

  |Cov(f,h)| <=(2/m)exp[-kappa d(X,Y)]
                        ||grad_x f||_2 ||grad_x h||_2.     (1.4)

Use the weight exp[-kappa d(i,X)] in that proof to obtain (1.4) without
a support-cardinality prefactor. All expectations and cumulants below
are for the current REAL normalized measure mu_(s,B).

For a scalar observable f define

  N_8(f)=||f||_(L^8)+||grad_x f||_(L^8(ell^2)).            (1.5)

Only fixed finite moments are requested. The previous real-response
note proves uniform local moments under (1.3), bounded local score at
zero, and a bounded row-sum Hessian of V_g. Alternatively uniform local
moments may be verified directly. Polynomial local observables with
bounded coefficient kernels then have the required N_8 norms. Smooth
finite-regulator differentiation is assumed dominated on smaller real
B neighborhoods. The estimates below make its subsequent spatial
summations uniform.

2. CUMULANTS THROUGH ORDER FOUR FROM PAIR COVARIANCE

For n>=2 the joint cumulant is the finite moment polynomial

  cum_n(f_1,...,f_n)
   =sum_(pi partition of {1,...,n})
       (|pi|-1)! (-1)^(|pi|-1) product_(C in pi) E product_(i in C)f_i.
                                                               (2.1)

No nonzero auxiliary exponential moment is needed for this definition.
Let T(X_1,...,X_n) be the minimum spanning-tree length of the n declared
support sets, where the weight of an edge uv is d(X_u,X_v). This set
distance need not satisfy a triangle inequality.

LEMMA 1 (finite-order connected clustering).
For 2<=n<=4, observables f_i supported in nonempty X_i satisfy

  |cum_n(f_1,...,f_n)|
     <=C_n exp[-kappa T(X_1,...,X_n)/(n-1)]
                              product_i N_8(f_i),         (2.2)

where C_n depends on n,m only, independently of volume and support sizes.

Proof. Cut a longest edge of a minimum spanning tree. This partitions
the labels into two nonempty groups A,B. The smallest distance between
their union supports is at least the removed edge length: a shorter
cross-edge would replace it and contradict minimality. The removed
edge has length at least T/(n-1).

Let q(S)=E product_(i in S)f_i. Compare it with
q_0(S)=q(S intersect A)q(S intersect B), the moments of a law in which
the two groups have their original marginal distributions but are
independent. The joint n-cumulant for q_0 is zero. For a set S meeting
both groups, q(S)-q_0(S) is exactly the covariance of the two products.
Apply (1.4). The product gradient rule and Holder give

  ||grad product_(i in C) f_i||_2
       <=|C| product_(i in C)N_8(f_i),

because at most four factors occur and exponents 2|C|<=8 suffice.
The undifferentiated moments are bounded by the corresponding products
of N_8. In the cumulant polynomial telescope each product of block
moments between q and q_0. Every nonzero difference has the covariance
factor exp[-kappa T/(n-1)], while the other moments supply the remaining
N_8 factors. There are finitely many terms depending only on n. For
example the loose constant

  C_n=(2/m)n^2 sum_pi |pi| (|pi|-1)!

suffices. This proves (2.2). QED.

This argument gives the clustering needed here without differentiating
the Helffer--Sjostrand inverse and without a separate high-order
mixing assumption. At most L^8 moments and first x-gradients are used.

3. ROOTED DIAMETER-WEIGHTED JET NORMS

Use connected finite sets X of lattice sites as declared supports.
An observable may depend on only some sites of its declared support.
Write diam X for its metric diameter. The exponential below is in
DIAMETER, not in the number of sites or in a spanning tree of every
site. Put

  omega(X)=|X|^3 exp[kappa diam X].                        (3.1)

A background multi-index J is an ordered tuple of length 0,1 or2.
The components are included in all sums; repeated indices are allowed.
For a marked function f rooted at r, suppose its real jets have
decompositions

  partial_J f=sum_(X contains {r} union J) f_(J,X),
  f_(J,X) depends only on x_X,
  sum_(|J|<=2) sum_(X contains {r} union J)
                  omega(X) N_8(f_(J,X)) <= A_f.           (3.2)

For an unrooted action family v_J=sum_X v_(J,X), require

  sup_z sum_(|J|<=2) sum_(X contains {z} union J)
                  omega(X) N_8(v_(J,X)) <= A_v.           (3.3)

These are bounds on derivatives of the SAME scalar functions. No
identity of unrelated activity coefficients is assumed. Suprema over
s,B and regulators are understood. Constants in x may be removed from
V_g before taking the norm, since they change no normalized measure.

For the two reference Gaussian background scores

  G_a=onehalf <x,(partial_a A(B))x>,
  G_ab=onehalf <x,(partial_ab A(B))x>,                     (3.4)

assume decompositions supported in X containing the indicated background
indices, with (3.3) bounded by H_1 for the |J|=1 family and H_2 for the
|J|=2 family. Such norms follow, for example, from diameter-weighted
coefficient sums for (partial_J A)_(kl), taking X to contain J,k,l,
and uniform local moments through order sixteen. This is an explicit
condition on Gaussian background derivatives, not their omission.

LEMMA 2 (summable rooted cumulants).
Let 1<=n<=4, with one family rooted at r as in (3.2) and n-1 unrooted
families as in (3.3). Assign at most two external labels a,b among
their jets in any fixed pattern. Then, for mu=kappa/32,

  sum_(a,b) exp[mu tau(r,a,b)]
     |sum_(support sets) cum_n(f_0,...,f_(n-1))|
        <= C A_(f_0) product_(v=1)^(n-1) A_(f_v).          (3.5)

For n=1, cum_1 means expectation. The sums are restricted to whichever
external labels are present; the application below has both. Here
tau(r,a,b) is the minimum spanning-tree length of the three points.
The constant depends only on m,kappa,K_(kappa/4) and n<=4.

Proof. For n>=2 use (2.2). Join the support sets by a minimal tree and
connect the marked root and external labels inside their containing
sets. There are at most 2(n-1)+3 selected incidence/label points. After
merging coincident ones, connecting those in each set costs at most
five times its diameter when n<=4. This first constructs a connected
network which may have extra Steiner vertices. Doubling that network
and shortcutting in the metric gives a tree on the three external
points of at most twice its length. Consequently

  tau(r,a,b) <= 10 sum_v diam X_v + 2T(X_0,...,X_(n-1)).

The outside exponential in (3.5) is therefore paid by part of (3.1)
and part of the connected decay in (2.2). At least kappa/4 times the
inter-set tree length remains, because
kappa/(n-1)-2mu >= kappa/3-kappa/16 > kappa/4.

Bound exp[-(kappa/4)T] by the sum over the finitely many labelled
trees of products exp[-(kappa/4)d(X_u,X_v)]. Each edge factor is at
most sum_(p in X_u,q in X_v)exp[-(kappa/4)d(p,q)]. Sum each resulting
tree from its leaves toward the marked root. The anchor norm (3.3)
and (1.1) sum a leaf; each edge can cost one cardinality factor at its
parent. No vertex has degree larger than three, so the |X|^3 in (3.1)
pays all such factors. The root sum is then (3.2). Summing the external
labels is already included in the jet norms; dropping any equality
constraints between labels only increases this positive majorant.
This proves (3.5). For n=1, both labels and r are in one support and
tau(r,a,b)<=2 diam X; its own norm pays the weight. QED.

The norms in (3.2)--(3.3) are sufficient, not claimed optimal. They
allow large birth supports at a polynomial cardinality cost. There
can still be an exp[kappa times birth diameter] cost when converting
from a source norm measured in larger birth cubes. That conversion
must be quantified; this theorem does not suppress it.

4. EXACT TWICE-DIFFERENTIATED NORMALIZED INTERPOLATION

Suppose the marked static-subtracted insertion is

  F_g(B,x)=W(Phi(B,gx))-W(Phi(B,0))
          =g L(B,x)+g^2 Q_g(B,x),                         (4.1)

with L(B,-x)=-L(B,x) for EVERY real B in the neighborhood. The
reference mu_(0,B) is even for every B, although A(B) can vary.
Consequently E_(0,B)L(B,x)=0 as an identity in B. Exact real
interpolation gives

  R_g(B):=E_(1,B)F_g(B,x)
     =-g integral_0^1 cum_2,s(L,V_g) ds+g^2 E_(1,B)Q_g.  (4.2)

Using (4.2) BEFORE differentiating is important: it retains the
parity cancellation and prevents a spurious order-g contribution
from derivatives of the Gaussian covariance.

Write S_a=partial_a F_s=G_a+s partial_a V_g and
S_ab=G_ab+s partial_ab V_g. For any smooth observables,

  partial_a cum_n(f_1,...,f_n)
   =sum_i cum_n(f_1,...,partial_a f_i,...,f_n)
                 -cum_(n+1)(f_1,...,f_n,S_a).            (4.3)

This follows by differentiating the normalized moment polynomial;
it is also the finite-order formal logarithmic generating identity.
For n=1 it is the usual expectation/covariance derivative formula.

In particular, writing V=V_g, one obtains EXACTLY

  partial_ab cum_2(L,V)
   =cum_2(L_ab,V)+cum_2(L_a,V_b)+cum_2(L_b,V_a)
       +cum_2(L,V_ab)
    -cum_3(L_a,V,S_b)-cum_3(L,V_a,S_b)
    -cum_3(L_b,V,S_a)-cum_3(L,V_b,S_a)
    -cum_3(L,V,S_ab)
    +cum_4(L,V,S_a,S_b),                                 (4.4)

and

  partial_ab E Q
   =E Q_ab-cum_2(Q_a,S_b)-cum_2(Q_b,S_a)
      -cum_2(Q,S_ab)+cum_3(Q,S_a,S_b).                    (4.5)

The five negative connected terms in (4.4) and its fourth cumulant
are the normalized-law response. The quadratic G_a,G_ab are retained
in EVERY occurrence of S. No fixed-covariance shortcut is taken.

5. THE SECOND-BACKGROUND RESPONSE THEOREM

THEOREM 3.
Assume (1.1)--(1.3), dominated finite-regulator differentiability,
and the following jet bounds uniformly in s,B,regulator:

  L has rooted norm A_L in (3.2);
  Q_g has rooted norm A_Q in (3.2);
  V_g has unrooted norm at most g A_V in (3.3);
  G_a,G_ab have the bounds H_1,H_2 in (3.4).

Put H=H_1+g A_V and H'=H_2+g A_V. Then the exact normalized
static-subtracted response (4.2) satisfies

  sum_(a,b) exp[(kappa/32)tau(r,a,b)] |partial_ab R_g(B)|
   <= C g^2 (A_Q+A_L A_V)(1+H+H'+H^2).                  (5.1)

The constant is independent of volume, regulator, and the cutoff
radius whenever the stated constants are. It can depend on the fixed
number of components and the fixed locality parameters.

Proof. Apply Lemma2 term by term to (4.4). Each of its ten terms has
one V or differentiated V, so retains a factor g A_V. The two-score
term costs H^2, single-score terms H, and the double score costs H'.
Thus the weighted kernel sum of partial_ab cum_2(L,V) is at most
C g A_L A_V(1+H+H'+H^2). Multiply by the first g in (4.2) and
integrate s. Apply Lemma2 to the five terms of (4.5); this costs
C A_Q(1+H+H'+H^2), multiplied by the second g^2 in (4.2). These
are exactly (5.1). Absolute summability justifies all interchanges.
QED.

This is stronger than a real-background sup bound: it directly controls
the spatial Hessian kernel, with every derivative of the normalized law
and the Gaussian background included. No inference from a real bound
to a complex Cauchy estimate is used. The bounds are on real jets of
the very functions inserted into the same conditional measure.

6. MAXWELL MOMENTS AND REGULATOR REMOVAL

The weighted sum in (5.1) controls any fixed polynomial spatial moment,
in particular

  sum_(a,b) d(a,b)^2 |partial_ab R_g(B)|
       <= C g^2 (A_Q+A_L A_V)(1+H+H'+H^2).                (6.1)

It also gives uniform exponentially small tails when either external
index leaves a ball about r. If finite-regulator kernels have a common
pointwise local limit on cofinal regulators, these tails justify passage
of their second moments to the limit. The existence and identification
of that local limit remain separate requirements; the theorem supplies
the uniform tail bound, not arbitrary regulator consistency.

For a translation-covariant family R=sum_r R_r, reindexing gives
the per-cell two-point moment from the rooted kernel:

  sum_u |u|^2 sum_r |partial_0 partial_u R_r|
     <=sum_(a,b)|b-a|^2 |partial_a partial_b R_0|.          (6.2)

The indices include the appropriate bond/color directions. Therefore
once the source Maxwell functional is identified with that transverse
second moment in the same normalization, (6.1) bounds the actual
Maxwell coefficient by the same g^2 majorant. Gauge invariance and
the relevant Euclidean symmetry must hold for the complete family;
otherwise retain the whole allowed quadratic tensor. Those are the
same identification conditions already isolated in Notebook42.

7. THE EXACT SOURCE INPUTS STILL TO VERIFY

For one fixed-radius marked input, smooth chart Taylor formulas give
the required L and Q jets from a finite number of derivatives of W
and Phi, with constants depending on that FIXED radius. In particular
Q_ab and its first x-gradient may use higher derivatives of W; they
must be bounded explicitly, not inferred from W's Hessian alone.
Uniform local fluctuation moments pay polynomial x factors without
powers of the logarithmic cutoff. This is a finite-order preparation
problem and can be approached with shell/local-response decompositions
rather than a full analytic activity norm.

The actual-source obligations are now precise:

  (a) Uniform real convexity and the weighted Hessian condition (1.3)
      for the true conditional integrand along all required real
      interpolations, with its independent retained variables.

  (b) The local jet decompositions through two B derivatives and one
      x-gradient in (3.2)--(3.4), including the ACTUAL background-dependent
      retained Gaussian precision and its derivatives. Norm conversion
      must account for birth supports, refinement, and running radii.

  (c) The O(g) bound for V_g and its first two B jets in the normalized
      local moment norm. The printed E_0 bound on a radius of order
      g(log g^-2)^q does not establish it: a fluctuation derivative
      incurs g/r, and higher inherited jets can give only inverse-log
      gains. Renormalization removes the Maxwell coefficient, not
      all higher jets. A fixed-radius regular part plus separately
      controlled small marks is one possible source representation.

  (d) Propagation of these finite-order norms along the actual descendant
      directions and control of static pullback, or another estimate
      bounding their sizes for all ages. Theorem3 proves the one-step
      projected response once those input norms are known. It does not
      replace their iterated stability by assumption.

      There is a real derivative mismatch: the stated full Q-jet norm
      can require up to five derivatives of the inserted W, whereas
      the conclusion controls only its output Hessian. This is NOT a
      closed C^5-to-C^5 stability theorem. Asking the same method for
      five output background derivatives would require still higher
      input jets. A separate propagation argument, analytic-radius
      mechanism, or directly bounded actual descendant family is needed.

  (e) The same source map, gauge/trace conventions, local thermodynamic
      limit, and coefficient normalization used in Notebook41/42.

Thus the REAL-TO-SECOND-BACKGROUND-DERIVATIVE step is proved under explicit
real local hypotheses. It no longer requires a separate complex-norm
