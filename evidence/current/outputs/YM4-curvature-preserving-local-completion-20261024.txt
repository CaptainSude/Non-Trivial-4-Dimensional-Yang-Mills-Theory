RESULT AND SCOPE

A small-curvature coarse field on a filled box has a finite-read
completion, equal to its canonical representative on the inner box and
flat outside the outer box. The completed plaquette bound is C delta,
provided R^2 delta is sufficiently small. Its raw-link bound is C R delta.
These two bounds must not be confused. CMP102 Theorem1 admits this field
as determining data for the actual homogeneous minimum: its fine
curvature AND physical current have cap C delta, independently of R.

Combined with the independently checked first-bare endpoint action and
physical-source comparisons, this gives finite-read approximations to an
original normalized first-success thin observable on the full admitted
small-curvature family. A finite telescoping expansion through order
N=O(r^2) has exponentially small neighborhood coefficients and a rare
terminal error. It does not require the original field to lie in one
global canonical small-link box. This note proves the real construction.
An analytic coefficient catalogue requires the separate complex/source
projection check; all-history source transport and continuum existence
are not asserted here.

1. GEOMETRY, ONE TREE, AND THE ACTUAL AREA COUNT

Work in coarse lattice units, dimension d=4, on an embedded filled cube
Q_(2R) centered at a chosen source root o=0. R>=R0 is integral; the cube
and one extra incident collar do not wrap the finite torus. Include every
bond/plaquette used below. Suppose

                 ||V(partial p)-1||_op <= delta.               (1)

Use coordinate order 1,2,3,4 for every radius. P_x goes from 0 to x by
the signed coordinate segments in that order. These paths form one
tree: the parent of x deletes one signed unit of its highest-index
nonzero coordinate toward zero. Restriction to a smaller centered cube
gives the same paths, without choosing a new gauge at each radius.
Let V(P_x) be the ordered parallel transport. Define

         C(V)_(x,y)=V(P_x) V_(x,y) V(P_y)^(-1).                (2)

For y=x+e_j, commute the single e_j edge past the later signed segments
k>j. The resulting loop is filled by width-one strips of total area
sum_(k>j)|x_k| <= 2(d-1)R. Signed prefix cancellation gives the same
bound when x_j=-1,y_j=0 or other coordinates are negative. The strips
stay in Q_(2R). The complete path length is at most C_d R. This proves
an O(R), not O(R^2), elementary-plaquette count for each canonical edge.

Each strip insertion changes the loop by a unitarily conjugated
plaquette or its inverse. For unitaries, ||prod U_i-1||<=sum||U_i-1||.
Therefore

                 ||C(V)_e-1|| <= C_d R delta.                 (3)

When C_d R delta is below a fixed logarithm margin, set
A_e=-i Log C(V)_e. A_e is Hermitian traceless for SU(2), A_(-e)=-A_e,
and ||A_e||<=C R delta. Equation(2) is a genuine gauge transformation
inside the cube, so its plaquette bound remains (1).

2. CUTOFF PROOF WITH ALL CROSSING PLAQUETTES

Choose an unoriented bond function chi_e in [0,1], equal to 1 on every
bond of Q_R including its required incident collar, and equal to 0
before the outer boundary of Q_(2R), with a complete one-cell zero
collar. Neighboring edges of a plaquette obey |chi_e-chi_f|<=C/R.
One may sample a fixed smooth radial/cube cutoff at bond midpoints,
with a fixed inner/outer spare fraction; enlarge R0 for rounding.
Set

 Vhat_R,e=exp(i chi_e A_e) inside Q_(2R), and Vhat_R,e=1 outside. (4)

This respects orientation and agrees exactly with C(V) on Q_R. It
depends only on the filled input Q_(2R); outside it is exactly flat.
To bound its curvature, orient a plaquette's four edges cyclically.
The four-factor analytic product expansion gives, for ||A_e||<=a_*:

 ||prod_(e in partial p)exp(i A_e)-1-i sum_e A_e||
                                      <= C max_e||A_e||^2.

Consequently (1),(3) imply

                  ||sum_e A_e|| <= C(delta+R^2 delta^2).      (5)

For any one edge e0 of the plaquette,
sum chi_e A_e = chi_e0 sum A_e +sum(chi_e-chi_e0)A_e.
The second sum is bounded by C R^-1 * R delta=C delta. Applying the
same four-factor estimate to chi A now proves

       ||Vhat_R(partial p)-1|| <= C(delta+R^2 delta^2).         (6)

Crossing plaquettes have their nonzero edges in the declared input
collar; the remaining A may be assigned zero because chi=0 there.
The same estimate applies, or all four completed edges are identity.
In particular, if

                          R^2 delta <= c_*,                  (7)

then every completed plaquette is <=C_* delta, with constants fixed
before R and delta. Condition(7), with c_* small, also implies the
logarithm margin required for (3) when R>=1. No bound on an unfilled
surface, total topological charge, or torus winding is being assumed.
This is a local completion on an embedded box, not a global flattening.

Under an arbitrary coarse gauge h on the input cube, C(V^h) is the
constant root conjugate h(0) C(V) h(0)^(-1). The principal log and (4)
respect this conjugation; the exterior identity does too. Thus Vhat_R
has exact covariance by the one root matrix. The whole family uses
the same root/tree; Vhat_R and Vhat_(R-1) agree on Q_(R-1).

At each real admitted input, (4) is holomorphic on a sufficiently
small complex relative-link neighborhood. Products contain O(R)
unitary factors. On a tube with R times its radius small, their first
and second variations have O(R) and O(R^2) sup-norm bounds. The log is
on a fixed branch and the exponential is on a fixed bounded chart.
Thus these completion derivative prices are polynomial. This finite
map assertion alone does not prove holomorphic normalized source laws.

4. ORIGINAL COARSE GAUGE COVARIANCE AND LOCAL APPROXIMANTS

Let the original full coarse field V have the actual homogeneous

Extend the tree gauge from Q_(2R) arbitrarily to coarse vertices of

the independent noise variables transform by orthogonal adjoint maps.
Their product balls and Lebesgue/Haar/coarea conventions are preserved.
Thus simultaneous coarse covariance (B119(1.17)) gives the same original
normalized gauge-invariant source value after this transformation.
It does not require invariance under arbitrary fine gauge changes of
the original noise cut. Denote this transformed original endpoint V^c.
It agrees with Vhat_R on Q_R and retains the original curvature/current
admission. Outside Q_R no raw-link comparison is needed.

Define F_R(V)=T_(Vhat_R)f by the same-form auxiliary law of Section3.
It is a function only of V on Q_(2R). It is gauge invariant because

 |T_V f-F_R(V)| <= C eta [P_I r^2 exp(-kappa(R-R0))

5. FINITE TELESCOPING WITH THE CUT TAIL KEPT

For R0<=n<=N use the same construction at integer radius n, and set

 a_R0(V)=F_R0(V),  a_n(V)=F_n(V)-F_(n-1)(V) for n>R0.

The two auxiliary endpoints agree on Q_(n-1), so the same proof gives

 |a_n(V)| <= C eta [P_I r^2 exp(-kappa(n-R0))
                                  +P_I exp(-c_r r^2)].        (9)

This is a genuine finite-read, gauge-invariant real expansion. Its
constant part stays in a_R0; every a_n, n>R0, annihilates constant
observables. The original exact identity is

 T_V f = sum_(n=R0)^N a_n(V) + E_N(V),  E_N=T_V f-F_N(V).       (10)

There is ONE terminal error from (8), not the sum of all cut errors.
If N<=sigma r^2 and sigma is fixed with kappa' sigma<=c_r/2, where
0<kappa'<kappa, the second term of (9) is bounded by a fixed multiple
of exp[-kappa'(n-R0)]. Hence all finite coefficients satisfy

 |a_n| <= C eta P_I(1+r^2) exp[-kappa'(n-R0)].                  (11)

If also N>=sigma0 r^2 for fixed sigma0>0 and r is sufficiently large,
(8) bounds the terminal error by eta poly(r) exp(-c' r^2).
The simultaneous geometric requirement is N^2 delta<=c_* and the
torus must contain the largest input cube and collar. In the native
weak regime delta<=C g r, r=A log^p(g^-2), any fixed N~r^2 satisfies
N^2 delta=O(g r^5)->0. These explicit conditions cannot be omitted for
a different radius/coupling prescription or a small wrapping torus.

The rare-tail floor is not claimed to vanish as n tends to infinity at
fixed g. We have used a FINITE depth n<=O(r^2) and retained a terminal
error. No assertion that F_n itself is holomorphic for nonsmooth f is
made. To use (11) as analytic RG source coefficients, replace F_n by
consistent analytic physical-source projections, prove their complex
domains at these completed backgrounds, and retain their cut errors.
That is the next finite interface. The signed native Gaussian drift,
all-scale source transport, original complement summation, continuum
state identification and nontriviality remain separate requirements.
